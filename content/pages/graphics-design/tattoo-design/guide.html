What a good tattoo design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Placement and size considered.
  • Style: line work, fine line, blackwork, color.
  • Stencil or line version.
  • Shaded or color version.
  • Resolution for printing at full size.
  • Originality of the design.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a mockup on a photo of the body area. Seeing the design on the right part of the body at the right size shows whether it flows with the shape and looks balanced.

Look at the designer's portfolio for healed tattoos made from their designs, if they have them. Photos of fresh tattoos or digital drawings can look sharper than real skin does after healing. Designs that still look clear after healing show an understanding of how tattoos age.

How to brief a tattoo designer

  1. The idea and what it means to you.
  2. Placement on the body.
  3. Approximate size in centimeters or inches.
  4. Style references.
  5. Color or black and grey.
  6. Text, spelled exactly.
  7. Any existing tattoos nearby.

Measure the area and include a photo with a ruler. Exact size helps the designer decide how much detail is realistic and how thick lines should be.

Share what the tattoo means to you. Symbols, dates and personal stories help the designer create something personal rather than a generic image.

Show the final design to your tattoo artist before booking. They know how designs heal on skin and can suggest small adjustments that make the tattoo last better.

Think about how the tattoo will look in ten or twenty years. Bold lines, clear shapes and enough space between details tend to age better than tiny elements packed closely together. A good designer will happily explain these trade-offs and adjust the design to keep it readable over time.

What drives the price

  • size and detail
  • color versus black and grey
  • custom illustration complexity
  • cover-up of an existing tattoo
  • number of revisions
  • rush delivery

A small line-work symbol costs much less than a large detailed sleeve design with shading and color.

Red flags

  • Very fine details planned for a small tattoo.
  • No question about placement or size.
  • Designs copied from other people's tattoos.
  • Low-resolution files.
  • Text with spelling mistakes.

Take your time before getting the tattoo. Live with the design for a while and print it at real size. A permanent decision deserves a few extra days of thought.

Ask for a few small variations if you are unsure, such as a slightly larger size, thicker lines or one element removed. Comparing versions side by side, printed on paper and held against your skin, helps you choose with confidence.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I measured the placement area.
  • I chose a style that suits the size.
  • Text is spelled exactly right.
  • I will show the design to my tattoo artist.
  • The design is original.
