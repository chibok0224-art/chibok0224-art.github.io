The three tiers

Many sellers offer a basic, standard and premium package. The basic tier is usually the smallest version of the job, such as one design concept or a short edit. The higher tiers add more concepts, longer lengths, more revisions, faster delivery or extra files. The names are not standard, so always read the list of what is included rather than relying on the label.

A useful habit is to write your brief first, then pick the lowest tier that covers everything in it. If no tier fits, ask for a custom offer.

Revisions

Revisions are rounds of changes after you see the work. Packages often include a fixed number, and some offer unlimited revisions. Unlimited sounds attractive, but a clear number with a good brief usually works better, because both sides know when the project is finished.

Check what a revision covers. Changing colors or wording is normally a revision. Asking for a completely new idea after approving the direction is usually a new request.

Delivery time

The delivery time is the number of days the seller has after the order starts. It usually starts once you send the requirements, not when you pay, so send your brief and files quickly. Faster delivery is often offered as an extra. Use it only when you really need it; rushed work leaves less time for thought and revisions.

Extras

Extras are add-ons to a package, such as additional concepts, extra pages, background music, faster delivery or commercial use rights. They are useful when you need them, but they can add up. Compare the full cost of what you need, including extras, when you compare sellers.

Source files and formats

The files you receive matter as much as the work itself. A logo delivered only as a small image is much less useful than a logo with vector files and different color versions. A video without the project file cannot easily be edited later. Source files are often included only in the higher tiers. Decide in advance whether you need them, and check the exact formats listed in the package.

Usage rights

Usage rights describe how you may use the work. Many offers include full rights after payment; others limit commercial use, or charge extra for it. This matters most for logos, illustrations, music, voice recordings and anything used in advertising. Also check whether the seller uses stock images, fonts or music that come with their own licenses. When in doubt, ask the seller to confirm the rights in a message before you order.

Custom offers

When your project does not fit any package, sellers can usually send a custom offer with a scope, price and delivery time built around your brief. Custom offers are common for larger or unusual projects. Make sure the offer lists the deliverables, revisions, files and rights in writing, just as a package would, and keep the discussion on the platform so there is a record of what was agreed.

Questions to ask before ordering

If anything in a package is unclear, ask before you pay. Useful questions include: what exactly counts as a revision, when the delivery time starts, which file formats you will receive, whether the work uses stock or licensed elements, and whether commercial use is included. A short written answer from the seller avoids most disagreements later.

How to compare two offers fairly

  1. List the deliverables your brief needs.
  2. For each seller, find the lowest package that includes them.
  3. Add the extras you need, such as source files or commercial rights.
  4. Compare the total cost, delivery time and revisions side by side.
  5. Then compare portfolio quality and reviews, which matter most.
