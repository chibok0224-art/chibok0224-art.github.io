What a good AI spokesperson package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Video length and number of videos.
  • Avatar: stock or custom, with consent.
  • Voice and languages.
  • Script writing or editing.
  • Editing: graphics, captions and music.
  • Tool terms and usage rights.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short sample with your script before the full video. It shows how natural the avatar and voice sound and whether pronunciation of product names is correct.

Combine the avatar with screen recordings, product shots or simple graphics. Constant talking-head footage becomes tiring, while visuals that show what is being explained keep attention.

Watch sample videos from the maker with the sound on and off. Check lip sync, eye movement and gestures, and whether captions match the speech. Small glitches in mouth movement are what most quickly make viewers feel uncomfortable with an avatar.

How to brief an AI spokesperson maker

  1. Purpose and audience.
  2. Script or key points.
  3. Avatar preference and consent details.
  4. Voice and languages.
  5. Brand assets and visuals.
  6. Formats and lengths.
  7. Deadline.

Write short sentences for spoken delivery. AI voices handle simple, clear sentences far better than long ones with many clauses, and viewers follow them more easily.

List names, product terms and acronyms with pronunciation notes. Mispronounced brand names are the most common and most noticeable problem in AI presenter videos.

If you create an avatar of a real employee, get written consent that covers how long the avatar can be used and what happens if the person leaves.

What drives the price

  • number and length of videos
  • custom avatar creation
  • languages and translations
  • editing and graphics
  • tool subscription costs
  • rush delivery

A short video with a stock avatar costs much less than a series with a custom avatar, several languages and edited visuals.

Ask whether tool subscription fees are included, since many AI avatar tools charge monthly or per minute of video.

Red flags

  • No consent for a real person's likeness.
  • Stiff, unnatural delivery in samples.
  • Mispronounced names left unfixed.
  • No disclosure where required.
  • Unclear tool terms.

Review the video with someone from your audience before publishing. If they find the avatar distracting, consider shorter avatar segments with more visuals.

Tips for a smoother project

Choose an avatar that suits your audience and topic. A formal presenter may fit compliance training, while a friendlier style may suit onboarding or social media. Testing two avatars with a short script helps you decide which one viewers respond to best.

Plan updates from the start. One advantage of AI presenters is easy editing, so keep scripts in a shared document with version dates. When prices, features or policies change, you can update only the affected sentences and regenerate the video quickly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Consent covers any real person's likeness.
  • Pronunciation notes are included.
  • I will review a sample first.
  • Visuals support the avatar.
  • AI use will be disclosed where required.
