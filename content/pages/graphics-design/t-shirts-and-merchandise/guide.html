What a good merchandise design package includes

Merch design gigs are usually priced per design. Check that the package states:

  • Number of designs and concepts.
  • Print-ready files: resolution, transparent background or vector.
  • Color limits for your print method.
  • Placement and size on the product.
  • Mockups on the products you sell.
  • Rights to sell the design commercially.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Think about fabric color early. A design made for a white shirt may disappear on black or grey. Ask for versions for each shirt color you plan to offer, or a design that works on all of them.

How to brief a merch designer

  1. The product: T-shirt, hoodie, mug, sticker, tote bag.
  2. Print method and your printer's file requirements.
  3. The idea: text, theme, mood, audience.
  4. Style references, with notes on what you like.
  5. Brand files, if the design should include your logo.
  6. Product colors the design must work on.

Order one sample product before a large run. Colors on fabric often look different from the screen, and size and placement are easier to judge when you hold the real item. It is also a good photo for your shop.

If you sell through a print-on-demand service, check its template and guidelines for each product. Print areas, safe zones and file sizes vary between products and suppliers. Sharing the exact template with the designer avoids designs that are cut off or printed smaller than expected.

What drives the price

  • number of designs and concepts
  • detailed illustration versus text-based designs
  • vector artwork and color separations
  • versions for different product colors
  • mockups and listing images
  • commercial rights and the designer's experience

Think about the full range, not just one shirt. A few designs that share a style, color palette or theme make a shop look more professional and encourage people to buy more than one item. Ask the designer whether they can plan a small collection rather than unrelated one-off designs.

Red flags

  • Designs using famous characters, logos or lyrics without permission.
  • Artwork that looks copied from other shops.
  • Low-resolution files that will print blurry.
  • Unclear rights for selling products.
  • No questions about the print method.

Search for similar designs online before launching. If you find a close match in another shop, ask the designer about it. It protects you from takedowns on print-on-demand platforms, which remove listings when rights holders complain.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know the product, print method and file requirements.
  • The design will work on all my product colors.
  • I have commercial rights to the design in writing.
  • The design contains no characters, logos or lyrics I have no rights to.
  • I will order a sample before a large run.
