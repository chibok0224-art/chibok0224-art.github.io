What a good presentation package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Purpose and audience.
  • Story outline before slides.
  • Number of slides.
  • Content writing and data charts.
  • Design, if included.
  • Speaker notes and editable files.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Approve a story outline before slides are made. Agreeing on the structure first prevents a beautiful deck that tells the wrong story.

Ask for a one-page summary or appendix with extra detail. Audiences often want backup information, and it keeps the main slides clean.

Look at decks the expert has created and ask what happened after they were presented. A pitch that led to funding or a sales deck that shortened deals shows they understand persuasion, not only how to make slides look attractive.

How to brief a presentation expert

  1. Audience and what they care about.
  2. The one message they should remember.
  3. Time available.
  4. Content: notes, data, past decks.
  5. Brand template.
  6. Delivery setting: live, online or sent as a file.
  7. Deadline.

Decide whether the deck will be presented or read. Slides for a live talk need few words and strong visuals; decks sent by email need more text to stand alone. Mixing both purposes weakens each.

Gather your numbers in one place with sources. Charts are only as good as the data behind them, and last-minute data changes cause errors and delays.

Rehearse with the deck at least twice before the real presentation. Practice shows where slides are confusing, where the story drags and where you need better transitions.

Think about tough questions the audience might ask and prepare short answers or backup slides. Confidence in the question round often decides how a presentation is remembered.

What drives the price

  • number of slides
  • content writing
  • data analysis and charts
  • custom design
  • speaker notes and coaching
  • rush delivery

Editing an existing deck costs much less than creating a new story with research, charts, custom design and coaching.

Red flags

  • No story outline.
  • Slides full of text.
  • Charts without sources.
  • Templates that ignore your brand.
  • No editable files.

Keep a master version of the deck and save tailored versions for different audiences. It avoids sending outdated numbers.

Tips for a smoother project

Investor and sales decks often need several rounds as feedback comes in, so agree on how many revisions are included.

After presenting, note which slides prompted questions or confusion and update them for next time.

Prepare a short version of your presentation as well. Meetings get shortened, and having a five-minute version ready lets you deliver the key message clearly even when time runs out unexpectedly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I approved a story outline.
  • Numbers have sources.
  • I will get editable files and notes.
