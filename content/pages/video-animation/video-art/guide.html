What a good video art package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Concept and mood.
  • Length and loop requirements.
  • Resolution and aspect ratio.
  • File formats for playback systems.
  • Sound, if included.
  • Usage rights.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short test clip at the real resolution and on the real screen or projector if possible. Colors, brightness and fine details often look different in a venue than on a laptop.

Discuss how the work will be installed: media players, projector settings and file names. Technical preparation prevents problems on opening night.

Look at the artist's work shown in real spaces: photos or videos of installations, stage shows or screens in venues. Seeing how their visuals interact with architecture, crowds and lighting tells you much more than clips viewed on a computer screen.

How to brief a video artist

  1. The setting: gallery, stage, screen, online.
  2. Mood and ideas.
  3. References from art, film or design.
  4. Technical specs.
  5. Length and loop needs.
  6. Sound or music.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Describe the feeling you want the audience to have, such as calm, curious or energized. Video art works through atmosphere, and clear emotional goals guide the artist.

Share photos or plans of the space. Wall size, lighting, viewing distance and surfaces affect what visuals will work best.

Allow creative freedom within clear limits. Video artists bring unique ideas, and too many fixed instructions can produce a generic result.

Discuss how long the work will be shown and in what conditions. Installations running for weeks need stable files and simple restart instructions, while a one-night event may allow more complex live control. Planning for the real run time prevents technical failures.

What drives the price

  • length and complexity
  • 3D or generative techniques
  • projection mapping
  • resolution and formats
  • sound design
  • installation support

A short abstract loop costs much less than a large projection mapping show with custom 3D content and technical support.

Ask for documentation of the work, such as a short video of it installed. Documentation is valuable for portfolios, future funding or promoting the event afterward.

Red flags

  • No test on the real display.
  • Unlicensed footage or music.
  • Wrong resolution or aspect ratio.
  • No loop planning for installations.
  • Unclear rights.

Keep master files and playback versions. Exhibitions and events often repeat, and high-quality masters allow new formats later.

Plan the technical rehearsal. Projectors, LED walls and media servers need time to set up and test. Book a session where the artist or a technician can adjust brightness, alignment and timing before the audience arrives.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Technical specs are shared.
  • I will see a test clip on the real display.
  • Loops are seamless if needed.
  • Music and footage are licensed.
  • Usage rights are clear.
