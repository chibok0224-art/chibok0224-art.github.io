What a good consultation and training package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: review, advice or training.
  • Sessions and duration.
  • Materials and recordings.
  • Deliverables: report or plan.
  • Independence from vendors.
  • Follow-up.
  • Pricing.

Ask for a written summary with prioritized recommendations, costs and risks. It helps leadership decide and keeps teams aligned.

For training, request exercises using your own systems so skills transfer directly to daily work.

Ask consultants for examples of past recommendations and what the client decided. Practical, honest advice sometimes means recommending a cheaper option or no change at all. Consultants who can explain such cases are more trustworthy.

For training, plan sessions in small blocks with time to practice between them. People retain more when they can apply new skills in real work before the next session.

How to brief a tech consultant or trainer

  1. The decision or skill gap.
  2. Current systems.
  3. Team size and skills.
  4. Budget and constraints.
  5. Goals.
  6. Timeline.
  7. Who decides.

Explain the business reason behind the request, such as reducing cloud costs or speeding up releases. Business goals help the consultant focus on what matters.

Include the people who will use the advice or training. Their questions reveal real obstacles that managers may not see.

Ask for recordings and materials that new team members can use later. Training investments last longer when knowledge is stored, not only remembered by the people who attended.

Choose consultants who ask about your team and constraints before recommending tools. Advice that fits your skills, budget and existing systems is far more useful than a list of popular technologies that would require a complete rebuild.

What drives the price

  • session length
  • number of participants
  • custom materials
  • review depth
  • follow-up support
  • travel

A single advisory call costs much less than a full architecture review with workshops and team training.

Red flags

  • Generic advice.
  • Undisclosed vendor commissions.
  • No written summary.
  • Training without practice.
  • Recommendations beyond your budget.

Revisit recommendations after a few months and measure the results.

Tips for a smoother project

Consider a short follow-up session after a few weeks. Questions appear once people start using new tools, and quick answers prevent bad habits from forming.

Ask for a clear agenda before each session. Knowing the topics in advance lets participants prepare questions and examples, which makes the time much more productive.

Measure the results of training with simple indicators, such as fewer support tickets, faster deployments or less time spent on routine tasks.

Invite questions in writing before sessions as well. Some team members are more comfortable asking privately, and collecting questions early helps the trainer cover what people really need.
