What a good article package includes

Article and blog gigs are usually priced by length and number of posts. Beyond the word count, check that the package states:

  • Length per article, and whether it is a minimum or a range.
  • Topic research: whether the writer chooses sources or works only from your notes.
  • Search basics: target phrase, headings, a title and meta description.
  • Originality: a plagiarism check, and the writer's policy on AI tools.
  • Revisions: how many rounds, and what counts as a revision versus a rewrite.
  • Format: a document, or uploaded straight into your website.
  • Images: whether they are included, and where they come from.

How to brief a writer

The brief is where most of the quality comes from. For each article, send:

  1. The reader: who they are and what they already know.
  2. The question the article must answer, and the main search phrase if you have one.
  3. The goal: what the reader should do after reading, such as book a call or read a product page.
  4. Points that must be included, and your own experience or opinions the writer cannot find online.
  5. Tone and style, with a link to an article you like.
  6. Sources to use or avoid, including competitors you do not want to link to.
  7. Pages on your site the article should link to.

For a series of posts, write a short style sheet once and reuse it: spelling (US or UK English), how to refer to your company and products, words to avoid, and how to format headings and lists. It saves a revision round on every article.

What drives the price

  • length and number of articles
  • how technical the subject is, such as medical, legal or finance topics
  • research depth, interviews and original examples
  • search optimization and keyword research
  • images, uploading and formatting in your website
  • the writer's experience in your field, and rush delivery

Red flags

  • Very long articles delivered very fast at a very low price, with no stated policy on AI use.
  • Samples that are generic, repetitive or full of claims with no sources.
  • Text that fails a plagiarism check or matches other sites.
  • Promises of first-page search rankings. Writing alone cannot guarantee that.
  • No samples in your subject area when the topic is technical.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a brief with the reader, the question and the goal.
  • I have agreed on the writer's use of AI tools.
  • The package states length, revisions and format.
  • I will check originality and facts before publishing.
  • I have read at least two samples in a similar subject.
