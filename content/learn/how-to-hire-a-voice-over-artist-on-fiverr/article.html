The short version

Decide the voice you need, shortlist three or four artists by listening to their demos, ask for a short custom sample, then book with a clear script and a written note on where the recording will be used. Most problems with voice-over orders come from a vague script or from usage rights nobody discussed, not from the artist's voice.

How voice-over pricing works

  • Length. Most artists price by word count or by minutes of finished audio. Count your words before you compare packages.
  • Usage. Personal, internal or organic social use is usually included. Paid ads, TV, radio and large campaigns often cost more because the voice becomes part of your advertising.
  • Editing. Clean, edited files take more work than raw takes. Check which you are buying.
  • Extras. Background music, syncing to a video, splitting into separate files, or several styles of read are common add-ons.
  • Delivery time. Rush delivery usually costs extra.

Choosing the right voice

  1. Write down what the voice should feel like in three words, for example "warm, calm, trustworthy" or "energetic, young, playful".
  2. Listen to demos in the same style you need. A great commercial voice may not suit a 30-minute training course, and the reverse.
  3. Listen with headphones for echo, background noise and uneven volume.
  4. Check the accent and language carefully, especially if your audience is regional.
  5. Ask your top two or three for a sample of two or three lines from your actual script.

What to send when you book

  • The final script, with word count. Changing it after recording usually costs extra.
  • Where it will be used: YouTube, website, paid ads, e-learning, phone system.
  • A reference voice or video you like, plus the pace and tone.
  • Pronunciation of names, brand terms and any unusual words, ideally with a short recording of you saying them.
  • File format and whether you want one file or separate files per section.
  • The video, if the read must match timing.

Human or AI voice?

AI voices are fast and cheap for drafts, internal training and quick tests. A human voice usually works better for ads, brand videos and anything where emotion and timing matter. A common approach is to test the script with an AI voice, then book a person for the final. Our article on voice-over vs AI voice compares them.

Common mistakes

  • Sending a script that is still being edited.
  • Forgetting to mention that the audio will run in paid ads.
  • Choosing from the demo reel alone without a custom sample.
  • Not checking pronunciation of the brand name before recording.
