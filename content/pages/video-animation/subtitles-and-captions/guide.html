What a good captioning service includes

Caption and subtitle gigs are usually priced per minute of video. Check that the service states:

  • Type: same-language captions, translated subtitles, or both.
  • Method: human transcription, or automatic with human review.
  • Accuracy: how names, terms and unclear audio are handled.
  • Timing: captions matched to speech and easy to read.
  • Formats: SRT, VTT, or burned into the video.
  • Style for burned-in captions: font, size, position and colors.
  • Turnaround and revisions.

How to prepare for captioning

  1. The video files, or links to them.
  2. The platform where the video will be published.
  3. Names and terms: people, products, places and jargon, spelled correctly.
  4. A script or notes, if you have them.
  5. Languages for subtitles, and the regional variant.
  6. Style rules: line length, sound cues, speaker labels.
  7. Burned-in style, if needed, with an example you like.

Watch the captioned video once with the sound off. Check that captions appear in time, stay on screen long enough to read, and never cover important parts of the picture, such as faces or on-screen text.

Keep the caption files with your video files. You can reuse them for translations, blog posts and show notes, and they are easy to upload again if you move the video to another platform.

What drives the price

  • length of the video
  • audio quality, accents and number of speakers
  • technical or specialist vocabulary
  • translation into other languages
  • burned-in styled captions
  • turnaround time

Red flags

  • Automatic captions delivered with no review.
  • Obvious errors in names and terms in the samples.
  • Captions that appear too early, too late, or too fast to read.
  • Translations with no native speaker involved.
  • Files in a format your platform does not accept.

If you publish videos regularly, agree on a caption style once and keep it for every video: the same font, size, position and way of marking speakers or sounds. Consistent captions look professional and make your videos easier to recognize in a busy feed. Share a finished example with the captioner each time, along with an updated list of names and terms. It reduces errors, speeds up turnaround and makes it easy for a different captioner to match your style if your usual one is unavailable, which helps you keep a steady publishing schedule.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know whether I need captions, subtitles, or both.
  • I have sent names, terms and any script.
  • Automatic captions will be checked by a person.
  • The file format works on my platform.
  • I will watch the result with the sound off.
