What a good pattern design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of patterns and colorways.
  • Repeat type and tile size.
  • Seamless check at full scale.
  • File formats: vector and high-resolution tiles.
  • Color codes for your print method.
  • License: commercial use and exclusivity.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a mockup of the pattern on your product at real size. A motif that looks perfect on screen may be too small or too busy on a dress, a pillow or a box.

Request extra colorways if you plan several products. Matching color versions of the same pattern create a collection and are usually cheaper to order together.

Look for designers whose portfolio shows patterns on real products, not only flat tiles. Mockups on fabric, packaging or wallpaper show that they understand scale and how a design behaves across large areas, which is the hardest part of pattern work.

How to brief a pattern designer

  1. The product and how it is printed.
  2. Theme and motifs.
  3. Style: hand-drawn, geometric, floral, minimal.
  4. Colors or a limit on the number of colors.
  5. Scale on the final product.
  6. Printer requirements and file format.
  7. Deadline.

Share your printer's technical sheet if you have one. Requirements like maximum colors, resolution and tile size affect how the designer builds the pattern from the start.

Think about the audience and season. A pattern for children's clothing, a luxury brand or holiday packaging needs different motifs, colors and density, even if the theme is the same.

If you will print on fabric, ask the designer to consider how the pattern looks on folds and seams. Very large motifs can be cut off awkwardly on small garments.

What drives the price

  • number of patterns and colorways
  • complexity of motifs
  • hand-drawn versus digital style
  • vector work
  • exclusive license
  • rush delivery

A simple geometric repeat costs much less than a detailed hand-painted floral pattern with several colorways and exclusive rights.

Red flags

  • Visible seams when tiles repeat.
  • Only low-resolution files.
  • No commercial license for selling products.
  • Patterns copied from known brands.
  • No color guidance for printing.

Order a physical sample before a large production run. Fabric, paper and plastic all change how colors and details appear, and a sample is far cheaper than a reprint.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep a simple record of each pattern: the file names, colorways, color codes, license terms and where it has been used. As your collection grows, this record makes reorders, new products and conversations with printers much easier.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know how the pattern will be printed.
  • The repeat is checked at full scale.
  • I will get vector and high-resolution files.
  • The license covers selling products.
  • I will order a sample before production.
