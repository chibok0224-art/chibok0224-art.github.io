What a good marketing consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Session length and format.
  • Preparation: whether they review your materials first.
  • Channels covered.
  • Written summary or action plan.
  • Follow-up questions after the call.
  • Recording, if allowed.
  • Industry experience.

Choose consultants who review your website, ads or analytics before the call. Preparation turns a general chat into specific advice.

Ask for a prioritized list at the end: what to fix first, what to test next and what to stop doing. Priorities matter more than a long list of ideas.

Check whether the consultant has worked with businesses at your stage. Advice for a company with a large team and budget often does not fit a founder doing everything alone, and the best consultants adjust recommendations to the time and money you really have.

How to brief a marketing consultant

  1. Business and offer.
  2. Audience.
  3. Goals for the next few months.
  4. Current channels and recent results.
  5. Budget and team time.
  6. Top questions.
  7. Access to materials or read-only analytics.

Share real numbers if you can, such as traffic, conversion rates and costs. Advice based on data is far more useful than advice based on guesses.

Give read-only access instead of passwords, and remove access when the work is done.

Limit your questions to the three that matter most. A focused session produces clearer answers than trying to cover every channel at once.

What drives the price

  • session length and number of sessions
  • depth of preparation and review
  • written plan or audit
  • consultant experience
  • number of channels
  • ongoing mentoring

A single call costs much less than a full marketing review with a written plan and monthly mentoring sessions.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed growth or sales.
  • Generic advice that ignores your data.
  • Constant pushing of their own done-for-you services.
  • Requests for full account passwords.
  • No summary or next steps.

Be wary of consultants who criticize everything without explaining why. Good advice explains the reasoning so you can make your own decisions later.

Tips for a smoother project

Record the session or take notes, then turn the advice into a short task list with owners and dates.

Test one recommendation at a time where possible. Changing many things at once makes it hard to see what worked.

Book a follow-up after a few weeks to review results. A second session based on real outcomes is often more valuable than the first.

Ask the consultant to explain which results to watch after each change, such as sign-ups, cost per lead or repeat purchases. Knowing what success looks like helps you judge whether the advice worked, and it gives you better questions for your next session. Keep the summary and your results together so that progress is easy to review.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My goals and questions are clear.
  • The consultant will review materials first.
  • I will get a written summary.
  • Access is read-only.
  • Nobody guarantees results.
