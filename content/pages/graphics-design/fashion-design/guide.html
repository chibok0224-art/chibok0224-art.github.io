What a good fashion design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of styles.
  • Stages: mood board, sketches, flats, tech pack.
  • Measurements and size range.
  • Materials and trims.
  • File formats.
  • Ownership.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask your factory for their preferred tech pack format before the designer starts. Using the factory's template avoids missing information and costly sample rounds.

Request a size chart with grading for all sizes you plan to produce. Fit problems are the most common reason for returns in clothing.

Look at the designer's tech packs as well as their sketches. Clear measurements, construction notes and material details show they understand how clothing is made, which matters more for production than beautiful illustrations alone.

How to brief a fashion designer

  1. Your brand and customer.
  2. Garment types and quantity.
  3. Mood and references.
  4. Fabrics and budget per piece.
  5. Size range.
  6. Factory or production plans.
  7. Season and deadline.

Share a target cost per garment. Fabric, trims and construction choices affect production cost, and designing within budget avoids redesigns later.

Describe your customer's lifestyle. A design for active outdoor use differs from one for evening wear, even in similar styles.

Plan samples and fit sessions. Even the best tech pack needs at least one sample round to check fit and finish.

Check that designs are original. Close copies of other brands can lead to legal problems and damage your reputation.

Consider sustainability and care requirements. Fabric choices affect durability, washing instructions and how customers perceive your brand. A designer can suggest materials that balance style, cost and longevity for your target customers.

What drives the price

  • number of styles
  • design complexity
  • tech pack detail
  • size range and grading
  • illustrations
  • sourcing help

Sketches for a few styles cost much less than a full collection with tech packs, size grading and sourcing support.

Factory samples and fabric costs are separate from design fees.

Red flags

  • No tech packs.
  • Designs copied from known brands.
  • Missing measurements.
  • No understanding of production.
  • Unclear ownership.

Keep tech packs updated after each sample round. The latest version should always reflect approved changes.

Tips for a smoother project

Ask how the designer prefers to receive feedback on samples. Photos of the sample on a real person, with notes on fit at shoulders, waist and length, help the designer update the tech pack accurately. Vague comments like it looks wrong slow the process down.

Think about your first production run. Starting with a few styles in limited sizes reduces risk while you test fit, quality and customer interest. A designer can help you choose which pieces to produce first.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared a target cost per garment.
  • Tech packs follow my factory's format.
  • Size charts cover all sizes.
  • Designs are original.
  • I own the files.
