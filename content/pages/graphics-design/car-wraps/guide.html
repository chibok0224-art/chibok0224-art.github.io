What a good car wrap design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Vehicle template for your exact model.
  • Coverage: full wrap, partial wrap or decals.
  • Mockups of all sides.
  • Print-ready files to the shop's specs.
  • Editable source files.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for mockups of every side, plus the back and the roof if visible. People often see vehicles from behind in traffic, so the rear needs a clear logo and contact detail.

Have the wrap shop review the files before printing. Installers know where vinyl stretches on curves and where details may distort, and they can suggest small changes that save money and rework.

Look at photos of wrapped vehicles in the designer's portfolio, not only flat mockups. Real photos show how graphics behave over curves, doors and wheel arches, and whether logos and text stay readable when the vehicle is seen from an angle.

How to brief a car wrap designer

  1. Vehicle make, model and year, with photos.
  2. Coverage: full, partial or lettering only.
  3. Brand assets: logo, colors and fonts.
  4. Message: what you do and one contact method.
  5. Images to include, if any.
  6. Wrap shop specs.
  7. Deadline.

Photograph the vehicle from all sides, including handles, lights and any existing graphics. Real photos help the designer avoid placing important text on areas that do not wrap well.

Choose one contact method and make it big: a website, a phone number or a social handle. Several small contact lines are harder to read and remember than one large one.

Think about how the design looks in traffic and parked. Bold colors and a strong logo work at a distance, while finer details are only seen up close by people walking past.

What drives the price

  • vehicle size
  • full or partial coverage
  • number of vehicles
  • custom illustration or photos
  • fleet variations
  • rush delivery

Simple door logos and lettering cost much less than a full wrap design with custom graphics for a fleet of different vehicles.

Red flags

  • No vehicle template or wrong model.
  • Text placed on handles, seams or deep curves.
  • Too much small text.
  • Low-resolution images for large prints.
  • Files not checked by the wrap shop.

Check local rules about vehicle graphics and window coverage before finalizing. Some areas limit what can be placed on windows or how reflective materials can be.

Tips for a smoother project

Think about the vehicle's life. If you may sell or replace it in a few years, partial wraps and removable decals can be easier to change. A modular design also helps when you add more vehicles of different sizes to your fleet later.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The designer has my exact vehicle template.
  • Important text avoids handles and curves.
  • There is one large contact method.
  • The wrap shop approved the files.
  • I checked local rules for vehicle graphics.
