What a good session musician package includes

Session gigs are priced by song length, number of parts and instruments. Check that the package states:

  • Instrument and number of parts or takes.
  • Written or improvised parts.
  • Files: WAV, sample rate, dry or with effects.
  • Alignment: files starting at bar one or a set point.
  • Revisions.
  • Rights and credits.
  • Delivery time.

Listen to raw or lightly mixed samples if possible. Finished songs can hide timing and tone issues; raw recordings show the musician's real playing and recording quality.

Ask whether the musician can send a short rough idea before recording the full part. A quick phone recording or a few bars over your track confirms the direction, and it prevents a fully produced part that does not fit the song's feel or style.

How to brief a session musician

  1. The rough mix or instrumental.
  2. Tempo and key, plus tempo changes if any.
  3. Chords or MIDI for the parts.
  4. Reference tracks for the sound and feel.
  5. Where the part plays and where it should be quiet.
  6. Tone preferences: clean, distorted, warm, bright.

Export your rough mix from the very start of the session with a click or count-in, and tell the musician the exact tempo and sample rate. Files that line up perfectly save hours of editing on your side.

Describe the role of the part in the song. A guitar that supports the vocal quietly is played very differently from a lead part in the chorus, and knowing this helps the musician make the right choices.

Give feedback with time stamps and examples. Musicians can adjust feel, tone and density quickly when they know exactly what to change.

What drives the price

  • song length and number of parts
  • instrument and difficulty
  • writing parts versus playing written parts
  • number of takes and doubles
  • editing and comping
  • the musician's experience and credits

Some instruments, such as live strings or brass sections, involve several players or overdubs and cost more than a single guitar or bass part.

Red flags

  • No raw samples.
  • Unclear rights or royalty terms.
  • Files that do not line up or are delivered with heavy effects only.
  • No questions about tempo, key or role.
  • Poor recording quality with noise or room echo.

Check the files in your project as soon as they arrive. Timing, tuning or noise issues are easiest to fix while the musician still has the session open.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep a list of musicians whose work you liked, with notes on their strengths. Building a small, reliable network of session players makes future songs faster to produce and gives your music a consistent quality from one release to the next.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Can I hear raw, unprocessed recordings of your playing?
  • Which tempo, key and file format do you need from me?
  • Will you deliver dry tracks that line up with my session, plus a processed version if wanted?
  • How many takes or variations are included?
  • Which rights do I get, and are there any royalties?

Send a rough mix with a click track and clear notes on the part's role, such as a simple supportive line or a featured solo. The more the musician knows about the song, the fewer retakes you need.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have sent a rough mix, tempo, key and chords.
  • I have described the part's role and tone.
  • Files will be dry WAVs aligned to my session.
  • Rights and credits are agreed in writing.
  • I will check files as soon as they arrive.
