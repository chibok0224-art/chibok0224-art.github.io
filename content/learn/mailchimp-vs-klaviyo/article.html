Mailchimp

Mailchimp is a general email marketing tool with a friendly editor, templates, sign-up forms, simple automations and reports. It suits newsletters, local businesses, creators and small shops that want an easy start. It can connect to many online tools. For complex store flows or deep segmentation by purchase behavior, it may feel less specialized.

Klaviyo

Klaviyo focuses on online stores. It connects to store platforms and records what customers view, add to the basket and buy, then lets you build messages that respond to those actions: welcome series, abandoned basket reminders, post-purchase flows and win-back campaigns. Segmentation is powerful, and so is reporting by revenue. The depth takes more time to learn, and costs grow as your list grows.

How to choose

If you send a monthly newsletter or announcements, Mailchimp is probably enough. If you run an online store and want automated messages based on what customers do, Klaviyo is built for it. Consider your list size, your store platform and who will manage the tool. A tool that is too complicated for your team will not get used, no matter how powerful it is.

What to ask a freelancer to set up

A good first setup includes a sign-up form, a welcome sequence, templates that match your brand, a regular newsletter layout and reports you can read. For stores, add abandoned basket and post-purchase flows. Ask for clear consent settings, an unsubscribe link in every message and a plan for keeping your list clean. Ask for a short training so you can send campaigns yourself.

Questions to ask

  • Which platform do you recommend for my store and why?
  • Which automated messages will you set up first?
  • How will you handle consent and unsubscribes?
  • Will the templates match my brand and work on phones?
  • Who owns the account?
  • What reports will I receive?

Budget and growth

Both platforms charge based on how many contacts you have, so prices rise as your list grows. Check the current plans for the size of your list today and in a year, and remember that cleaning out inactive contacts can lower costs and improve results. Ask the freelancer to estimate how many messages and flows you will send, since that affects both the plan and the setup time.

Measuring results

Look beyond open rates. Track clicks, revenue from emails, unsubscribe rates and how many messages reach the inbox. For automated flows, check which step loses people and test one change at a time, such as a subject line or an offer. Review reports monthly and write short notes about what you learned, so future campaigns improve on the last one instead of starting from scratch.

Try this first

Open a free account on each platform and build a simple welcome email with a sign-up form. Notice how easy it is to find what you need, how the editor feels and how the reports look. Import a small test list and send yourself a message on a phone and a computer. Ten minutes of hands-on use will tell you more than any feature table, and you can show your findings to a freelancer so they can recommend a setup that fits your habits.

Keep notes of what you tried and what you liked.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a list instead of building one with permission.
  • Sending without checking how messages look on a phone.
  • Setting up flows and never reviewing their results.
  • Choosing a tool by features you will not use.
