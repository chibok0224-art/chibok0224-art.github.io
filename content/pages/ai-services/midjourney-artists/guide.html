What a good Midjourney art package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of final images.
  • Resolution and upscaling.
  • Editing to fix flaws.
  • Style consistency across the set.
  • Usage rights and tool terms.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a few draft images before the final set. Choosing a direction early saves time and makes the final images more consistent with what you imagined.

Check every image at full size. AI images can hide extra fingers, warped objects or strange text that only show when you zoom in or print large.

Look at the artist's finished projects, not just single striking images. A set of matching images for a campaign or a book shows whether they can keep characters, colors and lighting consistent, which is the hardest part of working with AI image tools.

How to brief a Midjourney artist

  1. Purpose: ads, book, social, concept art.
  2. Subject and scene.
  3. Style references, not living artists' names.
  4. Colors and mood.
  5. Sizes and formats.
  6. Text, added afterwards by a designer.
  7. Usage and deadline.

Plan to add text separately. AI tools often produce unreadable lettering, so titles and slogans are best added in design software after the image is finished.

Be transparent with your audience where it matters. Some platforms, publishers and customers expect AI-generated images to be disclosed, and honest labeling protects your reputation.

Discuss the image sizes and shapes you need before generation. Wide banners, square posts, vertical stories and print covers each need different compositions. Planning formats upfront avoids cropping important parts of an image that was generated for another shape.

What drives the price

  • number of images
  • editing and retouching
  • upscaling for print
  • consistency across a series
  • commercial usage
  • rush delivery

A few social media images cost much less than a large consistent series edited for print and commercial use.

If people appear in the images, avoid generating recognizable real individuals without permission. Use fictional characters or licensed photos instead. It protects you from complaints and keeps your visuals clear of personality and likeness issues.

Red flags

  • Unclear commercial usage rights.
  • Visible errors left unedited.
  • Prompts imitating living artists or famous characters.
  • Low resolution for print.
  • No drafts before the final set.

Keep a record of how each image was made: the tool, the date and the main edits. It helps answer questions about rights and makes future images easier to match.

For hand-made art, see our illustration guide. For finishing touches, read our image editing guide and book cover guide.

Store final images with their source files and edit layers. If you need a different size, a variation or a fix later, having the edited files avoids starting again from new AI generations that may not match the originals.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I confirmed commercial use is allowed.
  • I will review drafts first.
  • Flaws will be edited out.
  • Text will be added separately.
  • The style is original.
