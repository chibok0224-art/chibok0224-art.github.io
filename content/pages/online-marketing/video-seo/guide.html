What a good video SEO package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of videos or a full channel audit.
  • Keyword research for your topics.
  • Titles and descriptions rewritten.
  • Chapters, tags and playlists.
  • Thumbnail and title feedback.
  • An action list for future videos.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

The most useful output is often a short list of changes for your next videos: topics people search for, title patterns and thumbnail ideas. It keeps working long after the first optimization.

Look at channels the expert has worked on and how their recent videos are titled and presented. Good examples show clear, honest titles that match the video, readable thumbnails and descriptions that help viewers, not lists of repeated keywords that look like spam.

How to brief a video SEO expert

  1. Your channel and main topics.
  2. Target viewers and countries.
  3. Goals: views, subscribers, leads or sales.
  4. Best and worst videos, in your view.
  5. Competitor channels you admire.
  6. Analytics access via channel permissions.
  7. Deadline.

Share your analytics, not only your videos. Watch time, traffic sources and click-through rate show where the real problem is: being found, being clicked or being watched.

Expect advice about content as well as words. If viewers leave early, better titles alone will not help much. A good expert will tell you what to change in the videos themselves.

Ask how the expert decides which older videos to update first. Videos that already get impressions but few clicks, or that rank for useful searches, often improve fastest. A clear way of choosing priorities shows the expert relies on your data rather than on generic rules.

What drives the price

  • number of videos
  • depth of the channel audit
  • keyword and competitor research
  • thumbnail work included
  • ongoing monthly support
  • multiple languages

Optimizing a handful of videos costs much less than a full audit with research, a content plan and monthly reviews.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed views, rankings or subscribers.
  • Offers of bought views, likes or comments.
  • Requests for your login password.
  • Keyword stuffing in titles and descriptions.
  • No explanation of what was changed and why.

Change titles and thumbnails thoughtfully and give each change time. Changing everything at once makes it hard to see what actually helped.

Tips for a smoother project

Track results in your own analytics for at least a few weeks after changes. Compare impressions, click-through rate and views from search with the period before. Writing down what was changed and when makes it much easier to learn what works for your channel.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I will share analytics through channel permissions.
  • No fake views or engagement are involved.
  • I know my target viewers and topics.
  • I will get an action list for future videos.
  • Nobody promised guaranteed numbers.
