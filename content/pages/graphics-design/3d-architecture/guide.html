What a good 3D visualization package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of views: exterior, interior, aerial.
  • Resolution and formats.
  • Materials and furniture.
  • Lighting: day, night, season.
  • Animation, if included.
  • Revisions by stage.
  • Delivery time.

Approve camera angles with quick clay renders before materials and lighting. Choosing the right views early saves full-quality re-renders.

Ask for a materials board or list showing finishes for floors, walls and facades. It avoids surprises in the final images.

Look at the artist's portfolio for projects similar to yours: homes, apartments, offices or commercial buildings. Realistic scale, believable materials and natural lighting are signs of skill. Overly perfect images with fake-looking grass or reflections are a warning.

How to brief a 3D visualization artist

  1. Drawings or model.
  2. Views you need.
  3. Materials and colors.
  4. Furniture and style references.
  5. Surroundings: street, landscape, neighbors.
  6. Use: marketing, approval, presentation.
  7. Deadline.

Provide final or nearly final drawings. Changes to the design after modeling begins add time and cost.

Share the real surroundings, such as neighboring buildings and views. Honest context makes renders more believable and avoids misleading buyers.

Choose lighting that matches how people will experience the space, such as warm evening light for homes or bright daylight for offices.

Consider adding people, furniture and seasonal touches carefully. They make spaces feel lived in and show scale, but too many props distract from the architecture. Ask the artist to keep the focus on the design while adding just enough life.

What drives the price

  • number of views
  • level of realism
  • interior detail and furniture
  • animation
  • landscape and surroundings
  • rush delivery

A few exterior views cost much less than a full set of interiors, exteriors and an animated walkthrough.

Ask whether the artist can provide a few quick variations, such as different facade colors or flooring. Comparing options side by side helps clients and buyers make decisions faster.

Red flags

  • No clay render or angle approval stage.
  • Unrealistic scale or proportions.
  • Misleading surroundings.
  • Low resolution for print.
  • Unclear revision limits.

Keep the 3D model files. Future marketing or design changes can reuse them.

Plan how the renders will be used before ordering. Brochures, websites, billboards and sales screens each need different resolutions and aspect ratios. Asking for the right sizes upfront saves extra rendering time later.

Agree on who supplies furniture and decor models. Some artists have large libraries; others charge extra for custom pieces. If specific branded furniture must appear, provide product links or models early so the renders match your real plans.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared final drawings.
  • Camera angles are approved first.
  • Materials are listed.
  • Surroundings are honest.
  • I will get high-resolution files.
