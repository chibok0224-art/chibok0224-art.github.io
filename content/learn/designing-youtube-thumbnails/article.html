1. Explain your channel

Tell the designer who your viewers are, what the channel is about and what style you prefer. Share links to your channel and a few videos so they can see your tone. Mention whether you want a consistent look across all thumbnails or each one to stand out. Consistent colors and fonts make a channel recognizable, which helps returning viewers spot new videos in a crowded feed.

2. Describe the video

For each thumbnail, give the title, a short summary and the main promise: what will viewers learn or feel? Point out the most interesting moment, the key object or the strongest emotion in the video. A thumbnail should match the content honestly. Misleading images may earn a click, but they lose trust and may lead people to leave quickly, which hurts the video.

3. Choose the elements

Decide what should be in the picture: your face with an expression, a product, a before-and-after comparison or a simple graphic. Send clear, high-quality photos, screenshots or frames from the video. If you want your face, send a few photos with different expressions and good lighting. Avoid cluttered backgrounds, since a thumbnail is seen at very small sizes and too many details become noise.

4. Keep text short

If you want words on the thumbnail, keep them to a few large, high-contrast ones that add to the title instead of repeating it. Provide the exact text and check spelling. Choose fonts and colors that stay readable on both light and dark backgrounds, and check how the thumbnail looks next to others on a phone screen.

5. Share references

Send examples of thumbnails you like, preferably from channels in your topic, and say what you like about each: the colors, layout, expression or text style. Also share examples you dislike. Avoid asking for a copy of another creator's thumbnail; a good designer will make something new that fits your channel while using the same principles of clarity and contrast.

6. Agree sizes and delivery

Confirm the dimensions, the file format and the maximum file size allowed by the platform. Ask for a layered source file if you want to edit text or swap images later. Agree on how many revisions are included and how quickly you expect each version. For a series, ask whether a reusable template is provided so that you can create consistent thumbnails quickly.

Questions to ask the designer

  • Can you show thumbnails for channels like mine?
  • How many concepts and revisions are included?
  • Can you deliver two versions for testing?
  • Will I receive the editable source file?
  • How fast can you turn around a thumbnail when I upload often?
  • What do you need from me for each video?

Judging the result

View the thumbnail at the small size it will appear on a phone, next to other videos. Ask whether you can tell what the video is about in a second. Check that the text is readable, the main subject stands out and the image is honest about the content. Give feedback that explains the effect you want, such as make the face larger and the text shorter, rather than dictating every pixel.

Budget and turnaround

Thumbnail prices depend on complexity: a simple text-and-photo layout is quicker than a composite with several cut-out elements. If you publish often, ask about a package for a set number of thumbnails per month, which usually costs less per image and keeps the style consistent. Share your upload schedule so that the designer can plan, and send materials as early as possible, since a rushed thumbnail rarely matches the effort put into the video itself.

Common mistakes

  • Putting too much text on the image.
  • Using photos that are blurry or poorly lit.
  • Promising something the video does not deliver.
  • Changing the style every week, which makes the channel hard to recognize.
