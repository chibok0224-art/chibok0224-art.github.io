What a good AI lessons package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Learning goals.
  • Tools covered.
  • Format: live, recorded, one-to-one or group.
  • Number and length of sessions.
  • Exercises with your tasks.
  • Materials and recordings.
  • Follow-up support.

Ask for a short outline before booking. It shows whether the lessons focus on your tasks and level, or follow a generic script.

Ask the tutor to show a short sample lesson or exercise. Good trainers explain clearly, use realistic examples and check understanding with practice tasks. A sample reveals whether their teaching style suits you or your team before you commit to several sessions.

How to brief an AI tutor

  1. Your role and typical tasks.
  2. Current skill level.
  3. Tools you have access to.
  4. Goals for after the lessons.
  5. Company rules on AI use.
  6. Format and schedule.
  7. Group size, for teams.

Bring real tasks to the session, such as an email you write often, a report you prepare or data you analyze. Practicing on your own work makes the skills stick.

Ask for a short list of prompts and workflows you can reuse. Practical templates are what most learners keep using after the lessons end.

For teams, include company rules on what data may be shared with AI tools. Clear guidance prevents accidental sharing of confidential information.

Ask whether lessons cover how to check AI output. Learners should know how to verify facts, spot made-up references and decide when a task needs human judgment. These habits matter more over time than knowing every feature of a particular tool.

What drives the price

  • number of sessions
  • one-to-one versus group
  • custom materials
  • tool depth
  • recordings and support
  • travel for in-person sessions

A single one-to-one session costs much less than a multi-week team program with custom materials, exercises and follow-up support.

Red flags

  • Generic tool tours with no practice.
  • No mention of privacy or checking output.
  • Outdated tool knowledge.
  • No materials to keep.
  • Promises of instant expertise.

Practice right after each session. Using new skills within a day or two makes them much easier to remember and apply.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan a follow-up a few weeks after the lessons. A short session to answer questions that came up in real use helps learners overcome obstacles and turns early enthusiasm into lasting habits, which is where most of the value of training appears.

For teams, mix skill levels thoughtfully. Beginners and advanced users need different pace and depth. Separate sessions or a short advanced follow-up keeps everyone engaged and avoids losing either group halfway through the training.
