What a good beta reading package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Word count covered.
  • Feedback format: report, inline comments or both.
  • Questions answered: pacing, characters, plot, clarity.
  • Genre experience.
  • Confidentiality.
  • Delivery time.
  • Follow-up questions, if allowed.

Ask for a sample of a past report with private details removed. It shows whether the reader gives specific, actionable notes or general impressions.

Consider using more than one beta reader. Different readers notice different things, and a problem mentioned by several people is clearly worth fixing.

Check how the reader describes their own reading habits. Someone who reads many books in your genre each year will compare yours to what readers expect. Their sense of pace, tropes and endings is exactly what you are paying to borrow.

How to brief a beta reader

  1. Genre and target readers.
  2. Word count and format.
  3. A short summary without spoilers, if preferred.
  4. Specific questions you want answered.
  5. Known weak spots you are worried about.
  6. Content notes for sensitive themes.
  7. Deadline.

Ask specific questions: where did you lose interest, which character did you care about, was the twist predictable? Targeted questions bring far more useful answers than asking whether the reader liked the book.

Mention sensitive content up front, such as violence or difficult themes. It lets the reader decide whether they are a good fit and prepares them to give thoughtful feedback.

Read feedback calmly and wait a day before reacting. Not every note needs action, but patterns across readers usually point to real problems worth solving.

Think about timing in your writing process. Beta readers are most useful after you have revised the draft yourself at least once but before you pay for detailed editing. Sending too early wastes their time on problems you already know about.

What drives the price

  • manuscript length
  • report depth
  • inline comments
  • genre specialization
  • multiple readers
  • rush delivery

A short general report costs less than a detailed report with chapter-by-chapter notes and inline comments.

Red flags

  • Readers who do not read your genre.
  • Vague feedback with no examples.
  • Heavy line editing instead of reader feedback.
  • No confidentiality.
  • Unrealistic praise without any criticism.

Keep a list of reader notes and the changes you made. It helps you see your progress and gives editors useful context later.

Decide in advance what you will do with the feedback. Some notes call for small fixes, others for rethinking a chapter or character. Sorting notes into quick fixes, bigger changes and points you disagree with makes revision manageable rather than overwhelming.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My draft is complete.
  • The reader knows my genre.
  • I sent specific questions.
  • Confidentiality is agreed.
  • I know which feedback format I will get.
