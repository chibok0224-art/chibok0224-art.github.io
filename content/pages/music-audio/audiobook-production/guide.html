What a good audiobook package includes

Audiobook gigs are often priced per finished hour. Check that the package states:

  • Narration: one narrator, or several voices.
  • Editing: removing mistakes, breaths and noise.
  • Mastering to your platform's technical requirements.
  • Files: one file per chapter, plus opening and closing credits.
  • Proofing: listening back against the text for errors.
  • Revisions for errors found in your review.
  • Rights and payment terms.

Ask for a short audition from the actual book, not a general demo. It shows how the narrator handles your writing style, dialogue and characters, and gives you something to compare between candidates before committing to many hours of recording.

How to brief an audiobook narrator

  1. The final manuscript, fully edited.
  2. Genre and audience.
  3. Character notes: ages, accents and personalities.
  4. Pronunciation list with audio examples if possible.
  5. Platform requirements.
  6. Deadline and release plans.

Approve the first chapter or first fifteen minutes before the narrator records the rest. It is the moment to adjust pace, character voices and tone, while changes are still cheap.

Listen to the finished files while following the text. Check for missed lines, mispronounced names and noises. Mark errors with chapter and time stamps so the narrator can fix them quickly.

Ask how the narrator records and edits. A quiet, treated room, a good microphone and careful editing make the difference between a professional audiobook and one that listeners find tiring. Also ask whether the narrator checks their own recordings against the text before delivery, since catching missed lines early saves you a lot of time during your review. Keep a clean copy of the final manuscript used for recording, so corrections can be checked against the same version.

What drives the price

  • length of the finished audiobook
  • number of voices and accents
  • editing and mastering included
  • proofing by a second listener
  • fee per hour versus royalty share
  • the narrator's experience

Red flags

  • No audition from your book.
  • Samples with background noise or room echo.
  • No knowledge of platform requirements.
  • Unclear rights or payment terms.
  • AI voices presented as human narration without disclosure.

Some platforms have rules about AI narration and require disclosure. If you consider AI voices, check the rules of every platform you plan to use, and be honest with listeners.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have heard an audition from my own book.
  • My manuscript is final and I have a pronunciation list.
  • The narrator knows my platform's requirements.
  • I will approve the first chapter before the rest.
  • Rights and payment terms are agreed in writing.
