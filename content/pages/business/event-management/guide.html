What a good event management package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Event type and size.
  • Planning deliverables: timeline, budget, run-of-show.
  • Vendor research and coordination.
  • Registration and communication.
  • Day-of support, remote or on-site.
  • Post-event follow-up.
  • Pricing and schedule.

Ask for a run-of-show document: a minute-by-minute schedule with who does what. It is the most useful single document on event day, for both virtual and in-person events.

Ask the planner for examples of similar events and what their role was. Planning a webinar series, a product launch and a destination retreat require different skills, and experience with your event type means fewer surprises and better vendor suggestions.

How to brief an event manager

  1. Event goal and audience.
  2. Date, location or platform.
  3. Expected attendance.
  4. Budget.
  5. Program: speakers, sessions, activities.
  6. Vendors already booked.
  7. Decision makers and deadlines.

Fix the budget early and include a contingency. Catering numbers, travel and technical needs often grow, and a reserve prevents last-minute cuts that hurt the experience.

Confirm every vendor in writing with times, quantities and contact details. Most event-day problems trace back to assumptions that were never confirmed.

For virtual events, schedule a technical rehearsal with all speakers. Sound, slides and screen sharing cause the most common and most visible problems.

Think about accessibility and comfort for attendees: step-free access, clear signs, captions for virtual sessions, dietary options and quiet spaces. Small considerations make events welcoming to more people and are easier to arrange during planning than on the day.

What drives the price

  • event size and complexity
  • planning time
  • number of vendors
  • virtual platform management
  • on-site support
  • travel and logistics

Planning a small webinar costs much less than coordinating a multi-day conference with travel, vendors and hybrid streaming.

Keep all contracts, invoices and confirmations in one shared folder. Having everything in one place makes it easy for you, the planner and any backup person to check details quickly, especially in the busy final days before the event.

Red flags

  • No written timeline or run-of-show.
  • Vendors not confirmed in writing.
  • Unclear budget tracking.
  • No backup plans.
  • No rehearsal for virtual events.

Collect feedback right after the event with a short survey. Answers are freshest then, and they guide the next event.

Decide who is the final contact on the day. Even with a remote planner, someone must make quick decisions when a speaker is late or a delivery is wrong. Naming that person in the run-of-show avoids confusion when things move fast.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The budget includes a reserve.
  • Vendors are confirmed in writing.
  • A run-of-show exists.
  • Virtual speakers will rehearse.
  • Feedback will be collected after.
