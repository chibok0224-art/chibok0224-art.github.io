What a good screencast package includes

Screencast gigs are priced by length, editing and voice-over. Check that the package states:

  • Length and number of videos.
  • Recording: by you or the creator.
  • Editing: cuts, zooms, highlights and cursor effects.
  • Voice-over and captions.
  • Intro and outro with branding.
  • Resolution and formats.
  • Revisions.

Ask for zooms on important clicks and text. Full-screen recordings often make buttons and menus tiny, especially on phones, and viewers lose track of where to look.

Decide on a consistent visual style for all your tutorials: the same intro, font for captions, zoom style and cursor highlight. Consistency makes a library of videos feel professional and helps users recognize your official help content. Ask the creator for a template you can reuse for future videos.

How to prepare for a screencast

  1. The task or feature to show, step by step.
  2. A demo account with realistic sample data.
  3. A script or bullet points for narration.
  4. Audience: new users, advanced users or buyers.
  5. Brand elements for intro and captions.
  6. Where it will be used: help center, website, sales emails.

Clean the screen before recording: close notifications, hide bookmarks and personal tabs, and use sample data. Private emails, customer names and internal tools appearing in the background are a common and avoidable mistake.

Plan the steps and practice once. Hesitations, wrong clicks and loading screens make videos longer and harder to follow, and they add editing time.

Name each video by the task it teaches, so users can find the right one in your help center.

What drives the price

  • length and number of videos
  • who records
  • editing depth and effects
  • voice-over and captions
  • branding and templates
  • turnaround time

A series of short task videos may cost more to set up than one long video, but it is much cheaper to update later.

Red flags

  • Recordings that show personal or customer data.
  • Tiny, unreadable text with no zooms.
  • Fast clicking with no explanation.
  • No captions.
  • Very long videos covering many tasks.

Check the final video on a phone. If you cannot read the text or follow the cursor, add zooms or record at a higher resolution.

Tips for a smoother project

Record audio in a quiet room with a decent microphone, or ask for a professional voice-over. Viewers forgive simple visuals, but poor audio with echo or background noise makes tutorials tiring to follow. Clear narration at a calm pace is one of the most important parts of a good screencast.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a demo account with sample data.
  • The screen is clean of private information.
  • Each video covers one task.
  • Zooms and captions are included.
  • I will check the video on a phone.
