What a good BIM package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: architecture, structure, MEP or all.
  • Software and version.
  • Level of detail by stage.
  • Standards and templates followed.
  • Deliverables: model, drawings, schedules.
  • Coordination and clash checks.
  • Timeline and revisions.

Share your BIM execution plan or office templates before work starts. Consistent naming, layers and families save huge amounts of time later.

Ask for regular model uploads, such as weekly, rather than one final delivery. Reviewing progress early catches misunderstandings.

Ask for sample models or drawings from similar projects, such as housing, offices or retail. Look at how clean the model is, how families are organized and whether sheets and schedules are clear. Good organization saves your team time for the whole project.

How to brief a BIM modeler

  1. Drawings, CAD or scans.
  2. Project type and size.
  3. Disciplines involved.
  4. Level of detail required.
  5. Templates and standards.
  6. Software version.
  7. Deadline and milestones.

Define what the model will be used for: design, permits, coordination, cost estimates or facility management. Each use needs different information.

Clarify which version of drawings is current. Working from outdated drawings is a common and costly mistake.

Plan coordination with other consultants. Models from different disciplines must align, and someone must own clash resolution.

Keep licensed professionals involved. Structural and safety decisions belong to qualified architects and engineers.

Discuss file sizes and performance. Large, detailed models can become slow to open and share. A good modeler organizes work into linked files and uses detail levels sensibly so the model stays usable for everyone on the project.

What drives the price

  • building size
  • number of disciplines
  • level of detail
  • conversion from CAD or scans
  • clash detection
  • custom families

Converting a small floor plan to a basic model costs much less than a detailed multi-discipline model with clash detection and custom families.

Point cloud processing usually costs extra.

Ask whether the modeler can produce quantity schedules for cost estimates. Accurate counts of doors, windows, walls and finishes help contractors price work and reduce surprises, as long as the model is kept up to date with design changes.

Red flags

  • No questions about standards or templates.
  • Unclear level of detail.
  • Wrong software version.
  • No progress uploads.
  • Claims to approve structural safety.

Archive model versions at each milestone. Being able to return to an earlier version protects you if changes go wrong.

Tips for a smoother project

Document families and naming rules for future team members.

Agree on how questions and design changes are communicated. A shared issue list or regular coordination call keeps everyone informed and prevents the modeler from making assumptions that conflict with the design team's decisions.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared templates and standards.
  • The level of detail is agreed.
  • Software versions match.
  • Progress uploads are scheduled.
  • Licensed professionals stay responsible.
