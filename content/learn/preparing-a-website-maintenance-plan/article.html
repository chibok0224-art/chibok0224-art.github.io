1. List what you have

Write down what makes up your site: the platform, theme, plugins or apps, hosting company, domain registrar, email setup, payment tools and any connected services. Note login owners and renewal dates. A maintenance provider can only take care of what they know about, and a clear list prevents surprises when something expires or breaks.

2. Understand the core tasks

Regular maintenance usually covers updating the platform, themes and plugins, checking that backups run and can be restored, scanning for security problems, monitoring that the site is online and loading quickly, fixing broken links and forms, and cleaning out unused items. Some plans also include small content changes, reports and advice on improvements. Ask which tasks are included and which are charged separately.

3. Decide how much risk you accept

Think about what happens if the site is down for a day: lost sales, missed enquiries or damaged trust. A shop needs faster response and more frequent backups than a small information site. Decide who may make changes without asking you and which updates should be tested first on a copy of the site, since updates sometimes break features.

4. Set up access safely

Create separate administrator accounts for the provider rather than sharing your own login, and keep ownership of the hosting, domain and key accounts in your name. Use strong passwords and two-step sign-in. Write down who has access and review the list at least once a year. When the arrangement ends, remove access promptly.

5. Agree on response times and reporting

Ask how to report a problem, how quickly the provider will respond to urgent issues and normal requests, and during which hours they work. Ask for a short report each month listing updates made, backups, security findings and fixes, so that you know what you are paying for. A plan that does not report is hard to evaluate.

6. Understand the costs

Plans may charge a monthly fee for a fixed set of tasks, a number of hours or a separate rate for extra work. Compare what is included, how unused hours are handled and what happens if there is an emergency. Remember the costs outside the plan: hosting, licenses for paid themes and plugins, domain renewal and security tools.

Questions to ask a maintenance provider

  • What exactly is included in each plan, and what costs extra?
  • How do you test updates, and what happens if one breaks the site?
  • How often are backups made, where are they stored and have you tested a restore?
  • How do you respond to security problems or a hacked site?
  • What are your response times for urgent and normal requests?
  • What reports will I receive, and can I cancel easily?

Reviewing the arrangement

After a few months, read the reports and ask whether the site is faster, safer and more reliable. Check that backups can be restored and that renewals are tracked. If your needs grow, consider whether to change the plan. If your site is changing often, ask for regular improvement time as well as routine upkeep. A good provider explains their work in plain language and tells you when the site needs a bigger project.

If something goes wrong

Write a short emergency plan before you need it: who to call, what to check first and where backups are kept. Know how to reach your hosting company and where your domain is registered. If the site is hacked or goes down, stay calm, tell the provider immediately, change important passwords and avoid making random changes that hide the cause. After the problem is fixed, ask for a short explanation of what happened and what will prevent it next time.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming hosting includes all maintenance.
  • Never testing a backup restore.
  • Sharing one login among several people.
  • Letting domain or hosting renewals lapse.
