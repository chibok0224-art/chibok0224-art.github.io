What a resume does

A resume is a focused document you send when applying for a specific job. Its purpose is to show, quickly, that you match the role. Recruiters often skim a resume in under a minute, and many companies use applicant tracking systems that scan for relevant skills and job titles. A good resume is concise, uses clear headings, highlights results with numbers where possible and is tailored to the role. Many professionals keep a master resume and adapt it for each application. A resume writer can help you turn duties into achievements and choose what to leave out.

What a LinkedIn profile does

A profile is a public page that works for you even when you are not applying. Recruiters search for candidates using keywords, locations and job titles, so your headline and skills need to reflect what you want to be found for. The profile has more room for personality: a summary written in your own voice, featured work, recommendations, articles and activity. It speaks to a broader audience, including potential clients, collaborators and future employers, not only the hiring manager for one role. A LinkedIn profile writer focuses on searchability and a compelling summary.

Keep them consistent

Employers often check your profile after reading your resume. Job titles, employers and dates should match. Differences in emphasis are fine; contradictions are not. If you changed careers or have gaps, decide how to explain them consistently in both places. When you update one, review the other.

Which should you invest in?

  • Actively applying for jobs now: start with the resume and a cover letter template.
  • Open to opportunities, or freelancing: focus on the profile so people can find you.
  • Changing careers: both, with a clear story that connects past experience to the new direction. A career counselor may help clarify that story first.
  • Senior or specialized roles: both, since recruiters often search for these candidates directly.

What to give the writer

Collect your current resume, a list of roles with dates, key achievements with numbers, skills and certifications, and two or three job postings that represent what you want next. For a profile, add your current headline and summary, the keywords you want to be found for and any work you want to feature. The more detail you provide about results, the stronger the writing will be.

How the writing process usually works

Most writers start with a questionnaire or a short call to understand your experience and goals. They then send a first draft for your comments, followed by one or two revision rounds. Answer the questionnaire in detail and be specific about achievements. Feedback such as this role was mainly about managing suppliers is far more useful than general comments. Ask whether you will receive editable files so you can tailor the resume yourself for future applications.

A note on design

Creative resume layouts can stand out in design fields, but heavy graphics, columns and icons can confuse screening software. Many people keep two versions: a clean, simple one for online applications and a designed version to share directly with people. Ask the writer or designer which suits your industry.

Whichever you choose, read the final text aloud and make sure it sounds like you. Interviewers will ask about anything on the page, so every claim should be something you can talk about confidently.
