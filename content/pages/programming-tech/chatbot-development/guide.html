What a good chatbot project includes

Chatbot gigs range from a simple FAQ bot on a website to AI assistants connected to your booking system or store. The project should state:

  • Type: rule-based, AI-based, or a mix.
  • Channels: website, WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram or others.
  • Knowledge: which documents, pages or data the bot will use to answer.
  • Actions: what the bot can do, such as book, collect details or check an order.
  • Human handover: how a user reaches a person, and how you are notified.
  • Testing: a list of test questions and results before launch.
  • Accounts and costs: which services are needed, in whose name, and what they cost to run.
  • Handover: admin access, documentation and how to update the bot's answers yourself.

How to brief a chatbot developer

  1. The job: the three to five things the bot must do well.
  2. The questions customers ask most, with your approved answers.
  3. What the bot must not do: give prices it cannot confirm, legal or medical advice, or promises.
  4. Channels and the systems it must connect to.
  5. Tone: formal, friendly, short or detailed.
  6. Handover rules: when to pass the chat to a person, and during which hours.
  7. Data rules: what may be collected and how long it is kept.

Start small. A bot that answers ten common questions well is more useful than one that tries to handle everything and gets some of it wrong. Add tasks once the first version works.

After launch, read a sample of real conversations every week for the first month. You will quickly see questions the bot handles badly, answers that need updating, and moments when users give up. Ask the developer to make these conversations easy for you to review.

What drives the price

  • rule-based versus AI-based
  • number of channels and connected systems
  • amount of content the bot must learn from
  • actions such as bookings, payments or order lookups
  • testing, monitoring and support after launch
  • ongoing costs of the AI and messaging services, paid separately

Red flags

  • Promises that the bot will answer every question correctly or replace your support team.
  • No human handover option.
  • Accounts for the AI or messaging service created in the developer's name.
  • No test plan, or no list of what the bot should refuse to answer.
  • Vague answers about where conversation data is stored.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have listed the main tasks and my approved answers.
  • I have written what the bot must not say or do.
  • Users can always reach a human.
  • All service accounts will be in my name with spending limits.
  • I know what data is collected and where it is stored.
