What a good talent package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Deliverables: photos, videos, number and length.
  • Usage rights: platforms, duration, regions.
  • Model release.
  • Wardrobe and props.
  • Location and equipment.
  • Revisions or reshoots.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a few test photos or a short test clip in the planned setting. It confirms lighting, framing and fit with your brand.

Look at the talent's portfolio for work similar to yours, such as product close-ups, lifestyle scenes or speaking roles. Experience with your type of content means fewer takes, more natural results and easier collaboration.

Respect the talent's boundaries and working conditions. Clear schedules, reasonable shoot lengths and professional communication lead to better performances and protect your reputation as a client.

How to brief models or actors

  1. Product or story.
  2. Audience.
  3. Look and wardrobe.
  4. Shots or script.
  5. Tone.
  6. Usage and rights.
  7. Deadline and shipping.

Share a shot list or script with examples. Clear direction helps talent deliver what you need in fewer takes.

Ship products early and include instructions on how they should be shown. Mistakes like wrong product angles or covered logos often happen when talent receives products at the last minute.

Think about diversity and representation. Featuring people who reflect your real customers in age, background and style makes content more relatable and helps more people see themselves using your product.

What drives the price

  • number of shots or videos
  • usage rights
  • location and travel
  • wardrobe and styling
  • talent experience
  • rush delivery

A few home-made product photos cost much less than a full ad shoot with broad, long-term usage rights.

Red flags

  • No release offered.
  • Unclear usage rights.
  • Requests for inappropriate content.
  • Poor sample quality.
  • No agreement on reshoots.

Store releases with the content files. You will need them if the content is used in ads later.

Tips for a smoother project

Agree on what happens if content needs changes after delivery, such as a different hook or a cleaner background. Clear reshoot terms prevent misunderstandings.

Agree on file delivery: raw files, edited files or both, and the formats you need. Clear delivery expectations save time when the content goes to your editor or ad manager.

Check local rules for advertising disclosures if talent posts content on their own channels. Honest labeling protects both of you.

Keep a list of talent you enjoyed working with. Reliable people make future projects faster.

Prepare a simple call sheet for each shoot with times, addresses, contacts, wardrobe and the shot list. Even for remote shoots, a clear one-page plan keeps everyone aligned and makes the session faster and calmer.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • A signed release is included.
  • Usage rights are clear.
  • Wardrobe and props are agreed.
  • I shared a shot list.
  • Reshoots are agreed.
