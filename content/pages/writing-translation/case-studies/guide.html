What a good case study package includes

Case study gigs are priced by length and whether interviews are included. Check that the package states:

  • Interview with the customer, and with your team if needed.
  • Structure: situation, challenge, solution and results.
  • Length and formats.
  • Quotes taken from real interviews.
  • Approval round with the customer.
  • Design: included or text only.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Choose customers whose story matches the buyers you want more of. A case study from a similar industry or size is far more convincing to prospects than one from an unrelated business, even if the results were bigger.

Ask the writer to prepare interview questions in advance and share them with the customer. Customers give better answers when they have time to think and check numbers, and they are more comfortable approving a case study built from questions they saw beforehand.

How to brief a case study writer

  1. The customer and their contact, with permission to interview.
  2. The project: what you did and when.
  3. Results with evidence the customer agrees to share.
  4. Your audience: who will read the case study.
  5. Format: web page, PDF, slides or all.
  6. Brand guidelines and examples you like.

Keep the customer at the center. Readers want to see themselves in the story: the problem they recognize and the result they want. Your product should appear as the means, not the hero.

Plan the approval process with the customer early, including who approves, how long it takes and whether their legal or communications team needs to review. Approvals are the most common cause of delays.

What drives the price

  • length and number of formats
  • interviews and research
  • design and layout
  • video or audio versions
  • approval rounds
  • the writer's experience in your industry

If you plan several case studies, ask for a template. Consistent structure and design make them easier to produce and to compare.

Red flags

  • Results or quotes invented or exaggerated.
  • No plan for customer approval.
  • Stories that focus only on your company.
  • No interview, only generic text.
  • Use of logos or names without permission.

Store the approval email or form with the case study. If a customer later asks, you can show what they agreed to publish.

Reuse each case study across your marketing: a web page, a short summary for sales emails, a quote for your homepage and a social post. One approved story can support sales for a long time if you plan these versions from the start.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The customer agreed to an interview and the case study.
  • Results and quotes are real and approved.
  • The story focuses on the customer.
  • Approval steps and timing are planned.
  • I will keep the written approval on file.
