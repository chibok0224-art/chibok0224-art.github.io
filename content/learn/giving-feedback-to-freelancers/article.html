Before you reply: review against the brief

When a draft arrives, resist the urge to reply immediately. Open your brief and check the work against it point by point. Does it include every deliverable? Does it follow the must-haves, such as colors, wording or length? Does it suit the audience you described? This turns your reaction from "I am not sure I like it" into specific observations.

If possible, look at the work in the place it will be used. A logo looks different on a phone screen than on a large monitor; a video feels different on a social feed than in a full-screen player. Context often explains why something feels wrong.

Make every comment specific

Good feedback says what to change, where and why. Compare these examples:

VagueSpecific
Make it pop.Increase the contrast of the headline; it is hard to read on the light background.
The video is too slow.Cut the intro from 0:00 to 0:08 and shorten the pauses between scenes.
The text does not sound like us.Use shorter sentences and a friendlier tone, like the attached email.
I don't like the colors.Replace the orange with the dark blue from our website.

For visual work, screenshots with arrows or notes are very effective. For video, use timestamps. For writing, use comments on the exact sentences.

Send one organized list

Collect all comments into one message, ordered by importance. If several people are involved, agree among yourselves first. Conflicting notes from different people, such as one person asking for a bigger logo and another asking for a smaller one, are a common cause of wasted revisions. Number your points so the freelancer can reply to each one.

Explain the reason, not only the fix

Sometimes the change you suggest is not the best solution. If you explain the problem behind it, an experienced freelancer can often propose something better. "Customers might not notice the button" leaves room for several solutions, while "make the button red" allows only one.

Use revisions wisely

Most packages include a fixed number of revision rounds, so make each round count. Wait until everyone involved has seen the draft, then send all comments together. Avoid sending a second list an hour after the first; the freelancer may already be working on the first set. If you are unsure about a change, say so and ask for the freelancer's opinion rather than requesting it and then reversing it later.

Keep the scope in mind. Feedback should refine the agreed work, not add new deliverables. If you realize you need something extra, such as another size or an additional page, ask for it separately and expect it to be priced as an extra. Freelancers appreciate buyers who recognize the difference, and they are often happy to help when asked fairly.

When the draft is far from what you expected

If the first version misses the mark completely, avoid listing dozens of small fixes. Instead, explain the main gap, refer to the brief and your examples, and ask the freelancer how they interpreted the request. Often the problem is a misunderstanding that a short conversation can fix. If the work clearly does not match what was agreed, and revisions do not resolve it, contact the platform's support rather than arguing.

Say what works, too

Point out what you like as well as what should change. It tells the freelancer what to keep, so a revision does not accidentally remove the best parts. It also keeps the working relationship positive, which matters if you want to work together again.

Before you accept the final version

  • All requested changes are done.
  • Every deliverable and file format is included.
  • Spelling, names and contact details are correct.
  • Usage rights are clear.
  • You have saved copies of all files.
