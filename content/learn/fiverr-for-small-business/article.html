The short version

The best first tasks to outsource are ones that are clearly defined, take you a lot of time and don't need deep knowledge of your business: a logo, a simple website, social media graphics, admin work, bookkeeping and short videos. Start with one small order, write a clear brief, and keep the freelancer who does it well.

Six tasks worth outsourcing first

1. A logo and basic brand files

A professional logo with proper files is a one-time job with a clear result. When we checked our logo design picks on 2 October 2026, their starting packages ranged from 100 to 140 dollars. See is Fiverr good for logo design? for what to check.

2. A simple website

A few pages on a website builder is a defined project most developers handle quickly. The website developers we feature mostly started between 100 and 300 dollars in early October 2026, before hosting, domain and extras. Our article on website costs on Fiverr breaks this down by platform.

3. Social media graphics and posts

Templates for posts and stories, or a monthly batch of content, are easy to brief and repeat. Once a designer understands your style, each new batch takes less explaining.

4. Admin work through a virtual assistant

Inbox sorting, scheduling, data entry, research and customer replies are common first tasks for a virtual assistant. The virtual assistants we feature were all Vetted Pro and started from 100 to 150 dollars when we checked on 4 October 2026. Our article on working with a virtual assistant explains how to hand tasks over.

5. Bookkeeping

Categorizing transactions and reconciling accounts every month is repetitive and important to get right. A bookkeeper keeps your numbers current so you are not catching up before tax deadlines. Keep the accounting software in your own name and give the bookkeeper user access.

6. Short videos

Editing product videos, short social clips or a simple promo from your own footage is easy to outsource once you know the style you want. See our video editing guide for editors and prices.

How to choose your first task

  1. List the tasks you did last week and roughly how long each took.
  2. Mark the ones you dislike or do slowly.
  3. Pick one that you can describe in a short paragraph and judge when it is finished.
  4. Set a budget and a deadline with some buffer.

Making the first order go well

  • Write a brief. What it is for, who will see it, examples you like, formats, deadline and what "done" looks like. Our article on how to write a brief has a template.
  • Compare two or three sellers. Look at samples like your project and read recent reviews.
  • Start small. A small paid test shows you how the seller communicates and handles feedback.
  • Keep access safe. Share accounts through user roles, not your password, and remove access afterwards.
  • Check before accepting. Open every file and compare it with your brief.

Budgeting over time

Once the first task works, think in months rather than single orders. Note what you spent, how many hours it saved you and whether the quality held up. If a task saves more of your time than it costs, it is a good candidate for a regular arrangement with the same freelancer. If it doesn't, try a different task next instead of a cheaper seller for the same one.

Common mistakes

  • Outsourcing a task you can't describe yet.
  • Choosing the cheapest option for work customers will see every day.
  • Hiring for five tasks at once instead of learning with one.
  • Giving a freelancer ownership of your domain, ad accounts or social pages.
