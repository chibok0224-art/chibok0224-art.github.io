Video editing

Editing starts lowest, at 50 to 100 dollars among the sellers we feature, because you supply the footage. The price then depends on the running time of the finished video, the amount of raw footage, and extras such as captions, motion graphics, sound mixing and color correction. A short social clip costs far less than a long interview with several cameras. Our article on video editing versus animation helps you decide which you actually need.

UGC videos

User-generated-content creators in our list start at 120 to 160 dollars. The price usually covers one video of a set length filmed by the creator with your product or app, and a script or a talking-points sheet. Extra costs include the product shipped to the creator, additional videos, raw footage and the right to use the video in paid ads. Always ask what usage rights are included before you order.

Animated explainers

Animated explainers show the widest range. Entry packages start at 150 to 445 dollars, but they are often short. Among the sellers we feature, packages for around 60 seconds ranged from about 300 to 950 dollars, and larger projects cost more. What you pay for is the script, the storyboard, custom characters and illustration, animation, voice-over and music. Delivery times also grow with length, from about a week for a short video to several weeks for a longer one. Our article on making an animated explainer walks through the steps.

What raises the price

  • Length. This is the biggest factor, especially for animation.
  • Custom artwork. Original characters and illustrations cost more than templates and icons.
  • Voice-over and music. Professional voice talent is often an add-on.
  • Revisions. Changes after animation starts are expensive; unlimited revisions are usually in larger packages.
  • Rights and extra files. Raw footage, project files and commercial usage rights can be priced separately.

Questions to ask before you order

  • What is the maximum running time covered by the price?
  • Is the script, voice-over and music included, or only the animation or edit?
  • How many revisions are included, and at which stage can I request changes?
  • Which file formats and aspect ratios do I receive for each platform?
  • For ads, what usage rights and duration are included?

Common budgeting mistakes

The biggest mistake is not fixing the length. A 30-second video and a 90-second video are very different projects, and some sellers price them several times apart. The second is buying a video before the message is clear: if the script changes after the animation or edit has started, you pay for the work twice. Third, many buyers forget platform formats. A video made for widescreen may need a vertical version for social media, and the second version is often an add-on. Finally, avoid sending footage in many small batches. Edits take longer and cost more when material arrives piece by piece, so send everything in one organized folder with a short description of what you want.

Planning your budget

  1. Decide the finished length first, because it sets the price tier.
  2. Write the script or talking points before you request quotes. See writing a video brief.
  3. Check the number of revisions and whether the script is included.
  4. Ask what usage rights you receive and for how long.
