What a good creative writing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Type and length of the piece.
  • Genre and tone.
  • Outline or concept stage.
  • Drafts and revisions.
  • Rights: personal, publishing or commercial.
  • Originality and AI use.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a short outline or opening paragraph before the full piece. It confirms the direction, characters and voice, and changes at this stage take minutes rather than days.

Read several samples from the writer in the same genre or format you need. A writer who shines at funny children's stories may not suit dark fantasy or poetry. Look for a voice you enjoy reading, clear storytelling and dialogue that sounds like real people talking.

How to brief a creative writer

  1. The idea, characters and setting.
  2. Purpose: gift, publication, game or marketing.
  3. Reader and age group.
  4. Tone: funny, warm, dark, whimsical.
  5. Length and format.
  6. Examples of writing you enjoy.
  7. Rights and deadline.

For personalized stories, share names, places and small details: a pet's habits, a shared joke or a favorite place. These details make the story feel written for one person rather than anyone.

Describe what you want readers to feel at the end. Satisfied, surprised, moved or amused are very different endings, and naming the feeling helps the writer shape the whole piece.

Discuss names, places and references before the story is written. Using real people's names, trademarks or recognizable places can be fine for a private gift but may cause issues if the work is published or sold. A careful writer will ask how the story will be shared.

What drives the price

  • length
  • research and world-building
  • genre complexity
  • number of revisions
  • publishing rights
  • rush delivery

A short poem costs much less than a long story with detailed world-building, several drafts and publishing rights.

Red flags

  • Plots or characters copied from known works.
  • No outline or concept stage.
  • Unclear rights.
  • Generic writing that ignores your details.
  • Undisclosed AI-generated text.

Read the final piece aloud. Rhythm, dialogue and pacing problems are easier to hear than to see on the page.

Plan how the piece will be presented. A printed gift story might need a title page and a dedication, a game needs text that fits interface limits, and a blog story needs short paragraphs for screens. Telling the writer the final format helps them shape the text to fit it.

Agree on how feedback will work. Point to specific lines or scenes and explain what you felt as a reader, such as this part felt rushed or I wanted more of this character. Concrete reactions help the writer improve the story while keeping its voice and energy intact.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared the idea and key details.
  • I will approve an outline first.
  • The length is agreed.
  • Rights are in writing.
  • The work is original.
