What a good arts and crafts order includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Item description, size and materials.
  • Personalization options.
  • Progress photos, if offered.
  • Making time.
  • Shipping method, cost and packaging.
  • Digital files for tutorials or patterns.
  • Revisions and refund policy.

Ask for a progress photo before the item is finished or shipped. Small changes are still possible, and you avoid surprises when the parcel arrives.

Look at photos of the maker's finished pieces from several angles and, if available, reviews with photos from buyers. Customer photos show how items look after shipping and in real homes, which can differ from carefully styled portfolio images.

How to brief an arts and crafts maker

  1. The item and purpose.
  2. Size and colors.
  3. Materials you prefer or want to avoid.
  4. Personal details: names, dates, themes.
  5. Reference photos.
  6. Delivery address and date needed.
  7. Budget.

Measure where the item will go, such as a shelf, wall or table. Handmade items are often smaller or larger than people imagine from photos.

Mention allergies or safety needs, such as items for children or pets. Some materials, small parts or finishes may not be suitable.

Allow extra time for international shipping and customs. Fragile handmade items also need careful packaging, which the maker should explain.

Double-check spelling of names and dates in writing. Handmade text is hard or impossible to correct after it is painted or carved.

Ask how to care for the item, such as cleaning, light exposure or humidity. Handmade pieces often need gentle care to last, and simple instructions from the maker help keep your gift or decoration looking good for years.

What drives the price

  • size and detail
  • materials
  • personalization
  • progress photos
  • shipping and packaging
  • rush making

A small personalized card costs much less than a large hand-painted piece with special materials and international shipping.

Shipping and customs fees are often separate from the making price.

Think about the occasion and presentation. Gift wrapping, a handwritten note or a display stand can make a handmade piece feel special. Many makers offer these extras when asked, and they are easier to arrange before the item is finished.

Red flags

  • Photos that look like mass-produced items.
  • No information about materials.
  • No shipping details.
  • Unclear refund policy.
  • Copies of designs from other makers.

Keep photos of the finished item and the maker's contact. It is useful for repeat orders or matching pieces later.

If you order several pieces, such as wedding favors or event decorations, ask for one sample first. Approving a single item before the full batch protects you from repeating a mistake many times.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I measured the space.
  • Materials and safety needs are clear.
  • Names and dates are spelled correctly.
  • Shipping time fits my date.
  • I will see a progress photo.
