Human voice over

A professional voice actor brings performance: emotion, timing, emphasis and the ability to take direction. They can make a script sound warm, funny, serious or urgent, and adjust after feedback. Human voices are the safer choice for brand videos, ads, characters, audiobooks, storytelling and any project where trust and personality matter.

Good voice actors also deliver clean, edited audio from a treated recording space, and can usually supply several takes of important lines.

AI voice

AI voice tools generate speech from text. They are fast, easy to update and consistent. They work well for internal training, quick prototypes, product walkthroughs that change often, long reference content and drafts used to test timing before a human recording. Quality varies by tool and language, and emotional range, pronunciation of names and natural pacing can still be weaker than a skilled actor.

Language support is another factor. Human voice actors are available for many languages and accents, and native speakers handle local expressions naturally. AI voices are strongest in widely spoken languages and may struggle with less common languages, regional accents or mixed-language scripts. Test carefully before committing to AI for international content.

Rights and consent

For a human voice, agree on usage: online video, paid ads, broadcast, internal use, and for how long. Broadcast and paid ad usage often cost more. For AI voices, check the tool's license for commercial use. Never use a cloned voice of a real person without their explicit permission. If a freelancer offers AI voice work, ask which tool they use and whether the voice is licensed for your purpose.

How to choose

  • Customer-facing brand or ad content: a human voice actor.
  • Audiobooks: usually human narration; check platform rules for AI. See our audiobook production guide.
  • Training modules that change often: AI voice may be practical.
  • Testing a script and timing: AI voice draft, then a human for the final.

How to brief either option

Share the final script, a pronunciation list for names and technical terms, the tone you want and reference clips. Mark words to emphasize and where pauses should be. Specify the file format, sample rate and whether you want separate files per line or one continuous track. For a human actor, ask how many revisions or pickups are included.

Judging quality

Listen to samples in the same style as your project, not just a general demo reel. For a human voice actor, ask for a short custom sample of a few lines from your script if the seller offers one. Check pronunciation, pacing, breathing sounds, background noise and how natural the emphasis feels. For AI voices, test your own script, including names and numbers, and listen on both speakers and headphones. Problems that are easy to miss on a laptop speaker often stand out in headphones.

Also think about your audience. Listeners who spend a long time with the audio, such as in courses or audiobooks, notice small unnatural patterns more than viewers of a short ad. The longer the listening time, the more a skilled human performance tends to matter.

A practical hybrid

Many teams use both: AI voices for internal drafts and frequently updated help content, human voices for the work customers see most. This keeps costs reasonable while protecting the quality of your most important messages. Whatever you choose, have the final audio checked by an audio editor if it will be mixed with music or video.
