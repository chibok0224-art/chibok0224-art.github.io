What a good podcast writing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Deliverables: script, outline, notes or descriptions.
  • Episode length and number of episodes.
  • Research and fact-checking.
  • Source list.
  • Revisions.
  • Ownership.
  • Delivery schedule before recording.

Ask for scripts formatted for reading aloud, with short paragraphs and clear speaker labels. Hosts stumble less and recordings need less editing.

Listen to shows the writer has worked on and read a sample script alongside the episode. Good podcast writers leave room for the host's personality, write lines that sound natural when spoken and know when to cut a detail that works on paper but slows the audio down.

How to brief a podcast writer

  1. Show format and audience.
  2. Past episodes to learn the voice.
  3. Episode topics and guests.
  4. Length and segments.
  5. Sources to use or avoid.
  6. Sponsor reads, if any.
  7. Recording dates.

Write for listeners who cannot scroll back. Signposts like first, next and the big question help people follow, and repeating the key point at the end helps them remember it.

For interviews, ask the writer to prepare questions in order with optional follow-ups. A good outline keeps the conversation focused while leaving room for natural moments.

Think about episode descriptions and titles as part of the writing work. Clear titles with the main topic and short descriptions with key points and guest names help new listeners decide to press play, and they make episodes easier to find in podcast apps and search engines.

What drives the price

  • number of episodes
  • full scripts versus outlines
  • research depth
  • fact-checking
  • show notes and descriptions
  • rush delivery

Show notes for existing episodes cost much less than fully researched narrative scripts with fact-checking.

Red flags

  • Scripts that read like essays.
  • No fact-checking or sources.
  • Ignores the host's style.
  • Late delivery close to recording.
  • Copied text from articles.

Keep a running file of sources and facts used in each episode. It helps answer listener questions and avoids repeating mistakes in later episodes.

Agree on a weekly or monthly workflow if you publish regularly. Fixed days for topics, drafts, feedback and final scripts keep the show on schedule. A predictable rhythm is often what separates podcasts that keep going from those that stop after a few episodes.

Ask the writer to mark places where the host can add personal comments or stories. Planned moments of spontaneity keep episodes lively and authentic, while the rest of the script keeps the structure, facts and timing on track for a tight, focused recording session.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I chose scripts or outlines for my format.
  • I shared past episodes.
  • Facts and sources will be checked.
  • Scripts arrive before recording.
  • Ownership is agreed.
