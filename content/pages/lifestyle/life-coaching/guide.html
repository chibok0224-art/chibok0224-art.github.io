What a good life coaching package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Session format: video, voice or messaging.
  • Number and length of sessions.
  • Focus areas.
  • Goal setting and action plans.
  • Support between sessions, if any.
  • Confidentiality.
  • Training or background.

Look for coaches who explain how they work, such as setting goals in the first session and reviewing progress in later ones. A clear structure keeps sessions from becoming pleasant chats with no direction.

Ask whether the coach offers a short introductory call. Feeling comfortable with your coach matters as much as their method.

Ask what a typical session looks like from start to finish. Some coaches use questions and reflection, others use worksheets, habit trackers or short assignments. Knowing the style in advance helps you choose a coach whose approach suits the way you think and learn.

How to brief a life coach

  1. What you want to change.
  2. Why now.
  3. What you have tried.
  4. Time you can give each week.
  5. Preferred style: gentle, direct, structured.
  6. Topics you do not want to discuss.
  7. Schedule and time zone.

Pick one or two goals to start. Small, specific goals, such as a regular morning routine or a weekly planning habit, are easier to measure than wanting a better life.

Tell the coach how you like to be held accountable. Some people want regular check-ins; others prefer to report at each session.

What drives the price

  • number and length of sessions
  • support between sessions
  • coach training and experience
  • assessments or workbooks
  • specialization
  • package length

A single session costs much less than a three-month program with weekly calls and messaging support.

Red flags

  • Promises to transform your life quickly.
  • Pressure to buy long or expensive packages.
  • Claims to treat anxiety, depression or trauma.
  • Advice on medical, legal or financial decisions.
  • No confidentiality.

A responsible coach will suggest a doctor, therapist or other professional if your needs go beyond coaching.

Tips for a smoother project

Start with a short package and review progress before committing to more. Good coaches are happy to work this way.

Keep brief notes after each session with what you agreed to do. Notes make it easier to see progress over time.

Expect some sessions to feel slower than others. Progress is rarely steady, and a good coach helps you adjust plans instead of giving up.

Think about what success will look like at the end of the first package. It might be a finished plan, a habit kept for six weeks or a decision you have been putting off. Agreeing on this with your coach at the start makes it easier to judge progress honestly and to decide whether to continue, change focus or stop.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My first goals are clear.
  • The session format suits me.
  • Confidentiality is agreed.
  • Nobody promises quick transformation.
  • I know coaching is not therapy.
