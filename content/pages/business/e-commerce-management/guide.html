What a good e-commerce management package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Platforms managed.
  • Task list and frequency.
  • Hours and working times.
  • Access level and limits.
  • Reporting: what and how often.
  • Response times for orders and messages.
  • Pricing and contract terms.

Write a short operations guide with your rules: shipping times, return policy, discount limits and how to handle problems. It lets the manager act consistently without asking about every case.

Ask for examples of stores the manager has run and how they kept listings accurate. Look for experience with your product type and order volume, because managing a few handmade items is very different from keeping thousands of variants in stock across several channels.

How to brief an e-commerce manager

  1. Your store and platforms.
  2. Products and catalog size.
  3. Order volume.
  4. Tasks you want to hand over.
  5. Rules for pricing, returns and discounts.
  6. Tools for inventory and support.
  7. Goals and reporting needs.

Start with a trial period and a limited task list, such as listings and order checks. Expand responsibilities once you trust their accuracy and communication.

Set clear limits on money decisions: maximum discounts, refunds and ad spend without approval. Limits protect you and make the manager's job easier.

Agree on a simple weekly report: sales, orders, stock warnings, returns and any problems. Short regular reports keep you informed without micromanaging.

Ask how the manager keeps track of tasks and changes, such as a shared checklist or task board. A visible log of what was updated and when makes it easy to review their work, spot mistakes early and hand over smoothly if responsibilities change.

What drives the price

  • number of platforms
  • catalog size
  • order volume
  • hours per week
  • advertising management
  • reporting depth

A few hours a week of listing updates costs much less than full daily management of several marketplaces with ads and customer service.

Red flags

  • Requests for owner or payment access.
  • No experience with your platform.
  • Vague task lists.
  • No regular reporting.
  • Unclear availability during busy periods.

Review access regularly and remove it promptly when the work ends. Store accounts often hold customer data and money settings.

Tips for a smoother project

Agree on how problems are escalated, such as stock errors, angry customers or account warnings from a marketplace. Quick escalation to you for serious issues protects your store's rating, which can be hard to rebuild once it drops.

Plan for peak seasons. Holidays, sales events and product launches bring more orders, messages and stock issues. Agree in advance on extra hours, response times and who to contact if something urgent happens outside normal working times.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Tasks and limits are written down.
  • Access is limited to what is needed.
  • I have an operations guide with my rules.
  • Weekly reports are agreed.
  • A trial period comes first.
