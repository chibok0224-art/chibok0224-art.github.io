1. Set goals and audience

Decide what social media should do for your business: build awareness, bring visitors to your website, generate inquiries, support customers or grow a community. Describe the audience on each platform, since people on a professional network and on a short video app expect different content. Goals shape what you post and how you measure success. If you need help setting direction, a social media strategist can create a plan before the calendar is built.

2. Choose content pillars

Content pillars are three to five recurring themes. Examples include practical tips, behind-the-scenes stories, customer stories, product features and community or industry news. Pillars keep content varied and on-brand, and they make planning faster: each week, you fill slots for each pillar instead of starting from a blank page. Aim for a balance where most posts give value and a smaller share promote offers directly.

3. Platforms, frequency and formats

Choose the platforms where your audience is active and that you can maintain. Set a realistic frequency for each, such as three posts a week on one platform and daily short videos on another. Decide formats: single images, carousels, short videos, stories, text posts or live sessions. Formats determine which freelancers you need, so this step matters for budgeting.

4. Build the calendar

A simple calendar can be a spreadsheet or a shared board with columns for date, platform, pillar, topic, format, caption status, visual status, link, owner and approval. Add key dates such as product launches, holidays relevant to your audience and events. Plan a month in detail and outline the next two months. Leave a few open slots each week for timely posts reacting to news, trends or customer questions.

5. Divide the work

Decide who creates what. A social media copywriter writes captions and hooks, a social media designer creates graphics and templates, and a video editor produces short videos. A social media manager can coordinate everything, schedule posts and respond to comments. Long videos, podcasts and webinars can be turned into many short clips through video repurposing, which fills the calendar efficiently.

6. Set deadlines and approvals

Work backwards from publishing dates. For example, captions and visuals are due a week before publishing, approvals two days before, and scheduling the day after approval. Decide who approves posts and how feedback is given, such as comments in the calendar. Clear deadlines prevent last-minute rushes and give freelancers time to do good work.

7. Prepare a shared asset library

Create a shared folder with logos, brand colors, fonts, templates, product photos, approved hashtags and brand voice notes. Templates for recurring post types, such as quotes, tips and announcements, save design time and keep the feed consistent. Record frequently used links and calls to action so they are not mistyped.

8. Review and adjust monthly

Each month, look at what performed well: reach, engagement, clicks, saves, shares and inquiries. Note which pillars and formats work on each platform. Use these insights to plan the next month, doing more of what works and testing new ideas. Share results with your freelancers so they can improve too.

Leave time for engagement

Publishing is only half the work. Plan time each day to reply to comments and messages, because conversations build relationships and often lead to sales.

Calendar checklist

  • Goals and audience for each platform.
  • Three to five content pillars.
  • Platforms, frequency and formats chosen.
  • Calendar with dates, topics, owners and status columns.
  • Roles for writing, design, video and scheduling.
  • Deadlines and approval process.
  • Shared asset library and monthly review.
