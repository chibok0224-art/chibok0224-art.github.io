What a good cover letter package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of letters and target jobs.
  • Tailoring to a specific job description.
  • Editable file in Word or Google Docs.
  • A reusable template, if included.
  • Matching design with your resume, if needed.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for an editable file and a short guide on which paragraphs to change for each new job. It saves money and keeps every application focused.

Ask to see a sample letter the writer has done for a similar level and field, with private details removed. It shows whether their style is concise and specific or long and generic. The best letters read like a confident person talking, not like a template.

How to brief a cover letter writer

  1. Your resume and LinkedIn profile.
  2. The job description and company.
  3. Why you want this job.
  4. Two or three achievements with results.
  5. Anything to explain, such as a career change or gap.
  6. Country and industry norms.
  7. Deadline.

Give real examples with results, such as a project you led, a problem you solved or a number you improved. Specific stories make a letter memorable; general claims like hard-working and passionate do not.

If you are changing careers or returning after a break, say so. A good writer can explain your story positively and connect transferable skills to the new role.

Tell the writer about the tone of the company. A startup, a bank and a charity expect different styles. Sharing the company website or a few posts from its social pages helps the writer match the tone while keeping your own personality in the letter.

What drives the price

  • number of letters
  • research on the company
  • seniority and complexity of the role
  • templates and extra versions
  • matching design
  • rush delivery

A single tailored letter costs less than a package with several versions and a reusable template.

Red flags

  • Generic letters that fit any job.
  • Invented skills or achievements.
  • No questions about the job you are applying for.
  • Overly formal or flowery language.
  • Promises of guaranteed interviews.

Read the final letter aloud and make sure you can talk about every point in an interview. Anything you cannot explain confidently should be changed or removed.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep a copy of every letter you send, along with the job description. When an employer calls, you can quickly remind yourself what you wrote, and over time you will see which approaches lead to interviews and can reuse them.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Will you tailor the letter to a specific job ad I send you?
  • What information will you need about my experience and achievements?
  • Can I get a version I can adapt for similar jobs?
  • How many revision rounds are included?
  • Will the tone suit my industry and the country I am applying in?

Check every claim in the letter is true and that you can talk about it in an interview. A strong letter only helps if you can back up each point in person.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I sent my resume and the job description.
  • I shared real achievements with results.
  • The letter is tailored to the job.
  • Everything in it is true.
  • I will get an editable file.
