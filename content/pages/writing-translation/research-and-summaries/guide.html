What a good research package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Topic and questions covered.
  • Length and format.
  • Source types and citations.
  • Date range of information.
  • Summary and key findings.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a short list of key findings at the top, followed by details. Busy readers can act on the main points, and the details remain available when needed.

Ask for a sample summary or research brief from a past project with private details removed. Look for clear structure, cited sources next to facts and a neutral tone. Strong researchers separate what the sources say from their own interpretation.

How to brief a researcher

  1. The decision or purpose behind the research.
  2. Specific questions to answer.
  3. Scope: regions, time period, industries.
  4. Preferred sources and ones to avoid.
  5. Length and format.
  6. What you already know.
  7. Deadline.

Turn your topic into questions. Instead of research electric bikes, ask which countries have the fastest growing demand and what prices buyers pay. Clear questions lead to focused, useful answers.

Say what you already know or have read. It avoids paying for information you have and lets the researcher dig deeper where you need it.

Consider asking for a short source quality note. Researchers can mark which sources are official, independent or promotional, and flag where information conflicts. This helps you judge confidence in each finding and decide where further checking is worthwhile before making important decisions.

What drives the price

  • topic complexity
  • number of questions
  • depth and length
  • paid or hard-to-find sources
  • data analysis
  • rush delivery

A short article summary costs much less than a detailed market brief with data, charts and many sources.

Red flags

  • No sources or citations.
  • Opinions presented as facts.
  • Outdated information.
  • Copied text from sources.
  • Offers to write graded academic assignments.

Store the report with its sources and date. Information ages quickly, and knowing when it was gathered helps you judge whether it is still reliable.

Decide how you will use the results before work starts: a presentation, a blog post, an internal decision or a report for clients. The format affects how detailed the research should be and how the findings are presented, so sharing it early avoids reworking the report later.

Agree on how updates will work if the topic changes quickly. Some projects benefit from a short follow-up after a few weeks or months, refreshing key numbers and adding new developments, rather than commissioning completely new research each time the question comes up again.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I wrote clear research questions.
  • Sources will be cited.
  • The scope and dates are clear.
  • Key findings come first.
  • The work is for business or personal use.
