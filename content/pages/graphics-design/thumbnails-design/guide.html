What a good thumbnail package includes

Thumbnail gigs are usually priced per thumbnail, with discounts for bundles. Check that the package states:

  • Number of thumbnails, and whether variations of the same one are included.
  • Size and format: 16:9, high resolution, within the upload file limit.
  • Source file: a layered, editable file you can reuse.
  • Image work: cutting you out of the background, color correction, effects.
  • Text: font choice and whether you can change the words later.
  • Image sources: your photos, licensed stock, or artwork made for you.
  • Revisions and delivery time, especially if you publish on a fixed schedule.

How to brief a thumbnail designer

  1. The video title and the one idea the thumbnail must show.
  2. Your photos: sharp images of your face with the expression you want, or of the product.
  3. A few frames from the video that show the key moment.
  4. Words on the image: no more than a few, and different from the title.
  5. Your channel style: colors, fonts and past thumbnails that worked.
  6. Examples from other channels you like, with a note on why.
  7. Your upload date, so the designer can plan delivery.

Before approving a thumbnail, look at it at a very small size next to other videos on your phone. If you cannot tell what it shows in a second, simplify it: fewer words, a bigger face or object, stronger contrast.

If you publish often, ask for a template once and simple edits afterwards. A consistent layout, font and color helps regular viewers recognize your videos, and changing words and photos on a template is quicker and usually cheaper than a new design each time.

What drives the price

  • number of thumbnails and variations
  • detailed photo editing, cutouts and effects
  • custom illustration or 3D elements
  • a reusable template or channel style guide
  • rush delivery for a fixed upload schedule
  • the designer's experience in your niche

Red flags

  • Portfolio thumbnails copied from well-known channels and presented as original work.
  • Use of movie stills, game art or other creators' images with no mention of rights.
  • No source file offered, even at extra cost.
  • Designs that promise something the video does not deliver. Misleading thumbnails hurt viewer trust.
  • Text so small or busy that it is unreadable on a phone.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have one clear idea for the thumbnail and a few words at most.
  • I am sending my own sharp photos or frames from the video.
  • I will receive a high-resolution image and an editable source file.
  • Every image used is mine, licensed, or made for me.
  • I have checked the draft at phone size before approving it.
