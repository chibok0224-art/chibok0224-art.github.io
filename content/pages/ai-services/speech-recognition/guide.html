What a good speech recognition package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Use case: transcription, commands, captions or analytics.
  • Service or model used.
  • Accuracy testing on your audio.
  • Custom vocabulary.
  • Languages.
  • Privacy and storage of audio.
  • Running costs, integration and support.

Ask for a test on a batch of your real recordings with an error report. It shows which words, speakers or conditions cause problems before you commit to a full integration.

Ask the expert to compare at least two services or models on your audio. Results vary a lot between providers depending on language, accent and noise, and a short comparison prevents committing to an option that performs poorly for your speakers.

How to brief a speech recognition expert

  1. The use case and users.
  2. Audio samples in real conditions.
  3. Languages and accents.
  4. Special vocabulary.
  5. Where it runs: cloud, device, private server.
  6. Privacy rules and consent.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Send difficult audio on purpose: crosstalk, phone quality, background noise and fast speakers. Systems tuned only on clean audio often struggle in real calls and meetings.

Decide whether you need speaker labels, timestamps or punctuation. These features change which service is suitable and how the output can be used.

Plan how humans will correct important transcripts. For legal, medical or financial use, a human review step is usually essential.

Consider accessibility as a benefit as well as a feature. Accurate captions and transcripts help people who are deaf or hard of hearing, non-native speakers and anyone in a noisy place. Planning for accessibility can widen the value of your speech project.

What drives the price

  • audio volume and length
  • languages and accents
  • custom vocabulary and tuning
  • real-time versus batch processing
  • integration work
  • privacy and hosting requirements

Batch transcription with a standard service costs much less than real-time recognition with custom vocabulary on private servers.

Red flags

  • Accuracy shown only on clean samples.
  • No custom vocabulary support.
  • Unclear audio storage.
  • No consent plan for recordings.
  • No running cost estimate.

Monitor errors over time. New products, names and staff change the vocabulary, and keeping the custom word list updated maintains accuracy.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan retention rules for audio and transcripts. Decide how long recordings are kept, who can access them and when they are deleted. Short, clear retention periods reduce risk and are often expected under privacy rules in many countries.

Think about what happens after transcription. Searching calls, summarizing meetings or tagging topics adds value, but each step adds cost and complexity. Start with the most useful step and add more once the transcripts are reliable.
