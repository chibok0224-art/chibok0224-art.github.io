Logo animation

A logo animation brings your existing logo to life in a short sequence, often three to six seconds. The pieces of the logo might draw themselves, assemble, bounce or reveal with light. Logo animations are flexible: they can open or close videos, appear in presentations, play on websites and loading screens, or be used in ads. Because they are short and focused on the logo, they work well for brands that want a consistent signature across many uses. Our logo animation guide covers the styles and packages available.

Intro and outro videos

An intro is a short opening sequence for a channel, series or show. It may include the logo, the show name, a host image, theme colors and music. It sets the tone and tells viewers what kind of content to expect. An outro closes the video, often with a call to action such as subscribe, visit the website or watch the next episode, and space for platform end screens. Intros and outros are common for online video channels, podcasts with video, courses and streamers. See our intro and outro guide.

Audio logos and sonic branding

An audio logo is a short, recognizable sound, such as a few notes or a distinctive effect, that represents your brand. Paired with a logo animation, it makes the moment memorable even when viewers glance away. Audio logos can also be used on their own in podcasts, phone systems, apps and radio ads. A sonic branding specialist can create one that fits your brand personality.

Keep it short

Viewers are impatient, especially on social media. A long intro before the content starts can make people leave. Many creators place a hook or the first moment of content before a very short branded sting, or move the branding to the end. If you want a longer intro for a series, ask for a short version too, so you can choose depending on the platform.

How to brief

  • Provide your logo as a vector file with separated elements if possible.
  • Share brand colors, fonts and the feeling you want: calm, energetic, playful or premium.
  • Send two or three examples of animations you like.
  • List where it will be used: video platforms, social media, website, presentations.
  • Say whether you need sound, and whether the music must be original or licensed.
  • Request formats: horizontal and vertical versions, transparent background and a web-friendly file.

How the process usually works

Most animators start with a short concept or storyboard describing the motion, then deliver a draft animation for feedback. Comment on timing and style at the draft stage, because big changes after sound and final polish take longer. Agree on revision rounds before work starts.

Which should you choose?

If you need one asset that works everywhere, start with a logo animation with a short sound. If you publish regular episodes on a channel, an intro and outro help viewers recognize the series and guide them to the next action. If audio is central to your brand, such as for podcasts or apps, add an audio logo. Many brands build all three over time, keeping the same colors, motion style and sound so they feel like one family.

Ask for source files where possible. They allow future edits, such as a new tagline, seasonal colors or an updated call to action, without starting from scratch.
