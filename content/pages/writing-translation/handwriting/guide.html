What a good handwriting order includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of notes or envelopes.
  • Writing style.
  • Paper and ink.
  • Hand or machine writing.
  • Mailing, if included.
  • Proof before full order.
  • Delivery time and pricing.

Ask for a sample with your text before a large order. It confirms the style, spacing and ink color.

Look at photos of real handwritten pieces from the service, ideally in the style and paper you want. Consistent letters, straight lines and clean ink show skill, while smudges or uneven spacing are warning signs for a large order.

Plan the full process for mailing: postage, return addresses and any inserts like business cards. Coordinating these details in advance prevents delays and ensures every note arrives looking professional.

How to brief a handwriting service

  1. Text for each note.
  2. Names and addresses, spelled correctly.
  3. Style and ink color.
  4. Paper or cards.
  5. Quantity.
  6. Mailing needs.
  7. Deadline.

Send names and addresses in a spreadsheet with clear columns. It reduces copying errors and speeds up work.

Keep messages short and personal. A few sincere lines feel more genuine than long letters.

Order early for weddings and holidays, when demand is high.

For business notes, mention the relationship with each recipient, such as a new client or a long-term partner. Small personal touches in the text make the note feel genuinely written for that person.

If you send notes regularly, such as monthly thank-yous to new customers, ask whether the service can work from a recurring list. A steady routine keeps the personal touch going without you preparing each batch from scratch, and it often lowers the cost per note.

What drives the price

  • quantity
  • writing style
  • paper and materials
  • machine versus hand
  • mailing and postage
  • rush delivery

A few handwritten cards cost much less than hundreds of calligraphy envelopes with mailing.

Red flags

  • No sample before large orders.
  • Unclear hand versus machine writing.
  • No mailing timeline.
  • Spelling errors in samples.
  • No refund policy.

Keep the final text and address list for future orders.

Tips for a smoother project

Choose ink and paper that suit the occasion. Elegant cards and dark ink feel formal, while colored paper and playful styles suit informal thank-yous and celebrations.

Ask about the paper and ink lifespan for keepsakes such as wedding notes. Acid-free paper and fade-resistant ink help special messages last for years, which matters when the note is meant to be kept.

Keep a copy or photo of what was sent to whom. It helps avoid duplicate messages and makes follow-up conversations easier.

Ask how errors are handled. A misspelled name on a handwritten envelope should be rewritten, and a clear policy shows professionalism.
