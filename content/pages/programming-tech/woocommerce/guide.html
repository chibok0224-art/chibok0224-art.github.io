What a good WooCommerce project includes

WooCommerce gigs range from store setup to custom features and fixes. Check that the project states:

  • Setup: WooCommerce, theme and essential plugins.
  • Products: how many will be added, with variations.
  • Payments: gateways connected and tested.
  • Shipping and taxes: zones, rates and rules.
  • Emails: order confirmations and notifications.
  • Mobile checkout tested end to end.
  • Handover: admin access, plugin list and a short guide.

Ask which theme will be used and whether it is designed for WooCommerce. A theme built for stores handles product pages, cart and checkout far better than a general theme adapted later, and it usually needs fewer extra plugins. Also ask whether the developer will use a child theme, so that your changes survive theme updates instead of being overwritten the next time the theme is updated.

How to brief a WooCommerce developer

  1. What you sell, how many products and their variations.
  2. Product data in a spreadsheet, with photos.
  3. Where you ship and how you charge for shipping.
  4. Payment methods you want to offer.
  5. Special features: subscriptions, bookings, wholesale prices, digital downloads.
  6. Hosting and domain details, in your name.
  7. Stores you like, with notes.

Place real test orders before launch: pay with each method, check the confirmation email, test a coupon, and process a refund. Checkout problems are the most expensive kind, because they cost you sales silently.

Tax rules for online sales differ between countries and regions. The developer can enter the settings, but check which rules apply to you with an accountant or tax adviser.

What drives the price

  • number of products and variations
  • theme customization or custom design
  • payment, shipping and tax complexity
  • custom features and paid plugins
  • migration from another platform
  • speed work, hosting setup and the developer's experience

Red flags

  • Payment accounts opened in the developer's name.
  • "Free" premium plugins from unofficial sources.
  • A long plugin list with no explanation.
  • No test orders before launch.
  • No plan for updates after launch.

Make sure your hosting suits an online store. Shared hosting that is fine for a small blog can struggle with carts, checkouts and many products. Ask the developer to recommend hosting with automatic backups and good support, and keep the account in your name. Before launch, also check that order emails do not land in spam, as stores often need a proper email sending setup.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Hosting, domain and payment accounts are in my name.
  • I have product data and photos ready.
  • Shipping rules and payment methods are decided.
  • Test orders and refunds will be done before launch.
  • I know who will update the store after launch.
