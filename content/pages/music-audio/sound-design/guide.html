What a good sound design package includes

Sound design gigs are priced by number of sounds, complexity and implementation. Check that the package states:

  • Sound list: effects, ambiences, interface sounds.
  • Variations for repeated actions.
  • Formats: sample rate, length, loops.
  • Naming and organization.
  • Implementation: included or not.
  • Licensing for any library material.
  • Revisions.

Ask for a small set of key sounds first, such as the main character's actions or the core interface clicks. Hearing them in context shows whether the style is right before the full list is produced.

Ask the designer to explain their process for creating unique sounds. Many great sound effects combine several layers, such as recordings, synthesized elements and processing. A designer who builds sounds rather than only choosing them from libraries usually delivers a more distinctive result that fits your project better.

How to brief a sound designer

  1. The project: game, film, app or video.
  2. Style: realistic, cartoony, sci-fi, retro.
  3. Sound list with descriptions.
  4. Video or gameplay capture for timing.
  5. Technical needs: formats, loops, engine or middleware.
  6. References: games or films whose sound you like.

Share visuals with every request. A sound for a heavy door and a small wooden door are different, and seeing the object, size and speed helps the designer create the right sound first time.

For games, ask for several variations of frequent sounds, such as footsteps or hits. Players notice when the same sound repeats exactly, and small variations make the game feel more alive.

Balance matters. Ask the designer to consider how sounds sit with music and dialogue, so important cues are heard and nothing is overpowering.

What drives the price

  • number of sounds and variations
  • custom recording versus processed libraries
  • implementation in engine or middleware
  • mixing for video
  • complexity and style
  • the designer's experience

Implementation can take as long as creating sounds. Clarify whether it is included, or whether your developer will integrate the files.

Red flags

  • Library sounds with unclear licenses.
  • Poorly named or disorganized files.
  • No variations for repeated sounds.
  • Sounds created without seeing the visuals.
  • Levels that clash with music and dialogue.

Test sounds in the real project, not just in isolation. A sound that seems perfect alone can feel wrong next to music, visuals and other sounds.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan the sound palette with your overall style. A cartoon game, a realistic thriller and a calm meditation app each need very different sounds. Agreeing on a small set of reference sounds early gives the designer a clear direction and keeps the whole project consistent.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Will you work to my video or game build, rather than creating sounds blind?
  • Are the sounds original or from libraries, and are the licenses clear for my use?
  • Will repeated sounds have several variations so they do not get tiring?
  • How will the files be named, organized and delivered?
  • Will the sound levels be balanced with my music and dialogue?

Give the sound designer a short list of the most important moments, such as a logo reveal or a game reward. Strong sounds on a few key moments matter more than sound on everything.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a sound list with visuals for reference.
  • Formats and implementation are agreed.
  • Library material is licensed for my use.
  • Frequent sounds have variations.
  • I will test sounds in the real project.
