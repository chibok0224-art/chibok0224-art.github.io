What a good explainer video package includes

Explainer gigs are usually priced by length and style. Two videos of the same length can involve very different amounts of work, so check that the package states:

  • Length of the finished video.
  • Style: 2D characters, motion graphics, whiteboard or 3D.
  • Script: written by the animator, edited from yours, or supplied by you.
  • Storyboard: sketches of each scene for you to approve before animation.
  • Voice-over, music and sound effects, and whether they are licensed for commercial use.
  • Revisions at each stage: script, storyboard and animation.
  • Files: resolution, formats, sizes for social media, and captions.
  • Rights to use the video commercially, including in ads.

How to brief an explainer animator

  1. Who will watch and where: your website, a sales call, social media or ads.
  2. The problem your viewer has, in their words.
  3. How your product solves it, in three steps or fewer.
  4. The next step you want viewers to take.
  5. Brand files: logo, colors, fonts and screenshots of your product if it is software.
  6. Examples of explainers you like, with notes on the style.
  7. Language and accent for the voice-over.

Work through the stages in order and approve each one in writing: script, then voice-over, then storyboard, then animation. It is the simplest way to avoid paying for the same scenes twice.

Ask for captions burned into a version of the video, or a separate caption file. Many people watch with the sound off, especially on social media, and captions also help viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing. If you plan to reuse the video in other languages, say so early so text on screen can be kept separate.

What drives the price

  • length of the video
  • style and detail, especially custom characters and 3D
  • scriptwriting and storyboarding
  • professional voice-over, extra languages and licensed music
  • extra sizes, captions and short cut-downs for social media
  • the animator's experience and rush delivery

Red flags

  • No storyboard stage, so you see the video for the first time when it is finished.
  • A portfolio where every video uses the same stock characters and scenes.
  • Music or voice with no license information.
  • Unclear rights, or rights that exclude advertising.
  • A long, detailed video promised in a day or two for a very low price.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know who will watch the video and where.
  • I have agreed who writes the script, and I have timed it read aloud.
  • I will approve the script, voice-over and storyboard before animation.
  • Music, voice and footage are licensed for commercial use.
  • I know which file sizes and formats I will receive.
