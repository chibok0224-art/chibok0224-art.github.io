What a good customer care consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Support review.
  • Ticket analysis.
  • Process improvements.
  • Templates and help content.
  • Tool recommendations.
  • Training.
  • Measures and follow-up.

Ask for an analysis of the most common ticket types. Fixing the causes of frequent questions reduces volume more than answering faster.

Request reply templates that sound human and can be personalized. Robotic templates frustrate customers.

How to brief a customer care consultant

  1. Support channels.
  2. Volume and team size.
  3. Tools used.
  4. Common issues.
  5. Customer feedback.
  6. Goals.
  7. Budget.

Export a sample of recent tickets, with personal details removed, for the consultant to review. Real conversations reveal tone, delays and recurring problems far better than summaries or opinions about how support is going.

Involve your support agents in the review. They know which questions repeat, which tools slow them down and which policies frustrate customers, and their involvement makes changes easier to adopt.

Share customer satisfaction feedback, including negative comments. Patterns in complaints often point to the most valuable improvements.

Ask consultants for examples of support teams they improved and what changed in satisfaction or response times. Concrete results show practical experience.

Plan regular template reviews, since product changes quickly make old answers wrong.

Think about the customer's whole journey, not only the support inbox. Many tickets come from unclear product pages, confusing checkout steps or missing shipping updates. A consultant who looks at these causes can reduce support volume while improving the experience for everyone.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • review or audit depth
  • written report or roadmap
  • size and complexity of your systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A review of templates and processes costs much less than a full support redesign with tool migration and training.

Improve the help center alongside templates. Clear self-service answers reduce tickets and help customers solve problems at any time of day.

Celebrate improvements with the team, as support work is often invisible but vital.

Ask how support can feed insights back to the business. Regular summaries of top issues, feature requests and confusing policies help product and marketing teams fix root causes, turning customer care from a cost center into a valuable source of improvements.

Red flags

  • Speed over quality.
  • Expensive tools without need.
  • Ignores agents' input.
  • Robotic templates.
  • No measures.

Review support measures monthly and update templates as products change.

Ask for a simple escalation guide so agents know when and how to involve specialists or managers.

Ask the consultant to suggest a simple quality review routine, such as checking a few conversations each week against clear criteria. Regular reviews keep standards high as the team grows.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Ticket types are analyzed.
  • Agents are involved.
  • Templates sound human.
  • Tools fit my size.
  • Measures are agreed.
