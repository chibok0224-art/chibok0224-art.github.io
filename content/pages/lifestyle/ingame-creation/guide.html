What a good in-game creation package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Game and version.
  • Build or mod scope.
  • Size and detail.
  • Files and installation.
  • Testing on your setup.
  • Ownership and platform rules.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for screenshots or a short video tour during the build. Early previews let you adjust layout and style before details are finished.

Request testing on a copy of your server or world. Installing directly on a live server can break existing content.

Look at the creator's previous builds or mods in the same game, ideally as videos or world downloads. Seeing their work in motion shows scale, detail and how well paths and features function for real players.

How to brief an in-game creator

  1. Game, version and platform.
  2. What to build.
  3. Style references.
  4. Size and limits.
  5. Mods or plugins used.
  6. Use: private, community, brand event.
  7. Deadline.

Describe how players will use the space. A spawn area, adventure map and minigame arena need different layouts and paths.

Mention performance limits. Very large or detailed builds can slow down servers or devices.

Check the game's rules for monetization and brand use before planning commercial projects.

Plan how players will discover and enjoy the creation. Signs, simple tutorials and clear starting points help new visitors understand what to do, especially at community events or brand activations.

Ask for a short guide for your moderators or community team explaining key areas, commands and settings. It helps them welcome players, answer questions and handle problems without needing the creator every time something comes up.

What drives the price

  • build size and detail
  • custom mechanics or scripts
  • mods and plugins
  • testing
  • server setup
  • rush delivery

A small decorative build costs much less than a full custom map with scripted minigames and server setup.

Agree on support after delivery, such as fixing bugs after a game update. Sandbox games update often, and small adjustments keep the creation working.

Red flags

  • Ignores platform rules.
  • Wrong game version.
  • No previews.
  • No backups.
  • Unclear ownership.

Back up worlds and files before and after installation.

Keep notes on versions and plugins used.

For game projects, see our game development guide. For testing, read our game testing guide and game art guide.

Discuss how the creation will be maintained if your community grows. Larger player numbers may need performance tweaks, extra areas or new features, and planning for growth avoids rebuilding later.

If the build includes your brand, provide logos and colors in high quality. Clear brand elements make in-game spaces recognizable and professional.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared my game version and platform.
  • I will see previews.
  • Testing happens on a copy.
  • Platform rules are checked.
  • I get files and instructions.
