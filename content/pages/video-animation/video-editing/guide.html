What to confirm in the package

Video editing gigs are usually priced by the length of raw footage, the length of the final video, or both. Make sure the package states:

  • Footage and final length: for example "up to 30 minutes of footage, final video up to 10 minutes".
  • What editing includes: cuts, pacing, color correction, audio cleanup, captions, simple motion graphics, sound effects.
  • Music: whether the editor adds licensed music, and where it comes from.
  • Formats: 16:9 for YouTube, 9:16 for Shorts, Reels and TikTok, or both from the same footage.
  • Revisions and delivery time.
  • Project files: useful if someone else may edit later. Often an extra.

What to send your editor

  1. The goal and platform. "A 10-minute YouTube tutorial that keeps viewers to the end" is a different job from "a 30-second product ad".
  2. Two or three reference videos with timestamps of moments you like.
  3. Organized footage. Clear file names, and a note on which takes to use.
  4. A rough outline or script, even a bulleted one.
  5. Brand assets: logo, fonts, colors, intro or outro if you have them.
  6. Must-cut moments: mistakes, private information, anything that must not appear.

What drives the price

  • amount of raw footage and length of the final video
  • complexity: motion graphics, animated captions, sound design, color grading
  • number of formats and versions
  • turnaround time
  • monthly packages versus single videos

Red flags

  • A portfolio built only from templates, or no examples in your format.
  • Vague answers about where the music comes from.
  • Recent reviews mentioning missed deadlines.
  • Requests to move payment or communication off Fiverr.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know the footage length and the final length I want.
  • The package lists what editing includes, the number of revisions and the delivery time.
  • Music licensing is agreed in writing.
  • I have reference videos with timestamps.
  • My footage is uploaded with clear file names.
