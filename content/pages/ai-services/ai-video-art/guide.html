What a good AI video art package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number and length of clips.
  • Style and tools used.
  • Editing, color and sound.
  • Resolution and formats.
  • Consistency across clips.
  • Usage rights and tool terms.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short style test before the full project. AI generation involves many attempts, and agreeing on a direction early prevents wasted rounds.

Check clips frame by frame in key moments. Warping hands, melting objects or flickering backgrounds are common and should be fixed or cut before delivery.

Look at the artist's finished videos rather than single impressive clips. Watch whether motion feels smooth, whether the style holds across cuts and whether sound and edits support the visuals. Finished pieces show the editing skill that turns AI generations into watchable videos.

How to brief an AI video artist

  1. Purpose: ad, art piece, loop or social post.
  2. Mood and style references.
  3. Scenes or ideas to show.
  4. Brand colors and assets.
  5. Length and formats.
  6. Music or sound, if any.
  7. Usage and deadline.

Describe the feeling you want viewers to have rather than only the objects in the scene. AI tools respond well to mood, lighting and camera directions, and artists can translate those into strong visuals.

Plan how AI clips fit with real footage or graphics. A mix of AI scenes, real product shots and clean text often looks more professional than a video made only of AI generations.

Ask about disclosure. Some platforms and audiences expect AI-generated content to be labeled, and clear labeling avoids confusion.

What drives the price

  • number and length of clips
  • editing and compositing
  • consistency across scenes
  • sound design or music
  • upscaling and formats
  • rush delivery

A single looping clip costs much less than a multi-scene video with consistent characters, editing and sound.

Ask whether the price includes the paid plans of AI tools needed for commercial use, since some plans restrict business use.

Red flags

  • Flickering or warped clips left unedited.
  • No style test.
  • Unclear commercial rights.
  • Imitations of famous films or characters.
  • Low resolution output.

Keep the final files, project notes and the list of tools used. It helps answer rights questions and makes it easier to create matching clips later.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan how the clips will be used over time. A set of short loops or scenes in one style can serve ads, social posts and website backgrounds for months. Ordering a small library at once is often more efficient than requesting single clips repeatedly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I approved a style test.
  • Clips are checked for flicker and warping.
  • Tool terms allow my use.
  • I will get the formats I need.
  • AI content will be labeled where required.
