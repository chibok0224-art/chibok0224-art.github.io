1. Decide what kind of help you need

There are three common roles. A coach or consultant teaches you how to run the store, reviews your numbers and suggests changes. A virtual assistant carries out tasks you define: updating listings, answering messages, checking stock, preparing reports. An account manager takes broader responsibility for results, including advertising and listing improvement. Choose the role that fits how involved you want to be and how well you understand the platform.

2. List the tasks and the time they take

Write down the jobs that repeat each week and each month. Next to each, note how long it takes and whether it needs judgment or just follows steps. Routine, step-based tasks suit an assistant. Tasks that need decisions, such as pricing and ad strategy, need someone with more experience and clear limits on what they may change without asking you.

3. Protect your account

Never share your main login. Use the platform's tools for adding users with limited permissions, and keep the account email and payment details under your control. Remove access as soon as the work ends. Keep a record of who has access and what each person is allowed to do. Be careful with anyone who asks for payment details or asks you to share two-step verification codes.

4. Write down your standards

Provide guidelines for how listings should look, how customers should be answered, what discounts are allowed and which actions need your approval. Share your brand voice, product details and any rules about returns. Good standards turn a stranger into a useful team member within days instead of weeks.

5. Agree on reporting

Decide what you want to see and how often: sales, advertising cost, stock levels, customer messages handled and listing changes made. A short weekly note and a monthly review work well for many sellers. Ask for explanations in plain language, not only spreadsheets, so that you understand what is happening and why.

6. Start with a small trial

Begin with a limited task or a short period, such as updating a few listings or preparing one advertising report. Judge the quality, speed and communication before widening the scope. If you also need new product text, photos or videos, treat them as separate projects with their own briefs.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which marketplaces and product categories have you worked with?
  • How do you protect the account, and what permissions will you need?
  • How will you report, and what will the report include?
  • How do you handle customer messages and negative reviews?
  • What happens if there is a policy warning or a listing is suspended?
  • How quickly do you respond, and during which hours do you work?

Growing the arrangement

If the first trial goes well, expand the work in steps. Add one new task at a time and record the instructions in a shared document so that the process does not live only in one person's head. Review results monthly: sales, advertising cost, stock problems and customer feedback. Keep control of key decisions such as pricing rules, budget limits and new products. Over time you may move from doing everything yourself to guiding a small, reliable support team, which gives you time to focus on sourcing, branding and growth.

Budget and fees

Pricing models differ: a fixed monthly fee, an hourly rate, a percentage of sales or a one-time project price. Each has trade-offs. A percentage can align incentives but may reward sales over profit, while a fixed fee is predictable but requires you to define the work clearly. Whatever the model, write down what is included, how extra requests are handled and how either side can end the arrangement. Keep advertising spend separate from fees so you can see what each costs.

Common mistakes

  • Handing over full access on the first day.
  • Giving vague instructions and then being surprised by the results.
  • Spending on advertising without a budget limit or regular reports.
  • Hiring a manager when you only needed training.
