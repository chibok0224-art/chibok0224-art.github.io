What a good logo package includes

Logo gigs are usually sold in three tiers. The cheapest tier often looks fine in the preview but leaves you without the files you will need later. Before ordering, check that your package includes:

  • Number of concepts: how many different initial ideas you will see.
  • Revisions: how many rounds of changes are included after you choose a concept.
  • Vector source files: SVG, AI, EPS or PDF. Without these, printing your logo large or handing it to another designer becomes a problem.
  • Transparent PNGs for websites, documents and social media.
  • Color variations: full color, black, white (for dark backgrounds).
  • Layout variations: horizontal, stacked, and an icon-only version for favicons and profile pictures.
  • Rights: copyright transfer or commercial use of the final design.

How to brief a logo designer

Designers work fastest when they know what the logo has to do. A one-page brief should cover:

  1. Name and tagline, spelled exactly as they should appear.
  2. What the business does and who it serves. One or two sentences.
  3. Three words for the feeling you want, such as "calm, premium, modern" or "friendly, bold, local".
  4. Logos you like and dislike, from any industry, with a note on why.
  5. Where it will be used: website, app icon, packaging, signage, embroidery. Small or stitched uses need simpler shapes.
  6. Colors or fonts to use or avoid, including competitors' colors.

What drives the price

  • number of concepts and revision rounds
  • whether source files and rights are included or sold as extras
  • extra deliverables such as a brand style guide, business cards or social media kit
  • custom illustration or lettering versus simpler type-based marks
  • the designer's experience and track record

Red flags

  • Many concepts delivered in a few hours for a very low price. This often means templates or clipart.
  • No vector files, or vector files only in the top tier at a large markup.
  • A portfolio where many logos look alike, or look like well-known brands.
  • No mention of originality or rights anywhere in the gig.

Before you start using a new logo, run a reverse image search on it. It takes a minute and protects you from launching with a design that already exists.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The package includes vector files and transparent PNGs.
  • I will get color, black and white versions, plus an icon version.
  • Copyright or commercial use of the final logo is confirmed in writing.
  • I have looked at at least five logos in the designer's portfolio.
  • My brief lists the name, the feeling, examples I like and where the logo will be used.
