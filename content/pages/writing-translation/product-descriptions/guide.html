What a good product description package includes

Product description gigs are usually priced per product and by length. Check that the package states:

  • Number of products and length per description.
  • Structure: opening line, benefits, specifications and care details.
  • Search basics: product title, meta description and key phrases.
  • Tone matched to your brand.
  • Format: a document, or your import spreadsheet.
  • Originality, including a policy on AI tools.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a consistent structure across the catalog: the same order of sections and the same way of listing sizes, materials and care. Shoppers compare products, and a predictable layout makes that easier. It also makes future descriptions faster and cheaper to write, because the writer can follow a pattern you have already approved.

How to brief a product description writer

  1. Your product sheet: names, specifications, materials, sizes and prices.
  2. Photos of each product, including details.
  3. Your customers and what they care about.
  4. Common questions from reviews and support messages.
  5. Brand voice, with one or two descriptions you like.
  6. Words to avoid and claims you cannot make.
  7. Where descriptions appear: your store, marketplaces or both.

Order a few products first as a test. Check that the facts are right, the tone fits your brand and the descriptions answer real customer questions. Once approved, use them as the model for the rest of the catalog.

If you sell on marketplaces as well as your own store, ask about each platform's rules. Marketplaces often limit length, formatting and certain words, and a description written for your store may need adjusting before it can be used there.

What drives the price

  • number of products and description length
  • research needed for technical or complex products
  • search optimization and keyword research
  • several languages or marketplace versions
  • formatting into your import file
  • the writer's experience in your product category

For large catalogs, ask whether similar products can share a template with product-specific details. It keeps quality high while reducing cost for items that differ only in size or color.

Red flags

  • Descriptions copied from the manufacturer or competitors.
  • The same phrases repeated on every product.
  • Claims about health, safety or results you cannot prove.
  • Keyword stuffing that reads unnaturally.
  • No questions about your products or customers.

Check every fact before publishing: sizes, materials, compatibility and care. A wrong detail in a description leads directly to returns and complaints, and it is the writer's source material, your product sheet, that decides accuracy.

Questions to ask before you order

  • What will you need about each product, such as features, materials, sizes and customer questions?
  • Will every description be original, not based on manufacturer or competitor text?
  • How will you keep descriptions varied across similar products?
  • Will you include search keywords naturally, and which ones?
  • Will the descriptions fit my store's format, such as bullet points and character limits?

Start with your ten best-selling products and check their sales and returns after the new text goes live. It shows whether the style works before you rewrite the whole catalogue.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a product sheet with accurate details.
  • I will test a few descriptions before the full catalog.
  • The descriptions will be original, not copied.
  • The format matches my store or import file.
  • I will check every fact before publishing.
