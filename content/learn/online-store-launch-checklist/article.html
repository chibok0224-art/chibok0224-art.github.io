1. Platform and theme

Choose your store platform based on your products, budget and how much you want to manage yourself. Our Shopify vs WooCommerce article compares two common choices. Pick a theme that suits your products and works well on mobile, since many shoppers browse on phones. A store developer can set up the theme, pages and apps, but create the main account yourself so you own it.

2. Product photos and descriptions

Product listings do most of the selling. Prepare clear photos on consistent backgrounds, plus lifestyle images where they help. Our product photos vs 3D renders article explains the options. Write descriptions that answer buyers' questions: size, materials, use, care and what is included. A product description writer can create consistent, search-friendly copy across your catalog. Organize products into simple categories and collections so shoppers can find them.

3. Store policies and information

Most stores need pages for privacy, returns and refunds, shipping and terms of service, plus contact details and an about page. Policies should match how you actually operate. Requirements differ by country, so check local rules or get professional advice where needed. Clear policies reduce customer questions and build trust.

4. Payments, taxes and shipping

Set up payment methods your customers expect and test them. Configure tax settings according to where you sell, with advice from a qualified professional if you are unsure. Set shipping zones, rates and delivery times, and decide on free shipping thresholds if you offer them. Double-check packaging sizes and weights, because incorrect settings can eat into margins.

5. Test everything

  • Place test orders on desktop and mobile, with different products and shipping addresses.
  • Check order confirmation and shipping emails.
  • Process a test refund.
  • Click every link, menu item and button.
  • Check page speed and how images look on phones.

Ask a friend who has never seen the store to buy something and tell you where they hesitated. Fresh eyes spot problems you have learned to ignore.

6. Tracking and email

Install analytics before launch so you can see where visitors come from and where they drop off. Set up email sign-up and a basic welcome email; our starting email marketing article explains how. If you plan to run ads, add the platforms' tracking tools now so early data is not lost.

7. Launch marketing

Plan how the first customers will find you: existing contacts, social media, search, ads, marketplaces or partnerships. Start with one or two channels you can manage well. An e-commerce marketer can help plan a launch, and e-commerce SEO builds search traffic over time.

8. Daily operations

Once orders arrive, someone must process them, answer customer messages, update stock and handle returns. Write down simple steps for each task, even if you do them yourself at first. This makes it easy to hand work to an e-commerce manager or virtual assistant as the store grows. Set realistic response times for customer questions and display them on your contact page, so shoppers know when to expect a reply. Keep a list of common questions and answers; it will become the basis for a helpful FAQ page and, later, for customer service templates that keep replies consistent.
