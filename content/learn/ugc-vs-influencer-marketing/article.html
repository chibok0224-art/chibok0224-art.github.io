UGC creators

User-generated content (UGC) creators make authentic-looking photos and videos with your product: unboxings, reviews, demonstrations and testimonials filmed in a natural, phone-first style. You receive the files and post them on your social accounts, product pages or paid ads. You are paying for content, not for the creator's followers, so even creators with small audiences can be excellent.

UGC is especially useful for testing ad ideas, because you can order several videos with different hooks and see which performs best.

Influencers

Influencer marketing pays creators to share your product with their audience. The value is their trust and reach within a community. Results depend on how well their audience matches your customers and how genuine the recommendation feels. Influencer campaigns are useful for awareness, launches and building credibility in a niche.

The two approaches also differ in control. With UGC, you approve the content before it goes anywhere and decide where it appears. With influencers, you usually approve key points, but the creator posts to their own audience in their own style. That loss of control is part of what makes influencer content feel trustworthy, so plan your approvals with a light touch.

Rights and disclosure

For UGC, agree on usage rights before ordering: organic posts only, or paid ads too, which platforms and for how long. Ask for raw files if you want to edit your own versions. For influencer campaigns, agree on the number and type of posts, approval steps and whether you may reuse the content. Paid partnerships generally must be clearly labeled as advertising; follow the rules in your market and the platform's branded content tools.

How to choose

  • You run paid social ads and need fresh creatives: UGC ads.
  • You want authentic videos for your product pages: UGC videos.
  • You want a niche community to discover your brand: influencer marketing.
  • You want a plan that combines both: UGC strategy.

Briefing creators

Give creators the product, the main benefit, who it is for and the key points to mention, but leave room for their own words. Over-scripted content loses the natural feel that makes this format work. Share examples you like, required claims or words to avoid, and any rules about what may not be said, such as health or income claims. Ask for a few different opening hooks; the first seconds decide whether people keep watching.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting sales from a UGC order without any plan to run the videos as ads or post them.
  • Choosing influencers by follower count instead of audience fit and genuine engagement.
  • Forgetting to agree on ad usage rights before running creator content as paid ads.
  • Writing a script so strict that the content no longer feels natural.
  • Ignoring disclosure rules for paid partnerships.

A simple way to start is a small test: order a few UGC videos with different opening hooks, run them as ads with a modest budget and keep the best ones. In parallel, approach a handful of niche creators whose audience matches your customers. After a few weeks you will know which approach brings results for your brand and can invest more where it works.

Measuring results

Measure UGC by ad performance: click rates, cost per purchase and how long people watch. Measure influencer work by traffic, sign-ups or sales from tracked links or codes, plus engagement quality. Avoid judging influencers on follower counts alone; audience fit matters more.
