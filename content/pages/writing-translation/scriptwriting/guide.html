What a good scriptwriting package includes

Scriptwriting gigs are usually priced by length and format. Check that the package states:

  • Format: YouTube, ad, explainer, podcast or film.
  • Length: target video length or word count.
  • Structure: hook, main points and call to action.
  • Visual notes: shots, graphics or scene directions.
  • Research: included or not.
  • Revisions, delivery time and rights.

Look for samples in your format. A good ad writer thinks in seconds and punchy lines; a good YouTube writer keeps attention over many minutes; a film writer builds scenes and characters. Samples in the wrong format tell you little about how the writer will handle yours.

How to brief a scriptwriter

  1. The goal: inform, entertain, sell, explain.
  2. Audience and where they will watch.
  3. Key points or the story outline.
  4. Target length.
  5. Who will speak: you, a presenter, a voice actor, characters.
  6. Tone, with example videos.
  7. Call to action, if any.

Read the script aloud before approving it, ideally in the voice of the person who will perform it. Sentences that look fine on paper often trip the tongue. Short sentences and everyday words work best on camera.

For YouTube, ask for extra attention on the first few lines. The opening decides whether viewers stay, so it should promise what the video delivers and get to it quickly.

Agree on how the script will be formatted. Two-column scripts, with spoken words on one side and visuals on the other, work well for videos and explainers. Film scripts use a standard screenplay format. Clear formatting helps everyone involved, from the presenter and voice actor to the editor and animator, understand exactly what happens in each moment. Ask for a version you can easily edit, so small changes on the day of filming are simple to make.

What drives the price

  • length and format
  • research and fact-checking
  • visual directions and shot lists
  • number of revisions
  • series versus single scripts
  • the writer's experience in your format

Red flags

  • Samples that read like articles, not spoken scripts.
  • No questions about audience, length or tone.
  • Factual claims with no sources.
  • Scripts that promise things the video cannot show.
  • Unclear rights to change or reuse the script.

For a series, share feedback and viewer data after each video. The writer can then adjust hooks, pacing and topics based on what actually keeps your audience watching.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know the format, goal and target length.
  • I have shared key points, audience and tone.
  • The writer has samples in my format.
  • I will read the script aloud before approving.
  • I have full rights to use and change it.
