What a good photography mentoring package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Format and length.
  • Topics: shooting, editing, portfolio, business.
  • Images reviewed.
  • Written notes or recording.
  • Assignments.
  • Mentor's specialty.
  • Pricing.

Ask for written notes with examples from your images. Specific comments help you apply advice in your next shoot.

Look at the mentor's own work and the progress of people they have mentored. Strong photographers are not always strong teachers, and examples of student improvement show teaching skill.

Prepare a small, focused selection of images rather than hundreds. Ten to twenty of your best and most typical photos give the mentor enough to see patterns.

How to brief a photography mentor

  1. Your photography type.
  2. Goals.
  3. Portfolio or samples.
  4. Equipment.
  5. Editing software.
  6. Specific questions.
  7. Schedule.

Choose a mentor who works in your type of photography, such as portraits, products or landscapes. Specialized experience leads to more relevant advice.

Ask about editing workflows as well as shooting. Many improvements come from consistent editing, file organization and choosing the right images.

Think about what kind of feedback motivates you. Some photographers want blunt critique, others need encouragement with clear next steps. Telling the mentor your preference helps them deliver advice in a way you can absorb and act on.

What drives the price

  • session length
  • number of images reviewed
  • editing coaching
  • business advice
  • package length
  • mentor reputation

A single portfolio review costs much less than a multi-week mentoring program with assignments and editing sessions.

Red flags

  • Vague feedback.
  • Pushes expensive gear.
  • No specialty match.
  • No notes or recording.
  • Promises of guaranteed income.

Revisit your portfolio after a few months of practice to see progress.

Tips for a smoother project

Practice between sessions with clear assignments, such as shooting in one light condition or with one lens. Focused practice builds skill faster.

Ask the mentor to recommend a few photographers or books to study in your genre. Learning from great examples, with guidance on what to notice, speeds up your development alongside personal feedback.

If you plan to sell photography, ask about building a portfolio for clients. Mentors can help you choose images that attract the right work.

Keep notes from each session and review them before shoots.

Share new work after applying feedback to see what improved.

Set a simple goal for the next month, such as a themed series or a portfolio update, and share it with the mentor.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I chose a mentor in my specialty.
  • My questions are specific.
  • I will get written notes.
  • Advice fits my equipment.
  • I will practice with assignments.
