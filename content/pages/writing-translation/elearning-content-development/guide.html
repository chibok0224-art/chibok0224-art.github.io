What a good eLearning package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Learning objectives for each module.
  • Course outline and number of lessons.
  • Content types: text, slides, video scripts, quizzes.
  • Authoring tool and format, such as SCORM.
  • Testing in your LMS.
  • Ownership and source files.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for one complete sample lesson before the full course is produced. It shows the writing style, visuals, interaction and length, and it is the best moment to adjust the approach.

Request a test upload to your LMS early. Technical issues with tracking, completion or mobile display are much easier to fix before all modules are built.

Look at courses the developer has built and try a lesson yourself. Notice whether goals are clear, lessons are short, examples are practical and quizzes test real understanding rather than memory of a single sentence. Experiencing a course as a learner reveals quality quickly.

How to brief an eLearning developer

  1. Audience: who learns and what they know already.
  2. Learning goals: what they should do after the course.
  3. Source material: manuals, slides, recordings.
  4. Subject expert available for review.
  5. Platform or LMS and format.
  6. Brand style.
  7. Deadline and launch date.

Write goals as actions: after this course, staff can process a refund or set up the device safely. Action goals make it clear which content to include and how to test learning.

Plan expert review time. Subject experts are often busy, and slow reviews are a common reason courses run late. Agree on review rounds and dates at the start.

Think about accessibility from the beginning: captions, readable text, good contrast and keyboard navigation. They help all learners and may be required in some organizations.

What drives the price

  • number of lessons and length
  • interactivity level
  • video and graphics
  • authoring tool and LMS setup
  • translation and localization
  • rush delivery

A short text-based module with a quiz costs much less than a full interactive course with videos, scenarios and tracking setup.

Red flags

  • Content copied directly from manuals.
  • No clear learning objectives.
  • No testing in your LMS.
  • Long lessons without activities.
  • No source files delivered.

After launch, review completion rates and quiz results. They show where learners struggle and which lessons need clearer explanations or more practice.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan how the course will be maintained. Products, policies and procedures change, and outdated training causes mistakes. Ask for source files and a simple structure that lets you update one lesson without rebuilding the whole course.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Learning goals are written as actions.
  • A subject expert will review content.
  • I will approve a sample lesson.
  • The course will be tested in my LMS.
  • I will own the content and source files.
