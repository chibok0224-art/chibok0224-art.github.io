1. Decide what the posts should do

Every batch should serve a simple goal. You may want more visits to your shop, more enquiries, more people at an event or simply a feed that looks active and trustworthy. Write the goal in one sentence and pick the one or two platforms where your customers actually spend time. A batch designed for one platform's size and style will perform better than generic posts copied everywhere.

2. Choose a few recurring themes

Themes make a batch easier to plan and easier for followers to recognize. Pick three to five, such as tips, behind-the-scenes moments, product highlights, customer questions and announcements. Decide roughly how often each appears, for example one tip and one product post each week. Recurring themes also let the designer build a small set of templates, which keeps the look consistent and makes future batches faster.

3. Gather your brand assets

Collect your logo files, brand colors, fonts, product photos and any images you have the right to use. Add a few posts from other accounts that you like and say what you like about each. If you have a brand style guide, send it. If you do not, the first few designs will effectively create one, so give clear feedback on them. Missing photos are the most common cause of delay, so list which posts need images you still have to take.

4. Write a simple content calendar

A calendar does not need special software. A table with the date, platform, theme, topic and any key message is enough. Include dates that matter to your business, such as launches, sales or seasonal events, and leave a few open slots for news that comes up. Give the freelancer the facts for each post, such as product names, prices or event details, so they do not have to guess. Wrong details in a scheduled post are easy to miss and awkward to correct.

5. Set review steps

Agree on how drafts reach you and how you give feedback. A shared folder or document where each post sits next to its caption works well. Approve the first few posts before the freelancer makes the whole batch, so changes in style happen early. Check spelling, dates, links and any claims, because once a batch is scheduled, mistakes repeat quickly.

Budget and timing

The cost depends on the number of posts, whether captions and hashtags are included, the number of formats, such as square posts, stories and carousels, and whether the freelancer creates new graphics or works from templates. Carousels and animated posts take more time than single images. Plan the batch at least two weeks before the first post is due, so there is room for feedback without rushing.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Which sizes and formats are included for each platform?
  • Will I receive editable templates as well as finished images?
  • Do you write the captions and hashtags, or only design the images?
  • Where do the photos and graphics come from, and may I use them commercially?
  • How many revision rounds are included for the whole batch?

Getting started

Send the goal, platforms, themes, assets and calendar in one message. Ask the freelancer to propose two or three sample posts before quoting the whole batch, or order a small first batch to test how you work together. Once the style is agreed, larger batches become faster and cheaper to produce.

Learning from the batch

After the posts go live, look at which ones people saved, shared or commented on, and which ones they ignored. Share these results with the freelancer before the next batch. Drop the themes that do not work and do more of what does. Over a few months, this simple loop usually matters more than any single design choice.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering posts with no goal or theme, so the feed feels random.
  • Using images without checking the rights.
  • Approving the whole batch without checking dates and details.
  • Sharing your account password instead of a limited role.
  • Never looking at results before ordering the next batch.
