What a good corporate video package includes

Corporate video gigs are priced by length, style and production needs. Check that the package states:

  • Purpose and audience.
  • Style: live action, animation, interviews or a mix.
  • Script or interview questions.
  • Editing: graphics, music, captions and color.
  • Length and cut-downs for social media.
  • Revisions and approval stages.
  • Rights for music, footage and use.

Ask for a short creative plan before production: the message, structure, style and who appears. Agreeing on this early is the best defense against endless revisions later.

Consider accessibility and different viewing settings. Many corporate videos are watched on phones, in offices without sound, or by people whose first language is different. Captions, clear on-screen text and a simple structure help the message reach everyone, and they are often expected for internal and public communications.

How to brief a corporate video producer

  1. The goal: hire, sell, inform or celebrate.
  2. Audience and where they will watch.
  3. Key message in one sentence.
  4. People and places to feature.
  5. Existing footage and photos.
  6. Brand guidelines.
  7. Deadline and approval process.

Choose real people and real moments. Authentic interviews and footage of your actual workplace usually build more trust than scripted lines or generic stock footage.

Prepare interviewees with the questions in advance, but let them answer in their own words. Natural answers are more convincing than memorized scripts.

Plan cut-downs for different channels: a full version for your website, short clips for social media and a vertical version for mobile.

What drives the price

  • length and style
  • filming or animation
  • interviews and editing time
  • graphics, music and captions
  • number of versions
  • approval rounds

Many revision rounds are usually caused by unclear goals or too many reviewers. Clear briefs and one decision maker keep costs down.

Red flags

  • No creative plan or script stage.
  • Music or stock footage with no license.
  • No consent forms for people on camera.
  • Vague answers about revisions.
  • One long video with no shorter versions.

Store signed consent forms with the project files. If someone leaves the company or asks to be removed, you will know what was agreed.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan where the video will live and how long it will stay relevant. Videos featuring specific employees, offices or figures can date quickly. Keeping some parts general, and filming extra footage you can reuse later, extends the useful life of the investment.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have one clear goal and message.
  • One person will approve the video.
  • Everyone on camera has given written consent.
  • Music and footage are licensed.
  • I will get cut-downs for each channel.
