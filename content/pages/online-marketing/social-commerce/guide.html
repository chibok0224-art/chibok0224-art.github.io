What a good social commerce package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Platforms and shop setup.
  • Catalog sync with your store.
  • Policy checks.
  • Content plan: posts, videos, live sessions.
  • Creator partnerships, if included.
  • Tracking of sales and costs.
  • Timeline and pricing.

Ask for a test order through the social shop before promoting it. It confirms that products, prices, shipping and payment work exactly as customers will experience them.

Request a policy check of your products and listings. Some categories and claims are restricted, and fixing issues before launch avoids shop suspensions.

Ask the expert which platforms they have sold on and what results their clients saw. Social shopping features differ between platforms and countries, and real experience with your target platform saves time and avoids policy mistakes.

How to brief a social commerce expert

  1. Products and catalog size.
  2. Store platform.
  3. Target platforms and markets.
  4. Content resources: photos, videos, creators.
  5. Fulfillment and shipping times.
  6. Budget for content and ads.
  7. Goals and timeline.

Make shipping and returns clear and reliable. Social platforms often rate shops on delivery and service, and poor performance can reduce visibility or block selling.

Create content made for each platform, such as short demos, behind-the-scenes clips and customer stories. Repurposed ads rarely perform as well as native content.

Test live shopping with a short, well-prepared session first. A host who knows the products, clear offers and good lighting matter more than long streams.

Keep customer data and relationships in mind. Encourage buyers to join your email list or visit your website, so you are not fully dependent on one platform.

Check which payment, tax and shipping settings each platform needs in your markets. Social shops often handle checkout themselves, and incorrect settings can lead to failed orders or unexpected fees that reduce your margins.

What drives the price

  • number of platforms
  • catalog size and sync complexity
  • content production
  • live shopping events
  • creator partnerships
  • ad management

Setting up one shop with catalog sync costs much less than ongoing management with content, live events and creators across several platforms.

Platform fees, commissions and ad spend are separate.

Red flags

  • No test order.
  • Ignores platform commerce policies.
  • Catalog not synced with inventory.
  • Promotion-only content.
  • No tracking of sales and costs.

Monitor stock and prices across channels. Selling an item that is out of stock damages ratings quickly.

Tips for a smoother project

Review results by platform monthly and focus on the channels that bring profitable sales.

Prepare customer service for social buyers. Questions and complaints may arrive as comments, direct messages or platform tickets. Decide who answers them and how quickly, since slow responses on social platforms are visible to everyone.
