What a good songwriting package includes

Songwriting gigs are usually priced per song and by what is included. Check that the package states:

  • Scope: lyrics, melody, or complete song.
  • Structure: verses, choruses, bridge and length.
  • Demo: included or not, and what it sounds like.
  • Files: lyric sheet, chords, audio demo.
  • Revisions and delivery time.
  • Rights and credits: ownership, credit and royalty share.

Listen to several finished songs from the writer, not just short samples. You want to hear how they build a chorus, tell a story and fit words to melody. Pick someone whose past songs already feel close to what you want.

How to brief a songwriter

  1. What the song is for: release, a gift, a brand, an event.
  2. The story or message, with specific details and names.
  3. Genre and mood, with two or three reference songs.
  4. Who will sing it, and their vocal range if known.
  5. Words or themes to include or avoid.
  6. Deadline, especially for gifts and events.

Give feedback on the lyrics before the melody is finalized. Changing words after the melody is set can break the rhythm, and small changes may mean rewriting whole lines.

If you plan to release the song, keep a written record of who wrote what. Distributors, publishers and royalty collection societies may ask for writer details and splits when you register the song.

If the song is a gift, think about how it will be delivered. Some songwriters can add a simple lyric video or a recording with a vocalist, which makes the moment more memorable. Plan the deadline with extra time, since personal songs often need a revision round to get names and details exactly right, and a gift that arrives late loses much of its effect.

What drives the price

  • lyrics only, melody only, or both
  • demo quality
  • number of revisions
  • rights: full ownership versus credit or royalty share
  • the writer's experience and credits
  • rush delivery

Red flags

  • Vague answers about ownership or credits.
  • Samples that sound very close to famous songs.
  • Generic lyrics that ignore the details you shared.
  • Promises of hits, charts or streams.
  • No written agreement on rights.

Read the lyrics aloud or sing them before approving. Words that look fine on paper can be awkward to sing, and it is easier to fix them now than in the recording studio.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know whether I need lyrics, melody or both.
  • I have shared the story, details and reference songs.
  • Ownership, credits and royalties are agreed in writing.
  • The song will be original.
  • I will review lyrics before the melody is final.
