What a good fine-tuning package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Goal and success measures.
  • Base model and provider.
  • Data preparation and cleaning.
  • Evaluation on held-out test examples.
  • Comparison with the base model and prompts.
  • Deployment and running cost estimate.
  • Documentation and ownership.

Ask for a simple evaluation report: test examples, outputs from the original and fine-tuned models, and how they were scored. Without this, it is impossible to know whether the work improved anything.

Ask the expert to explain, in plain words, why fine-tuning is the right approach for your case. A good expert will mention alternatives they considered and the trade-offs, such as cost, maintenance and how often your data changes.

How to brief a fine-tuning expert

  1. The task and why current results fall short.
  2. Examples of ideal input and output.
  3. Available data and its format.
  4. Quality standards.
  5. Where the model will run.
  6. Privacy limits for training data.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Clean your examples before training. Inconsistent or wrong examples teach the model inconsistent or wrong behavior, and no amount of training fixes poor data.

Keep a separate set of test examples that is never used for training. It is the only fair way to measure whether the fine-tuned model really performs better.

Check the base model's license and the provider's terms before training. Some models restrict commercial use, require attribution or limit where the fine-tuned model can be hosted. Knowing these rules early avoids building on a model you cannot legally use as planned.

What drives the price

  • amount of data preparation
  • model size and provider
  • number of training rounds
  • evaluation depth
  • deployment work
  • ongoing updates

A small fine-tune on clean data with a simple evaluation costs much less than a large project with data collection, cleaning, repeated training and production deployment.

Budget time for several rounds. The first training run rarely gives the best result; reviewing errors, improving examples and training again is a normal part of the process, and it should be reflected in the timeline and quote.

Red flags

  • Recommends fine-tuning without asking about alternatives.
  • No held-out test set.
  • No comparison with the original model.
  • Training data with private information.
  • Unclear ownership or provider terms.

Plan for updates. Your data and needs will change, so keep the training data and scripts organized so the model can be retrained when necessary.

Tips for a smoother project

Decide how the fine-tuned model will be monitored once it is in use. Track a small sample of outputs over time and compare them with your quality standards. Drifting results are a signal that the data or the model needs an update.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I tried prompts and document search first.
  • I have clean, consistent examples.
  • A test set is kept separate.
  • Results will be compared with the base model.
  • Ownership and running costs are clear.
