Short-form editing

Short-form videos usually last from a few seconds to about a minute and are shown in a vertical feed. Viewers decide within the first seconds whether to keep watching, so the opening hook matters most. Editors cut tightly, remove pauses, add on-screen captions, use zooms and cuts to keep movement, and often add music or sound effects that suit the platform. They deliver in vertical format with safe areas for each platform's buttons and captions.

Long-form editing

Long-form videos, often several minutes to an hour, need structure. The editor shapes the story, keeps the pace steady, adds b-roll, graphics and chapters, balances audio and keeps a consistent look across episodes. Retention matters here too, but it is earned by clear structure and good storytelling rather than constant fast cuts. Long-form editors often work from a script or outline and handle more footage per project.

Platform rules and trends change often, so ask editors how they keep up. A short-form editor who follows each platform closely will know current caption styles, safe areas and lengths that perform well, while a long-form editor should know how chapters, end screens and thumbnails work on your channel.

Repurposing: getting both from one recording

A popular approach is to record long content, such as a podcast, webinar or YouTube video, and have an editor cut it into short clips. This gives you a steady stream of short videos without filming separately. Good repurposing editors find moments that stand alone, add a hook and captions and reformat for vertical viewing. How to choose an editor

  • For short-form, look for a portfolio of vertical videos in your niche, and ask how they choose hooks.
  • For long-form, watch full videos they edited, not only highlight reels, to judge pacing over time.
  • For both, ask about turnaround for regular batches, file transfer and revision rounds.
  • Ask whether thumbnails, titles or descriptions are included or separate.

What to include in the brief

Share example videos you want to match, the platforms and formats, caption style, music preferences and brand assets. For short-form, say how many clips you want from each recording and whether the editor should choose the moments. For long-form, share the outline, chapter points and any sections to remove. Provide raw footage in an organized folder with clear file names.

Measuring what works

Each format has its own signals. For short-form, watch how many viewers stay past the first few seconds, how many watch to the end and how often videos are shared or saved. For long-form, look at average view duration, where viewers drop off and how many click through from thumbnails. Share these numbers with your editor. A good editor will use them to adjust hooks, pacing and structure, and over a few months your videos should hold attention better.

Avoid judging an editor on a single video. Results depend on the topic, the recording and timing as well as the edit, so review trends over a batch of videos instead.

Set up a repeatable workflow

Video content works best when it is regular. Agree on a weekly or monthly batch, a shared folder structure, a naming system and a fixed feedback day. A short style document, covering fonts, colors, caption style, intro and outro, keeps every video consistent, even if you later add a second editor. Regular, predictable work also tends to get faster and better as the editor learns your style.
