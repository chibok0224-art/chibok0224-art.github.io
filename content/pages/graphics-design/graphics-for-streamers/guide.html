What a good stream graphics package includes

Stream graphics gigs are priced by the number of items and animation. Check that the package states:

  • Items: overlays, webcam frame, scenes, alerts, panels, emotes.
  • Static or animated versions.
  • Sizes and formats for your platform and software.
  • Brand consistency: colors, fonts and logo.
  • Setup help or scene files.
  • Source files and rights.
  • Revisions.

Ask for overlays that leave space for the content. Game interfaces, chat and your webcam need room; an overlay that covers important parts of the screen frustrates viewers, however good it looks.

How to brief a stream graphics designer

  1. Your channel: link, games or content, and audience.
  2. Platform and software: Twitch, YouTube, Kick; OBS, Streamlabs.
  3. Brand: logo, colors and personality.
  4. Items needed.
  5. Examples of channels you like.
  6. Layout: where your webcam and chat appear.

Share screenshots of your current stream layout. The designer can then see what the game or content looks like and design around the parts viewers need to see.

Keep animations short and quiet enough not to distract. Alerts that play loud, long animations every few minutes can annoy regular viewers.

Plan for growth. If you may add new games or segments, ask for a style that can be extended with new scenes without redesigning everything.

Ask for your panels and offline banner to include clear, current information: your schedule, social links and what viewers can expect from your streams. These are often the first things new visitors see on your channel page, and outdated information makes a channel look inactive. Ask for editable versions so you can update the schedule yourself when it changes, without ordering a new panel each time.

What drives the price

  • number of items
  • static versus animated
  • custom illustration or characters
  • emotes and badges
  • setup help
  • the designer's experience with streaming

Emotes and animated alerts usually cost more per item than static panels, because they need extra work to stay readable at tiny sizes or to animate smoothly.

Red flags

  • Designs using copyrighted characters.
  • Files in the wrong size or format.
  • Templates sold as custom work.
  • Overlays that cover gameplay or chat.
  • No source files.

Test every scene and alert in a private or unlisted stream before going live. It is the easiest way to catch sizing and timing problems.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know which items I need and for which platform.
  • Files will match my streaming software.
  • Overlays leave space for content and chat.
  • Designs are original, with no copyrighted characters.
  • I will test everything before going live.
