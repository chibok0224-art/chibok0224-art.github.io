Shopify

Shopify is an e-commerce platform. It offers product variants, inventory tracking, discounts, shipping tools, abandoned basket features and a large app store for extras such as subscriptions and reviews. It works well for stores that expect to add products, sales channels and marketing over time. The trade-off is that blog and page design are less central, and extras often come as paid apps that add to monthly costs.

Squarespace

Squarespace combines polished website templates with store tools. It is a good fit for brands that want a beautiful site, strong content and a modest catalog: artists, makers, boutiques and service providers who also sell a few products. Its all-in-one approach is simple to manage. For large catalogs, complex shipping rules or advanced marketing automation, it may feel limited.

How to choose

List what you will sell, how many products you expect in a year and which sales channels you want, such as marketplaces and social media. If the store is the main business and you plan to grow it, Shopify is a natural choice. If the website is the main brand experience and the store is part of it, Squarespace may feel better. Test both with a few products and a mock checkout before deciding.

Briefing a freelancer

Share your product list with photos and descriptions, your brand assets, shipping and return rules and payment methods you want to offer. Mention tax settings, since they differ by country. Ask the freelancer to set up the account in your name, test the checkout from start to finish, and show you how to add products and manage orders. Ask which apps or extensions they plan to use and what they cost each month.

Questions to ask

  • Which platform do you recommend for my products, and why?
  • What monthly costs should I expect, including apps and fees?
  • How will you test checkout, shipping and tax settings?
  • Will product pages be written for search and for customers?
  • Do you handle photos and descriptions or only the setup?
  • Will you train me to manage orders and add products?

Budget and fees

Compare the full monthly cost, not just the plan price: payment processing fees, transaction fees, paid apps or extensions, a domain name and any help from a freelancer. Estimate your monthly sales and see what each platform would cost at that level. Free trials let you build a few products and test checkout before paying. Keep records of every subscription so the costs stay visible.

Before you launch

Make a short checklist: product photos and descriptions are complete, prices and tax settings are correct, shipping and returns are written down, payment methods are tested, and legal pages such as privacy and terms are in place. Place a real test order and refund it. Ask a friend to buy something on a phone and tell you where they got confused. A small round of testing prevents most launch-day problems.

Try this first

Choose three products and write down their names, prices, photos and shipping details. Set up a trial store on both platforms and add those products. Go through checkout as a customer on your phone, then look at the order from the owner side. Notice what feels clear and what feels clumsy. Bring screenshots to a freelancer so they can see exactly what you liked and advise on the setup that matches your needs.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping a test order before launch.
  • Ignoring shipping and return rules until customers ask.
  • Adding many paid apps for features you do not need yet.
  • Using manufacturer text and photos that other sellers also use.
