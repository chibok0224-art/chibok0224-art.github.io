What a good trend forecasting package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Market and category.
  • Time horizon.
  • Sources and evidence.
  • Visuals and mood boards.
  • Implications for your business.
  • Format and presentation.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for evidence behind each trend, such as sales data, search growth or examples from markets. Evidence separates real signals from personal taste.

Request recommendations specific to your products and customers. General trend lists are easy to find; tailored implications are what you pay for.

Look at past forecasts from the researcher and compare them with what actually happened. Forecasters who can show both hits and misses, and explain what they learned, are more trustworthy than those who only highlight successes.

Combine trend reports with your own customer data. Sales, returns, reviews and social comments show how your audience actually responds, and they help you decide which trends to adopt, adapt or ignore.

How to brief a trend forecaster

  1. Your brand and products.
  2. Customers and markets.
  3. Decisions the report should support.
  4. Time horizon.
  5. Competitors.
  6. Format.
  7. Deadline.

Explain which decisions depend on the report, such as next season's colors or a new product line. Clear decisions keep research focused.

Think about your production timeline. A trend that peaks next season is useless if your products take a year to develop. Ask the forecaster to align insights with your planning calendar.

Ask how the forecaster separates short fads from longer shifts. A viral product may disappear in weeks, while changes in values, technology or lifestyle can shape demand for years. Understanding the difference helps you invest wisely in what lasts.

What drives the price

  • market scope
  • time horizon
  • custom research
  • visual mood boards
  • presentation or workshop
  • rush delivery

A short trend summary costs much less than a tailored report with original research, visuals and a team workshop.

Avoid chasing every trend. Choosing a few that fit your brand values and customers creates a clearer identity than copying everything that is popular.

Red flags

  • No sources.
  • Generic trend lists.
  • No connection to your business.
  • Guaranteed predictions.
  • Copied reports.

Revisit forecasts after the season to see which trends developed. It improves future planning.

Ask for a short presentation for your team. Sharing the reasoning behind trends helps designers, buyers and marketers use the insights consistently.

Save visual references in a shared board so teams can return to them during design and campaign planning.

Review which trends performed well for you after launch. Your own results are the best guide for future forecasts.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Decisions the report supports are clear.
  • Each trend has evidence.
  • Recommendations fit my brand.
  • The time horizon is agreed.
  • Nobody guarantees predictions.
