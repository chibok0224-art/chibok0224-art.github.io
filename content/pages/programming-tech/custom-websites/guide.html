What a good custom website package includes

Custom projects need a clear scope. Check that the offer states:

  • Pages and features, listed one by one.
  • Technology and why it suits your project.
  • Content editing options for you.
  • Responsive design for phones and tablets.
  • Basic SEO and speed work.
  • Hosting, domain and launch setup.
  • Code ownership, bug-fix period and timeline.

Ask for a staging link where you can see the site as it is built. Reviewing real pages early is better than seeing everything at once near the deadline.

Ask the developer to explain, in plain words, which parts of the site will be custom and which will use existing tools or libraries. A clear answer shows they have thought about your project and helps you understand where the cost and the maintenance effort really are.

How to brief a website developer

  1. The site's goal: sales, leads, information or bookings.
  2. Pages and features you need.
  3. Design files or sites you like, with reasons.
  4. Content: who writes text and supplies images.
  5. Integrations: payments, forms, CRM, analytics.
  6. Who will update the site and how often.
  7. Budget and launch date.

Explain why you want a custom build. A good developer may suggest a simpler option if your needs fit a standard platform, and that honesty is a good sign.

Prepare your content early. Missing text and images are one of the most common reasons website launches slip.

Ask about accessibility as well. Clear contrast, readable text, keyboard navigation and descriptive image text help people with disabilities use your site, and they also tend to improve the experience for everyone. It is much easier to build this in from the start than to add it later.

What drives the price

  • number of pages and templates
  • custom features and logic
  • design work included or supplied
  • content editing tools
  • integrations with other systems
  • performance and security requirements

A site with a few pages and a contact form costs far less than one with user accounts, payments and a custom admin area.

Red flags

  • No questions about your goals before quoting.
  • Hosting or domain registered in the developer's name.
  • No way for you to edit content when you need to.
  • No staging site or progress reviews.
  • Unclear who owns the code.

At handover, ask for a short document with logins, how to deploy changes and which services the site depends on. It makes switching developers possible later.

Tips for a smoother project

Before launch, test the site yourself on a phone, a laptop and a slow connection. Fill in every form, click every button and read every page. Send your notes in one list, so the developer can fix everything in one round rather than many small ones.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Which technology will you build with, and why does it suit my project better than a builder?
  • Will I get a staging site to review progress before launch?
  • How will I edit text and images after launch without calling you?
  • Who owns the code, and will I receive the full source and a handover document?
  • How long will you fix bugs after launch, and what does later work cost?

Ask to see a site the developer built at least a year ago and whether the client still edits it themselves. A custom site that only its builder can change becomes expensive quickly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know why I need a custom site.
  • Hosting and domain are in my name.
  • I can edit content myself if needed.
  • I will review a staging site during the build.
  • Ownership and bug fixes are in writing.
