Email marketing

Email lets you send newsletters, product updates, offers, educational content and automated sequences. It supports images, links and longer text, so it works well for building relationships over time. Costs per message are low, and most email tools include templates, segmentation and automation. The challenge is attention: inboxes are crowded, so subject lines, relevance and timing matter.

SMS marketing

Text messages are short and usually read quickly. They suit time-sensitive messages such as flash sales, appointment reminders, delivery updates, restock alerts and event reminders. Because SMS feels personal, too many messages can annoy people quickly. Each message costs more than an email, and consent and opt-out rules are often strict.

Consent and rules

Both channels require care with permission. Collect clear consent, explain what people will receive, make unsubscribing easy and keep records. SMS often has extra requirements, such as specific wording at sign-up and quiet hours. Rules vary by country and change over time, so ask your freelancer which rules they follow and check the requirements for your market. Never buy contact lists.

Start with automations

For most businesses, the highest-value setup is a few automated messages: a welcome series for new subscribers, an abandoned cart reminder for online stores, a post-purchase follow-up and a re-engagement message for inactive customers. What to send

The best messages are useful to the reader, not just announcements. For email, mix offers with helpful content: how-to tips, product guides, stories behind your products, answers to common questions and early access for subscribers. A simple rhythm, such as one helpful email and one offer each month, keeps people opening without feeling sold to all the time.

For SMS, keep messages short, clear and worth the interruption. Say who you are, give the one key piece of information and include a link if needed. Avoid sending at night or too often, and do not use text messages for long stories or general newsletters. People sign up for texts expecting something timely and valuable, such as a restock alert or an appointment reminder.

Growing your list

Both channels depend on people choosing to join. Offer a clear reason to sign up, such as a useful guide, early access or a first-order discount, and place sign-up forms where visitors naturally look: the website footer, checkout, blog posts and a gentle pop-up. Explain how often you will send messages. A smaller list of interested people is worth far more than a large list of people who barely remember signing up, and it protects your sender reputation.

How to choose

  • Building a relationship and sharing useful content: email marketing.
  • Online store with frequent offers or restocks: email first, then add SMS for urgent messages.
  • Appointment-based business: SMS reminders plus occasional email.
  • Small list or limited budget: start with email.

What a freelancer should set up

A good email or SMS freelancer will connect sign-up forms, set up consent wording, build templates that match your brand, create segments, write or design the first messages and set up automations. They should also configure sender authentication for email so messages reach inboxes, and test everything on phones and computers. Ask for a short report after the first month showing opens or clicks, conversions and unsubscribes, and agree on what to improve next. If you need help with the words themselves, an email copywriter can write subject lines and messages that people actually read.
