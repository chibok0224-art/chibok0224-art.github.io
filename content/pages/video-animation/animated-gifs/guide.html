What a good animated GIF package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of GIFs and their length.
  • Dimensions and file size targets.
  • Loop: seamless or play once.
  • Transparency, if needed.
  • Extra formats such as MP4, WebM or source files.
  • Revisions and usage rights.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for the source file as well as the GIF. If you later need a different size, a version without text or an update for a new product, the source makes changes quick.

Request a preview at the real size and on the real background. A GIF that looks fine large on a white screen may be unreadable small in an email or on a dark social feed.

Look at the maker's samples in the places they were made for. A GIF for a chat sticker, a newsletter banner and a website hero each needs a different size, speed and level of detail. Samples that match your use show the maker understands those differences.

How to brief a GIF maker

  1. Where it will be used: email, social, website or stickers.
  2. Size and file limits of that platform.
  3. The message or action to show.
  4. Brand assets: logo, colors and fonts.
  5. Examples of GIFs you like.
  6. Length and loop style.
  7. Deadline.

Keep the idea simple. A GIF works best with one clear action, such as a product turning, a button being pressed or a logo appearing. Too many elements make the file heavy and the message hard to catch.

For emails, remember that some email apps only show the first frame. Put the most important message in that first frame so nobody misses it.

Plan a small set rather than a single GIF if you post often. A few matching animations, such as a greeting, a thank you and a product highlight, give your brand a consistent look in feeds and emails, and ordering them together is usually more efficient.

What drives the price

  • number of GIFs
  • animation complexity
  • custom illustration versus existing assets
  • length of the animation
  • extra formats and sizes
  • rush delivery

A simple text or logo animation costs much less than a fully illustrated character animation with many frames.

Red flags

  • No mention of file size or platform limits.
  • Loops that visibly jump.
  • Only the final GIF with no source files.
  • Grainy colors not discussed beforehand.
  • Uses copyrighted characters or clips.

Avoid GIFs made from movie or TV clips for business use. They often involve rights you do not have, especially in ads.

Tips for a smoother project

Test the final files where they will live. Upload the GIF to your email tool, social platform or website and check how fast it loads, how it loops and whether colors look right. Report any problems with screenshots so the maker can adjust the file.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know the size and file limits of my platform.
  • The idea is one clear action.
  • The first frame works on its own.
  • I will get source files and extra formats.
  • Usage rights are agreed.
