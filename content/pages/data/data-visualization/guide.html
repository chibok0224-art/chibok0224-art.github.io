What a good data visualization project includes

Data visualization gigs range from a few charts to live dashboards. Check that the project states:

  • Purpose: the decisions or questions the visuals support.
  • Tool and file format.
  • Charts or dashboard pages to be built.
  • Data connection and refresh method.
  • Design: colors, labels and brand style.
  • Accessibility: readable colors and text.
  • Handover: editable files and instructions.

Ask for a sketch or wireframe of the dashboard first: which numbers appear where and in what order. Agreeing the layout before building saves rework and keeps the focus on what users actually need to see.

How to brief a data visualization expert

  1. Who will use it and how often.
  2. The questions it must answer.
  3. The data, with a description of each field.
  4. Key numbers and how they are calculated.
  5. Existing reports you want to replace.
  6. Tool and access your team has.

Agree on definitions before building. Terms like active customer or revenue can be calculated in different ways, and a dashboard that uses a different definition from finance or sales will cause confusion and mistrust.

Keep dashboards short. A single page with the handful of numbers that matter is used far more than many pages of charts. Additional detail can live in a separate page for those who need it.

Ask the expert to add short notes next to charts, such as what a number means, where the data comes from and when it was last updated. People who open the dashboard months later, or who were not part of the project, will understand it without asking, and it reduces the risk of numbers being misread or quoted out of context.

What drives the price

  • number of charts or dashboard pages
  • data preparation and cleaning
  • live data connections
  • custom design
  • calculations and complexity
  • training and documentation

Messy data often takes more time than the charts themselves. Ask whether data cleaning is included, and share a sample early for an accurate quote.

Red flags

  • Charts with distorted scales or missing labels.
  • Dashboards crowded with every available metric.
  • No explanation of how numbers are calculated.
  • Files that cannot be edited or refreshed.
  • Colors that are hard to tell apart.

Check a few numbers on the finished dashboard against your source data. If they do not match, find out why before sharing the dashboard with others.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know who will use the visuals and for which decisions.
  • Key definitions are agreed.
  • The tool is one my team can use and update.
  • I will get editable files and refresh instructions.
  • I will check numbers against the source.
