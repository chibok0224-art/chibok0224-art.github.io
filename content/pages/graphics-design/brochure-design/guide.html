What a good brochure package includes

Brochure gigs are priced by format and number of pages or panels. Check that the package states:

  • Format: bi-fold, tri-fold or booklet, and the finished size.
  • Pages or panels to be designed.
  • Print-ready file built on your printer's template, with bleed.
  • Digital PDF for screen use.
  • Images: yours or licensed stock.
  • Source file and revisions.

Folded brochures are read in a specific order: the cover, then the inside flap, then the inside spread. Ask the designer to show you a simple mockup of how the panels fold, so the most important information lands where people look first.

How to brief a brochure designer

  1. The purpose: sales, events, information, recruitment.
  2. The audience and where they will get the brochure.
  3. Final text for each panel or page.
  4. Photos and brand files.
  5. The action you want readers to take, with contact details or a QR code.
  6. Printer template and file requirements.

Print a home copy, fold it and read it as a customer would. Check the order, the text size and every contact detail. Test the QR code with your phone. It is much cheaper to find mistakes on a home printout than on a box of printed brochures.

Ask whether the designer will create a mockup showing the brochure folded and in use. A realistic preview helps you and your team approve the design, and the same image is useful for your website or social posts announcing the new material.

What drives the price

  • format and number of pages or panels
  • custom illustrations or photo editing
  • copywriting included
  • print and digital versions
  • number of concepts and revisions
  • the designer's experience with print

Plan the print quantity with future changes in mind. Prices, staff and services change, and a large print run of outdated brochures is waste. If details change often, keep them on a separate insert or point readers to your website, so the main brochure stays current for longer.

Red flags

  • The designer does not ask for the fold type or printer template.
  • Files delivered without bleed or in low resolution.
  • Stock images with no license information.
  • Dense text in small fonts with no clear order.
  • No source file offered.

Paper and finish change how a brochure feels. Ask your printer for samples of paper weights and finishes, and tell the designer which you chose, since colors can look different on matte and gloss.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have chosen the fold and have my printer's template.
  • My text is final and proofread.
  • I have good photos or licensed images.
  • I will get print-ready, digital and source files.
  • I will print and fold a test copy before the full run.
