What a good jewelry design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Piece type and style.
  • Sketches and renders.
  • CAD file for manufacturing.
  • Sizes, such as ring size.
  • Stone details and settings.
  • Metal type and thickness.
  • Ownership, revisions and delivery.

Ask for realistic renders from several angles before final files. Renders help you see proportions and how the piece looks when worn.

Send the CAD file to your manufacturer for a check before production. Jewelers can spot thin areas or settings that will be difficult to cast.

Look at photos of finished pieces made from the designer's files, not only renders. Real jewelry shows whether settings, proportions and details translate well from screen to metal, which is the true test of a jewelry designer's skill.

How to brief a jewelry designer

  1. Piece type and occasion.
  2. Style references.
  3. Metal and color.
  4. Stones with exact sizes.
  5. Sizes.
  6. Manufacturer and file needs.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Measure stones precisely, including length, width and depth. Small differences decide whether a stone fits its setting securely.

Think about daily wear. Rings that catch on clothing or earrings that are too heavy are uncomfortable, however beautiful they look on screen.

Discuss metal weight. Thicker designs are stronger but use more metal and cost more to produce.

For collections, ask for consistent design elements across pieces, such as a shared motif or shape.

Ask about allergies and comfort when choosing metals. Some people react to certain alloys, and pieces worn every day should be comfortable and safe. A designer can suggest materials suited to your customers and your price range.

What drives the price

  • design complexity
  • number of pieces
  • stone settings
  • CAD detail
  • rendering quality
  • rush delivery

A simple band design costs much less than a detailed engagement ring with many stones and photorealistic renders.

Manufacturing, casting and stones are separate costs.

Share where and how you plan to sell the pieces. Designs for an online shop, a boutique or custom commissions may need different price points, packaging and levels of detail, and the designer can adapt the collection to fit.

Red flags

  • No CAD or wrong format.
  • Settings that do not match stone sizes.
  • Very thin parts.
  • Designs copied from known brands.
  • Unclear ownership.

Keep the CAD files safe. Resizing, repairs and reorders are much easier with the original model.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan for sizing and personalization. Rings may need several sizes, and engraving or birthstone options add appeal. Ask the designer to prepare the files so these variations can be produced without redesigning the piece each time.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Stone sizes are exact.
  • I will see renders first.
  • The manufacturer checked the CAD file.
  • The design is comfortable to wear.
  • I own the design.
