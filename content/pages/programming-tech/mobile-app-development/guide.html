What a good app project includes

App gigs range from small fixes and single features to complete apps. For a new app, the project should state:

  • Platforms: iPhone, Android or both, and the framework or language used.
  • A feature list, screen by screen, agreed before work starts.
  • Design: whether screens are designed by the developer or supplied by you.
  • Backend: user accounts, a database, an admin panel, and where they are hosted.
  • Milestones: what will be delivered at each step, and how you can test it on your own phone.
  • App store publishing: whether the developer submits the app for review.
  • Handover: full source code, logins for every service, and build instructions.
  • Support: how long bugs are fixed after launch.

How to brief an app developer

  1. The problem the app solves and who will use it.
  2. Core features for the first version, separated from later ideas.
  3. Screens: a simple sketch or list of each screen and what happens on it.
  4. Similar apps you like, with what you like about them.
  5. Accounts and payments: sign-in methods, subscriptions or in-app purchases.
  6. Other services the app must connect to, such as maps, payments or your website.
  7. Your deadline and budget range, so the developer can suggest what fits.

App stores review every app before it goes live, and they can reject it for missing privacy details, broken sign-in or content rules. Ask the developer to include the review in the timeline, and prepare your privacy policy, app description and screenshots early.

What drives the price

  • number of screens and features
  • one platform or both, and native or cross-platform code
  • backend work: accounts, database, admin panel, notifications
  • payments, subscriptions and connections to other services
  • custom design and animation
  • app store publishing, testing and support after launch

Red flags

  • The developer wants to publish the app under their own store account.
  • No milestones, only one delivery at the end.
  • Source code is offered only as an expensive extra, or not at all.
  • A full app with many features promised in days for a very low price. This often means a resold template.
  • Portfolio apps that cannot be found in the app stores or tested.

Split a large app into milestones and review each one on your own phone before approving it. This keeps problems small and cheap to fix.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a short list of core features for the first version.
  • The app store accounts will be in my name.
  • I will receive the full source code and build instructions.
  • The project is split into milestones I can test.
  • I know what support is included after launch.
