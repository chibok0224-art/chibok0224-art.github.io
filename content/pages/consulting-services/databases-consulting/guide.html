What a good database consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Review scope.
  • Access method: copy or read-only.
  • Performance findings.
  • Backup and security review.
  • Prioritized recommendations.
  • Migration or scaling plan, if needed.
  • Follow-up.

Ask for measurements before and after any suggested change, such as query times. Evidence shows which recommendations matter most.

Request a check that backups exist and can be restored. It is the most important safety net for any database.

How to brief a database consultant

  1. Database type and version.
  2. Size and growth.
  3. Slow queries or errors.
  4. Application and users.
  5. Hosting.
  6. Team skills.
  7. Deadline.

Describe the symptoms you see, such as slow pages at certain times, timeouts or growing storage costs. Specific symptoms help the consultant focus on the right parts of the database quickly instead of reviewing everything at the same depth.

Share expected growth in users and data over the next year or two. Advice that works today may fail at ten times the volume, and planning ahead avoids rushed migrations later.

Explain who will apply the recommendations. If your team is small or not specialized in databases, simple, well-documented changes are better than advanced tuning that only experts can maintain.

Ask consultants for examples of performance or design problems they solved and what improved. Clear results such as faster pages or lower costs show practical experience beyond theory.

Keep the consultant's report alongside your technical documentation and update it when you make changes, so future developers understand why the database looks the way it does.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • review or audit depth
  • written report or roadmap
  • size and complexity of your systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A short performance review costs much less than a full design audit with migration planning and team training.

Plan a test environment for trying recommended changes. Testing on a copy protects your live system and builds confidence before changes reach customers.

Consider a short follow-up after implementing the top recommendations to confirm they worked and to decide on the next steps.

Red flags

  • Requests for full admin access for a review.
  • No backup check.
  • Changes on live systems without testing.
  • Technology choices beyond your team's skills.
  • No measurements.

Remove access when the review ends and keep the report with your technical documentation.

Ask for a short list of monitoring checks your team can run regularly, such as slow query logs, disk space and backup status. Simple monitoring prevents many future emergencies.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Access is limited or a copy is used.
  • Backups are checked.
  • Findings include measurements.
  • Advice fits my team.
  • Access is removed afterward.
