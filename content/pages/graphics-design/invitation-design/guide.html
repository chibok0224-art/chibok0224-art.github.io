What a good invitation package includes

Invitation gigs are priced by items, sides and concepts. Check that the package states:

  • Items: invitation, save-the-date, reply card, menu, signs.
  • Size and single or double-sided.
  • Concepts and revisions.
  • Print-ready files with bleed.
  • Digital version for email or messaging.
  • Editable file for detail changes.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for the digital version to be sized for phones as well as email. Many guests will open it on a phone, and a tall, readable image or a simple link works better than a print file shrunk to fit a screen.

How to brief an invitation designer

  1. The event: type, style and mood.
  2. All details: names, date, time, place, dress code, reply method.
  3. Colors or theme.
  4. Examples of invitations you like.
  5. Formats: printed, digital or both.
  6. Printer requirements and quantity.
  7. Deadline for sending.

Confirm every detail before design starts, especially the venue, time and names. Changes after printing mean reprinting, and changes after sending mean awkward corrections to every guest.

Ask someone else to proofread the final version. Names, dates and times are easy to misread when you have seen the design many times. A fresh pair of eyes catches mistakes quickly.

If guests need more information, such as travel, accommodation or gift details, put it on a simple web page or separate card rather than crowding the main invitation.

Think about envelopes and postage early for printed invitations. Unusual sizes, thick card or extra inserts can increase postage costs or need special envelopes. Ask the designer and printer to plan the full package, including envelopes, so there are no surprises at the post office when you are ready to send. A quick weigh-in of one finished invitation set tells you the exact postage before you buy stamps for every guest.

What drives the price

  • number of items in the set
  • custom illustration or calligraphy
  • number of concepts and revisions
  • digital and print versions
  • special finishes like foil or embossing
  • rush delivery

Special finishes and paper types can change how files must be prepared. Choose them with your printer before the final files are made.

Red flags

  • No questions about print size or printer.
  • Files without bleed.
  • Designs that cannot be edited if details change.
  • Stock images or fonts with no license.
  • No digital version when you need one.

Order a single printed sample before the full run, especially for special paper or finishes. Colors and textures often look different in person.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • All event details are confirmed.
  • I know the formats and printer requirements.
  • I will get print and digital versions as needed.
  • Someone else will proofread the final version.
  • I will order a printed sample first.
