What a good poster design package includes

Poster gigs are usually priced by size, complexity and concepts. Check that the package states:

  • Size and orientation.
  • Concepts and revision rounds.
  • Print-ready file with bleed and correct color settings.
  • Digital versions for social media and web.
  • Images: yours, licensed stock or custom illustration.
  • Source file for later edits.
  • Delivery time, especially before an event.

Ask to see the design at its real printed size before approving, for example by printing a section at full scale or viewing it on a large screen from a distance. Text that looks fine on a laptop can be unreadable from across a room.

How to brief a poster designer

  1. The purpose: an event, a film, a sale, a campaign or decoration.
  2. The headline and all details, spelled exactly.
  3. Where it will hang and how far viewers will be.
  4. Brand files: logo, colors, fonts.
  5. Images you want included.
  6. Printer specifications: size, bleed and file type.
  7. Examples of posters you like.

Decide the one thing people must remember, such as the event name and date. Everything else supports it. A designer can create a strong hierarchy only when they know what matters most.

Proofread every detail before approval: dates, times, addresses, web links and prices. Test any QR code with a phone. Mistakes on a printed run are expensive and embarrassing to fix.

If you plan a series of posters, for example for a festival or a season of events, ask for a template so later posters stay consistent and cost less.

Think about where the poster will compete for attention. A poster in a busy street or noticeboard needs bold contrast and a single striking image, while one in a quiet lobby can carry more detail. Telling the designer the setting helps them choose the right balance.

What drives the price

  • size and complexity
  • custom illustration or photo editing
  • number of concepts and revisions
  • digital versions and formats
  • template for future posters
  • rush delivery

Printing costs are separate. Ask your printer for a quote early, since paper type and quantity affect both cost and how the design should be prepared.

Red flags

  • No questions about print size or printer requirements.
  • Low-resolution files for large prints.
  • Images with no license information.
  • Crowded layouts with too many fonts.
  • No source file offered.

Order a single printed proof if you are printing many copies or a large format. Colors and details can look different on paper than on screen.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have one clear headline and all details.
  • I know the print size and printer requirements.
  • I will check the design at real size.
  • All images are licensed or original.
  • I will proofread every detail before printing.
