What a good 3D product animation package includes

3D product animation gigs are priced by model complexity, length and realism. Check that the package states:

  • Modeling: from your files or built from scratch.
  • Materials and textures: how realistic, and for which parts.
  • Animation: length, camera moves and effects.
  • Scenes: plain background, studio or lifestyle setting.
  • Formats: horizontal, vertical, square and still renders.
  • Music and text, if included and licensed.
  • Files and rights: video, stills and the 3D model.

Ask for a still preview of the model before animation begins. Approving the shape, proportions, colors and materials in a single image is quick; fixing them after the animation has been rendered takes much longer.

How to brief a 3D animator

  1. Product files: CAD or 3D models, or detailed photos and measurements.
  2. Materials: finishes, colors and textures, with references.
  3. Features to show and the order to show them.
  4. Where it will be used: store, ads, crowdfunding, presentations.
  5. Brand files: logo, colors and fonts.
  6. References: product animations you like.

Make sure the product design is final. Changes to the product after modeling mean the model must be updated, and every scene rendered again. If the design may still change, start with a short, simple animation.

Share physical samples or photos taken in daylight if you can. Real products often have subtle details, such as texture, reflections and seams, that are easy to miss from drawings, and they make the difference between a convincing render and an obviously artificial one.

Ask how revisions work at each stage. Changing a camera move or text is usually quick, but changing the model or materials after rendering can take much longer. Knowing this upfront helps you review each stage carefully and keeps the project on budget and on schedule.

What drives the price

  • model complexity and whether files exist
  • number and difficulty of materials
  • animation length and scenes
  • realism and rendering time
  • formats, stills and variations
  • the animator's experience and rush delivery

Red flags

  • Portfolio renders that look like stock models.
  • No preview stage before animation.
  • Materials that look plastic when they should be glass or metal.
  • No agreement on who owns the 3D model.
  • Music with no license information.

Keep the animation honest. Rendered products can look better than reality, but features, colors and size should match what customers will receive. Accurate visuals reduce returns and protect your reputation, and some advertising rules also require it.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have sent product files or detailed photos and measurements.
  • The product design is final.
  • I will approve a still preview before animation.
  • I will get the formats and stills I need.
  • Ownership of the 3D model is agreed.
