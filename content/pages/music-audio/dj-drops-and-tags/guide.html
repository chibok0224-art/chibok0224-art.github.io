What a good DJ drop package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of drops or tags.
  • Voice style and gender options.
  • Effects and sound design.
  • Dry and processed versions.
  • File formats, such as WAV.
  • Usage rights.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for both a short and a longer version. A quick tag fits inside beats without distracting, while a longer drop works for intros and transitions in mixes and shows.

Listen to the creator's samples inside real mixes or beats, not just alone. A good drop stays clear and exciting over music, with the name easy to understand. Samples that only sound good in isolation may disappear in a loud club or a busy track.

How to brief a DJ drop creator

  1. Your name with a pronunciation recording.
  2. Exact script for each drop.
  3. Genre and style.
  4. Voice preferences.
  5. Effects references you like.
  6. Use: mixes, beats, radio or streams.
  7. Deadline.

Keep scripts short and punchy. A name plus a short phrase is easier to remember than a long sentence, and it fits neatly into fast-moving mixes.

Test the tag in a real mix or beat at club or listening volume. Effects that sound exciting alone can make the name unclear when music is playing.

Order a small set with variations, such as different energy levels. Using the right version for each moment keeps the tag fresh rather than repetitive.

Think about how your tag will age. A name and a simple phrase usually last for years, while references to trends or specific events can quickly feel dated. If you expect your brand or name to change, order a smaller set first and expand it later.

What drives the price

  • number of drops
  • voice talent level
  • sound design complexity
  • versions and lengths
  • commercial or broadcast rights
  • rush delivery

A single simple tag costs much less than a pack of radio imaging with several voices, effects and broadcast rights.

Red flags

  • Mispronounced names in samples.
  • Effects so heavy the name is unclear.
  • Only processed versions delivered.
  • Low-quality MP3 files.
  • No clear usage rights.

Use tags with restraint. A tag at the start or once in a while identifies you, while too many can annoy listeners and artists who use your beats.

Organize your drops by length and energy in your DJ software or production folder. Having the right tag ready for intros, transitions and outros makes it easy to use them naturally and consistently in every mix or beat you release.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I sent a recording of my name.
  • Scripts are short and clear.
  • I will get dry and processed versions.
  • The tag stays clear over music.
  • Usage rights are agreed.
