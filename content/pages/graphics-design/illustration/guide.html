What a good illustration package includes

Illustration gigs are priced per piece, by complexity and by use. Check that the package states:

  • Number of illustrations and their size or detail level.
  • Style, with portfolio examples that match.
  • Stages: sketch, line art and color, with approval at each stage.
  • Revisions at each stage.
  • Files: resolution, formats, vector or raster, and source files.
  • Usage rights: where and how the art may be used.
  • Process: whether AI tools are used.

How to brief an illustrator

  1. What the art is for: book, website, packaging, merchandise, social media.
  2. The scene or subject, described clearly, with any text that must fit.
  3. Sizes and formats where it will appear, including print sizes.
  4. Reference images for characters, products, places or poses.
  5. Style references, ideally from the illustrator's own portfolio.
  6. Colors to use or avoid, including brand colors.
  7. Your deadline, with time for each approval stage.

Spend your feedback at the sketch stage. Changing a pose, layout or composition is quick in a sketch and slow once the piece is colored and finished.

If you need a series, such as illustrations for a website or a children's book, order one piece first. Once the style and characters are agreed, the rest of the series goes faster and stays consistent.

What drives the price

  • detail and complexity: a simple spot illustration versus a full scene
  • number of characters and backgrounds
  • size and resolution, especially for large prints
  • usage rights: personal, website, book, products or ads
  • vector artwork and source files
  • the illustrator's experience and rush delivery

Red flags

  • Portfolio pieces in many unrelated styles, which can mean the work is not all theirs.
  • No sketch stage before the final art.
  • Vague answers about usage rights.
  • Unclear answers about AI use when you asked for hand-made work.
  • Art that closely copies a well-known character or artist.

For book covers specifically, read our book cover guide. If the art will become part of your brand, our brand style guides page explains how to keep it consistent.

Ask how the illustrator will deliver files for each use. A piece for a website, a printed poster and a T-shirt may each need different sizes, color settings and formats. Sorting this out at the start avoids paying again for exports later, and it prevents blurry prints or odd colors when the art is used somewhere you had not planned. Keep the original high-resolution files safe, because they are hard to recreate later.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The illustrator's portfolio already shows the style I want.
  • I have sent the use, sizes and reference images.
  • I will approve a sketch before coloring.
  • Usage rights are agreed in writing.
  • I know whether AI tools are used in the process.
