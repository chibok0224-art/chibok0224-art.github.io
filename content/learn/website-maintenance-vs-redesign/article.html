What maintenance covers

Website maintenance keeps an existing site healthy. A maintenance freelancer updates the platform, themes and plugins, makes regular backups, monitors security and uptime, fixes broken links and forms, and handles small content changes. It is ongoing, usually monthly, and prevents the slow decline that turns small issues into emergencies. Our website maintenance guide covers what plans typically include.

Targeted improvements

Between routine maintenance and a full redesign sits a useful middle option: fixing specific problems. A developer can speed up slow pages, repair mobile layouts, simplify navigation, improve forms or update a few key pages. A conversion specialist can find where visitors drop off and suggest changes. These projects are faster and cheaper than a redesign and often deliver most of the benefit. See our troubleshooting and improvements guide.

What a redesign involves

A redesign rethinks the site's structure, design and often its content. It may also move the site to a new platform. It is the right choice when the site no longer reflects your business, the structure does not match what visitors need, or the technology is so outdated that every change is a struggle. A redesign takes planning: content, page structure, design, development, testing and careful launch steps to protect search rankings.

Start with an audit

If you are unsure, pay for a short audit before deciding. Ask a developer to review speed, mobile display, security, the platform version and how hard the site is to edit, and ask a designer or marketer to review clarity and conversions. A written list of issues, ranked by impact, often shows that a few fixes solve most of the problems. If the list is long and the fixes are expensive, it also gives you a clear case for a redesign.

Protecting what already works

Whatever you choose, protect the parts of the site that bring results. Before a redesign, list the pages that get the most visits and inquiries, and make sure they keep their addresses or get proper redirects. Keep analytics running through the change so you can compare before and after. Back up the old site in full before anyone starts work. These steps take little time but prevent the drop in traffic that sometimes follows a rushed launch.

Comparing costs fairly

A redesign is a larger one-time cost, while maintenance and improvements are smaller ongoing costs. To compare fairly, add up what you expect to spend on fixes over the next two years, including the time your team loses to an awkward site, and compare it with the cost of a rebuild plus its maintenance. If patching costs are rising year after year, the rebuild is often the better value. Either way, get written quotes for both options so you are comparing real numbers rather than impressions.

Who to hire

  • Routine care: a maintenance freelancer for your platform, such as WordPress.
  • Specific problems: a developer for troubleshooting and improvements.
  • Fresh look on the same structure: a website designer.
  • Full redesign or rebuild: a designer and developer, or a full-service website developer, plus a writer for website content.

Even after a redesign, plan for maintenance. A new site left without updates will face the same problems within a year or two. Our website project preparation article explains what to gather if you do decide to rebuild.
