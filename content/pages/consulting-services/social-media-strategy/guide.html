What a good social media strategy consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Channel audit with findings.
  • Audience insights.
  • Platform priorities with reasons.
  • Content pillars and example posts.
  • Posting rhythm that fits your team.
  • Success measures.
  • Review session and delivery format.

Ask for example posts for each content pillar. Concrete examples make the strategy easier for your team to follow than abstract descriptions.

Request a realistic posting rhythm based on your time. Consistent, manageable posting beats ambitious plans that stop after two weeks.

How to brief a social media strategy consultant

  1. Your business and goals.
  2. Current accounts and analytics.
  3. Audience and customers.
  4. Competitors you watch.
  5. Team time for content.
  6. Budget for ads or creators.
  7. Deadline.

Share what has worked before, even if it was by accident. A few strong posts from the past often reveal the topics and formats your audience prefers.

Be realistic about the time your team has. A consultant can only build a useful plan if they know how many hours per week are available for content.

Ask how the strategy connects to sales or leads. Social media works best when posts guide people toward a next step, such as visiting a page or joining a list.

Look at consultants' past strategy work and ask what results clients saw. Plans that led to steady engagement or leads show practical experience, not only good presentation skills.

Agree on what happens after the strategy is delivered. Some consultants offer a monthly check-in to review results and answer questions, which helps your team stay on track. Others hand over the plan and finish. Knowing this upfront lets you plan support and budget properly.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • research and audit depth
  • written plan or report
  • number of channels or systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A short audit with recommendations costs much less than a full strategy with research, calendars, templates and a follow-up review.

Plan a review after the first month of posting. Early data shows which pillars attract attention and which need adjusting, while there is still time to change direction.

Red flags

  • Generic advice that ignores your data.
  • Every platform recommended at once.
  • No measures of success.
  • Requests for passwords.
  • Guaranteed follower growth.

Track the agreed measures monthly and adjust. Strategies improve when they respond to real results.

Ask for a simple template for monthly reports. Tracking the same few numbers every month keeps progress visible.

Keep the strategy document in a shared place so anyone creating content can check the pillars and tone.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared analytics and goals.
  • Platforms are prioritized.
  • Example posts are included.
  • The rhythm fits my team.
  • Success measures are agreed.
