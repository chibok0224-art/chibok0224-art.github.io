What a good audio logo package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of concepts.
  • Length and versions.
  • Instrumentation and style.
  • File formats and loudness.
  • Stems for future adaptation.
  • Ownership or exclusive rights.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Listen to concepts on phone speakers, laptops and headphones. A sound that is rich on studio monitors may lose its character on small speakers, where many people will hear it.

Ask for a short guide on when and how to use each version. Consistent use is what makes a sound recognizable over time.

Listen to audio logos the designer has made for other brands and notice how quickly you recognize each one. Strong sonic logos are simple, distinctive and easy to hum. Complex sounds with many layers may impress at first but are harder for people to remember.

How to brief an audio logo designer

  1. Brand personality and values.
  2. Visual logo and brand guidelines.
  3. Where it will play: ads, apps, videos, podcasts.
  4. Reference sounds you like or dislike.
  5. Competitors' sounds to avoid.
  6. Versions needed.
  7. Deadline.

Describe your brand in feelings rather than musical terms: trustworthy, playful, calm or energetic. The designer translates these into notes, instruments and rhythm.

Consider connecting the sound to your visual logo animation. When the sound and motion happen together, people remember both more easily.

Test the shortlisted sounds with a few people from your audience. Ask what the sound makes them feel and whether they would remember it.

What drives the price

  • number of concepts
  • number of versions
  • custom instruments or recordings
  • voice elements
  • full sonic identity versus single logo
  • exclusive rights

A single short audio logo costs much less than a full sonic identity with brand music, app sounds and many versions.

Red flags

  • Sounds too close to well-known brands.
  • No exclusive rights.
  • Only one version delivered.
  • Sounds that only work on good speakers.
  • Stock sounds sold as custom.

Once chosen, use the audio logo consistently in every ad, video and app moment. Repetition is what turns a few notes into a brand memory.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep stems and source files. They let composers adapt the sound to new formats, longer music or special campaigns while keeping it recognizable.

Decide where the sound will appear first and roll it out step by step: video endings, ads, app moments or phone systems. A clear plan helps your team use it consistently, and consistency is what makes listeners connect the sound with your brand.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I described my brand's personality.
  • I will test concepts on small speakers.
  • I will get several versions and stems.
  • I own the sound or have exclusive rights.
  • The sound is not similar to other brands.
