What a good game testing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Type: bug testing, playtesting or both.
  • Platforms and devices.
  • Hours of play or test scope.
  • Report format: bug list, video, survey.
  • Confidentiality.
  • Number of testers.
  • Timeline and pricing.

Ask for bug reports with clear steps to reproduce, expected and actual results, and screenshots or clips. Reports like these save developers hours of guessing.

Request recorded play sessions with the tester thinking aloud. Hearing where players feel confused or bored is often more valuable than any written summary.

Ask testers for a sample report from a past project with private details removed. Clear structure, concrete examples and sensible priorities show they understand what developers need, which matters more than the number of games they claim to have played.

How to brief a game tester

  1. The game, genre and build version.
  2. What to focus on: tutorial, level, controls, balance.
  3. Known issues to ignore.
  4. Platforms and settings.
  5. Target players.
  6. Questions you want answered.
  7. Deadline.

Give testers specific goals, such as finish the first three levels and tell us where you got stuck. Focused tests produce clearer answers than open-ended play.

List known bugs so testers do not waste time reporting them. It also shows respect for their time and leads to more useful findings.

Choose testers who match your target players. Feedback from a hardcore strategy fan differs from that of a casual mobile player, and both matter only if they fit your audience.

Protect your build with personal keys or limited access, and agree that testers do not share screenshots or videos publicly.

Consider testing with players new to the game as well as experienced ones. First-time players reveal onboarding and tutorial problems, while returning testers can judge balance and long-term fun. Both views help build a game that welcomes players and keeps them.

What drives the price

  • test hours
  • number of testers
  • devices and platforms
  • recorded sessions
  • report detail
  • rush delivery

A short playtest by one tester costs much less than structured QA across several devices with detailed reports and videos.

Compatibility testing on many devices usually costs more because of hardware access.

Red flags

  • Vague feedback like it is fun.
  • Bug reports without steps.
  • No confidentiality.
  • Testers who do not match your audience.
  • No recordings or evidence.

Track bugs in a shared list with status updates. Testers can then confirm fixes in the next round.

Tips for a smoother project

Repeat playtests after major changes to see if problems were really solved.

Plan time to act on feedback before the next test. Testing is most valuable as a cycle: test, fix, and test again. Booking several shorter rounds usually brings better results than one large test right before launch.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Testers have specific goals.
  • Known bugs are listed.
  • Reports include steps and evidence.
  • Confidentiality is agreed.
  • Testers match my players.
