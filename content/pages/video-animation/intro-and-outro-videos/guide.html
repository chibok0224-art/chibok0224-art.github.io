What a good intro and outro package includes

Intro and outro gigs are usually priced per video element and style. Check that the package states:

  • Items: intro, outro, or both.
  • Length of each.
  • Style: logo reveal, animated text, character or footage.
  • End screen layout with space for clickable elements.
  • Music and sound, licensed for monetized videos.
  • Formats, resolution and a transparent version if needed.
  • Revisions and source files.

Watch the intro as a first-time viewer would, at the start of one of your real videos. If it delays the moment people came for, shorten it. Many successful channels open with the most interesting moment and show a brief branded sting only after that hook.

How to brief an intro and outro creator

  1. Your channel: link, topic and audience.
  2. Brand files: logo, colors, fonts and channel art.
  3. Mood: calm, energetic, playful, serious.
  4. Examples from channels you like.
  5. Outro contents: which end screen elements you use.
  6. How you edit, so files work in your editing software.

Ask for the outro to include a short spoken or written call to action that feels natural for your channel, such as suggesting a related video. Viewers are more likely to click when they know why the next video is worth watching.

Keep the audio level of the intro close to your normal voice level. A sudden loud sting after a quiet opening makes viewers reach for the volume or leave, especially when they watch many of your videos in a row.

Ask for the files in the format your editing software handles best, and for a version with a transparent background if the intro will play over your footage. Test the files in your own editing project before approving, so you know they import cleanly, play smoothly and line up with your audio.

What drives the price

  • intro, outro or both
  • style: simple text and logo versus custom animation
  • length
  • custom music or sound design
  • editable templates and source files
  • the creator's experience and delivery time

Red flags

  • Long intros offered as standard.
  • Music with no license for monetized videos.
  • Outros with no space for end screen elements.
  • The same template sold to many channels.
  • No source file or editable version.

Revisit your intro every so often. As your channel grows and your style changes, a refreshed intro and outro keep it feeling current. A source file makes updating much cheaper than starting from scratch.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The intro is short and comes after my opening hook.
  • The outro leaves space for end screen elements.
  • Music is licensed for monetized videos.
  • The style matches my thumbnails and channel art.
  • I will get source files or editable versions.
