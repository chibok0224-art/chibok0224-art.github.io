What a good data entry job includes

Data entry gigs are priced by time, by row or by document. Check that the job states:

  • Volume: the number of rows, pages or documents.
  • Source: scans, PDFs, images, websites or paper forms.
  • Output: spreadsheet, database, CRM or online store.
  • Format rules: column names, date and number formats, spelling.
  • Accuracy checks and how errors are fixed.
  • Turnaround for each batch.
  • Confidentiality and how files are handled.

How to set up a data entry job

  1. A template with the exact columns, plus a few correctly filled rows.
  2. The source files, organized and numbered.
  3. Rules for unclear cases: missing values, unreadable text, duplicates.
  4. Formats: dates, phone numbers, currencies and capitalization.
  5. A small first batch to check before the full job.
  6. Access to the system where data goes, through a separate user account.

Check the first batch carefully. Pick ten random rows and compare them with the source. If the error rate is low, continue; if not, clarify the rules before the rest is done.

Use tools to catch errors: drop-down lists for fixed values, data validation for dates and numbers, and conditional formatting to highlight blanks. A well-built template reduces mistakes more than any instruction.

What drives the price

  • volume of data
  • quality of the source: clean digital files versus handwriting or poor scans
  • number of fields per record
  • research needed to find missing information
  • entry into specific systems
  • turnaround and accuracy requirements

Red flags

  • Very large volumes promised very fast, with no mention of checks.
  • Requests for your main account passwords.
  • Offers to scrape personal data from sites that do not allow it.
  • No questions about format or unclear cases.
  • Files kept in the specialist's own accounts after the job.

For ongoing work, write a one-page instruction sheet and keep it updated whenever a new question comes up. Include the template, the formats, the rules for unclear cases and who to ask. It lets you switch specialists or add a second one without training them from scratch, and it keeps the data consistent over months. Consistent data is much easier to search, analyze and import into other tools later, which is often the real reason you needed the data entered in the first place.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a template with example rows.
  • I have written rules for unclear cases and formats.
  • I will check a small first batch.
  • Access is through a separate account, not my password.
  • Any data from websites is allowed to be collected.
