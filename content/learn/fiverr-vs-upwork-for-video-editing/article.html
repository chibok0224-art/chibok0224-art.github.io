The short answer

Fiverr tends to suit clearly defined edits you can describe up front, such as a YouTube video, a set of short clips or a promo, because you pick a package with a known price and delivery time. Upwork tends to suit longer or open-ended work, such as an ongoing editor for several hours a week, because you post a job, compare proposals and often pay by the hour. Many good editors work on both.

How hiring works on each

Both platforms charge buyers fees on top of the editor's price, and the amounts change from time to time. Check the total on the checkout or contract page rather than relying on a figure from an article.

When Fiverr is the better fit

  • You need one video or a fixed batch edited, and you can describe the style.
  • You want to see prices and delivery times before contacting anyone.
  • You want to try two or three editors with a small paid test quickly.
  • You prefer not to write a job post and screen many proposals.

When Upwork is the better fit

  • You need an editor for a set number of hours each week, with changing tasks.
  • The work mixes editing with other jobs, such as channel management or scripting.
  • You want editors to pitch their approach to your project.
  • You are comfortable interviewing and managing a longer contract.

What matters on either platform

  1. Samples in your format. Short-form and long-form editing are different skills. Ask for examples like your videos.
  2. A clear brief. Length, style references, music, captions, aspect ratio, deadline. See writing a video brief.
  3. Revisions and files. How many rounds, and whether you get the project file or only the export.
  4. A paid test. One short video tells you more than any profile.
  5. Turnaround for regular uploads. Agree on how many days each video takes before you commit to a schedule.

Questions to ask any editor

  • Can you show two or three videos close to the style and length of mine?
  • Which software do you use, and will I get the project file?
  • How many revision rounds are included, and what counts as a revision?
  • Where do the music, sound effects and stock footage come from, and are they licensed for my use?
  • How many days do you need per video if I send footage every week?

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by price alone and paying twice when the edit has to be redone.
  • Sending raw footage with no notes on what to keep.
  • Skipping a test before handing over a whole channel.
  • Moving payments off the platform to save on fees, which removes your protection.
