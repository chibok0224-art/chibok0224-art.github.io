What a good video marketing strategy consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Goals for each video type.
  • Audience journey mapping.
  • Channel plan.
  • Production approach and budget.
  • Distribution plan.
  • Measurement.
  • Calendar and deliverables.

Ask for a short list of priority videos with their purpose and channel. Priorities keep production focused on the videos that matter most.

Request a distribution plan for each video. Videos without promotion rarely reach their audience.

How to brief a video marketing strategy consultant

  1. Business goals.
  2. Customers and questions.
  3. Existing videos and results.
  4. Budget and team.
  5. Channels.
  6. Brand guidelines.
  7. Timeline.

List the questions customers ask before buying. Videos that answer these questions often perform best, because they meet real needs at the right moment.

Share past videos and their results, including disappointments. Understanding what did not work helps the strategist avoid repeating it.

Think about repurposing. One longer video can become several short clips, quotes and posts, which stretches your budget.

Look at strategists' previous plans and the videos produced from them. A plan that led to a coherent series shows practical thinking.

Consider accessibility and silent viewing in the plan. Many people watch videos without sound or need captions, so the strategy should include captions, on-screen text and clear visuals. These details increase reach and make videos useful to more viewers on every platform.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • research and audit depth
  • written plan or report
  • number of channels or systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A planning session costs much less than a full strategy with research, scripts outlines and a production calendar.

Ask the strategist to suggest a simple starter set, such as one explainer, three short clips and one customer story. A small, focused first batch is easier to produce well and gives you real data before committing to a larger video budget.

Red flags

  • Production advice without distribution.
  • Trend-chasing without fit.
  • No measurement.
  • Budgets far beyond your means.
  • Generic templates.

Review results by video type each quarter and invest more in what works.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan production in batches where possible. Filming several videos in one session saves time and money.

Ask for guidance on video length per channel. Ideal lengths differ between platforms and goals, and clear guidance prevents videos that are too long for their audience.

Keep a simple library of videos with purpose, channel and results. It helps plan the next round.

Ask for a short guide on thumbnails, titles and captions for each channel.

Plan who will own video results inside your team. A named person who reviews performance and briefs new videos keeps the strategy alive after the consultant leaves.

  • Distribution is planned.
