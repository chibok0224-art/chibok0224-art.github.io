Wix

Wix is built around a visual editor where you can drag elements almost anywhere on the page. That freedom is useful when you have a specific layout in mind, and the platform offers a wide range of templates, apps and built-in tools for bookings, forms and small stores. The trade-off is that freedom makes it easy to end up with a page that looks cluttered or behaves differently on a phone, so careful checking of the mobile version matters.

Squarespace

Squarespace is known for clean, design-led templates. You edit within sections and blocks, which limits how far you can stray but makes professional-looking results easier to reach. It suits portfolios, studios, restaurants, consultants and other businesses that value a polished first impression. Customizing beyond the template can take more effort, and the editing model is less flexible than Wix.

How to choose

Start from what the site must do. If you need bookings, a blog, a small shop and a few extras, look at how each platform handles those exact tasks. If your main goal is to look professional with minimal fuss, a Squarespace template may get you there faster. If you want a layout that does not fit a standard template, Wix may be less restrictive. Most plans can be tried for free, so build a sample page in both before paying.

When to hire a specialist

You can do a basic site yourself. A specialist helps when the site needs to look distinctive, when you have many pages or products, or when you want the technical parts done properly: connecting a domain, setting up business email, adding tracking, and making sure the mobile layout is checked. Share the pages you need, examples of sites you like, your logo and colors, and the text and images you already have. Ask whether the work includes training you to make later edits yourself.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which platform do you recommend for my goals, and why?
  • Will the site be built on an account in my name, with me as the owner?
  • How many pages, products and revisions are included in the price?
  • Will you test the site on phones and tablets as well as computers?
  • Can you connect my domain and set up business email?
  • Will you show me how to edit text, images and prices myself?

After the site goes live

A website is never quite finished. Plan to review it every few months: check that forms still send messages to the right inbox, that links work, that prices and opening hours are current and that photos still reflect your business. Keep a simple note of your logins, plan renewal dates and domain expiry date in a safe place. If you expect to add pages or products often, ask the freelancer to build reusable page layouts so that new content looks consistent without extra design work.

Finally, remember that the builder is only the container. Clear writing, good photographs and a simple path to contact you matter more than any feature, so spend time on those even if you choose a specialist to handle the technical setup.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a template for its demo photos rather than its structure.
  • Skipping the mobile check, since most visitors may arrive on a phone.
  • Forgetting that you must own the domain name yourself and keep the account in your name.
  • Adding too many apps, which slows pages and adds monthly costs.
