What a good data typing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of pages or entries.
  • Source type: scans, handwriting, images.
  • Output format.
  • Formatting rules.
  • Accuracy checks.
  • Privacy and deletion.
  • Delivery time.

Ask the typist to mark unclear words, for example with a symbol, rather than guessing. Marked uncertainties are easy to review; hidden guesses are not.

Ask for a short test of a few pages from your most difficult documents. It reveals how the typist handles your handwriting, layouts and abbreviations, and it gives you a realistic idea of accuracy and speed before the full job.

Agree on how special cases are handled, such as crossed-out words, margin notes, stamps or signatures. Clear instructions prevent inconsistent results across the batch.

How to brief a data typist

  1. The files or samples.
  2. Output format and template.
  3. Rules for dates, numbers and names.
  4. What to skip.
  5. Volume.
  6. Privacy needs.
  7. Deadline.

Send a template showing exactly how the output should look. Columns, headings and formats in a sample file prevent inconsistent results.

Spot-check a random sample of the finished work against the originals. Checking a few entries from different parts of the batch catches systematic errors early.

Think about how the typed data will be used next, such as searching, analysis or importing into software. The intended use decides whether you need exact copies of the original layout or a simpler, structured format, and telling the typist saves rework.

What drives the price

  • number of pages
  • handwriting difficulty
  • complex layouts
  • languages
  • accuracy checks
  • rush delivery

Typing clean printed pages costs much less than transcribing difficult handwriting in several languages.

If documents contain personal information, consider removing or covering details that are not needed before sending. Sharing less data reduces risk for everyone.

Agree on turnaround for corrections. If you find errors during your spot-check, a quick fix round keeps the project on schedule and ensures the final file is ready for use.

Red flags

  • No accuracy checks.
  • Guessed words not marked.
  • Ignores formatting rules.
  • Careless with private documents.
  • No sample before the full job.

Keep originals with the typed files for reference.

For larger data work, see our data entry guide and data formatting guide. For PDFs, read our file conversion guide.

For large jobs, ask for delivery in batches. Reviewing the first batch lets you correct instructions before the remaining pages are typed.

Keep a short list of recurring names, terms and abbreviations for the typist. It improves consistency, especially when several typists work on one project.

Ask whether the typist can work in your preferred spelling and language conventions, such as British or American English. Consistent conventions save editing time later.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I sent a sample and template.
  • Formatting rules are written.
  • Unclear words will be marked.
  • Privacy is agreed.
  • I will spot-check results.
