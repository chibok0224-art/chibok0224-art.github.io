What to confirm in the package

  • Word count covered by the package, and the price for extra words.
  • Language and accent: "English" can mean American, British, Australian and more. Pick the one your audience expects.
  • Audio format: WAV for editing and video work, MP3 for quick use. Ask for the sample rate your editor needs if you are not sure, 48 kHz is common for video.
  • Editing: breaths and mouth noises removed, levels normalized, split into separate files per line if you need them.
  • Usage rights: online, paid ads, broadcast, and for how long.
  • Revisions: what counts as a free retake and what counts as a new script.

Listen to several samples from the same voice actor in different styles, such as a calm explainer, an energetic ad and a character read. Range shows experience, and hearing a sample close to your script's tone is the best way to judge whether the voice suits your brand.

How to prepare your script

  1. Finalize the text. Script changes after recording are the most common extra cost.
  2. Describe the tone in a few words: warm and reassuring, energetic and upbeat, calm and authoritative.
  3. Add a pronunciation guide for brand names, product names and unusual words. A short voice note from you is ideal.
  4. Share the context: the video it goes with, the audience and, if it must fit a timeline, the target length in seconds.
  5. Include a reference: a link to a read you like.

Mark pronunciation for names, brands and technical terms in the script, or send a short recording of how they should sound. Mispronounced words are the most common reason for re-recording, and a few minutes of preparation prevents a full revision round.

What drives the price

  • word count and the length of the finished audio
  • usage: organic online use costs less than paid ads, radio or TV
  • editing, file splitting and syncing to video
  • character voices or multiple takes in different styles
  • rush delivery

Red flags

  • Demos with background noise, echo or heavy music hiding the voice.
  • Only one demo for every style the gig claims to offer.
  • No clear answer on usage rights.
  • Unclear whether the voice is human or synthetic, when that matters to you.

Ask for the raw recording as well as the edited file if you plan further editing. Raw files give your video editor more flexibility to adjust timing, and they are useful if you later need a shorter version of the same script.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Can I hear samples in the style, language and accent my project needs?
  • Is a short test read of my script included before I order the full recording?
  • How many retakes are included, and does a changed script count as a new order?
  • Do I get a clean file without background noise, and in which formats?
  • What usage rights do I receive, for example online, broadcast or ads, and for how long?

Read your script aloud yourself before sending it. Words that are hard for you to say are hard for the voice actor too, and fixing them costs nothing at this stage.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My script is final, and I know its word count.
  • I chose the language, accent and tone.
  • The package states the file format, editing and revisions.
  • Usage rights for my purpose are confirmed in writing.
  • I included a pronunciation guide for names and brand terms.
