What a good design consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Format: live call, video review or written report.
  • Length of session.
  • Materials reviewed.
  • Prioritized recommendations.
  • Examples or quick sketches.
  • Follow-up questions.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a written summary with priorities after a call. Spoken advice is easy to forget, and a list ranked by impact turns it into a plan.

Request reasons with every recommendation, connected to your audience or goals. Reasons help you judge advice and explain changes to your team.

Look for consultants with experience in your type of design and industry. Packaging advice from a branding expert, or app advice from a UX specialist, is more useful than general opinions from someone who works in a different field.

How to brief a design consultant

  1. The design to review.
  2. Goals: sales, clarity, trust, consistency.
  3. Audience.
  4. Problems you have noticed.
  5. Data or feedback you have.
  6. Constraints: budget, time, brand rules.
  7. Questions you want answered.

Write down specific questions before the session, such as is our homepage clear about what we sell. Focused questions produce focused advice.

Share real results, such as conversion rates, customer comments or test feedback. Evidence helps the consultant separate taste from real problems.

Ask which changes give the biggest improvement for the least effort. Quick wins help you act immediately while planning bigger changes.

Be open to hearing difficult feedback. The value of a consultation is honest outside perspective.

Consider booking a short follow-up after you make changes. A second look confirms that the fixes solved the problems and often reveals the next most valuable improvement, which is cheaper than a full new review later.

What drives the price

  • session length
  • number of materials
  • written report
  • examples or sketches
  • follow-up support
  • expert seniority

A short review of one design costs much less than a detailed audit of a full brand system with a written report and follow-up session.

Red flags

  • Vague feedback without reasons.
  • Advice based only on personal taste.
  • No written summary.
  • Pushes you to buy a full redesign.
  • Ignores your goals and audience.

Act on the top priorities first, then review again. Small improvements tested with real users often show what to do next.

Tips for a smoother project

Some consultants offer packages that combine review with a later check after changes.

Ask whether the consultant can point you to good examples in your industry. Seeing what works for similar businesses gives you and your designer concrete inspiration and a shared reference for the next round of design work.

Save the report for briefing future designers.

Bring the people who will make the changes to the session if possible. Designers and developers can ask practical questions immediately, and they understand the reasons behind the advice, which leads to better implementation.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared results and feedback.
  • Advice will be prioritized.
  • I will get a written summary.
  • Recommendations include reasons.
