What a good marketing concept package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of concepts.
  • Explanation of each idea.
  • Examples: headlines, visuals, scripts.
  • Channel ideas.
  • Production notes and rough effort.
  • Ownership of ideas.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short reason with every concept: why it fits your audience and goal. Ideas with a clear rationale are easier to judge and to sell internally.

Request rough examples, such as a sample post, headline or storyboard frame. Seeing an idea in a real format makes it far easier to imagine the campaign.

Look at concepts the expert has created and which ones were actually produced. Ideas that made it into real campaigns show that the person balances creativity with practicality, which is exactly what turns a brainstorm into results.

How to brief a marketing ideation expert

  1. The goal of the campaign.
  2. Audience and what they care about.
  3. Product and key message.
  4. Budget and channels.
  5. Brand personality and limits.
  6. Past campaigns and competitors.
  7. Deadline.

Write one clear sentence for what the campaign should achieve, such as get students to try our app in the first week of term. A sharp goal leads to focused ideas.

Share boundaries early: topics to avoid, legal limits and brand rules. Ideas outside these limits waste time, however exciting they are.

Test favorite concepts with a few people from your audience before investing in production. Quick reactions reveal whether the idea is clear and appealing.

Check that ideas are original. A quick search for similar campaigns helps avoid looking like a copy of a competitor or a famous campaign.

What drives the price

  • number of concepts
  • research depth
  • examples and mockups
  • workshops
  • revision rounds
  • rush delivery

A short list of social campaign ideas costs much less than full campaign concepts with research, mockups and a presentation.

Production of the chosen concept is a separate cost.

Red flags

  • Ideas unrelated to your audience.
  • Concepts impossible on your budget.
  • Copies of well-known campaigns.
  • No explanation behind ideas.
  • Unclear ownership.

Keep unused concepts in a file. Ideas that do not fit today can be perfect for a later launch or season.

Tips for a smoother project

After a campaign runs, note what worked to inform the next ideation brief.

Involve the people who will produce the campaign in choosing the concept. Designers, video producers and social managers can quickly tell which ideas are realistic within your timeline and budget, and their early input avoids disappointment later.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The goal fits in one sentence.
  • Budget and limits are shared.
  • Each concept has a reason.
  • I will test favorites with my audience.
  • I own the ideas.
