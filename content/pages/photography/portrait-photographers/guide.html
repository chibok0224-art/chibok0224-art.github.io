What a good portrait package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Session length and location.
  • Outfit changes and backgrounds.
  • Number of edited photos.
  • Retouching level.
  • Proof selection process.
  • Usage rights.
  • Delivery time and file sizes.

Look at full galleries from the photographer, not only their favorite shots. Consistent results across many people show they can make anyone look comfortable and natural.

Agree on retouching before the session. Natural skin, removing temporary blemishes and leaving permanent features is a common, respectful approach.

Ask how the photographer helps people relax during a session. Many use conversation, simple pose guidance and short breaks, and some show a few images on the camera screen early so you can see how you look. A calm session almost always produces better portraits.

How to brief a portrait photographer

  1. Purpose: LinkedIn, website, acting, family.
  2. Style references.
  3. Location: studio, outdoors, office.
  4. Outfits and props.
  5. Number of people.
  6. Usage and deadline.
  7. Concerns, such as nervousness.

Share where the photos will be used. A corporate headshot, a creative website banner and a family wall print need different framing and backgrounds.

Tell the photographer if you feel nervous on camera. Good photographers guide poses, talk you through the session and take breaks to help you relax.

Plan shots in landscape and portrait orientation, and with space for text if photos will appear on websites or social banners.

Bring the items you need, such as glasses, a blazer or a product, and arrive with time to settle in.

For team headshots, ask for a consistent setup: the same background, lighting and framing for everyone. Consistency makes a website team page look professional, and it lets you add new team members later with matching photos.

What drives the price

  • session length
  • location and travel
  • number of outfits and people
  • edited images
  • retouching level
  • commercial usage

A short headshot session costs much less than a personal branding shoot with several locations, outfits and many edited images.

Prints and albums are usually extra.

Red flags

  • No full galleries in the portfolio.
  • Unclear retouching style.
  • No agreement on usage rights.
  • Very slow delivery.
  • Heavy filters that change how people look.

Update headshots every few years or when your look changes. Up-to-date photos build trust with clients and colleagues.

Plan where you will keep the final files. High-resolution images for print, smaller versions for websites and square crops for profile pictures are all useful. Asking for these versions upfront saves resizing later and keeps your photos looking sharp everywhere.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know where the photos will be used.
  • Retouching level is agreed.
  • Outfits and props are planned.
  • Usage rights are clear.
  • I chose a style from full galleries.
