What a good business consulting package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • The problem and scope.
  • Discovery: interviews, data review.
  • Deliverables: findings, recommendations, plan.
  • Sessions and format.
  • Success measures.
  • Follow-up support.
  • Timeline and pricing.

Ask for an action plan with owners, deadlines and expected impact for each step. Recommendations without a plan rarely turn into change.

Request a short summary you can share with your team. Clear, simple explanations help people understand and support the changes.

Ask consultants to describe how they would approach your problem in the first two weeks. A thoughtful answer that mentions data, conversations and quick checks shows a practical method. Vague promises of transformation suggest a generic approach.

How to brief a business consultant

  1. The problem in one sentence.
  2. Background: business, market, team.
  3. Numbers: revenue, costs, key metrics.
  4. What you have tried.
  5. Constraints: budget, time, people.
  6. Decision makers.
  7. Desired outcome and deadline.

Describe the problem as specifically as you can, such as repeat customers dropped this year, rather than we need to grow. A sharp problem leads to sharp advice.

Share what you have already tried and what happened. It saves time and prevents recommendations you know will not work in your business.

Be clear about resources. Advice that requires hiring three people is useless if you can hire one. Good consultants adapt recommendations to your capacity.

Schedule a follow-up session after a few weeks of implementation. Real progress often raises new questions that are worth discussing while momentum is high.

Decide how you will judge the advice before work starts. Pick two or three measures linked to the problem, such as margin, repeat customers or delivery times, and note their current values. Comparing them a few months later shows clearly whether the consulting made a difference.

What drives the price

  • scope and duration
  • discovery depth
  • number of sessions
  • industry expertise
  • implementation support
  • travel

A single advisory session costs much less than a multi-month engagement with research, workshops and implementation support.

Fixed-price projects need a clear scope; open-ended problems often suit hourly or monthly arrangements.

Red flags

  • Generic advice without your numbers.
  • Promises of guaranteed growth.
  • No action plan.
  • Recommendations beyond your resources.
  • Advice on legal or tax matters without qualifications.

Measure results against the goals agreed at the start. It shows whether the advice worked and what to adjust.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep the report and plan in one place so new team members understand the decisions.

Protect sensitive information with a simple confidentiality agreement before sharing financial details, customer data or plans. Most professional consultants expect this and will sign without hesitation.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The problem is specific.
  • I shared numbers and past attempts.
  • Recommendations fit my resources.
  • I will get an action plan with owners.
  • A follow-up session is planned.
