What a good videography package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Filming time and locations.
  • Crew and equipment.
  • Sound recording.
  • Deliverables: raw footage, edited video or both.
  • Formats and resolution.
  • Usage rights.
  • Travel, revisions and delivery time.

Agree on a shot list before filming. It ensures key moments, products and people are captured, and it helps the videographer plan lighting and timing.

Ask how sound will be recorded. Clear audio is essential for interviews and speeches, and poor sound is very hard to fix in editing.

Watch full videos the videographer shot, not just short highlight reels. Look at stable framing, natural colors, clear interviews and how well they capture moments as they happen. Complete work shows consistency across a whole shoot, which a highlight reel can hide.

How to brief a videographer

  1. The purpose and audience.
  2. Location, date and schedule.
  3. Shot list or key moments.
  4. People to film and interviews.
  5. Style references.
  6. Deliverables and formats.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Share a schedule for the day with times and contacts. Events move fast, and a clear plan helps the videographer be in the right place for key moments.

Arrange consent for people who appear on camera, especially in commercial videos. Release forms protect you when using footage in ads or on websites.

Plan for vertical and horizontal formats if you need both. Framing for social media and widescreen differs, and planning ahead avoids awkward crops later.

Visit or share photos of the location. Lighting, noise and space affect equipment choices and shot plans.

Think about the style of the final video before filming. Documentary, cinematic, social-first and corporate styles need different shots, movement and lighting. Sharing a few reference videos helps the videographer plan the look you expect.

What drives the price

  • filming hours and days
  • crew size
  • equipment and lighting
  • editing
  • travel
  • usage rights

A short shoot with one camera costs much less than a multi-day production with crew, lighting, drones and full editing.

Overtime on event days is often charged separately.

Red flags

  • No shot list discussion.
  • No sound plan.
  • Unclear ownership of footage.
  • No backup equipment.
  • Samples with shaky or poorly lit footage.

Ask for a backup copy of raw footage. Storage drives fail, and footage from a one-time event cannot be reshot.

Agree on how and when footage will be delivered after the shoot: a download link, a drive or cloud storage, and the expected date. Large video files take time to transfer, and clear arrangements prevent delays before your editing can start.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • A shot list is agreed.
  • Sound recording is planned.
  • Consent and permits are arranged.
  • Deliverables and formats are clear.
  • Raw footage is backed up.
