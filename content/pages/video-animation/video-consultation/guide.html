What a good video consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Format and length.
  • Materials reviewed.
  • Analytics review, if included.
  • Specific recommendations.
  • Written notes or recording.
  • Follow-up.
  • Pricing.

Ask for time-stamped notes on specific videos. Advice like the hook is slow at 0:05 is far more useful than general comments about pacing.

Request a short action plan ranked by impact, so you know what to fix first.

Look for consultants with experience on your platform and in your niche. Advice for short vertical videos, long tutorials and paid ads differs greatly. Ask for examples of channels or campaigns they have helped and what changed afterward.

How to brief a video consultant

  1. Your channel or videos.
  2. Goals: views, sales, leads, brand.
  3. Analytics: retention, click rate, traffic.
  4. Audience.
  5. Equipment and skills.
  6. Specific questions.
  7. Timeline.

Share analytics, not only links. Retention graphs and click rates show exactly where viewers lose interest.

Ask about your strongest videos as well as weak ones. Understanding why something worked helps you repeat it.

Be realistic about time and resources. Advice should fit what you can produce each week.

Ask whether the consultant can review your workflow as well as finished videos. Advice on planning, batching recordings and simple editing shortcuts can save hours each week, which matters as much as creative improvements.

What drives the price

  • session length
  • number of videos reviewed
  • analytics depth
  • written report
  • follow-up sessions
  • consultant experience

A single video review costs much less than a full channel audit with analytics, a content plan and follow-up sessions.

Red flags

  • Generic advice.
  • Promises of guaranteed views.
  • Ignores your analytics.
  • No notes or recording.
  • Outdated platform advice.

Review the advice after a month of new uploads and see what changed in your analytics.

Tips for a smoother project

Ask for examples of strong videos in your niche that illustrate the advice. Concrete examples make it easier to understand and apply recommendations.

Think about the bigger plan as well as single videos. A consultant can help you define content pillars, a realistic upload schedule and formats that suit your audience, which turns individual improvements into steady growth over time.

Choose two or three changes to test over your next few videos rather than trying everything at once. Focused tests show which advice actually improves your results and make it easier to build good habits.

Schedule a short follow-up after you apply the advice. A second review shows what improved and what to focus on next, while momentum is high.
