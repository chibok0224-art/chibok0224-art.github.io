What a good blockchain development package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Use case and network.
  • Smart contracts and features.
  • Testing and test networks.
  • Security review or audit plan.
  • Key management.
  • Front-end and integration.
  • Ownership, documentation and support.

Insist on testing on a test network before any real deployment. It lets you find bugs without risking real funds.

Plan an independent audit for contracts that hold value. Audits are a normal part of responsible launches.

Ask developers to share contracts they have deployed and any audit reports. Public code and independent reviews are strong evidence of skill and responsible practices in this field.

Plan user experience carefully. Wallets, fees and transaction waits can confuse users. Clear instructions, helpful error messages and simple flows make blockchain features usable for people who are not experts.

How to brief a blockchain developer

  1. The problem you want to solve.
  2. Why blockchain helps.
  3. Users and expected volume.
  4. Network preferences.
  5. Features.
  6. Legal review status.
  7. Budget and timeline.

Explain the problem before the technology. If a simpler system solves it, you save money and complexity.

Decide how upgrades and emergencies will be handled, such as pausing a contract. These decisions affect contract design from the start.

Consider monitoring after launch. Alerts for unusual activity, failed transactions or contract errors help you react quickly to problems.

Think about privacy. Data written to public blockchains is usually visible and permanent. Avoid storing personal information on-chain, and ask the developer how sensitive data will be handled off-chain instead.

What drives the price

  • contract complexity
  • number of integrations
  • front-end work
  • testing depth
  • audit costs
  • ongoing support

A simple test contract costs much less than a full application with several contracts, integrations and an external audit.

Keep communication with users honest and clear, especially about risks, fees and what the system does and does not guarantee.

Plan the cost of transactions for your users. Network fees vary and can affect how practical your application is, so test realistic usage before launch.

Red flags

  • No testing on test networks.
  • No audit plan for valuable contracts.
  • Developer keeps the keys.
  • Investment or profit promises.
  • Ignores legal requirements.

Keep documentation of contracts, addresses and admin procedures in a secure place.

Document all contract addresses, versions and admin procedures for your team, and store sensitive information securely.

Ask how the developer handles upgrades. Some contracts use upgrade patterns, while others are fixed once deployed. Each approach has trade-offs for security and trust, and the choice should be made deliberately and explained clearly to users.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I confirmed blockchain is needed.
  • Testing uses test networks.
  • An audit is planned.
  • My business controls the keys.
  • Legal advice is arranged.
