What a good automation project includes

Marketing automation gigs range from a single workflow to full CRM setups. Check that the package states:

  • Tools used, and whose accounts.
  • Workflows: triggers, steps, timing and messages.
  • Contact data: fields, tags and segments.
  • Consent handling and unsubscribes.
  • Testing of every workflow.
  • Reporting.
  • Documentation and training.

Ask for a simple diagram of each workflow: what triggers it, what happens next and when it ends. It is the easiest way for you to check the logic before anything goes live.

How to brief an automation expert

  1. Goals: which manual tasks you want to replace.
  2. Your customer journey: from first contact to repeat customer.
  3. Tools and access, through separate users.
  4. Existing contacts and how they signed up.
  5. Messages: existing emails or who will write them.
  6. Who will manage it after launch.

Start with one or two high-value workflows, such as welcome emails or lead follow-up. Get them working well before adding more. Too many workflows at once become hard to manage.

Review messages regularly. Automated emails can run unchanged for years; outdated offers or prices in them quietly damage trust.

Clean your contact list before building automations. Remove duplicates, fix obvious errors and separate contacts who agreed to marketing from those who did not. Automations built on messy data send the wrong messages to the wrong people, and fixing that later is much harder.

What drives the price

  • number and complexity of workflows
  • CRM setup and data migration
  • integrations between tools
  • copywriting for messages
  • testing, documentation and training
  • the expert's experience with your tools

Tool subscriptions are usually separate from the expert's fee and may rise with the number of contacts. Ask for an estimate.

Red flags

  • Messages sent to contacts without consent.
  • Accounts created in the expert's name.
  • No testing before launch.
  • No documentation of workflows.
  • Complex setups nobody on your team understands.

Check the rules for marketing messages where your contacts live, especially for email and text messages. The expert should help you follow them, but responsibility stays with your business.

Name one person on your team as the owner of the automations. They should know what runs, check reports each month and be the contact for changes. Without an owner, automations are often forgotten until something goes wrong. Ask the expert for a short handover session with that person, and keep the workflow diagrams where the owner can find them.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know which tasks to automate first.
  • Tools and accounts are in my name.
  • Consent and unsubscribes are handled.
  • Every workflow will be tested.
  • I will receive diagrams and documentation.
