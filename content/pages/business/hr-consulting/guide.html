What a good HR consulting package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: which policies, processes or templates.
  • Country or region the work is written for.
  • Deliverables and file formats.
  • Number of revisions.
  • Calls or workshops, if included.
  • Confidentiality of employee information.
  • A clear note on what needs legal review.

Ask for documents in an editable format, such as a shared document or word processor file. HR material changes as your business grows, and you should be able to update it without going back to the consultant.

Request a short summary explaining each policy in plain language. Managers follow rules they understand.

How to brief an HR consultant

  1. Company size and how fast you are hiring.
  2. Where your staff work: countries, states, remote or office.
  3. Current documents, even if they are rough.
  4. Problems you want to solve, such as slow hiring or unclear roles.
  5. Employee types: full-time, part-time, contractors.
  6. Tools you already use.
  7. Deadline.

Never send personal employee data unless it is truly needed. Use anonymized examples where possible, and agree on how files will be stored and deleted.

Be specific about the problem. A consultant who knows that new hires leave within three months will design a different onboarding plan from one who only hears that you want an onboarding document.

What drives the price

  • number of policies or templates
  • company size and number of locations
  • custom work versus adapted templates
  • workshops or manager training
  • consultant experience and specialization
  • urgency

A small set of templates costs much less than a full handbook, performance system and manager training for staff in several countries.

Red flags

  • Legal opinions from someone who is not a qualified lawyer.
  • Templates written for a different country without adjustment.
  • Requests for sensitive employee data without a clear reason.
  • Policies too long or complex for your team size.
  • No editable files.

Be careful with anyone who suggests classifying workers in a way that mainly saves cost. Worker classification rules are strict in many places, and mistakes can be expensive.

Tips for a smoother project

Start with the documents that solve your most urgent problem. A clear hiring process or onboarding checklist often helps more than a long handbook that nobody reads.

Ask the consultant to include a simple review schedule, such as once a year or whenever you open a new location. Rules and team needs change, and dated policies cause confusion.

Plan how you will introduce new policies to the team. A short meeting, a summary and a place for questions build trust, while surprise rule changes can damage morale.

Ask whether the consultant can train managers on the new processes. Documents only help when the people using them understand when and how to apply them, and a short session for managers often prevents inconsistent decisions later.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My scope and locations are clear.
  • Documents will be editable.
  • I know which parts need legal review.
  • Employee data is protected or not shared.
  • Revisions are agreed.
