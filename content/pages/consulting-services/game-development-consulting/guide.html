What a good game development consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: review, planning, launch.
  • Build review, if included.
  • Written feedback.
  • Production plan.
  • Store and pitch advice.
  • Confidentiality.
  • Follow-up.

Ask for honest feedback on scope. Many indie games fail because they are too big; cutting features early can save the project.

Request time-stamped notes on build reviews so your team can find each issue quickly.

How to brief a game development consultant

  1. Your game and genre.
  2. Team size and skills.
  3. Engine and platforms.
  4. Build or prototype.
  5. Budget and timeline.
  6. Goals: launch, funding, publisher.
  7. Questions.

Share your team's real capacity and budget. Advice that assumes a larger studio is useless for a two-person team.

Send a playable build if possible, even if rough. Playing reveals much more than design documents.

List the questions you most need answered, such as whether the core loop is fun or whether the store page is clear.

Ask consultants which games they have worked on and in what role. Relevant experience in your genre and platform matters.

Keep notes from each session and track which advice you applied.

Discuss marketing early, not only before launch. Wishlists, community building and a clear store page take months to develop, and a consultant can help you plan small, steady steps that build an audience while the game is still in production.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • review or audit depth
  • written report or roadmap
  • size and complexity of your systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A single build review costs much less than ongoing advisory support through production and launch.

Plan player testing alongside consulting. Real players confirm or challenge expert opinions.

Consider a follow-up before launch to review the store page and trailer.

Ask the consultant to help you define a realistic minimum version of the game, sometimes called a vertical slice. A short, polished section that shows the core experience is valuable for testing with players, pitching to publishers and keeping the team motivated with a clear target.

Red flags

  • Encourages oversized scope.
  • Guaranteed publisher deals.
  • Generic advice without playing the game.
  • No confidentiality.
  • Pushes trends that do not fit your game.

Revisit the advice at key milestones such as vertical slice and beta.

Ask for a short list of comparable games to study for scope and pricing.

Ask for feedback on accessibility options such as remappable controls, subtitles and colorblind modes. These features widen your audience and are easier to add early.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My team and budget are shared.
  • Scope will be reviewed honestly.
  • Feedback is time-stamped.
  • Confidentiality is agreed.
  • Advice fits my game.
