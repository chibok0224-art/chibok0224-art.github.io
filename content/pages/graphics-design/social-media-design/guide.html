What a good social media design package includes

Social media design gigs are sold per design, in bundles, or as template sets. Check that the package states:

  • Number of designs, and which types: posts, carousels, stories or banners.
  • Sizes for each platform you use.
  • Editable files: templates in a tool you can use, or layered source files.
  • Brand use: your logo, colors and fonts applied consistently.
  • Images: your photos, licensed stock, or illustrations made for you.
  • Revisions and delivery time.
  • Rights to use the designs commercially, including in ads.

How to brief a social media designer

  1. Platforms and formats you post in.
  2. Post types you publish most, such as tips, products, quotes, events or offers.
  3. Brand files: logo, colors, fonts and any style guide.
  4. Photos of your products, team or work.
  5. The text for each design, final and checked.
  6. Accounts you like, with notes on what you like about their look.
  7. Accessibility: enough contrast and text large enough to read on a phone.

Look at the drafts as they will appear: on a phone, in a feed, next to other posts. A design that looks balanced on a big screen can be unreadable at feed size.

A consistent look matters more than any single post. Ask the designer to set simple rules for the templates, such as where the logo sits and which colors are used for which post type, so future posts stay consistent even if someone else makes them.

What drives the price

  • number of designs and sizes
  • editable templates versus finished images only
  • custom illustrations, photo editing or animation
  • carousels with several slides
  • whether a brand style needs to be created first
  • one-off sets versus a monthly design plan

Red flags

  • No editable files offered, so every change needs a new order.
  • Stock photos or fonts with no license information.
  • A portfolio where every account looks the same, suggesting one template reused.
  • Designs with small or low-contrast text that cannot be read on a phone.
  • Offers to include followers or likes with the designs.

Before ordering a large set, ask for one or two sample designs using your own text and photos. It shows whether the designer understands your brand, how well they handle your real content rather than placeholder images, and how quickly they respond to feedback. If the samples need many rounds of changes, a big bundle will too.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have listed my platforms, formats and post types.
  • I have final text and my own photos ready.
  • I will receive editable templates or source files.
  • All images and fonts are licensed for commercial use.
  • I will check drafts at phone size before approving.
