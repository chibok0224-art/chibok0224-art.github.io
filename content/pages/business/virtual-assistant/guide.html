What a good virtual assistant package includes

Virtual assistant gigs are sold per task, per hour or as monthly support. Check that the package states:

  • Tasks covered, listed specifically rather than "anything you need".
  • Hours or volume: how many hours, emails, entries or posts are included.
  • Working hours and time zone, and expected response times.
  • Tools the assistant already knows, such as email, calendars, spreadsheets or your CRM.
  • Reporting: how tasks and time are logged and shared with you.
  • Language level for any writing or customer contact.
  • Confidentiality and how access to your accounts will be handled.

How to brief a virtual assistant

  1. A task list, starting with two or three tasks rather than everything at once.
  2. Written steps for each task. A short screen recording of you doing it once is often faster than writing.
  3. Examples of finished work: a good reply, a correctly filled row, a finished report.
  4. Rules: what the assistant may decide alone and what needs your approval.
  5. Access: which tools, through separate accounts or delegated access.
  6. Deadlines and how you want to be updated.
  7. Who to contact when something is unclear, and how.

Run a short trial with one well-defined task before handing over more. It shows the assistant's accuracy and communication, and it forces you to write down instructions you will reuse later.

Keep your written instructions in a shared document that grows over time. Each time the assistant asks a question, add the answer. After a few weeks you have a manual for your business that makes the assistant faster and makes it easy to bring in help when they are away.

What drives the price

  • number of hours or volume of tasks
  • skill level: simple data entry versus research, writing or customer contact
  • tools and software experience required
  • language level for writing and calls
  • availability outside normal hours or at short notice
  • one-off work versus an ongoing monthly arrangement

Red flags

  • Requests for your main email, bank or social media passwords.
  • Suggestions to move payment or communication off the marketplace. That removes your buyer protection.
  • Vague offers to do "any task" with no mention of tools or experience.
  • No way to see what was done and how long it took.
  • Poor written communication when the job involves writing to your customers.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have written steps and examples for the first tasks.
  • Access will be through separate accounts or delegated access, not my passwords.
  • We have agreed on working hours, response times and reporting.
  • I know what the assistant may decide alone and what needs my approval.
  • I will start with a short trial task.
