What a good signage package includes

Signage gigs are priced by number of signs, sizes and complexity. Check that the package states:

  • Signs: shopfront, window, wall, banner, wayfinding.
  • Exact sizes and materials.
  • Production files in the sign maker's format.
  • Mockups on photos of your location.
  • Colors matched to your brand and material.
  • Revisions and source files.
  • Delivery time.

Measure the space carefully and share photos from where people will actually see the sign, such as across the street or at the entrance. The designer can then plan size and contrast for real conditions.

How to brief a signage designer

  1. Location photos and measurements.
  2. Viewing distance and lighting, day and night.
  3. Text: business name, services, hours, contact.
  4. Brand files: logo in vector, colors, fonts.
  5. Sign maker requirements and materials.
  6. Local rules or landlord guidelines.

Keep text short. People passing by read only a few words, so focus on your name, what you do and one key detail. Extra information belongs on a website or inside the shop.

Consider night visibility. A sign that looks good in daylight may disappear after dark. If it will be lit, discuss how lighting affects colors and contrast with the designer and sign maker.

Ask the sign maker to review the files before production. They know their machines and materials, and can catch issues such as thin lines or colors that are hard to reproduce.

Think about accessibility. Clear, high-contrast text, simple fonts and sensible mounting heights help everyone, including people with low vision or who use wheelchairs. For wayfinding inside buildings, symbols alongside words make signs easier to understand for visitors who speak other languages. Some places have accessibility rules for signs in public buildings, so check what applies to your premises.

What drives the price

  • number and size of signs
  • complexity of the design
  • mockups and location visuals
  • production-ready file preparation
  • brand work if you have no logo
  • rush delivery

Production and installation are usually separate costs from design. Get quotes from sign makers early, since materials and size affect both price and design choices.

Red flags

  • No questions about viewing distance or location.
  • Raster files for large signs.
  • Colors that do not match your brand on the chosen material.
  • No mockups before production.
  • Ignoring local sign rules.

Check that your logo exists in vector format before ordering signage. If not, ask the designer to recreate it, since large signs need sharp, scalable artwork.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have photos, measurements and viewing distances.
  • I have the sign maker's file requirements.
  • My logo is available in vector format.
  • I will see mockups on my location.
  • I have checked local rules for outdoor signs.
