What a good supply chain package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: sourcing, inventory, logistics or all.
  • Data analysis of costs and lead times.
  • Supplier evaluation criteria.
  • Recommendations with numbers.
  • Templates or tools for planning.
  • Follow-up support.
  • Timeline and deliverables.

Ask for a comparison table for supplier or shipping options with total landed cost, lead time, minimum order and risks. A full picture prevents choosing the cheapest unit price that ends up costing more.

Request a simple reorder plan or spreadsheet you can keep using. Practical tools continue to help long after the project ends.

Ask for examples of businesses similar to yours that the expert has helped, including the size of orders and the markets served. Supply chain advice for a small online brand is very different from advice for a large distributor.

How to brief a supply chain expert

  1. Products and specifications.
  2. Sales history and forecasts.
  3. Current suppliers and terms.
  4. Lead times and shipping methods.
  5. Problems: stockouts, overstock, delays.
  6. Markets you sell to.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Share real numbers, including the uncomfortable ones, such as stock you could not sell or delays that hurt sales. Honest data leads to useful advice.

Always order samples before committing to a new supplier. Quality, packaging and communication during the sample stage predict how a full order will go.

Plan for disruption with a backup supplier or safety stock for key products. A single delayed shipment can empty your shelves during a peak season.

Check product compliance early, such as labeling and safety standards in your markets. Problems found after production are expensive or impossible to fix.

What drives the price

  • scope of work
  • number of products and suppliers
  • data analysis depth
  • international sourcing
  • factory visits or inspections
  • ongoing support

A one-time shipping cost review costs much less than full sourcing with supplier visits, negotiations and an inventory system setup.

Third-party inspections, samples and travel are usually extra costs on top of the consultant's fee.

Red flags

  • No questions about your numbers.
  • Supplier recommendations without vetting.
  • Prices without total landed cost.
  • Promises about customs or compliance without sources.
  • Pressure to pay suppliers through the consultant.

Pay suppliers directly through secure methods and keep contracts in your name. It protects you if the consultant relationship ends.

Tips for a smoother project

Review inventory and supplier performance every quarter, especially after big seasons or price changes.

Keep a simple supplier scorecard after the project: quality, on-time delivery, communication and price changes. Reviewing it regularly helps you notice problems early and gives you facts when negotiating or choosing between suppliers.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared real sales and cost data.
  • Options compare total landed cost.
  • I will order samples first.
  • Suppliers are paid directly by me.
  • Compliance is checked with official sources.
