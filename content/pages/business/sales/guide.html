What a good sales package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Tasks: research, outreach, meetings, closing.
  • Channels: email, phone, social.
  • Targets and activity levels.
  • Tools and accounts used.
  • Reporting and CRM updates.
  • Compliance with outreach rules.
  • Payment: fee, commission or both.

Ask for weekly activity and pipeline reports: contacts reached, replies, meetings booked and deals in progress. Activity numbers show effort; pipeline shows whether it is working.

Ask for examples of messages or scripts the expert has used and the results they produced. Strong sales people can explain why a message worked for a particular audience, and their examples show whether their style fits the tone of your brand.

How to brief a sales expert

  1. Your product and value.
  2. Ideal customer: industry, size, role.
  3. Pricing and offers.
  4. Common objections and answers.
  5. Sales process and who closes.
  6. Tools: CRM and email.
  7. Goals and timeline.

Define your ideal customer precisely. A sharp target, such as dental clinics with several locations, makes outreach far more effective than a broad list of small businesses.

Share real customer stories and results you can prove. Specific examples make outreach credible; vague claims sound like every other message.

Keep all contacts and conversations in your CRM. Relationships should belong to your business, not to the freelancer's personal accounts.

Protect your sender reputation if outreach uses email. New domains and high sending volumes can land messages in spam. Ask the expert how they warm up accounts, limit volume and handle replies and opt-outs, so your regular business email keeps working.

What drives the price

  • tasks covered
  • volume of outreach
  • industry complexity
  • language and markets
  • commission structure
  • tools and data costs

Appointment setting for one market costs much less than full-cycle sales with closing across several countries.

Start with a short pilot, such as a few weeks of outreach to one segment. Review results and conversations, adjust the message and only then scale up. A pilot protects your budget and your reputation while you learn what works.

Red flags

  • Promises of guaranteed sales.
  • Bought contact lists without consent checks.
  • Outreach from accounts you do not control.
  • No reporting or CRM updates.
  • Aggressive scripts that could harm your brand.

Review conversations, not only numbers. Listening to calls or reading emails shows whether your brand is represented well.

Tips for a smoother project

Agree on how leads are handed over. Define what makes a meeting qualified, who follows up and how quickly. A clear handover prevents good leads from going cold and avoids disputes about which deals the freelancer should receive commission on.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My ideal customer is clearly defined.
  • Outreach follows local rules.
  • Contacts stay in my CRM.
  • Payment terms are clear.
  • Weekly pipeline reports are agreed.
