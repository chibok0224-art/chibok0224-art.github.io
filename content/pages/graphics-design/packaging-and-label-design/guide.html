What a good packaging design package includes

Packaging gigs cover labels, boxes, bags, pouches and sleeves. Check that the package states:

  • Items covered: one label, one box, or a range of sizes and flavors.
  • Concepts: how many initial directions you will see.
  • Print-ready files built on your printer's dieline, with bleed and correct color settings.
  • All sides of the package, not just the front.
  • 3D mockups for approval and online listings.
  • Revisions and delivery time.
  • Source files and rights to the final design.

How to brief a packaging designer

  1. The dieline and file requirements from your printer.
  2. The product: what it is, who buys it, and its price range.
  3. Where it is sold: store shelves, online, or both. Online, it must work as a small image.
  4. All required text: product name, weight or volume, ingredients, warnings, barcode, contact details.
  5. Brand files: logo, colors, fonts and style guide.
  6. Competitors you will sit next to, and how you want to stand out.
  7. Printing method and materials, if known, such as kraft paper or special finishes.

Order a printed proof before the full run, and check it in real light: colors, text size, barcode scanning and how it looks next to competing products. A proof costs far less than a reprint.

If you plan several variants, such as flavors or sizes, ask for a system rather than separate designs: one layout where only the color and name change. It keeps the range recognizable and makes new variants quick to add.

What drives the price

  • number of items, sizes and variants
  • number of concepts and revision rounds
  • custom illustration or photography
  • special finishes such as foil, embossing or spot varnish
  • 3D mockups and renders
  • the designer's experience with print production

Red flags

  • The designer does not ask for a dieline or your printer's requirements.
  • Only front-of-pack designs in the portfolio, with no print samples.
  • Files delivered in RGB only for print jobs, or with no bleed.
  • Offers to write legal claims or certifications for you.
  • Unclear rights or no source files.

Think about the whole life of the package, not just the shelf. Ask whether it fits your shipping boxes, survives transport without scuffing, and opens easily. If you sell online, the unboxing moment is part of the experience, so an inside print or a simple thank-you card can be worth including in the design brief.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have the dieline and file requirements from my printer.
  • I have all required text, checked for my product and market.
  • The package covers every side and every variant I need.
  • I will order a printed proof before the full run.
  • I will receive source files and full rights to the design.
