1. Finish the draft

Complete your full draft before hiring most freelancers. Some authors work with a ghostwriter or writing coach at this stage, but for most, the job is simply to finish. Revise it yourself at least once; it makes editing faster and cheaper.

2. Get reader feedback

Beta readers read your manuscript as ordinary readers and tell you where they were confused, bored or excited. Their feedback is especially useful for fiction and for checking whether non-fiction is easy to follow. Make changes before paying for detailed editing.

3. Edit in stages

Editing usually happens in layers. A developmental edit looks at the big picture: structure, pacing, character or argument. A line or copy edit improves sentences, clarity and consistency. Not every book needs every level, so ask a book editor to review a sample and recommend what yours needs. Agree on the editing level and timeline before the work starts.

4. Design the cover

Once the manuscript is nearly final, commission the cover. A book cover designer needs your genre, title, subtitle, author name, trim size and, for print, the page count to size the spine. Look at bestselling covers in your genre: readers judge quickly, and a cover that fits genre expectations helps them recognize the kind of book they are getting.

5. Format the interior

Interior formatting turns your manuscript into a professional book: chapter headings, fonts, margins, page numbers and image placement. Print books need a fixed layout at the right trim size; ebooks need reflowable files that adapt to screens. A book designer can prepare both. Picture books and illustrated titles need an illustrator first; see our children's book illustration guide.

6. Proofread the final version

Proofreading comes last, after layout, because errors can appear when text is formatted. A proofreader checks spelling, punctuation, typos and layout problems such as missing words or odd line breaks. See our proofreading and editing guide.

7. Consider extra formats

An audiobook can reach new readers. An audiobook producer or narrator can record and master it to platform standards; check each platform's rules on narration before you start. A short book trailer can support social media promotion.

8. Plan the launch

Marketing works best when it starts before release. Prepare your book description, author bio and reader reviews, and plan the first weeks after launch. Book marketing freelancers can help with launch plans, listings and advertising. Be wary of anyone selling guaranteed rankings or paid reviews, which can break platform rules.

Rights, files and contracts

Before you hire anyone, decide what rights you need. For covers and illustrations, check that you can use the artwork on the book, in ads and on merchandise if you plan to. Ask for source files as well as final images, so changes are possible later. For editing and formatting, make sure you receive editable files, not only finished PDFs. If you work with a narrator, confirm who owns the recordings and how royalties, if any, are handled. Writing these points down at the start avoids surprises when you publish new editions, translations or formats.

Also check stock images and fonts used by your designer. They should be properly licensed for commercial use, and the designer should tell you where they came from.

Keep all final files, licenses and contracts together. You will need them for new editions, translations and new formats later. Self-publishing is a series of small projects rather than one big one, and taking them in the right order keeps costs under control and the quality high.
