What a good tutoring package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Subject and level.
  • Lesson length and frequency.
  • Format: live video, chat or recorded.
  • Materials and practice tasks.
  • Progress updates.
  • Cancellation and rescheduling rules.
  • Pricing per lesson or package.

Book a trial lesson before a package. It shows whether the tutor's style suits the student and whether communication works well online.

Ask how progress will be shared, such as a short note after each lesson. Regular updates help parents and students see improvement and focus on weak areas.

Ask tutors how they explain a topic the student finds hard. Good tutors use examples, check understanding with questions and let the student try problems themselves. A short answer about their teaching approach often reveals more than a long list of qualifications.

How to brief a tutor

  1. Subject and level, with curriculum.
  2. Goals: grades, test, confidence.
  3. Current strengths and difficulties.
  4. Upcoming tests and dates.
  5. Learning preferences.
  6. Schedule and time zone.
  7. Budget.

Share recent tests or homework so the tutor sees where the student struggles. Real examples help them plan lessons that target actual gaps.

Set one clear goal for the first month, such as solving equations confidently. Focused goals make progress visible and motivating.

For younger students, agree on safety rules: lessons on approved platforms, a parent nearby and no private contact outside agreed channels.

Encourage the student to prepare questions before each lesson. Active participation makes sessions far more effective than passive listening.

Consider how the tutor communicates with parents. For younger students, a short message after each session about what was covered and what to practice keeps parents involved without needing to sit in on every lesson.

What drives the price

  • subject and level
  • tutor experience
  • lesson length and frequency
  • test preparation materials
  • language of instruction
  • package size

Occasional homework help costs much less than intensive test preparation with materials, mock exams and frequent lessons.

Packages of lessons are often cheaper per session but check cancellation rules before buying.

Choose a regular time slot that works with the student's energy and school schedule. Lessons right after a long school day or late in the evening are often less effective than slightly shorter sessions at a calmer time.

Red flags

  • Offers to complete graded assignments.
  • No trial lesson.
  • Irregular availability.
  • No progress feedback.
  • Requests to move contact off safe platforms with minors.

Review progress every few weeks and adjust goals. Good tutoring adapts as the student improves.

Celebrate small wins. Confidence often grows before grades do.

Agree on how homework and practice will work between lessons. Short, targeted practice tasks reinforce what was learned and give the tutor useful information about where the student still needs help at the next session.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The student had a trial lesson.
  • One clear goal is set.
  • Progress updates are agreed.
  • Safety rules suit the student's age.
  • The tutor teaches, not does the work.
