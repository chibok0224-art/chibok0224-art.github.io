What a good AI content editing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Word count or number of pieces.
  • Fact-checking with sources.
  • Voice and style alignment.
  • Structure and readability edits.
  • Originality checks.
  • Tracked changes or summary.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for tracked changes on a sample piece. It shows how much the editor improves the draft and whether the edits match your expectations.

Request a list of claims that were checked and corrected. It highlights where the AI went wrong and helps you improve future prompts.

Ask the editor to describe their process for checking AI drafts. A clear method, such as verifying every statistic, checking names and dates and testing instructions, shows they treat accuracy seriously rather than only polishing language.

Decide which content is suitable for AI drafting at all. Simple overviews may work well, while opinion pieces, expert advice and personal stories often need human writing from the start. Using AI only where it helps protects quality and trust.

How to brief an AI content editor

  1. The drafts and their purpose.
  2. Audience.
  3. Brand voice guide or examples.
  4. Sources you trust.
  5. Facts that must be accurate.
  6. Length and format.
  7. Deadline.

Add your own knowledge before editing: real examples, opinions, data and customer stories. These are what AI cannot supply and what make content valuable.

Flag sensitive topics, such as legal, financial or health information. They need extra checking and sometimes expert review.

Track how long editing takes. If editing AI drafts takes as long as writing from scratch, a different workflow may be better.

Consider readability as well as accuracy. Editors can shorten long sentences, add helpful headings and remove repeated phrases that AI tools often produce, making content easier and more pleasant to read.

What drives the price

  • word count
  • fact-checking depth
  • subject complexity
  • voice alignment
  • SEO optimization
  • rush delivery

Light proofreading of AI drafts costs much less than deep editing with fact-checking, new examples and SEO improvements.

Ask the editor to add internal links and clear calls to action where appropriate. Small additions like these help readers find related content and take the next step.

Red flags

  • No fact-checking.
  • Text still sounds generic.
  • Ignores your voice guide.
  • Copies text from other sites.
  • No record of changes.

Keep a list of common AI errors in your topics and share it with editors and prompt writers. It improves quality over time.

Review a few edited pieces after publishing to see how readers respond. Comments, time on page and questions reveal whether the content truly helps people.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Facts are checked with sources.
  • My voice guide is shared.
  • I added real examples.
  • Tracked changes are provided.
  • Sensitive topics get expert review.
