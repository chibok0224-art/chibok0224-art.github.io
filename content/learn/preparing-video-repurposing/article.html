1. Pick the right source videos

Not every long video makes good short clips. The best sources have clear sound, a speaker who explains one idea at a time and a few moments that work on their own without context. Interviews, talks, tutorials and podcast episodes with video usually work well. Long silent screen recordings or videos with poor audio are harder. Choose one or two strong videos for a first order rather than sending a large archive.

2. Mark the moments you like

Watch the video once and note timestamps of moments that made you want to share it: a clear tip, a surprising fact, a funny story or a strong opinion. You do not need to find them all, but a few examples show the editor what you value. If you have a transcript, highlight the lines you like. Ask the editor to suggest additional moments and explain why each one works on its own.

3. Decide formats and length

Each platform has its own preferred shape. Vertical clips suit short-video feeds, square clips work in many feeds and horizontal clips suit websites and some channels. Decide where the clips will appear and the rough length for each, such as under a minute for short feeds. Ask how the editor will reframe horizontal footage for vertical clips, because a speaker cut off at the edge of the frame looks careless.

4. Set the style

Agree on captions, fonts, colors, logo placement and any intro or ending card. Captions should be checked by hand, especially names, product terms and numbers. Decide whether you want a short hook in the first seconds, such as a text line that states the main idea, and whether clips should end with a simple call to action. Send one or two clips from other creators whose style you like.

5. Check rights and permissions

Make sure you can reuse the original footage in new places. If the video features guests, check that they agreed to appear in clips on social media. Music from the original recording may not be licensed for the new use, so ask the editor to replace it with licensed music or leave it out. Logos, screenshots and other people's content shown in the video also deserve a quick check.

Budget and timing

The cost depends on the number of clips, their length, whether the editor chooses the moments, the amount of caption work, reframing and graphics, and how many versions you need for different platforms. A small test order of a few clips shows quickly whether the style fits. Regular repurposing, such as clips from every weekly episode, can often be arranged as a recurring package with a set turnaround time.

Questions to ask a video repurposing editor

  • Will you choose the moments, or work only from my timestamps?
  • Which formats and lengths are included in the package?
  • Are captions checked by hand, and can I approve the text?
  • Where does any added music come from, and is it licensed for business use?
  • Will I receive the project files or templates for future clips?

Getting started

Share the source video, your marked moments, the platforms and formats, the style examples and the rights notes. Ask for one sample clip first. Once you approve its look, the editor can produce the rest quickly and consistently. Keep the approved settings in a short note so future orders start from the same place.

Reviewing and learning

Watch each clip on a phone with the sound off. Check that the first two seconds explain why someone should keep watching and that the captions are correct. After posting, note which clips people watched to the end and which they skipped, and share this with the editor. Over time this shows which topics and moments your audience values most, which can guide your next long video too.

Common mistakes

  • Sending long videos with poor sound and expecting good clips.
  • Clips that start slowly instead of with the key idea.
  • Auto-generated captions with errors left in.
  • Reusing music or guest footage without permission.
  • Posting the same horizontal clip everywhere.
