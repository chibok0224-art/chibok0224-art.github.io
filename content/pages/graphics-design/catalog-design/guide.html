What a good catalog design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of pages and products.
  • Layout templates for product pages.
  • Cover and section pages.
  • Print-ready file with bleed and correct color.
  • Digital PDF with links, if needed.
  • Editable source files.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for one sample spread with real products before the whole catalog is laid out. It confirms style, text size and photo treatment, so the rest of the pages follow an approved design.

If you print, confirm paper size, binding and quantity with your printer before design starts. These details change margins and page counts.

Look at the designer's previous catalogs as a buyer would. Can you find a product, its code and its size within a few seconds? Clear navigation, consistent product blocks and readable text are more valuable than decorative layouts that slow buyers down.

How to brief a catalog designer

  1. Purpose and audience: retail buyers, wholesale or consumers.
  2. Product data in a spreadsheet.
  3. Photos, named by product code.
  4. Brand guidelines: logo, colors and fonts.
  5. Sections and order.
  6. Print or digital, with specs.
  7. Deadline, including printing time.

Finalize product data before layout begins. Changing prices, codes and descriptions after pages are designed is the most common source of mistakes and extra charges.

Check that photos are consistent: similar backgrounds, angles and lighting. A catalog with mixed photo styles looks disorganized, however good the layout.

For catalogs you update every season, ask the designer to build master pages and paragraph styles. They make future editions much faster and cheaper.

Ask how the designer handles product photos that need work, such as uneven backgrounds or color differences. Some include basic photo cleanup, while others expect finished images. Knowing this early avoids surprises in the quote and keeps product pages looking consistent.

What drives the price

  • number of pages and products
  • custom layouts versus templates
  • photo editing
  • print preparation
  • interactive PDF features
  • rush delivery

A short digital price list costs much less than a large printed catalog with many sections, photo editing and interactive links.

Red flags

  • No sample spread before full layout.
  • Files without bleed or print settings.
  • Inconsistent product layouts.
  • No editable source files.
  • Text too small to read in print.

Proofread a printed copy, not only the screen version. Errors in prices and product codes are easier to spot on paper and expensive to fix after printing.

Plan how you will share the digital version. A PDF sent by email should be small enough to open quickly on a phone. Ask for a lighter version for email and a high-quality version for printing, so each audience gets the best file.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My product data is final.
  • Photos are named by product code.
  • I will approve a sample spread first.
  • Print specs are agreed with my printer.
  • I will get editable source files.
