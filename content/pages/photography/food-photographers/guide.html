What a good food photography package includes

Food photography gigs are priced by number of dishes or products, styling and editing. Check that the package states:

  • Number of dishes or products and images per item.
  • Styling: included, or by you.
  • Backgrounds and props.
  • Editing and color correction.
  • Sizes for menus, apps and social media.
  • Usage rights.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a consistent style across all photos: the same background, angle and light for similar items. Consistency makes menus and delivery listings look professional and helps customers compare dishes.

Consider seasonal or limited-time items. Planning a shoot that includes upcoming specials saves organizing another shoot later. Ask the photographer whether they can keep the same setup and style for future shoots, so new dishes match your existing photos on menus and delivery apps without looking out of place.

How to brief a food photographer

  1. Shot list: dishes or products and angles.
  2. Where photos will be used, with size requirements.
  3. Brand style: colors, mood, props.
  4. Examples of food photos you like.
  5. Timing: when dishes will be ready, or shipping details.
  6. Special requirements: vegan props, allergen labels, packaging visible.

Prepare dishes exactly as customers receive them, then let the photographer style the scene around them. Photos that match the real dish reduce complaints and build trust with repeat customers.

Plan the order of dishes in a shoot. Foods that wilt, melt or lose steam quickly should be photographed first and fast, while sturdy items can wait.

Ask for a few lifestyle shots as well, such as hands reaching for a dish or a table setting. They work well on social media and websites alongside plain menu shots.

What drives the price

  • number of dishes or products
  • styling and props
  • studio versus location
  • editing and retouching
  • sizes and formats
  • usage rights and exclusivity

Photos for packaging and ads often need broader rights than photos for a menu. Clarify all uses before the shoot.

Red flags

  • Editing that makes portions or ingredients misleading.
  • No questions about platform sizes.
  • Unclear usage rights.
  • Inconsistent style across a menu.
  • No shipping plan for remote product shoots.

Compare final photos with the real dishes. If a photo sets expectations the dish cannot meet, customers notice immediately.

Think about the platforms' cropping. Delivery apps and social media often crop images into squares or circles. Ask the photographer to leave space around the dish and to check how the photos look in the actual app layout, so important parts of the food are not cut off.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a shot list and platform size requirements.
  • Dishes will be prepared as customers receive them.
  • Styling and props are agreed.
  • Usage rights cover all my uses.
  • Edits will keep dishes accurate.
