WordPress

WordPress is open software, so you choose your own hosting and install themes and plugins for almost any feature: stores, memberships, bookings, multilingual sites and more. Its editor is familiar to many people, and a large pool of developers can work on it. The responsibility for hosting quality, security, backups and updates is yours or your developer's, and a site with many plugins needs careful upkeep.

Webflow

Webflow lets designers build custom layouts visually while it produces the underlying code and hosts the site. Because there is no pile of plugins, pages are usually clean and fast, and many platform tasks are handled for you. It suits marketing sites, portfolios and landing pages where design control matters. It has a learning curve for editing structure, and some advanced features rely on third-party tools.

How to choose

Think about who will run the site day to day. If a non-technical team will publish often, test the editing experience of both. If your site needs special features such as memberships or a complex store, check that your choice supports them without awkward workarounds. If the design is the priority and the site is mostly pages, Webflow is attractive. If the content volume is the priority, WordPress is a safe pick.

What to tell a freelancer

Share the pages you need, examples of sites you like, your brand assets and any features such as forms, a blog, a store or a booking tool. Say who will edit the site after launch and how often. Ask what is included: design, build, mobile testing, basic search settings, tracking and a handover session. For WordPress, ask who will handle hosting, backups and updates. For Webflow, ask who owns the account and what the yearly plan costs.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Why do you recommend this platform for my project rather than the other?
  • Who will own and pay for the hosting, domain and any paid plugins or plans?
  • What will it cost each year to keep the site running after launch?
  • How will you make the site fast and check it on different screen sizes?
  • Will I be able to edit the content myself without breaking the layout?
  • Do you provide a short handover with written notes or a recorded walkthrough?

After launch

Every site needs some care. On WordPress, schedule regular updates and backups, and decide who responds if an update causes a problem. On Webflow, keep an eye on plan limits for pages, content items and form submissions, and decide who manages the account. In both cases, keep a record of where the domain is registered, when it renews and who can log in. Set up basic tracking so that you can see which pages visitors use, and review the site every few months to remove outdated content.

If you may need further changes later, agree up front how you will work together: a small monthly arrangement, an hourly rate for fixes or a new quote for each change. Knowing this in advance avoids surprises and keeps the site healthy over time.

Common mistakes

  • Picking the platform before listing what the site actually needs to do.
  • Ignoring ongoing costs such as hosting, plugins and plans.
  • Letting a freelancer create the account in their own name.
  • Skipping a handover so that small edits always need paid help.
