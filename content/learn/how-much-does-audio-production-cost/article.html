Voice over

A starting voice-over package typically covers a script of a set length, a professional recording and a number of revisions. The price rises with word count, with the use of the recording, for example broadcast advertising compared with internal training, and with extras such as editing, sound effects or a quick turnaround. A prepared script reduces revisions, and our article on preparing a voice-over script shows how to write one that is easy to read aloud. If you are choosing between a human voice and a synthetic one, see voice over versus AI voice.

Podcast production

Podcast production starts as low as 20 dollars and goes up to about 200 dollars for the sellers we feature, with larger packages generally adding more editing and production work. The price usually depends on the length of the episode and the amount of cleanup needed, such as removing filler words, long pauses and background noise. Our article on podcast launch steps covers what to prepare before your first episode.

What raises the price

  • Length. Longer scripts and longer episodes cost more.
  • Rights. Commercial and broadcast use is priced above personal or internal use.
  • Editing depth. Heavy cleanup, music and effects take more time.
  • Revisions. Script changes after recording usually cost extra.
  • Speed. Rush delivery is a common add-on.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Which usage rights are included, and for how long?
  • How many words or minutes does the price cover?
  • How many revisions are included, and do pronunciation fixes count?
  • Which file formats will I receive?
  • Is editing, cleanup or mixing included, or only the recording?

Common budgeting mistakes

The most expensive mistake is recording before the script is final. A change to a single paragraph can mean a new session and a new charge. The second is ignoring usage rights: a recording bought for internal training may not be licensed for a paid advertisement, and upgrading the license later can cost more than buying it at the start. For podcasts, the typical mistake is expecting editing to fix poor recordings. A quiet room, a decent microphone and a consistent recording setup reduce cleanup time more than any package upgrade. Also agree on a naming system for episodes and files, so deliveries and revisions are easy to track as the show grows.

How to read reviews for audio work

Reviews tell you more when you read them for specifics. Look for comments about clear communication, on-time delivery and how the seller handled revisions, not just the star rating. For voice work, listen to the sample or demo and check that the tone matches your project, since a warm storytelling voice and a crisp corporate voice are different skills. For podcast editing, look for mentions of consistent quality across many episodes, because a good first edit is easy and a consistent weekly one is the real test.

Planning your budget

  1. Finalize the script before ordering, to avoid paying for a second recording.
  2. State where the audio will be used so the seller can confirm the rights.
  3. Ask for the file formats you need.
  4. For podcasts, agree on the length and the editing level for each episode.
