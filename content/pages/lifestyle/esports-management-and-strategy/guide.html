What a good esports management package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Role: manager, analyst or both.
  • Tasks and responsibilities.
  • Hours and availability.
  • Reports or analysis deliverables.
  • Communication tools.
  • Confidentiality of strategies.
  • Pricing and duration.

Ask for a sample opponent report or schedule. It shows how organized and practical their work is.

Define who makes final decisions about rosters and strategy. Clear authority avoids conflicts inside the team.

Ask candidates about teams they have managed or analyzed and what changed under their guidance. Improvements in organization, results or player retention show practical experience beyond theory.

How to brief an esports manager

  1. Game and competitive level.
  2. Team members and roles.
  3. Goals: tournaments, ranking, sponsorship.
  4. Schedule and time zones.
  5. Current problems.
  6. Budget.
  7. Communication preferences.

Share upcoming tournaments and deadlines. Managers can plan practice, scrims and registrations around them.

Discuss player well-being: practice limits, breaks and healthy routines. Burnout harms performance and teams.

Put agreements in writing, including prize money and sponsorship splits.

Use simple shared tools for schedules, notes and replays. When everyone can see the plan and the analysis, players arrive prepared and practice time is used well.

Ask how the manager handles conflicts inside the team, such as disagreements over roles or playing time. Calm, fair processes for difficult conversations keep teams together through losing streaks and stressful tournaments, which is often where seasons are won or lost.

What drives the price

  • team size
  • hours per week
  • analysis depth
  • sponsorship work
  • travel
  • duration

Part-time scheduling help costs much less than a full manager and analyst handling strategy, travel and sponsorships.

Red flags

  • No clear role definition.
  • Informal money arrangements.
  • No confidentiality of strategies.
  • Unrealistic sponsorship promises.
  • Poor communication.

Review the season together at the end: results, team health and what to change next time.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan for community and content as well. Social media updates, highlight clips and fan engagement help teams attract sponsors and build support beyond competition results.

Keep scrim notes and opponent reports organized for future events.

Clarify how information is shared with players. Some teams prefer short written summaries before matches, others video reviews or quick voice briefings. Matching the format to your players makes analysis more likely to be used.

Discuss scheduling across time zones and school or work commitments. Realistic practice schedules keep players committed and reduce last-minute cancellations.

Ask the manager to prepare a simple team handbook with roles, rules, communication channels and contacts. It helps new members settle in quickly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Roles and authority are clear.
  • Agreements are in writing.
  • Strategies stay confidential.
  • Tournament deadlines are shared.
  • Player well-being is considered.
