What a good AI music video package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Video length and style.
  • Sync to beat and structure.
  • Lyrics, if included.
  • Formats: widescreen and vertical.
  • Resolution.
  • Tool terms and usage rights.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short test clip set to part of your song. It shows the style, motion and timing before the creator spends hours generating and editing the full video.

Watch complete videos the creator has made, from start to finish. Notice whether the visuals keep a clear style, whether faces and objects stay stable, and whether cuts land on the beat. These details separate polished AI videos from rough experiments.

How to brief an AI music video creator

  1. The song, final mix.
  2. Mood and story or themes.
  3. Visual references.
  4. Lyrics with timing.
  5. Artist photos, if you want to appear.
  6. Release platforms and formats.
  7. Release date.

Describe how the song changes: quiet verse, building chorus, a drop or a bridge. Visuals that follow these moments make the video feel connected to the music rather than a random slideshow.

Ask for short vertical clips from the best moments. They are ideal for promoting the release on social platforms before and after the full video launches.

Plan the release schedule together. A full video, short clips and a visualizer can be released over several weeks to keep attention on the song. Asking for all formats in one project is usually more efficient than ordering them separately later.

What drives the price

  • video length
  • level of animation and editing
  • consistency work
  • lyrics and text
  • extra formats and clips
  • rush delivery

A looping visualizer costs much less than a full story-driven AI music video with consistent characters and many scenes.

Share any visual identity you already use, such as your album cover, logo or photos from recent shoots. Echoing these colors and motifs in the video helps fans connect it with your release and makes your music feel like one consistent world across platforms.

Red flags

  • No sync to the music in samples.
  • Random style changes between shots.
  • Unclear tool terms for commercial use.
  • Low resolution output.
  • No test clip before full production.

Check the final video on a phone and a large screen. Flickering, warped faces or unreadable lyrics are easy to miss on one device and obvious on another.

Agree on disclosure and credits. Some platforms ask creators to label AI-generated content, and fans often appreciate knowing how a video was made. Decide early how you will present it in descriptions and credits.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I own or licensed the song.
  • I will review a test clip.
  • Visuals follow the song's structure.
  • I will get vertical clips too.
  • Tool terms allow commercial use.
