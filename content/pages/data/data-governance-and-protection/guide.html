What a good data governance package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Data inventory.
  • Access review and recommendations.
  • Policies: retention, access, incidents.
  • Security basics review.
  • Action plan with priorities.
  • Training for staff, if included.
  • Timeline and deliverables.

Ask for a prioritized action list. Fixing the biggest risks first, such as shared passwords or old exports with customer data, matters more than perfect documents.

Ask the expert about similar businesses they have helped and what changed afterward. Practical improvements, such as fewer people with admin rights or clear deletion schedules, show their advice leads to real action.

Involve the people who handle data every day, such as support, sales and finance staff. They know where data is copied, exported or shared, and their input makes policies realistic.

How to brief a data governance expert

  1. Your business and data types.
  2. Systems and tools.
  3. Who accesses data.
  4. Countries where customers live.
  5. Known risks or incidents.
  6. Existing policies.
  7. Budget and timeline.

List all tools that hold customer or employee data, including spreadsheets and email. Hidden copies of data are a common risk.

Prepare a simple plan for data incidents: who to contact, how to contain the problem and when to inform customers or authorities. Knowing the steps in advance reduces panic and damage.

Ask how the expert will measure progress, such as the number of systems reviewed, access rights removed or policies adopted. Simple measures make governance tangible and help leadership see the value of the work.

What drives the price

  • number of systems
  • data sensitivity
  • policy writing
  • staff training
  • legal coordination
  • ongoing reviews

A basic inventory and checklist costs much less than full policies, training and regular audits for a larger business.

Keep policies short and practical. Staff follow one-page guidelines with clear examples far more often than long documents written in legal language.

Plan how new tools will be approved in the future. A simple checklist for data, access and vendor terms prevents new risks from appearing every time a team adopts a new app.

Red flags

  • Copied template policies.
  • No action plan.
  • Claims to replace legal advice.
  • Ignores how staff actually work.
  • No priorities.

Review governance at least yearly and after major changes, such as new tools or markets.

For security, see our cybersecurity guide. For data systems, read our database guide and data engineering guide.

Schedule short refresher training for staff. Regular reminders about phishing, password managers and safe sharing prevent many common problems.

Ask for a simple register of who has access to which systems, reviewed regularly. Access often grows over time and is rarely removed unless someone checks.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I listed all tools holding data.
  • Risks are prioritized.
  • Policies fit how we work.
  • Legal advice is involved where needed.
  • Reviews are scheduled.
