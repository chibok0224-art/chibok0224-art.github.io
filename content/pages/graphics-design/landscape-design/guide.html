What a good landscape design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Site plan with dimensions.
  • Planting plan and plant list.
  • Materials for paths, patios and edges.
  • 3D views or sketches, if included.
  • Phasing for gradual work.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time and formats.

Ask for plants that suit your climate zone and conditions. A beautiful plan fails if plants struggle with your winters, summers or soil.

Request a phased plan if your budget is limited. Building in stages, such as structure first and planting later, makes a big garden project manageable.

Look at the designer's past plans and, if possible, photos of finished gardens built from them. Real results show whether their designs work in practice, mature well and suit the kinds of homes and climates they claim to know.

How to brief a landscape designer

  1. Photos from several angles.
  2. Measurements and a rough sketch.
  3. Sun and shade through the day.
  4. Soil and drainage notes.
  5. Climate or location.
  6. How you use the space: play, dining, growing food.
  7. Budget and maintenance time.

Measure carefully and include fixed features: doors, windows, trees, slopes, pipes and fences. Accurate measurements prevent plans that do not fit.

Describe how you want to use the garden: children playing, entertaining friends, growing vegetables or simply relaxing. Use comes before style.

Check local rules before building structures, changing drainage or removing trees. Some changes need approval or licensed contractors.

Ask about water use and wildlife. Drought-tolerant plants, rain gardens and native species can lower maintenance, reduce watering and attract birds and pollinators. A thoughtful designer will suggest options that suit your climate and values.

What drives the price

  • garden size
  • level of detail
  • 3D visuals
  • planting complexity
  • revisions
  • rush delivery

A simple planting plan costs much less than a full design with layouts, 3D views, materials and phased plans.

Red flags

  • Plants unsuited to your climate.
  • No dimensions on plans.
  • Structures without mention of permits or engineering.
  • Generic templates.
  • No maintenance considerations.

Keep the plan and plant list for reference when buying and replacing plants over the years.

Tips for a smoother project

Request a simple maintenance calendar with the plan: when to prune, feed, mulch and replace plants. A calendar helps the garden stay healthy and looking as planned after the designer's work is done.

Think about how the garden will change over time. Young plants grow, trees cast more shade and hedges need trimming. A good plan considers mature sizes and how the space will look and feel in five or ten years, not only on the first day.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Measurements and photos are accurate.
  • Sun, soil and climate are described.
  • Plants suit my climate.
  • Permits are checked for structures.
  • The plan fits my maintenance time.
