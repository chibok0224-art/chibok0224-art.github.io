What a good book trailer package includes

Book trailer gigs are priced by length, style and formats. Check that the package states:

  • Length and style: text-based, animated, or live footage.
  • Script or text: provided or written.
  • Images and footage, licensed.
  • Music and sound, licensed.
  • Formats: vertical, square, horizontal.
  • Cover and author branding.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask the creator to open with the hook, not the title. Viewers decide quickly whether to keep watching; a strong question, image or line from the book works better than a slow logo reveal.

Think about where readers will see the trailer first. On short-form platforms, the first seconds decide everything, so a striking line or image matters more than production value. On your website or a launch page, a slightly longer version can set the mood and include more detail about the book and author.

How to brief a book trailer creator

  1. The book: genre, blurb and cover.
  2. The hook: what makes readers pick it up.
  3. Mood and tone.
  4. Key images or scenes, without spoilers.
  5. Release date and where to buy.
  6. Trailers you like.

Match the trailer to the genre. Thrillers, romance, fantasy and non-fiction each have their own visual language, and readers look for familiar signals.

Plan how the trailer will be used: a pinned post, ads, your newsletter or a launch event. Knowing this helps the creator choose length and format.

Keep the call to action simple: pre-order now, available on a date, or link in bio.

What drives the price

  • length and style
  • custom animation or footage
  • script writing
  • number of formats
  • music and voice-over
  • rush delivery before launch

Several short versions for different platforms often work better than one long trailer, and cost little extra when planned together.

Red flags

  • Images or music with no license.
  • Trailers that reveal major plot points.
  • Only one format for all platforms.
  • Fake reviews or endorsements.
  • Slow openings with long logos.

Test the trailer with a few readers in your genre before launch. If they cannot say what the book is about or why it is exciting, the hook needs work.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep the source files and assets, such as images, music licenses and text layers. You may want to update the trailer for a paperback release, a new edition, a sale or award news. Having the files makes these updates quick and inexpensive instead of starting over.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have the hook, blurb and cover ready.
  • All images and music are licensed.
  • The trailer avoids spoilers.
  • I will get formats for each platform.
  • I will test it with readers in my genre.
