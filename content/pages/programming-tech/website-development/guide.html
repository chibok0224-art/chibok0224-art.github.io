What a good website package includes

Website gigs range from a single landing page to a full site with a store and custom features. Two packages with the same title can deliver very different things, so check that yours states:

  • Number of pages, and which pages they are (home, about, services, contact, blog).
  • Platform: WordPress, Shopify, another builder, or custom code.
  • Responsive design: the site must work on phones, not just on a desktop screen.
  • Features: contact forms, booking, payments, a blog, multiple languages.
  • Revisions: how many rounds of changes are included.
  • Basic speed and search setup: page titles, image compression, a sitemap.
  • Handover: admin access, a short guide or video on how to edit pages, and source files for custom code.
  • Delivery time and how long the developer will fix bugs after launch.

How to brief a website developer

A clear brief is the best protection against delays and surprise extra charges. Send:

  1. What the site is for. For example: get calls for a local service, sell products, or show a portfolio.
  2. A page list with one line on what each page contains.
  3. Must-have features, separated from nice-to-have ones.
  4. Two or three sites you like, with a note on what you like about each.
  5. Your content: logo files, final text, and images you have the right to use.
  6. Accounts: your domain and hosting, or ask the developer to recommend where to set them up in your name.
  7. Your deadline, and any date the site absolutely must be live.

What drives the price

  • number of pages and how many unique page layouts they need
  • custom design versus a ready-made theme or template
  • features such as payments, bookings, memberships or integrations with other tools
  • whether the developer writes or edits the content for you
  • speed optimization, search setup and extra languages
  • support and maintenance after launch
  • the developer's experience and how quickly you need it

Red flags

  • The developer wants to register the domain or hosting in their own name.
  • A full custom website promised in a day or two for a very low price. This usually means a template with your logo added.
  • The portfolio has only screenshots and no live links you can open on your phone.
  • The gig does not say what platform will be used or how many pages are included.
  • The developer asks you to pay or talk outside the marketplace. That removes your buyer protection.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a page list and a short list of must-have features.
  • The package states the platform, page count, revisions and delivery time.
  • The domain and hosting are, or will be, in my name.
  • I have opened at least three live sites from the developer's portfolio on my phone.
  • I know what support is included after launch and what costs extra.
