What a good text-to-speech package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Word count or length.
  • Voice options and languages.
  • Pronunciation fixes.
  • Pacing and pauses edited.
  • File format and audio quality.
  • License for your use.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short sample using a paragraph of your real script. It reveals pronunciation issues and helps you choose between voices before the full narration is produced.

Request audio split by section or slide if you are making a course or video. Separate files make editing, syncing and later updates much easier.

Compare several voices with the same paragraph before choosing. Voices differ in warmth, speed and clarity, and the right choice depends on your audience. A calm voice may suit training, while a brighter one may suit short social videos.

How to brief a text-to-speech expert

  1. The final script.
  2. Use: video, course, app, audiobook.
  3. Voice preferences: age, accent, tone.
  4. Pronunciation list.
  5. Languages.
  6. File format and structure.
  7. Deadline.

Edit your script for listening. Remove long lists, parentheses and abbreviations that read awkwardly aloud. Short sentences with clear punctuation help AI voices place natural pauses.

Mark emphasis where meaning depends on it. A note like stress the word not prevents a sentence from sounding confusing when spoken by a voice that cannot guess your intention.

Keep the script final before production. Even small text changes require regenerating and re-editing sections, which adds time.

What drives the price

  • script length
  • number of voices and languages
  • pronunciation and pacing work
  • background music and mixing
  • commercial voice licenses
  • rush delivery

A short narration in one voice costs much less than a long course in several languages with music and section files.

Some premium voices require paid plans for commercial use. Confirm whether that cost is included in the quote.

Think about accessibility when planning narration. Clear, steady speech helps people with reading difficulties, language learners and anyone listening on the move. Offering captions or a transcript next to the audio makes your content useful to an even wider audience.

Red flags

  • Robotic delivery in samples.
  • Mispronounced names left unfixed.
  • No commercial license.
  • Low-quality or noisy audio.
  • Unclear revision limits.

Keep the script and settings used for each file. Future updates will match the original narration much more closely if the same voice and settings are reused.

Listen to the final narration on the devices your audience uses, such as phone speakers, laptops and headphones. Mix levels, background music and sibilant sounds can behave differently on each, and small adjustments improve the experience for everyone.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My script is final and written for listening.
  • I sent a pronunciation list.
  • I chose a voice from a sample.
  • The license covers my use.
  • Files are split the way I need.
