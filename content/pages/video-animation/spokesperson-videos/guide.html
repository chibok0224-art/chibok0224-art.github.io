What a good spokesperson video package includes

Spokesperson gigs are priced by script length, editing and usage rights. Check that the package states:

  • Script length or video duration.
  • Setting: studio, green screen or location.
  • Takes and retakes included.
  • Editing: raw footage or finished video.
  • Formats: horizontal, vertical, square.
  • Usage rights: platforms, paid ads and duration.
  • Delivery time.

Listen to several samples of the presenter, not just their best one. Check whether their tone, pace and accent suit your audience, and whether they sound natural rather than reading.

Consider your audience's language and region. A presenter whose accent and style feel familiar to your customers often connects better than a generic voice. If you sell in several countries, ask whether the presenter can record versions in different languages, or plan separate presenters for key markets.

How to brief a spokesperson

  1. The final script, timed and read aloud.
  2. Pronunciation of names and terms.
  3. Tone: friendly, expert, energetic, calm.
  4. Clothing and setting preferences.
  5. Where the video will be used.
  6. Brand elements for editing: logo, colors, captions.

Write for speaking, not reading. Short sentences, everyday words and a clear call to action work best. Long, formal sentences sound stiff when spoken, even by a skilled presenter.

Ask for a short test read before the full recording. A few lines show whether pace and tone are right, and small adjustments at this stage save a full re-record.

Plan versions for different platforms: a longer version for your website, and shorter cuts for ads and social media.

What drives the price

  • script length
  • setting and production quality
  • editing and graphics
  • number of formats and versions
  • usage rights, especially paid ads
  • rush delivery

Paid advertising rights often cost more than organic use. Be clear about where you will run the video to avoid paying for rights you do not need, or lacking rights you do.

Red flags

  • Unclear usage rights.
  • Samples with poor sound or lighting.
  • No retakes included.
  • AI avatars presented as real people without disclosure.
  • No questions about pronunciation or tone.

Keep the written license with the video files. If you use the video years later or in new places, you can check what is allowed.

Tips for a smoother project

Think about how long the video will stay in use. Prices, offers and dates in a script make the video go out of date quickly. Keeping time-sensitive details in on-screen text or captions, rather than in the presenter's words, lets you update them without re-recording the whole video.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My script is final, timed and written for speaking.
  • I have heard several samples of the presenter.
  • Usage rights cover every place I will use it.
  • I will get the formats I need.
  • I will keep the license with the files.
