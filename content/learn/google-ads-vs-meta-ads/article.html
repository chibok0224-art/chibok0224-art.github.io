Google Ads

Google Ads places your message in search results and across other Google properties. The key idea is intent: the person typed what they want, so ads for words such as a service name plus a city can reach people ready to act. Success depends on choosing the right search terms, writing clear ads, excluding irrelevant searches and sending visitors to a page that answers their query.

Meta Ads

Meta Ads appear in Facebook and Instagram feeds, stories and other placements. The key idea is interruption with relevance: you target people by interests, location and behavior and show them something eye-catching. Creative quality matters a great deal, so images and short videos often decide results. Meta is strong for building awareness, promoting visual products and re-engaging people who visited your site.

How to choose a starting point

Ask how your customers find solutions today. If they search for what you sell, start with Google. If your product is something people discover rather than look for, start with Meta. If you have a very small budget, pick the platform where your best customers clearly spend time, and avoid spreading money too thin. Whichever you choose, set up conversion tracking first so that results are measurable.

Working with a specialist

Share your offer, your target area, your margins, your website and any past results. Ask how they will report: which numbers, how often and in what format. Ask who owns the ad account and what happens to the data if you stop. A good specialist explains their plan in plain language and will tell you if your landing page needs fixing before spending more.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • How will you decide on the audience, search terms and budget split?
  • Which results will you report, and how often?
  • Will the ad account stay in my name, and will I have full access?
  • What do you need from me: images, video, offers or a better landing page?
  • What is a realistic test period before we judge results?
  • How do you handle ad spend: do I pay the platform directly?

Measuring results

Whichever platform you choose, decide in advance what success looks like. For a service business it may be phone calls or form submissions. For a shop it may be orders and revenue compared with ad spend. Track the cost of each result, not just clicks, because cheap clicks that never buy are not a bargain. Give campaigns enough time to learn, usually a few weeks, and change one thing at a time so you can see what made the difference.

Keep a simple log of what you changed and when. If you work with a specialist, ask them to share the log and explain each change in plain language. Over time you will learn which messages, offers and images your customers respond to, and that knowledge is useful beyond advertising.

Setting a starting budget

Start small enough that a poor first test does not hurt, but large enough to produce data. Split the money across a few clear tests rather than many tiny ones, and agree in writing what you will do if results are good (increase slowly) or poor (pause and fix the page or message first). Keep some budget aside for improving the landing page, since better conversion lowers the cost of every future click.

Common mistakes

  • Sending ad clicks to a home page instead of a focused page.
  • Judging results after only a few days.
  • Running ads without tracking.
  • Changing many things at once, so you cannot tell what worked.
