What a good live-action explainer package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Video length and versions.
  • Script writing or editing.
  • Talent: actor, presenter or your team.
  • Location and filming.
  • Editing, graphics and music.
  • Usage rights for talent and music.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask to approve the script and choose the presenter before filming. Changes after filming may require a reshoot, which costs far more than changes on paper.

Request subtitles and shorter versions for social media from the same footage. They extend the use of the video at little extra cost.

Watch complete videos from the maker's portfolio, not only highlight reels. Pay attention to how clearly the message comes across, whether the presenter feels natural and how well the product is shown. A smooth, clear explanation matters more than impressive camera moves.

How to brief a live-action explainer maker

  1. What you are explaining and to whom.
  2. The one key message.
  3. Tone: friendly, expert, energetic.
  4. Presenter preferences.
  5. Product and props to show.
  6. Where it will be used.
  7. Deadline.

Describe your audience in detail. A presenter and tone that suit young consumers may not suit business buyers. Matching the person on screen to your audience makes the message more believable.

Keep the script simple: a problem, your solution, how it works and what to do next. Every extra feature added to the script makes the video longer and the main message weaker.

If your team appears, prepare them. A short rehearsal and a calm filming setup help non-actors look natural on camera.

Ask who handles releases for actors, locations and any music. Signed releases protect you if the video is later used in ads or on new platforms. A professional producer will have standard forms and keep them with the project files.

What drives the price

  • video length
  • actors and presenters
  • locations and filming days
  • graphics and animation
  • usage rights for talent
  • extra versions

A presenter filmed in a studio with simple graphics costs much less than a video with several actors, locations and custom animation.

Red flags

  • No script approval before filming.
  • Unclear talent or music rights.
  • Poor sound in sample videos.
  • A presenter who does not fit your audience.
  • No social media versions.

Sound quality matters as much as picture. Viewers forgive simple visuals, but unclear audio makes them stop watching quickly.

Tips for a smoother project

Before filming, agree on a shot list that includes close-ups of the product and any screens or details that viewers need to see. Missing shots are a common reason for weak explanations, and they are expensive to capture after the crew has left.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I approved the script before filming.
  • The presenter fits my audience.
  • Talent and music rights cover my use.
  • Sound quality is good in samples.
  • I will get social media versions.
