What a good sales strategy consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Ideal customer profile.
  • Sales process map.
  • Scripts and templates.
  • Objection handling.
  • Pipeline stages and metrics.
  • Tool recommendations.
  • Training and follow-up.

Ask for a simple sales playbook your team can use daily: steps, scripts, email templates and answers to common objections.

Request pipeline stage definitions. Clear definitions make forecasts and reports reliable.

How to brief a sales strategy consultant

  1. Products and prices.
  2. Current customers and wins.
  3. Sales cycle length.
  4. Team and roles.
  5. Tools used.
  6. Problems you face.
  7. Goals.

Share recent wins and losses with details. Why customers bought, or chose a competitor, is the most valuable input for a sales strategy.

Describe how long sales usually take and who is involved in decisions. A quick consumer sale and a months-long business deal need very different processes.

Involve the people who sell today. Their experience with objections and customer questions makes scripts realistic.

Ask consultants for examples of playbooks they created and the results teams saw. Practical experience matters more than theory.

Think about the customer's experience as well as your targets. A sales process that respects customers' time, answers questions honestly and follows up helpfully builds referrals and repeat business. Pressure tactics may bring short-term wins but often cost more in reputation and returns.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • research and audit depth
  • written plan or report
  • number of channels or systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A strategy session costs much less than a full playbook with scripts, CRM setup and team training.

Test new scripts and offers with a small group before rolling them out widely.

Ask the consultant to define a few early warning signs, such as deals stuck too long in one stage. Spotting them early helps you act before targets are missed.

Discuss how marketing and sales will work together. Shared definitions of a qualified lead, agreed follow-up times and regular feedback between teams prevent leads from being lost and help marketing attract the right people for your sales process.

Red flags

  • Generic scripts.
  • Aggressive or misleading tactics.
  • No process or metrics.
  • Ignores your sales cycle.
  • Guaranteed revenue.

Review pipeline metrics monthly and refine the process.

Keep the playbook updated as you learn from new customers.

Schedule a follow-up to review results after the first month of using the process.

Record a few sales calls, with permission, and review them with the consultant. Real conversations show where the process works and where customers hesitate.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My ideal customer is defined.
  • The process has clear stages.
  • Scripts fit my brand.
  • Metrics are tracked.
  • Tactics are honest.
