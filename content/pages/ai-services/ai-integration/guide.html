What a good AI integration package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Workflow before and after.
  • Tools and services connected.
  • Data sent to AI services.
  • Human review steps.
  • Testing with real records.
  • Documentation and handover.
  • Running costs, timeline and support.

Ask for a diagram or short written description of the new workflow: what triggers the AI, what it does, where the result goes and who checks it. It makes the system understandable for your whole team.

Ask for examples of integrations the expert has built and how much time they saved. Concrete results, such as fewer minutes per support ticket or faster report preparation, show they focus on business value rather than on using AI for its own sake.

How to brief an AI integration expert

  1. The task you want to speed up.
  2. Tools involved and plans or tiers.
  3. Volume: how many items per day.
  4. Examples of input and ideal output.
  5. Who reviews results.
  6. Privacy and data limits.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Measure the current process first: how long a task takes and how often it happens. Without a baseline, you cannot tell whether the integration actually saves time.

Start with internal use before anything goes to customers. Let your team use the AI drafts for a while, collect feedback and only then consider automating customer-facing steps.

Think about failure modes. What happens if the AI service is slow, unavailable or returns an error? A solid integration falls back gracefully, for example by leaving a task for a person to handle, instead of losing data or stopping your workflow entirely.

What drives the price

  • number of tools connected
  • complexity of the workflow
  • data preparation
  • custom code versus no-code platforms
  • testing and monitoring
  • training for your team

A simple automation that drafts replies in one tool costs much less than a multi-system workflow with document search, approvals and monitoring.

Start small and expand step by step. One well-tested integration that your team trusts creates a template for the next one, while launching many automations at once makes it hard to tell which parts work and which cause problems.

Red flags

  • No human review for customer-facing output.
  • Requests for admin passwords.
  • No running cost estimate.
  • No documentation.
  • Sensitive data sent without discussion.

Review results regularly. Check a sample of AI outputs each week at first, then less often, and adjust prompts or rules when you find mistakes.

  • I have real input and output examples.
