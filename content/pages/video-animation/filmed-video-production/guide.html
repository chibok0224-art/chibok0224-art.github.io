What a good production package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Pre-production tasks.
  • Shoot days, crew and equipment.
  • Locations and permits.
  • Casting and talent rights.
  • Editing, color and sound.
  • Deliverables and formats.
  • Budget breakdown and timeline.

Ask for a budget broken down by stage: pre-production, shoot, post-production and extras. A clear breakdown shows where money goes and where you can save.

Approve the script and storyboard before the shoot. Changes on set are expensive and stressful for everyone.

Watch full productions the team has delivered for similar clients and ask what their role was. Some companies handle everything, while others subcontract key parts. Knowing who actually directs, films and edits helps you judge quality and responsibility.

How to brief a production team

  1. The goal and audience.
  2. Key message.
  3. Style references.
  4. Where it will run: web, social, TV.
  5. People and locations available.
  6. Budget.
  7. Deadline and approval process.

Name one decision maker for the project. Productions slow down when many people give conflicting feedback, especially on shoot days.

Plan for all formats from the start: a main video, shorter cuts and vertical versions. Filming with every format in mind saves reshoots.

Prepare people who will appear on camera, such as staff or customers. A short call explaining what to expect helps them feel comfortable and natural.

Think about accessibility from the beginning: captions, clear audio and, where needed, audio description. Planning these during production is easier and cheaper than adding them later, and it helps the video reach a wider audience.

What drives the price

  • shoot days and crew size
  • locations and permits
  • actors and talent rights
  • equipment and lighting
  • post-production complexity
  • music licensing

A one-day interview shoot costs much less than a multi-day production with actors, several locations and advanced post-production.

Agree on a clear approval process for each stage: concept, script, cast, rough cut and final. Written approvals at every step avoid confusion about what was agreed and keep the project on schedule and budget.

Red flags

  • Little or no pre-production.
  • No budget breakdown.
  • Unclear talent or music rights.
  • No backup plan for weather or illness.
  • No agreement on revisions.

Archive raw footage and project files. They can be reused for future edits, anniversaries and campaigns.

Tips for a smoother project

Request a rough cut before final editing. Reviewing structure and pacing early is far easier than making major changes after color and sound are finished.

Hold a pre-production meeting with everyone involved before the shoot. Walking through the schedule, shot list, locations and responsibilities prevents most problems on the day and gives you a chance to adjust plans calmly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The script and storyboard are approved.
  • The budget is broken down.
  • Permits and rights are arranged.
  • One decision maker is named.
  • All formats are planned.
