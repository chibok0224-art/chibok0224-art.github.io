What a good eLearning video package includes

eLearning gigs are priced by finished minutes, style and number of lessons. Check that the package states:

  • Style: slides with narration, animation, screen recording or presenter.
  • Number and length of lessons.
  • Narration: yours or a voice actor.
  • Captions and transcripts.
  • Formats for your course platform.
  • Templates for consistency.
  • Source files and revisions.

Ask for one pilot lesson before producing the whole course. It lets you test style, pace and length with a few learners, and fix problems once instead of in every lesson.

Ask how the producer handles consistency across a long course: the same fonts, colors, narrator and layout in every lesson. A course that looks and sounds consistent feels more professional and is easier for learners to follow. Templates agreed at the start make this possible even when production takes several weeks.

How to brief an eLearning producer

  1. Learning goals for each lesson.
  2. Scripts or outlines, final and reviewed.
  3. Audience: beginners, professionals, employees.
  4. Course platform and its requirements.
  5. Brand elements and templates.
  6. Accessibility needs.

Start each lesson with what learners will be able to do by the end. Clear goals help learners focus and help you check whether the video actually teaches what it should.

Include short practice or questions between videos in your course platform. Watching alone rarely makes skills stick; small exercises help learners remember.

Keep factual content, such as prices or software screens, in separate short videos where possible, so updates do not require re-recording whole lessons.

What drives the price

  • finished minutes and number of lessons
  • animation versus slides or screen recordings
  • professional voice-over
  • captions, transcripts and translations
  • templates and branding
  • revision rounds

Custom animation costs more per minute than slides with narration. Many courses mix styles: animation for key ideas, simple formats for detailed steps.

Red flags

  • Very long lessons with no structure.
  • No captions or transcripts.
  • Files that do not match your platform's requirements.
  • No pilot lesson before full production.
  • No source files for updates.

Check accessibility requirements if the course is for employees, schools or public organizations. Captions, readable colors and transcripts are often expected.

Tips for a smoother project

Collect feedback from learners after the first few lessons are live. Questions, drop-off points and comments show where explanations are unclear or too fast. Small fixes to early lessons improve the whole course, and the lessons learned help the producer make later lessons better from the start.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Each lesson has clear learning goals.
  • I will test a pilot lesson first.
  • Captions and transcripts are included.
  • Files match my course platform.
  • I will get templates and source files.
