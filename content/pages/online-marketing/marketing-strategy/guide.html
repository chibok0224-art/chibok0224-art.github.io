What a good marketing strategy package includes

Marketing strategy gigs range from a single consultation to a full written plan. Check that the package states:

  • Research: your customers, competitors and current results.
  • Target customers: who to focus on first, and why.
  • Positioning and message: what makes you the better choice.
  • Channels: which to use, which to skip, and why.
  • Plan: priorities for the next few months, with owners and dates.
  • Budget: how to split spending between channels.
  • Measurement: which numbers to track and how.
  • Format: document, presentation or call, and follow-up support.

What to prepare for a marketing strategist

  1. Your goal: for example more first-time customers, more repeat sales or a new market.
  2. Your budget for marketing, and your time and team.
  3. Who buys from you now and why, ideally in their own words.
  4. What you have tried and the results, good and bad.
  5. Competitors and how you are different.
  6. Access to data: analytics, sales reports and ad accounts, read-only where possible.

Ask for the plan to end with the first three actions and who does each one. A strategy that names clear next steps gets used; a strategy that ends with principles usually stays in a folder.

Set a review date when you order. After a few months, compare results with the plan, keep what worked and cut what did not. Many strategists offer a follow-up session for exactly this.

What drives the price

  • depth of research into customers and competitors
  • number of channels and markets covered
  • a written plan versus a consultation
  • workshops or calls with your team
  • follow-up support and reviews
  • the strategist's experience in your industry

Red flags

  • Guaranteed growth or sales figures.
  • A template strategy with your name added and no real research.
  • Recommendations to be on every channel at once.
  • No way to measure whether the plan is working.
  • No questions about your budget or resources.

Be realistic about your own time. A strategy that needs daily posting, weekly emails and constant ad testing will not work for a one-person business with no budget for help. Tell the strategist how many hours a week you or your team can spend on marketing, and ask for a plan that fits. A smaller plan that you actually follow for several months beats an ambitious one you abandon after a few weeks, and it gives you real results to build on next time.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a clear goal and a realistic budget.
  • I have shared customers, past results and competitors.
  • The strategy will include channels, priorities and measurement.
  • It will end with concrete next steps and owners.
  • I have set a date to review the results.
