What a good mobile app consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: idea review, planning, app audit.
  • Platform recommendation.
  • Feature priorities.
  • Cost and time ranges.
  • Store and launch advice.
  • Written summary.
  • Follow-up.

Ask for a feature list split into a first version and later versions. A small first release reduces cost and risk.

Request cost estimates with clear assumptions, so you can compare quotes from developers fairly.

How to brief a mobile app consultant

  1. Your idea or existing app.
  2. Target users.
  3. Competitors.
  4. Features you imagine.
  5. Budget.
  6. Timeline.
  7. Business model.

Talk to a few potential users before the consultation and share what you learned. Real feedback makes the consultant's review far more useful than discussing an untested idea.

Describe how the app will make money or support your business. Business models affect features, platforms and launch plans.

List apps you admire and what you like about them. Examples help the consultant understand your expectations quickly.

Ask consultants about apps they have helped launch and what they learned. Practical experience with app stores and user feedback is valuable.

Keep the planning documents to update as you learn from real users.

Ask for advice on analytics and feedback from the start. Deciding which actions to track, such as sign-ups, key feature use and drop-off points, lets you judge the first version properly and decide what to build next based on evidence rather than guesses.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • review or audit depth
  • written report or roadmap
  • size and complexity of your systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A single idea review costs much less than a full planning package with feature specs, estimates and launch strategy.

Think about maintenance costs after launch. Updates, bug fixes and new operating system versions require ongoing budget.

Plan how you will collect feedback after launch, such as in-app surveys or reviews.

Ask how the consultant would test the idea cheaply before full development, such as a landing page, a clickable prototype or interviews. Early, low-cost tests can save months of work and help you secure funding or partners with real evidence.

Red flags

  • Advice that always leads to their development services.
  • Estimates without requirements.
  • No discussion of users.
  • Promises of app store success.
  • Ignores your budget.

Use the summary to brief several developers and compare their proposals.

Ask for a simple launch checklist covering store listings, privacy details and analytics.

Ask for a realistic view of the market, including how crowded your app category is and how competitors attract users. Honest context helps you decide whether to proceed, adjust the idea or focus on a niche.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Advice is independent.
  • The first version is small.
  • Estimates include assumptions.
  • Users are part of the plan.
  • I will compare developer quotes.
