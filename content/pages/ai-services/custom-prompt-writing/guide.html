What a good prompt writing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of prompts and tasks.
  • Target tool or model.
  • Testing on real examples.
  • Templates with variables.
  • Documentation and usage tips.
  • Ownership.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short test report: example inputs, the outputs produced and notes on weaknesses. It shows the prompt's real performance rather than one perfect demonstration.

Request a version history. As tools update, prompts may need adjusting, and knowing what changed helps you or the writer fix them quickly.

Ask the writer to explain the structure of their prompts in plain language: the role, the rules, the examples and the output format. Understanding why a prompt works lets your team adjust it later without breaking it, which matters as your needs evolve.

How to brief a prompt writer

  1. The task and desired result.
  2. Examples of good and bad outputs.
  3. Tool and model you use.
  4. Inputs the prompt will receive.
  5. Format of the output.
  6. Rules and limits, such as tone or banned claims.
  7. Deadline.

Share real inputs, not ideal ones. Messy customer emails, incomplete data and unusual requests are where prompts fail, so they belong in the test set.

Describe what a perfect output looks like, including length, structure and tone. A clear target makes the prompt easier to write and easier to evaluate.

Plan where people will check the AI's work. Prompts work best as part of a process with review steps, especially for anything customers or decision makers see.

What drives the price

  • number of prompts
  • complexity of the task
  • testing depth
  • multiple tools or models
  • documentation and training
  • rush delivery

A single prompt for one task costs much less than a library of tested prompts with documentation and training for your team.

Ongoing maintenance is often a separate monthly arrangement. Ask about it if your tools update frequently.

Red flags

  • Prompts tested only on one example.
  • Promises of perfect, error-free output.
  • No documentation.
  • Prompts that hide instructions to bypass tool rules.
  • Unclear ownership.

Store prompts in a shared place with the date and tool version. Teams quickly end up with many slightly different copies otherwise.

Tips for a smoother project

Ask how prompts handle unclear or incomplete inputs. Good prompts tell the AI to ask for missing details or to say when it cannot answer, rather than inventing information. This behavior is especially important for prompts used with customers or business data.

Run a short trial with your team after delivery. Ask a few people to use the prompts in real work for a week and collect their notes. Practical feedback often reveals small wording changes that make outputs noticeably better.
