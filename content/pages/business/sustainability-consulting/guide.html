What a good sustainability package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: operations, products, suppliers.
  • Data needed.
  • Footprint method.
  • Action plan with priorities.
  • Reporting support.
  • Communication guidance.
  • Timeline.

Ask for a prioritized list of actions with rough savings and effort. Practical steps, such as energy contracts or packaging changes, often deliver results quickly.

Request guidance on honest communication. Clear, specific claims build trust; vague green language can mislead customers.

Ask consultants about businesses similar to yours that they have helped and what changes those businesses made. Practical results, such as lower energy use or less packaging waste, show they focus on action rather than only reports.

How to brief a sustainability consultant

  1. Your business and operations.
  2. Energy, travel and shipping data.
  3. Products and packaging.
  4. Suppliers.
  5. Goals.
  6. Customer or partner requirements.
  7. Budget.

Gather basic data first: energy bills, travel, shipping volumes and packaging use. Even rough numbers help the consultant estimate impact and prioritize actions.

Involve the people who run operations. They know where waste happens and which changes are realistic.

Start with a few meaningful changes and measure them before making public claims.

Consider the financial side of sustainability actions. Many improvements save money over time, while others cost more upfront. A balanced plan shows both, so you can choose steps that fit your budget and values.

Think about your customers' and partners' expectations. Some large clients now ask suppliers for basic environmental data or policies. A consultant can help you prepare simple, accurate answers so you are ready when such requests arrive, which can protect important business relationships.

What drives the price

  • business size
  • data collection
  • footprint scope
  • supplier reviews
  • reporting
  • ongoing support

A basic footprint estimate costs much less than a full program with supplier reviews, reporting and annual updates.

Red flags

  • Exaggerated claims.
  • Guessed data presented as fact.
  • No action plan.
  • Certification promises without accredited bodies.
  • Ignores your operations.

Update your baseline yearly and report progress honestly, including what has not improved yet.

Tips for a smoother project

Think about how to engage employees, such as small challenges or regular updates on progress. Shared involvement makes changes stick.

Look for quick wins that also improve daily work, such as reducing print, consolidating deliveries or switching to reusable packaging. Visible early changes build support for bigger steps later.

Ask for a simple way to track progress over time, such as a yearly dashboard of energy, waste and travel.

Ask the consultant to explain the method behind any footprint numbers in plain words. Understanding what is included and estimated helps you use the results correctly and answer questions from customers or partners.
