What a good search ads package includes

Search engine marketing (SEM) gigs usually cover Google Ads, sometimes Microsoft Ads as well. They are sold as a one-time setup, an audit of an existing account, or monthly management. Whichever you choose, the package should state:

  • Scope: new setup, audit, or ongoing management, and for how long.
  • Number of campaigns and ad groups that will be built or reviewed.
  • Keyword research, including negative keywords that block irrelevant searches.
  • Ad copy: how many ads per ad group, and who approves them.
  • Conversion tracking: what will be tracked (sales, calls, forms) and how it will be tested.
  • Reporting: what you will receive and how often.
  • Account ownership: work done inside your own ad account.

What to prepare before you order

  1. What counts as a result: a sale, a phone call, a booking or a form. Be specific.
  2. Your products or services, with your best sellers and the ones that make the most profit.
  3. Where you sell: countries, cities or a radius around your shop.
  4. Your ad budget and the limit you do not want to pass.
  5. Access: your ad account, your analytics, and a way to add tracking to your website.
  6. Past results, if you have run ads before, including what did not work.
  7. Competitors you often see in search results.

Before launch, test your own tracking: make a test purchase, call or form submission and check that it appears in the account. Many campaigns run for weeks without recording a single result, simply because this step was skipped.

What drives the price

  • setup only versus ongoing monthly management
  • number of campaigns, products and locations
  • whether tracking must be set up from scratch
  • landing page advice or changes
  • shopping campaigns with a product feed
  • how much ad spend the specialist will manage
  • depth and frequency of reporting

Red flags

  • The specialist guarantees a number of sales, a top position or a fixed return on ad spend. Nobody controls the auction.
  • They want to run ads from their own account, or ask for your ad account password instead of user access.
  • No mention of conversion tracking or negative keywords.
  • Reports show only clicks and impressions, never sales, calls or leads.
  • The fee is hidden inside the ad budget, so you cannot see what was actually spent on ads.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have an ad account in my own name with my payment method.
  • I have defined what counts as a result.
  • I have set an ad budget limit, separate from the specialist's fee.
  • Conversion tracking is part of the job.
  • I know what reports I will get and how often.
