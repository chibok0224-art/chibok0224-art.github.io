What SEO is

SEO is the work of making your website easier for search engines to understand and more useful for the people searching. It usually covers three areas: technical health, such as speed and indexing; content, such as pages that answer real questions; and authority, such as other websites linking to yours. An SEO freelancer might run an audit, research keywords, improve pages, fix technical issues or plan content.

SEO results build over time. Good pages can keep bringing visitors for a long time without paying for each click, but it is slow to start and hard to predict exactly.

What SEM is

SEM, in everyday use, means paid search advertising. You bid on search terms, write ads and pay when people click. A paid search freelancer sets up campaigns, chooses keywords, writes ads, sets budgets, adds conversion tracking and improves campaigns over time.

Paid search is fast and measurable. You can test offers and messages within days. But traffic stops when the budget stops, and costs can climb in competitive markets.

Where to start

  • New website, need customers now: start with a small, well-tracked paid search test, while fixing basic SEO.
  • Established site with steady demand: invest in SEO content and technical fixes.
  • Local service business: start with local SEO, such as your business profile and local pages.
  • Unsure what customers search for: a short paid search test can reveal which terms bring buyers, then guide your SEO.

Set up tracking first

Whichever you choose, make sure you can measure results. Ask the freelancer to confirm that analytics and conversion tracking work before campaigns or SEO changes begin. Without tracking, you cannot tell whether the money spent is working. Our web analytics guide explains what to ask for.

What to ask each freelancer

For SEO, ask the freelancer to explain what they will check first and how they will report progress. A good starting point is an audit that lists technical problems, content gaps and quick wins in order of importance. Ask how they choose keywords, whether they write content or only advise, and how they build links. Avoid anyone who will not explain their methods.

For paid search, ask how they will structure campaigns, which keywords they will exclude, how they will track conversions and how often they will report. Ask what they would change after the first few weeks of data. Agree on a monthly ad budget and a management fee as separate amounts, so you always know where your money goes.

For both, ask for examples of similar businesses they have helped, and what results looked like over time. Honest freelancers talk about ranges and trends, not guarantees.

How the two work together

SEO and paid search are not rivals. Paid search data shows which search terms lead to sales, which helps decide which pages to improve for SEO. Strong SEO pages can also lower the need for ads on your most important terms over time. A sensible plan for many small businesses is a modest, well-tracked ad budget for immediate leads, plus steady monthly work on the website and content. Review both together each month and move effort toward whatever brings customers at the lowest cost.

Red flags for both

  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed sales.
  • Requests for full account ownership instead of access.
  • Large numbers of cheap backlinks.
  • No reporting or vague reports.
  • Ad accounts set up under the freelancer's name instead of yours.

Keep ownership of your website, analytics and ad accounts, and give freelancers access that you can remove later.
