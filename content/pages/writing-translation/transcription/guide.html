What a good transcription service includes

Transcription gigs are usually priced per audio minute. Check that the service states:

  • Style: verbatim or clean verbatim.
  • Method: human, or automatic with human review.
  • Speaker labels and time stamps.
  • Formats: document, subtitles, or plain text.
  • Accuracy and how unclear words are marked.
  • Turnaround.
  • Confidentiality and file deletion.

Ask how unclear sections will be marked, for example with a tag and time stamp. It lets you check those moments yourself instead of discovering guesses later.

Ask for a sample of the transcriptionist's work in a similar setting, such as interviews with several speakers or technical discussions. A clean single-speaker sample tells you little about how they handle overlapping voices, accents or specialist terms, which are the difficult parts of most real recordings.

How to prepare recordings for transcription

  1. The audio or video files, in the best quality you have.
  2. Number of speakers and their names.
  3. Names and terms: people, places, products and jargon.
  4. Style: verbatim or clean.
  5. Format and time stamp needs.
  6. Deadline.

Improve recordings where you can: use a separate microphone for each speaker, record in a quiet room and ask people not to talk over each other. A few minutes of preparation saves hours of correction.

For research interviews, check what your consent forms say about sharing recordings with third parties. You may need participants' permission before sending files to a transcriptionist.

What drives the price

  • length of audio
  • audio quality and accents
  • number of speakers
  • technical, medical or legal vocabulary
  • verbatim versus clean style
  • turnaround time

Poor audio can cost more to transcribe and still contain gaps. Send a short sample first to get an accurate quote and see the quality.

Red flags

  • Very low prices with no mention of human review.
  • No speaker labels in samples.
  • Unclear handling of confidential files.
  • Guessed words not marked as unclear.
  • No confirmation of deletion after delivery.

Check a few minutes of the transcript against the audio. If names or key terms are wrong there, they are probably wrong throughout.

Decide how the transcript will be used before ordering. Research analysis, subtitles, show notes and legal records each need different levels of detail and formatting. Saying this upfront avoids paying for detail you do not need, or receiving a transcript that lacks the time stamps or labels you rely on. It also helps the transcriptionist quote accurately.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have sent the best-quality files and speaker names.
  • I have listed names and technical terms.
  • The style and format are agreed.
  • Confidential files are shared securely and deleted after.
  • I will spot-check the transcript against the audio.
