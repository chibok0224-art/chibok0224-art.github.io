What a good bookkeeping service includes

Bookkeeping gigs are sold as monthly plans or one-off clean-ups. Check that the service states:

  • Transactions: categorizing income and expenses, and a monthly volume limit.
  • Reconciliation of bank and card accounts.
  • Reports: profit and loss, balance sheet and cash flow, and how often.
  • Invoices and bills: whether they are managed or only recorded.
  • Software used, and whose subscription it is.
  • Catch-up work for past months, if needed.
  • What is not included, such as tax filing, payroll or advice.
  • Confidentiality and data handling.

What to prepare for a bookkeeper

  1. Your business type, country and the currency you work in.
  2. Your accounting software, or a request for a recommendation.
  3. Bank and card accounts used for the business, with statements.
  4. Receipts and invoices, ideally in one shared folder or app.
  5. Transaction volume: roughly how many per month.
  6. Deadlines: tax dates, loan applications or investor reports.
  7. Your accountant's details, if you have one.

Separate business and personal spending before you start. Mixed accounts take longer to sort out, cost more, and make mistakes more likely.

Review the monthly reports, even briefly. Ask about anything you do not understand. Good bookkeepers welcome questions, and you will spot errors or unusual spending sooner.

What drives the price

  • number of transactions and accounts
  • monthly plan versus one-off clean-up
  • months of catch-up work and missing records
  • invoicing, bill payment and payroll support
  • multiple currencies or business entities
  • the bookkeeper's qualifications and experience in your country

Red flags

  • Requests for your online banking login or password.
  • Accounting software set up in the bookkeeper's name.
  • Tax advice or filings offered without qualifications for your country.
  • No written list of what the monthly fee covers.
  • Unclear answers about how your financial data is stored.

Tax and legal rules differ from country to country. Check filing requirements and deadlines with a qualified accountant or tax adviser where your business is registered.

Set a regular rhythm with your bookkeeper, such as sending receipts weekly and reviewing reports on a fixed day each month. Small, regular updates are faster and cheaper than a big catch-up before a tax deadline, and they give you an accurate picture of cash flow when you need to make a decision about hiring, stock or spending.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My accounting software is in my name, with the bookkeeper as a user.
  • No banking passwords will be shared.
  • I know what the fee covers and what it does not.
  • My receipts and statements are in one place.
  • I know who handles my tax filings.
