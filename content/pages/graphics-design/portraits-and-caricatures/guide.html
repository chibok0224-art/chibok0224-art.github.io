What a good portrait package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of people and whether pets are included.
  • Style: realistic, cartoon, line art or caricature.
  • Crop: head, half body or full body.
  • Background: plain, simple or detailed.
  • Resolution and size for printing.
  • Revisions at sketch and final stage.
  • Usage rights and delivery time.

Look at several portraits by the same artist, not just their best one. Consistent likeness across many faces is the clearest sign of skill.

Ask for a sketch before the final rendering. Likeness problems, such as the shape of the eyes or smile, are much easier to correct before color and detail are added.

Choose the style with the person and occasion in mind. A realistic portrait suits a memorial or anniversary, while a playful caricature suits a birthday or a retirement party. If you are unsure, ask the artist which style works best with the photos you have.

How to brief a portrait artist

  1. Photos of each person, clear and well lit.
  2. Style chosen from the artist's portfolio.
  3. Pose, outfit and expression.
  4. Background and props.
  5. Text, such as names or a date.
  6. Size and use: print, frame or profile picture.
  7. Deadline, especially for gifts.

Mention details that make the person recognizable: glasses, a hairstyle, a favorite hat or a typical smile. Small personal details often matter more than perfect accuracy.

For gifts, order early. Allow time for a sketch, a revision and printing or framing. Rush fees and stressful deadlines can be avoided with a few extra days.

If you combine people from different photos, try to choose photos with similar lighting and angles. It helps the artist make the group look natural together.

What drives the price

  • number of people and pets
  • level of detail and realism
  • background complexity
  • print size
  • commercial rights
  • rush delivery

A simple head-only cartoon costs much less than a detailed full-body painting of a family with a scenic background.

Red flags

  • Samples that look like stock photos with a filter.
  • No sketch stage.
  • Low-resolution delivery only.
  • Ignores the photos you sent.
  • Unclear revision limits.

Respect the people in the portrait. Use photos you have permission to share, and keep private images out of the artist's public portfolio unless everyone agrees.

Tips for a smoother project

When the final portrait arrives, view it at the size it will be printed or shown. Check the eyes, smile and hair, which carry most of a likeness. If something feels slightly off, describe exactly what, such as the nose looks longer than in the photo.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Which photos should I send, and how many angles or poses help most?
  • Will I see a sketch before the final piece?
  • How many revisions are included at the sketch and final stages?
  • Which resolution and formats will I receive, and is a print-ready file included?
  • Can I use the portrait commercially, for example on a website or merchandise?

Send clear, well-lit photos where the face is not hidden by sunglasses or shadows. The likeness can only be as good as the reference photos the artist works from.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My photos are clear and well lit.
  • I picked a style from the artist's portfolio.
  • I will approve a sketch first.
  • The file is large enough to print.
  • I ordered early enough for my deadline.
