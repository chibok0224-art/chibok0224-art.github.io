What a good DJ mix package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Mix length.
  • Style and genres.
  • Track selection process.
  • Audio quality and file format.
  • Track list.
  • Music rights information.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a track list with the mix. It is needed for licensing, platform uploads and crediting artists.

Listen to full mixes from the DJ, not just short previews. Smooth transitions, sensible track order and steady volume across the whole mix show real skill. Mixes that sound great for a minute but drift in energy or level are less useful for events or venues.

How to brief a DJ

  1. Purpose: event, store, workout, radio, personal.
  2. Mood and energy curve.
  3. Genres and eras.
  4. Must-play and avoid lists.
  5. Length.
  6. Where it will be played or uploaded.
  7. Deadline.

Describe the energy over time, such as start calm, build up and finish strong. A well-planned energy curve is what separates a good mix from a random playlist.

Check music licensing before using a mix in public spaces, ads or online videos. Many businesses use licensed background music services or royalty-free music for this reason.

Ask for clean versions if the mix will play in family settings or workplaces.

Think about volume and loudness for the playback setting. A mix for a café should stay gentle and even, while a club warm-up needs more punch. Telling the DJ where it will play helps them master the mix to the right level.

What drives the price

  • mix length
  • custom track selection
  • edits and transitions
  • branded intros
  • licensed or royalty-free music
  • rush delivery

A standard genre mix costs much less than a custom event mix with requested tracks, edits and branded intros.

Ask whether the DJ can provide a shorter edit and a longer version. Different lengths are useful for different moments, such as a short mix for social media and a long mix for an event.

Red flags

  • No track list.
  • Poor audio quality.
  • Ignores rights questions.
  • Mix does not match the brief.
  • Unclear revision policy.

Keep the track list with the file. It makes licensing and future updates easier.

Decide whether you need a live-style mix or a carefully edited one. Live mixes feel energetic and natural, while edited mixes can be more precise, with perfect transitions and exact timing for events or videos. Each has its place.

Keep the master file and stems if offered. They allow small edits later, such as removing a track that becomes unavailable for licensing or adjusting the ending for a different event.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I described the mood and energy.
  • Must-play and avoid lists are shared.
  • I will get a track list.
  • Music rights fit my use.
  • Audio quality is high.
