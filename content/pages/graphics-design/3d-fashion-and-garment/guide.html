What a good 3D garment package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of garments and variants.
  • Pattern accuracy and sizes.
  • Fabric simulation and textures.
  • Renders and angles.
  • File formats and software.
  • Ownership.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a turntable or several angles of each garment on an avatar. Draping problems such as stiff folds or floating seams are easiest to spot when the garment is seen in motion or from all sides.

Request fabric settings that match your real materials. Weight, stretch and thickness strongly affect how a garment looks, and generic settings make every fabric behave the same.

If you plan to produce the garment, ask for patterns and measurements that match your tech pack, so digital and physical samples stay in sync.

Look at the designer's portfolio for garments similar to yours: knitwear, tailoring, sportswear or dresses each behave differently. Close-up renders of seams, collars and trims show whether the designer pays attention to the details that make digital clothing believable.

How to brief a 3D fashion designer

  1. Tech packs or patterns.
  2. Measurements and size.
  3. Fabrics with photos and specs.
  4. Colors and prints.
  5. Avatar or model size.
  6. Use: sampling, e-commerce, games.
  7. Deadline.

Send real fabric photos and specifications, such as weight and stretch. Visual references alone rarely capture how a fabric behaves.

Choose the avatar size that matches your sample size. Fit decisions made on the wrong body size can mislead the whole team.

Decide how renders will appear in your store: on a model, flat lay or ghost mannequin style. Consistent presentation makes a catalog look professional.

Share prints and logos as high-resolution or vector files so they look sharp on the 3D garment.

Consider how digital garments will be shown to customers. Realistic renders can boost online sales, but they should match the final product closely. Clear notes about color and fit, and real photos when available, keep returns low and customers satisfied.

What drives the price

  • garment complexity
  • pattern work needed
  • fabric simulation detail
  • number of colorways
  • renders and animation
  • game optimization

A simple T-shirt render costs much less than a detailed jacket with linings, trims, colorways and animation.

Game-ready optimization usually adds cost.

Red flags

  • Inaccurate patterns or measurements.
  • Unrealistic fabric behavior.
  • Files incompatible with your software.
  • No renders from several angles.
  • Unclear ownership.

Keep project files and fabric libraries organized. They make the next season's digital samples faster.

Compare digital and physical samples when possible to improve accuracy over time.

Plan how the files will move between teams. Designers, pattern makers, factories and e-commerce teams may all use the 3D garment. Agree on file formats and naming early so everyone can open and use the latest version.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I sent tech packs and real fabric specs.
  • The avatar matches my sample size.
  • I will see several angles.
  • Formats work in my software.
  • I own the files.
