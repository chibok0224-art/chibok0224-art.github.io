What a good cartoon illustration package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of characters and whether a background is included.
  • Style, shown in the portfolio.
  • Size and resolution for your use.
  • File formats, including source files if needed.
  • Revisions at sketch and final stages.
  • License: personal or commercial use.
  • Delivery time.

Choose an illustrator whose portfolio already looks like what you want. Asking an artist to work far outside their usual style rarely gives the best result.

Look closely at hands, faces and expressions in the portfolio. These are the hardest parts to draw well and the first things people notice. An illustrator who handles them consistently will usually deliver a polished result across the whole picture.

How to brief a cartoon illustrator

  1. The purpose: gift, mascot, comic, social post or product.
  2. Photos or references for people or objects.
  3. Style examples from the illustrator's portfolio.
  4. Pose, expression and outfit.
  5. Background and text, if any.
  6. Size, format and use.
  7. Deadline.

Approve the sketch carefully. Pose, composition and likeness are easy to change at this stage, while changes after coloring take much more time and may cost extra.

For brand mascots, ask for a few variations, such as happy, surprised and waving. A small set of poses makes the character far more useful for posts, packaging and presentations.

If the cartoon is a gift, mention the occasion and any personal details, such as a hobby, pet or favorite place. These touches make the artwork feel personal.

Agree on credit and sharing. Some illustrators like to post client work in their portfolio, which is usually fine for gifts and public brands. If the art is private or part of an unreleased product, say so before work begins.

What drives the price

  • number of characters
  • background detail
  • full color versus line art
  • commercial license
  • extra poses or character sheet
  • rush delivery

A single head-and-shoulders cartoon costs much less than a full scene with several characters and a detailed background.

Red flags

  • A portfolio with mixed styles that look like different artists.
  • No sketch stage.
  • No commercial license for business use.
  • Low-resolution files only.
  • Art that closely copies famous characters.

Avoid copying well-known cartoon characters or studio styles too closely. Original characters protect your brand and avoid problems with rights holders.

Tips for a smoother project

Think about where the art will appear and at what size. A cartoon for a phone screen can be simple, while a printed poster or mug needs higher resolution and cleaner details. Telling the illustrator early lets them prepare the file correctly.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I picked an illustrator whose style I like.
  • I sent clear photos or references.
  • I will approve a sketch first.
  • The license covers my use.
  • I will get high-resolution files.
