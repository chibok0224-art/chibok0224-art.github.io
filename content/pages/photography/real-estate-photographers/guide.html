What a good real estate photo package includes

Real estate photo gigs are priced per image and type of edit. Check that the package states:

  • Edit types: HDR, color, verticals, sky, object removal, staging.
  • Number of images.
  • Style: natural, bright, consistent.
  • Output sizes for listing platforms.
  • Disclosure for virtual staging.
  • Revisions.
  • Turnaround.

Ask for straight vertical lines in interiors. Tilted walls make rooms look distorted and less professional; correcting them is a small edit that makes a big difference.

Ask whether the editor can create a twilight version of the exterior or a set of detail shots, such as kitchen fittings or a view from a window. A few standout images at the start of a listing can attract more clicks, as long as they represent the property accurately.

How to brief a real estate photo editor

  1. Original photos, with bracketed exposures if available.
  2. Notes per photo: what to fix or remove.
  3. Style references.
  4. Staging style for empty rooms.
  5. Platform requirements.
  6. Deadline.

Shoot with editing in mind: use a tripod, keep the camera level, turn on all lights and open curtains. Good originals give the editor much more to work with and produce more natural results.

Keep staging realistic. Furniture should match the room's real size and the property's style, so buyers get an accurate sense of the space.

Deliver a consistent set. All photos for one listing should share the same brightness and color style, so the property feels coherent as buyers scroll.

What drives the price

  • number of images
  • edit complexity
  • virtual staging
  • twilight or sky replacement
  • turnaround speed
  • volume discounts for regular listings

Regular listings often qualify for volume pricing and faster turnaround with the same editor, who learns your style.

Red flags

  • Edits that hide damage or permanent features.
  • Undisclosed virtual staging.
  • Unrealistic colors or oversized furniture.
  • Tilted verticals.
  • No revisions for errors.

Check local rules and platform policies on edited and staged property photos. In many places, misleading property marketing can have legal consequences.

Keep an archive of original and edited photos for each property. If a listing is relaunched, a price changes or questions arise about what was shown, you can quickly check the originals. It also helps when you need different crops or sizes for new platforms or printed brochures.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have original, high-quality photos.
  • Edits keep the property accurate.
  • Virtual staging will be disclosed.
  • The style is consistent across the listing.
  • I have checked local and platform rules.
