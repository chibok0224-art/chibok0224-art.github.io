What a good custom AI voice package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Consent process and documentation.
  • Recording guidance or session.
  • Tool or platform used.
  • Voice quality testing.
  • Ownership and access.
  • Usage limits.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a recording guide before you record. Microphone placement, a quiet room and consistent reading style have a huge effect on how natural the final voice sounds.

Test the voice on different scripts: a calm explanation, an energetic ad and a list of difficult names. A voice that works only in one style may disappoint later.

Ask the creator which voice platforms they use and what security options those platforms offer, such as access controls, watermarking or usage logs. Strong safeguards are important, because a voice model in the wrong hands can be used to impersonate someone.

How to brief a custom AI voice creator

  1. Whose voice and signed consent.
  2. Purpose and where it will be used.
  3. Style: calm, friendly, energetic.
  4. Languages.
  5. Recordings you have or can make.
  6. Security needs.
  7. Deadline.

Write a consent agreement that covers what the voice can say, where it can be used, how long and what happens if the person leaves your company. Clear terms protect both the speaker and your business.

Consider creating an original synthetic voice instead of cloning a person. A designed brand voice avoids many consent and reputation questions and still gives you a unique sound.

Disclose AI voices where it matters, such as in customer calls or ads. Honest disclosure builds trust and may be required by platform rules or local law.

What drives the price

  • recording time
  • tool and plan
  • quality testing
  • languages
  • security and access setup
  • ongoing management

A simple voice from existing clean recordings costs much less than a high-quality multilingual voice with recording sessions, testing and access controls.

Many voice platforms charge monthly fees and usage costs. Ask for an estimate of ongoing costs, not only the setup fee.

Red flags

  • Offers to clone voices without consent.
  • Celebrity or public figure voices.
  • No information on where the voice model is stored.
  • Shared access with no controls.
  • Poor recording guidance.

Keep a log of what the voice is used for. If questions arise about a message or recording, a clear record shows it was authorized.

Review the voice regularly once it is in use. Listen to recent outputs, check that they match the approved style and confirm that usage stays within the agreed limits. Regular reviews protect the speaker and keep the brand voice consistent.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Written consent is signed.
  • Recordings follow a quality guide.
  • Ownership and access are clear.
  • Usage limits are agreed.
  • AI voice use will be disclosed where needed.
