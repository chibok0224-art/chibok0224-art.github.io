What a good vocal tuning package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of tracks: lead, doubles, harmonies.
  • Style: natural or stylized.
  • Timing alignment, if included.
  • File format and sample rate.
  • Files aligned to your session start.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for a short before and after sample from the engineer's past work. Listen on headphones for unnatural jumps between notes or a metallic tone, which are signs of heavy-handed tuning.

Keep your original, untuned vocals safely. If you later want a different style or a lighter touch, you can start again without re-recording.

Ask the engineer which tools and methods they use and how they decide how much to correct. Experienced engineers listen to the musical context and tune note by note, rather than applying the same strong setting to every word.

How to brief a vocal tuning engineer

  1. Dry vocal tracks, clearly labeled.
  2. Instrumental or rough mix.
  3. Tempo and key.
  4. Desired style with reference songs.
  5. Notes to keep, such as expressive slides.
  6. Which tracks need timing work.
  7. Deadline.

Mark any notes that are intentionally off pitch, such as a blue note or a deliberate bend. Without guidance, an engineer may correct the very notes that give the performance its character.

Use reference songs to describe the sound you want. Some genres expect perfectly locked vocals, while others prefer a raw, live feel. A reference is clearer than words like natural or polished.

Discuss how harmonies and doubles should sit against the lead. Tuning every layer to exactly the same pitch can make a chorus sound thin, while small natural differences add width. A good engineer will balance accuracy with the fullness of the arrangement.

What drives the price

  • number of vocal tracks
  • song length
  • timing alignment
  • stylized effects
  • revisions
  • rush delivery

Light tuning of one lead vocal costs much less than detailed work on lead, doubles and many harmonies.

Red flags

  • Robotic sound when you asked for natural.
  • Files that do not line up with your session.
  • Compressed or low-quality exports.
  • No before and after samples.
  • Changes to notes you asked to keep.

Listen to the tuned vocals in the full mix, not alone. Small artifacts that stand out solo may disappear, and problems hidden solo may appear with the music.

Tips for a smoother project

If you record often, ask for feedback on your raw vocals as well. Notes about pitch tendencies, breath control or microphone technique can improve future recordings, which means less tuning is needed and the voice keeps more of its natural character.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Can I hear before-and-after examples in a style similar to mine?
  • How natural or obvious should the tuning sound, and can you match my choice?
  • Will the files line up exactly with my session and keep the original length?
  • Which format and sample rate will you deliver?
  • Will you keep notes and slides I mark as intentional?

Mark any notes you want left alone, such as an intentional bend or a raw moment. Tuning everything to perfect pitch can remove the emotion that made the take worth keeping.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I sent dry, labeled vocal tracks.
  • I chose natural or stylized tuning.
  • I marked notes to keep.
  • Files will line up with my session.
  • I kept the original vocals.
