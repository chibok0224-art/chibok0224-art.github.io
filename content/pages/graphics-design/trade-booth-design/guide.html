What a good booth design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Booth size and elements covered.
  • Graphics for each panel or banner.
  • 3D mockup of the booth, if needed.
  • Print-ready files on the supplier's templates.
  • Editable source files.
  • Revisions.
  • Delivery time before print deadlines.

Ask for a mockup showing how the booth looks from the aisle. It reveals whether the brand and message are visible above people's heads and whether the graphics feel balanced across panels.

Make sure the designer uses your booth supplier's exact templates. Panel sizes, folds and hardware positions differ between systems, and a design that ignores them may print with logos cut off.

Look at photos of real booths the designer has worked on, not only digital mockups. Printed fabric, lighting and crowded aisles change how a design looks. Designers with show-floor experience understand what stays visible and what disappears in a busy hall.

How to brief a booth designer

  1. The event, booth size and type.
  2. Supplier templates and print specs.
  3. Your main message and audience.
  4. Brand assets: logo, colors and fonts.
  5. Images of products or services.
  6. Elements: walls, counters, screens, banners.
  7. Print and shipping deadlines.

Write your message for visitors walking past. A short headline that explains what you do, readable from several meters away, does more than a list of services.

Think about where people will stand. Graphics low on the wall are hidden by tables and visitors. Put the logo and key message high, and keep lower areas simple.

Plan for screens and lighting if you use them. A screen placed in a bright spot or in front of important graphics can make the booth feel cluttered rather than modern.

What drives the price

  • booth size and number of panels
  • 3D booth design
  • custom illustrations or photos
  • multiple event versions
  • editable modular design
  • rush delivery

Graphics for a small pop-up stand cost much less than a full custom booth design with 3D plans, counters and several displays.

Red flags

  • No use of the supplier's templates.
  • Small text that cannot be read from a distance.
  • Low-resolution images for large prints.
  • No mockup before printing.
  • No time left for printing and shipping.

Print a small proof of the main graphics and look at it from across a room. If you cannot read the message quickly, visitors will not either.

Tips for a smoother project

After the show, note what worked: which graphics people commented on, where visitors stopped and what questions they asked. Sharing these notes with the designer helps improve the booth for the next event, often with only a few panel changes.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have the supplier's templates and specs.
  • My message is short and readable from afar.
  • I will see a mockup before printing.
  • Files are print-ready at full size.
  • Deadlines leave time for printing and shipping.
