What a good music production package includes

Music production gigs are usually priced per song and by how complete the production is. Check that the package states:

  • Production: full arrangement, or a beat or instrumental only.
  • Genre and instruments, live or programmed.
  • Vocals: whether recording, editing or tuning of your vocals is included.
  • Mixing and mastering: included, rough only, or separate.
  • Deliverables: WAV, MP3, stems, instrumental version.
  • Revisions and delivery time.
  • Rights: ownership, credits, royalty share and sample clearance.

How to brief a music producer

  1. Your song: a demo, voice memo or chord chart, plus lyrics.
  2. Two or three reference tracks, with the parts you like in each: the drums, the mood, the vocal sound.
  3. Tempo and key, if you know them.
  4. Structure: verses, choruses, bridge and length.
  5. Where it will be released: streaming, video, live or sync licensing.
  6. Your vocals: whether you will record them before or after the production.
  7. What you want to own, and what credit the producer asks for.

Give feedback with time stamps and references, such as "at 0:45 the chorus needs more energy, like the drums in the reference". Musical feedback in words alone, like "make it pop", leads to guesswork and extra revision rounds.

Keep all agreements in the order messages or a short written contract: who owns what, who is credited, and whether the producer gets a share of royalties. Distributors and publishers may ask for this later.

What drives the price

  • how complete the production is, from beat to full arrangement
  • live instruments and session musicians versus programmed parts
  • vocal production
  • mixing, mastering and stems
  • rights: full ownership, license, or royalty share
  • the producer's experience and credits in your genre

Red flags

  • No samples in your genre.
  • Vague answers about ownership, credits or royalties.
  • Beats sold as exclusive that may already be sold to others.
  • Samples from well-known recordings with no clearance.
  • Promises of streams, charts or playlist placements.

Start with one song before committing to an album or EP. It shows how the producer works, how well they understand your references, and how smoothly feedback and revisions go. If the first song works, you can agree on a consistent sound for the rest of the project and often a better price for the remaining tracks. Save the project notes and references from the first song so the rest of the release sounds like one body of work.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a demo or voice memo, lyrics and reference tracks.
  • I know whether mixing, mastering and stems are included.
  • Ownership, credits and royalties are agreed in writing.
  • All samples are cleared or royalty-free.
  • I will give feedback with time stamps and references.
