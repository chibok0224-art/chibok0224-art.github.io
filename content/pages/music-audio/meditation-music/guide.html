What a good meditation music package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number and length of tracks.
  • Mood and instruments.
  • Seamless loops, if needed.
  • Voice-over friendly versions.
  • File formats and loudness.
  • License: uses and exclusivity.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Listen to the full track, not just the beginning, with headphones in a quiet room. Sudden peaks, clicks or a jump at the loop point break the calm and are easy to miss in a short preview.

Ask for gentle fade-ins and fade-outs. Abrupt starts and endings can startle listeners at exactly the moment you want them relaxed.

Listen to samples of the composer's work for long stretches, not just a minute. Meditation music must stay calm and interesting without becoming boring or distracting over many minutes. Long samples reveal whether the composer can hold that balance.

How to brief a meditation music composer

  1. Use: app, video, class or sleep content.
  2. Mood: calm, focus, sleep, healing.
  3. Instruments you like or want to avoid.
  4. Length and loops.
  5. Voice-over plans.
  6. Reference tracks.
  7. License needs and deadline.

Describe how listeners should feel at the start and end of the session. Music that slowly becomes quieter or simpler can guide people into relaxation more naturally than a track that stays the same.

If a voice guides the meditation, send a sample recording. The composer can then leave room in the frequencies and dynamics where the voice sits, so words remain clear without turning the music down too far.

Avoid claims that music heals or treats conditions. Describe the music as relaxing or calming rather than making health promises.

What drives the price

  • number of tracks and total length
  • live instruments versus electronic
  • seamless loops
  • voice-over versions
  • exclusive license
  • rush delivery

A few short ambient tracks cost much less than a library of long, exclusive tracks with loops and voice-over versions.

Red flags

  • Sudden loud sounds or harsh tones.
  • Loops that click or jump.
  • No clear license for apps or streaming.
  • Stock tracks sold as custom.
  • Health claims about the music.

Check loudness consistency across tracks. Listeners should not need to adjust the volume between sessions, especially when using headphones at night.

Keep a catalog of tracks with moods, lengths and licenses. It makes it easy to choose music for new sessions and avoid repeating the same track too often.

Think about your listeners' environment. Sleep content is often played quietly through phone speakers or earbuds, while yoga classes use room speakers. Telling the composer how people will listen helps them choose sounds that work well in that setting.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I described the mood and use clearly.
  • Loops are seamless if needed.
  • There is a version for voice-over.
  • The license covers my platforms.
  • Tracks have no sudden loud sounds.
