What a good data scraping package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Source websites and pages covered.
  • Fields collected, listed by name.
  • Number of records or pages.
  • Output format and file structure.
  • Cleaning: duplicates removed and formats standardized.
  • Delivery: one-time data, recurring runs or the script.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a small sample first, such as 50 rows. Checking a sample shows whether the fields, formats and accuracy are right before the full run.

Describe the data you need in terms of the decision it supports. If you want to compare competitor prices once a week, the expert can design the right schedule and fields. If you only need a one-time list, a simpler and cheaper approach may be enough.

How to brief a scraping expert

  1. The websites and example pages.
  2. The fields you need, with an example row.
  3. How many records and how often.
  4. Output format and where it should go.
  5. How you will use the data.
  6. Any limits: personal data, terms, rate limits.
  7. Deadline.

Check whether the data is available another way first. Many sites offer an official API, export or dataset, which is more reliable and avoids terms problems.

Plan how you will keep the data fresh. Prices and listings change, so a one-time scrape may be out of date quickly.

Think about storage and privacy before the data arrives. Decide where the files will live, who can open them and how long you will keep them. Even public data can become sensitive when collected in bulk, so treat the final dataset with the same care as other business records.

What drives the price

  • number of sites and pages
  • complexity of the site, such as dynamic content
  • number of fields and records
  • data cleaning and formatting
  • recurring runs or maintenance
  • delivering a reusable script

Simple tables from one site cost much less than large, recurring scrapes from complex sites that load data dynamically.

Red flags

  • Offers to scrape personal data without any questions.
  • Promises to get around logins, paywalls or blocks.
  • No sample before the full delivery.
  • Messy data with missing or mixed formats.
  • No plan for site changes.

Respect the websites you collect from. Reasonable request rates and following terms protect you and the people who run those sites.

When the data arrives, check it against the source website for a few random records. Look for missing values, wrong columns, odd characters and duplicates. Reporting problems with concrete examples helps the expert fix the scraper rather than only patching the delivered file.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I checked for an official API or export.
  • I have an example row with every field.
  • I will review a sample first.
  • The collection respects terms and privacy rules.
  • I know whether I need the script or only the data.
