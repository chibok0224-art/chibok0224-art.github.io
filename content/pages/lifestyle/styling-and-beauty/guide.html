What a good styling package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Service: audit, outfits, shopping, makeup.
  • Format: call, mood board, guide.
  • Number of looks.
  • Budget-friendly options.
  • Follow-up questions.
  • Privacy of photos.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for outfit combinations using items you already own. Getting more from your existing wardrobe is often the most valuable result.

Look at the stylist's previous client work and see whether it fits your taste. Some stylists favor bold, trendy looks; others focus on classic or practical styles. Choosing someone whose aesthetic matches yours makes the advice easier to use.

Plan small changes first. Trying a few new combinations or one new color is less overwhelming than replacing a whole wardrobe, and it helps you learn what feels right.

How to brief a stylist

  1. Goals: work, events, confidence, new style.
  2. Photos and sizes.
  3. Lifestyle and dress codes.
  4. Budget.
  5. Styles you like and dislike.
  6. Comfort needs.
  7. Deadline.

Describe your daily life honestly, such as walking a lot, working outdoors or attending formal meetings. Practical advice matches real routines.

Ask for shopping options at different price levels. Good stylists offer alternatives, so you can choose based on budget without losing the overall look.

Share what makes you feel uncomfortable, such as certain fits, colors or makeup styles. Good stylists build on your comfort zone and stretch it gently, rather than suggesting looks you would never wear.

What drives the price

  • number of looks
  • wardrobe audit depth
  • shopping research
  • makeup tutorials
  • follow-up support
  • rush delivery

A single event look costs much less than a full wardrobe plan with shopping lists and follow-up sessions.

Consider sustainability: secondhand shopping, tailoring and caring for clothes can extend your wardrobe and save money.

For events, plan a trial run of the full look a few days before. Testing outfit, shoes and makeup together avoids last-minute surprises and lets you adjust anything that feels wrong.

Red flags

  • Ignores your budget.
  • Generic advice without your photos.
  • Pressure to buy specific brands.
  • Medical skin advice from unqualified people.
  • Unclear photo privacy.

Keep your favorite combinations in a photo album for quick daily choices.

Take photos of outfits you love and note why they work. A personal lookbook makes daily choices quick.

Revisit your style once a season. Small updates keep your wardrobe current without big expenses.

Ask for a simple capsule plan: a small set of pieces that mix and match easily. A well-chosen capsule makes getting dressed quicker, reduces impulse purchases and helps you build a wardrobe you genuinely enjoy wearing.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared photos and sizes.
  • My budget is clear.
  • Advice uses what I own.
  • Comfort needs are included.
  • Photo privacy is agreed.
