What a good event photography package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Coverage hours.
  • Shot list and key moments.
  • Number of edited photos.
  • Same-day previews, if needed.
  • Delivery time and format.
  • Usage rights.
  • Travel and overtime costs.

Share a schedule with times for speeches, awards and group photos. Event photographers need to be in position before key moments, not arrive after them.

Assign someone to point out VIPs, speakers and sponsors. Without help, a photographer cannot know who matters most to you.

Look at complete event galleries, not only highlight images. A full gallery shows whether the photographer covers speakers, crowd moments, details and candid interactions consistently, and how well they handle difficult light throughout a long day.

How to brief an event photographer

  1. Event type and schedule.
  2. Venue and lighting.
  3. Key people and moments.
  4. Brand elements to capture.
  5. Group photos needed.
  6. Delivery needs: same day or later.
  7. Usage and budget.

Describe the venue lighting. Dark conference halls and evening parties need different equipment, and the photographer may bring extra lights.

List brand moments, such as banners, product displays or sponsor logos. These images are often needed for reports and marketing.

Plan group photos at a set time when everyone is present. Chasing people later in the event rarely works.

Inform guests that photography will take place, for example on invitations or signs, and offer a simple way to opt out.

Think about privacy for sensitive events, such as internal meetings or events with children. Agree on what may be photographed, where photos may be published and how long they are stored, and share these rules with guests.

What drives the price

  • hours of coverage
  • number of photographers
  • same-day editing
  • travel
  • number of edited photos
  • usage rights

A two-hour party shoot costs much less than full-day conference coverage with two photographers and same-day previews.

Overtime is common at events, so agree on the hourly rate in advance.

Red flags

  • No shot list discussion.
  • No low-light samples.
  • Slow delivery with no date.
  • Unclear usage rights.
  • No backup equipment.

Organize photos into folders, such as speakers, guests and details, so your team can find what they need quickly.

Tips for a smoother project

Share any branding guidelines for event photos, such as how logos and sponsor signs should appear. Clear guidance helps the photographer capture images your marketing team can use immediately.

Decide how photos will be shared with attendees and partners. A shared online gallery with download options, or a curated selection for sponsors and press, helps your event reach further and makes the investment in photography more valuable.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The schedule is shared.
  • Someone will point out key people.
  • Group photo times are set.
  • Delivery and rights are agreed.
  • Guests can opt out.
