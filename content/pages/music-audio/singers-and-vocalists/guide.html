What a good session singer package includes

Vocal gigs are usually priced per song and by the number of parts. Check that the package states:

  • Parts: lead, harmonies, doubles, ad-libs.
  • Takes: how many, and whether they are comped into one best take.
  • Editing and tuning: included or raw takes only.
  • Files: dry WAV stems at your session's sample rate.
  • Revisions and delivery time.
  • Rights: fee only, credit, or royalty share.

Listen to unprocessed samples if possible, not only finished songs. Heavily produced demos can hide weaknesses. Raw or lightly mixed vocals show you the singer's real tone, pitch and timing.

How to brief a session singer

  1. The instrumental as a high-quality file.
  2. Lyrics, final and clearly formatted.
  3. Guide melody: a rough vocal, a piano line or a MIDI file.
  4. Tempo and key.
  5. Vocal style references, with time stamps.
  6. Pronunciation notes for names, languages or unusual words.

Confirm the key before anything is recorded. Ask the singer to sing a short section over the instrumental first. A key that sits badly in their range will sound strained no matter how good the singer is.

Give feedback with time stamps and specific directions, such as "softer in the first verse" or "more energy in the last chorus". Singers can adjust delivery easily when they know exactly where and how.

Ask the singer how they would approach your song before you order. A short note or a quick voice memo with their ideas for phrasing, harmonies or ad-libs shows whether they understand the style you want. Good session singers often suggest small improvements that make the song stronger, but only if they know the brief well and have heard the reference songs you shared.

What drives the price

  • number of parts and harmonies
  • song length and difficulty
  • editing and tuning included
  • languages and styles
  • rights: fee only versus credit or royalties
  • the singer's experience and rush delivery

Red flags

  • Only heavily processed samples.
  • Vague terms about ownership or royalties.
  • Files delivered with effects and no dry version.
  • No questions about key, tempo or style.
  • Poor recording quality, such as room echo or background noise.

Check the recording quality as well as the performance. A home studio should still produce clean, noise-free vocals. Echo and hum are very hard to remove in mixing.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have the instrumental, lyrics, guide melody, tempo and key.
  • The singer has confirmed the key suits their range.
  • I will receive dry WAV stems for each part.
  • Rights and credits are agreed in writing.
  • I have heard unprocessed samples.
