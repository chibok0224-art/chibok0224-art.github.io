What a good lifestyle shoot package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Shoot time and locations.
  • Team: models, stylist, makeup.
  • Shot list.
  • Number of edited images.
  • Usage rights for images and models.
  • Formats.
  • Delivery time.

Create a mood board together before the shoot. It aligns everyone on light, colors, poses and atmosphere.

Ask for model releases covering your planned uses. Without them, images may not be usable in ads.

Look at complete campaigns or lookbooks in the photographer's portfolio. Consistent lighting, color and styling across many images show they can deliver a cohesive set, which matters more than a few standout shots.

Plan products and outfits in order of priority, with backups. Clothes may not fit models perfectly and products may need cleaning, so extra options keep the shoot moving.

How to brief a lifestyle photographer

  1. Products and how they are used.
  2. Brand mood and references.
  3. Audience.
  4. Locations.
  5. Models and styling.
  6. Formats and uses.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Prioritize your shot list. Shoots move fast, and knowing the must-have images ensures they are captured first.

Think about diversity in casting. Featuring people who reflect your customers helps more people connect with your brand.

Discuss locations and permissions early. Shooting in shops, cafés or public spaces may need permission or permits, and having them in place prevents interruptions on the day and keeps the team focused on creating images.

What drives the price

  • shoot length
  • team size
  • locations and permits
  • number of products
  • editing level
  • usage rights

A half-day shoot with one model costs much less than a multi-location campaign with a full team and broad usage rights.

Ask for behind-the-scenes clips if you need social content. Short videos from the shoot are popular and cost little extra to capture.

Red flags

  • No mood board.
  • No model releases.
  • Unclear usage rights.
  • Missing key products.
  • Over-edited images.

Organize images by product and use so your team can find them quickly.

Tips for a smoother project

Think about how images will be cropped for each platform. Leaving space around subjects makes it easier to create square, vertical and banner versions without cutting off products or faces.

Agree on retouching limits, especially for skin and body shapes. Natural, honest images build trust with customers.

Plan delivery in batches if you need quick social content. Early selects help your team start posting while the full set is edited.

Keep a shared folder with mood boards, shot lists and final images for future shoots.
