What a good slideshow video package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Length and number of photos or clips.
  • Music: your choice or licensed tracks.
  • Text: titles, captions and dates.
  • Transitions and effects style.
  • Format and resolution for your screen.
  • Photo cleanup, if included.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a short preview before the final render. It lets you check the order, timing and mood before the whole video is finished.

Watch the editor's sample videos all the way through, not just the first seconds. Notice whether the pace matches the music, whether faces are cut off by movement effects, and whether text stays on screen long enough to read comfortably.

How to brief a slideshow editor

  1. The occasion and audience.
  2. Photos and clips, numbered in the order you want.
  3. Music choice or style.
  4. Text in a document, with correct spelling.
  5. Mood: joyful, emotional, elegant or energetic.
  6. Where it will be shown and the length.
  7. Deadline, with the event date.

Organize your photos before sending. Rename files with numbers in the order you want, remove duplicates and mark favorites. A tidy folder saves time and prevents mix-ups.

Leave a buffer before the event. Allow time for one round of changes and for testing the video on the actual screen or projector you will use.

For emotional events, such as memorials or anniversaries, consider gentle pacing and fewer effects. Simple fades and enough time on each photo let people recognize faces and share memories. Ask the editor to suggest a pace that fits the occasion and the music.

What drives the price

  • video length and number of photos
  • photo restoration or cleanup
  • custom titles and animation
  • licensed music
  • multiple formats
  • rush delivery

A simple slideshow with standard transitions costs less than one with restored photos, animated text and several versions for different screens.

Red flags

  • No preview before the final video.
  • Unlicensed music for public or business use.
  • Low resolution that looks blurry on large screens.
  • Overused effects that distract from the photos.
  • Unclear revision limits.

Keep the original photos and the final video in a safe place. Slideshows often become treasured keepsakes, and you may want copies for family years later.

Test the final video on the actual equipment before the event. Play it on the TV, projector or laptop you will use, with the real speakers. Checking sound levels and the file format in advance avoids stressful surprises in front of guests.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My photos are numbered in order.
  • My text is in a document with correct spelling.
  • The music can be used where the video will be shown.
  • I will see a preview first.
  • The format fits the screen I will use.
