What a good art direction package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: campaign, brand, shoot or game.
  • Mood boards and style frames.
  • Visual guidelines.
  • Direction of the creative team.
  • Review rounds and feedback process.
  • Availability and meetings.
  • Timeline and pricing.

Ask for two or three distinct visual directions at the start. Comparing options helps you choose consciously instead of reacting to a single proposal.

Request a short style guide at the end: colors, typography, image style and examples of do and don't. It keeps future work consistent after the art director leaves.

Look at the art director's past campaigns as complete sets, not single hero images. Consistency across posters, social posts, video frames and packaging shows they can hold a visual idea together across many formats and many contributors.

How to brief an art director

  1. The project and goal.
  2. Brand guidelines.
  3. Audience.
  4. Team members to direct.
  5. References you like and dislike.
  6. Channels and formats.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Share what has not worked visually before. Knowing which images or styles failed with your audience helps the art director avoid repeating them.

Introduce the art director to the creative team early and confirm their authority. Designers and photographers need to know whose feedback is final.

Think about every format the campaign will use, from social posts to print and video. A good direction works across all of them.

Agree on what happens when timelines or budgets change. Art directors can suggest simpler executions that keep the visual idea intact, such as fewer shoot locations or a smaller set of assets. Early warning lets them adapt rather than cut quality at the last moment.

What drives the price

  • project size
  • number of team members
  • shoots or productions
  • deliverables like style guides
  • duration
  • travel

A concept phase with mood boards costs much less than ongoing direction across a campaign with several shoots and teams.

Red flags

  • No visual options at the start.
  • Vague feedback to the team.
  • Ignores brand guidelines.
  • No final style guide.
  • Unclear authority.

Keep the mood boards and style guide with your brand files. They save time on every future campaign.

Tips for a smoother project

Ask for a short presentation explaining the chosen direction to your wider team. When everyone understands why certain colors, images and styles were chosen, they make better decisions in their own work.

Plan how feedback flows. Decide who gives notes to the art director and how the art director passes them to the team. A single, clear channel prevents mixed messages that confuse designers and slow production.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I will see several visual directions.
  • The team knows whose feedback is final.
  • All formats are considered.
  • I will get a style guide.
  • Brand guidelines are shared.
