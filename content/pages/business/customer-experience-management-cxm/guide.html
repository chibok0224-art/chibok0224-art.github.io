What a good CX package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Scope: which journeys and channels.
  • Research: data review, interviews, surveys.
  • Journey map with pain points.
  • Action plan ranked by impact and effort.
  • Measures to track improvement.
  • Workshops, if included.
  • Timeline and deliverables.

Ask for evidence next to each pain point, such as quotes from reviews, ticket counts or survey results. Evidence turns opinions into priorities your team can agree on.

Request a short list of quick wins you can fix within weeks. Early improvements build momentum and show customers that their feedback matters.

Ask the expert for an example of a change they recommended and the result it had, such as fewer support tickets after rewriting a confusing page. Concrete before-and-after stories show they focus on practical improvements rather than only on maps and frameworks.

How to brief a CX expert

  1. Your customers and main segments.
  2. Channels: website, app, store, support.
  3. Data: reviews, tickets, surveys, analytics.
  4. Known problems and complaints.
  5. Goals: loyalty, fewer complaints, more reviews.
  6. Teams involved.
  7. Budget and deadline.

Share raw feedback, not only summaries. The exact words customers use reveal frustrations and expectations that averages and scores hide.

Involve front-line staff. Support agents and store staff hear problems every day and often know the fixes. Their input makes the action plan realistic.

Pick one or two journeys to start, such as onboarding or returns. Focused work produces visible results faster than trying to redesign everything at once.

Close the loop with customers when you fix something. Telling them that their feedback led to a change builds trust and encourages more useful feedback.

What drives the price

  • number of journeys and channels
  • research depth
  • number of interviews
  • data analysis
  • workshops
  • implementation support

A review of one journey with a short action plan costs much less than a full CX program with interviews, surveys and ongoing measurement.

Implementation support, such as rewriting help pages or redesigning forms, is often priced separately from the research.

Red flags

  • Workshops without customer data.
  • Long reports with no priorities.
  • No measures of success.
  • Ignores front-line staff.
  • Generic best practices copied from other industries.

Track a few measures every month after changes. Steady tracking shows which fixes worked and keeps CX on the agenda.

Share results with the whole team. Seeing improvements in satisfaction or fewer complaints motivates people to keep improving.

Assign an owner for each action in the plan. Customer experience touches many teams, and improvements stall when everyone agrees but nobody is responsible. A named owner and a date for each step keep the plan moving.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared raw customer feedback.
  • Front-line staff are involved.
  • Each pain point has evidence.
  • Quick wins are identified.
  • Improvement measures are agreed.
