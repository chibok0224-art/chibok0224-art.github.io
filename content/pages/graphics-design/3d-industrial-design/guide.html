What a good 3D product package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Model accuracy and dimensions.
  • Materials and textures.
  • Number of renders and angles.
  • Backgrounds and lifestyle scenes.
  • File formats.
  • Print or manufacturing preparation.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a basic model check from several angles before rendering. Fixing proportions early is much faster than changing a finished render.

Request renders on a white background as well as styled scenes. Online stores often need clean images, while ads benefit from context.

Look at the modeler's renders of products similar to yours. Metal, glass, fabric and plastic each need different skills to look real. Close-up details like seams, buttons and reflections reveal quality more than wide shots.

How to brief a 3D product modeler

  1. Dimensions and drawings.
  2. Photos or sketches.
  3. Materials and finishes.
  4. Logo and label files.
  5. Use: renders, store, printing.
  6. Render styles and references.
  7. Deadline.

Provide exact dimensions and material samples or photos. Realistic renders depend on accurate scale and believable surfaces.

Send logo and label artwork as vector files. Sharp labels make product renders look professional.

Tell the modeler if the model will be used for manufacturing. Manufacturing files need precise geometry and formats that differ from marketing models.

Ask how the modeler handles confidential products. Unreleased designs need secure file sharing and an agreement that renders will not appear in portfolios until launch. Most professionals are used to this and will agree readily.

What drives the price

  • product complexity
  • number of variants
  • number of renders
  • lifestyle scenes
  • animation
  • print or manufacturing preparation

A simple model with a few studio renders costs much less than many color variants, lifestyle scenes and an animation.

Red flags

  • Guessed dimensions.
  • Plastic-looking materials.
  • Blurry labels.
  • Files unusable for your purpose.
  • Renders that misrepresent the product.

Keep the model for future variants and campaigns. New colors or packaging updates are much faster with an existing model.

Tips for a smoother project

Request a simple file list with the delivery: model formats, render sizes, texture maps and naming. A clear list helps your team, printers and marketing partners find the right file quickly.

Plan variants from the start. Colors, sizes and packaging versions are much faster to produce when the model is built with them in mind, and ordering them together usually costs less than adding them one by one later.

Discuss lighting style for renders, such as soft studio light, dramatic contrast or natural daylight. Lighting shapes how premium a product looks, and a few reference images help the modeler match your brand's photography style.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I provided exact dimensions.
  • Materials are described with references.
  • Labels are vector files.
  • The model suits its purpose.
  • I approved the model before rendering.
