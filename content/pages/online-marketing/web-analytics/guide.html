What a good analytics setup includes

Web analytics gigs range from basic setup to full tracking plans and dashboards. Check that the package states:

  • Tool: which analytics platform, and tag manager if used.
  • Tracking plan: which actions will be recorded.
  • Setup on your site or store platform.
  • Testing: proof that each event records correctly.
  • Consent: how tracking works with your cookie banner.
  • Reports or dashboards.
  • Documentation of what was set up.

Ask for a written tracking plan before setup: a simple list of each action, what it is called and when it fires. It prevents confusion later, and makes it easy to add or change tracking without breaking existing reports.

How to brief a web analytics expert

  1. Your goals: the actions that matter to your business.
  2. Your site platform and any store, booking or form tools.
  3. Access to the analytics account and site, through separate users.
  4. Ad platforms you use, if conversions should be shared with them.
  5. Questions you want the data to answer.
  6. Privacy requirements for your regions.

Test tracking yourself after setup. Complete a test purchase or form, then check that it appears in reports. Many sites have tracking that looks installed but records nothing useful.

Keep the number of tracked actions focused. Tracking every click creates noise; tracking the few actions that matter creates clarity.

Ask the expert to connect analytics with the tools you already use, such as your store, email platform or search console, where it makes sense. Seeing the full path from first visit to purchase in one place is far more useful than several disconnected reports.

What drives the price

  • complexity of the site and number of tools
  • number of events and conversions
  • e-commerce tracking
  • consent and privacy setup
  • dashboards and custom reports
  • training and documentation

Budget a little for regular checks. Site updates often break tracking quietly, and a short monthly review catches problems before months of data are lost.

Red flags

  • Analytics accounts created in the expert's name.
  • No testing of each tracked action.
  • Tracking set up without regard to consent rules.
  • Reports full of numbers with no link to your goals.
  • No documentation of what was installed.

If you run ads, ask the expert to make sure conversions are not counted twice between your analytics and ad platforms. Double counting makes campaigns look better than they are and leads to poor spending decisions.

Schedule a short review after the first month. Look at the dashboard together, check that numbers match your own records, and decide which reports you will actually use. Dropping reports nobody reads keeps the setup simple and focused.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The analytics account is in my name.
  • I have a written tracking plan.
  • Every tracked action has been tested.
  • Tracking respects consent rules for my regions.
  • I have a simple dashboard of key numbers.
