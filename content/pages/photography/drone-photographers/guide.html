What a good drone photography package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Location and flight plan.
  • Licenses and permissions.
  • Insurance.
  • Deliverables: photos, video, edits.
  • Weather and rescheduling policy.
  • Usage rights.
  • Delivery time and formats.

Ask the pilot to share how they check airspace and permissions for your location. A careful answer shows professionalism and protects you from fines or unsafe flights.

Plan the time of day. Morning and late afternoon light often look best, and calmer winds make footage smoother.

Look at the pilot's previous aerial work for similar subjects. Real estate, construction, events and landscapes need different flight paths, heights and camera settings. Relevant samples show they know how to capture the details that matter for your purpose.

How to brief a drone photographer

  1. Location and address.
  2. Purpose: listing, progress, event, marketing.
  3. Shots needed: angles, heights, paths.
  4. People and property nearby.
  5. Date options.
  6. Deliverables and formats.
  7. Usage and budget.

Share a map with the area to capture and points of interest. A clear plan saves flight time and battery life.

Tell neighbors or event guests about the flight when appropriate. Advance notice reduces concerns about privacy and noise.

For construction or farms, plan repeat flights from the same positions. Consistent angles make progress comparisons clear.

Combine aerial shots with ground photos for listings and marketing. Each tells part of the story.

Discuss privacy around the flight area. Aerial cameras can capture neighbors' gardens, windows and people. A responsible pilot plans angles that focus on your property or event and avoids recording private spaces unnecessarily.

What drives the price

  • location and travel
  • flight time
  • photos versus video
  • editing
  • permissions needed
  • repeat visits

A short flight for a few listing photos costs much less than a multi-location video project with editing and special permissions.

Permission applications and travel can add costs.

Confirm how the footage will be delivered and edited. Raw aerial video files are large, and color correction, stabilization and selecting the best clips take time. Clear expectations about editing help you receive footage that is ready to use.

Ask for a short list of the planned shots before the flight, such as a high overview, a slow approach and a top-down view. Agreeing on shots in advance helps the pilot use battery time efficiently and ensures you get the views you need.

Red flags

  • No mention of licenses or rules.
  • No insurance.
  • No weather plan.
  • Flights over crowds without permission.
  • Shaky samples.

Keep a record of each flight's date and position for repeat projects.

Plan the shoot with backup dates. Weather often decides whether a drone can fly safely, and having two or three possible days avoids rushing a flight in poor conditions or missing your deadline.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The pilot meets local rules.
  • Airspace and permissions are checked.
  • Insurance is confirmed.
  • Weather rescheduling is agreed.
  • Deliverables are clear.
