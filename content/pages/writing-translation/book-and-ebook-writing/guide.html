What a good ghostwriting project includes

Ghostwriting gigs range from short ebooks to full-length books. Check that the project states:

  • Length and number of chapters.
  • Process: interviews, outline, drafts and revisions.
  • Research: who provides facts and sources.
  • Milestones: outline, sample chapter, full draft.
  • Revisions at each stage.
  • Rights and credit: full rights to you, and any credit arrangement.
  • AI use and originality.

Ask for a paid sample chapter after the outline is approved. It shows whether the writer has captured your voice and ideas before you commit to the full book, and it gives both sides a clear reference for the chapters that follow.

Discuss the author's voice early. Share recordings of you speaking, emails you have written or articles you like, so the ghostwriter can match how you express ideas. A book that sounds like you is easier to promote and more convincing to readers who know you.

How to brief a ghostwriter

  1. The idea: what the book is about and why you are writing it.
  2. Readers: who the book is for.
  3. Your material: notes, talks, articles, recordings.
  4. Books you admire in the same genre.
  5. Length and format: ebook, print, audiobook.
  6. Timeline and your availability for interviews.
  7. Publishing plan: self-publishing or a publisher.

Record your interviews and keep copies. They are a valuable source for the writer, a record of what you said, and useful if you ever need another writer to continue the project.

Read chapters as they arrive rather than waiting for the full draft. Early feedback keeps the book on track, and problems in tone or structure are much easier to fix in chapter two than chapter twelve.

What drives the price

  • length of the book
  • research and interviews
  • complexity of the subject
  • milestones and revision rounds
  • the writer's experience and published work
  • timeline

Pay by milestone: outline, sample chapter, sections of the draft and final delivery. It spreads the cost and protects both you and the writer.

Red flags

  • No sample chapter or outline stage.
  • Unclear rights or credit.
  • Very fast delivery for a long book.
  • Vague answers about AI use.
  • Samples that appear copied from other books.

Check originality before publishing. Run sections through a plagiarism checker, and make sure any facts, quotes and statistics have sources you can verify.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep a shared document of decisions: chapter titles, key terms, names and the book's main message. Long projects drift, and a single reference keeps both of you aligned from the first chapter to the last.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a clear idea, audience and material to share.
  • Rights, credit and AI use are agreed in writing.
  • The project has an outline and a sample chapter stage.
  • Payments are tied to milestones.
  • I will check originality and facts before publishing.
