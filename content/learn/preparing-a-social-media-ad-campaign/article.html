1. Choose one goal

Decide what the campaign should achieve: sales, enquiries, bookings, app installs, newsletter sign-ups or simply attention for a launch. Choose one main goal, because campaigns aimed at everything do none of it well. Write down the number that would make you consider it a success, for example how much you could pay to win a customer and still make money. That number guides every later decision.

2. Describe your customers

Explain who your best customers are: their age range, location, interests, problems and what usually makes them buy. Share your existing customer data, if you have it, and examples of people you want more of. A specialist turns this into audiences on the platform, but they cannot invent insight about your customers. The more specific you are, the less money is wasted on people who will never buy.

3. Prepare a clear offer

Ads work best when they promise something specific: a discount for first-time buyers, a free consultation, a limited-time bundle or a clear benefit. Make sure the offer is true, easy to understand and available when the ad runs. Also decide what happens after someone responds: where they land, how quickly you reply and who handles their questions. A strong ad leading to a slow reply wastes the budget.

4. Plan the creative

Gather images, product photos, short videos, customer quotes and brand assets. Social ads depend on creative more than most people expect, since a thumb-stopping image or opening line decides whether anyone notices. Ask the specialist how many versions they will test and who produces them. If you need new images or videos, treat them as a separate piece of work with their own brief and budget.

5. Set up tracking first

Before spending, make sure you can measure results: the platform's tracking installed on your website, conversion events for purchases or enquiries, and consent settings that suit your visitors' region. Without tracking, you cannot tell which ads lead to customers and the platform cannot learn who to show them to. Ask the specialist to test each event and show you the result.

6. Sort out accounts and budget

Create the business and ad accounts in your own name and give the specialist the access they need. Decide how ad spend is paid, ideally directly to the platform by you. Agree on a daily or monthly budget limit, and what the specialist may change without asking. Agree how often you will receive reports and in what format.

Questions to ask the specialist

  • Which platforms and campaign types do you recommend for my goal?
  • How will you split the budget between testing and scaling?
  • Which numbers will you report, and how often?
  • What do you need from me: creative, offers, access, approvals?
  • What is a fair test period before we judge the results?
  • What happens to the accounts and data if we stop working together?

Budget and timing

Start with a test budget split across a few ad versions and audiences, and give the platform time to gather data before making big changes. Plan the timeline around your business: a seasonal offer needs to start early enough to learn and still have time to benefit from what you learn. Keep part of the budget in reserve for scaling what works, and agree in advance what result would make you increase spending, and what result would make you pause and fix the message or the page first.

Reviewing the first month

At the end of the first month, ask for a short review: which ads and audiences cost least per result, which messages people responded to, what the specialist would change and what they need from you. Write down the lessons even if the results were modest, since knowing what does not work saves money later. Decide together whether to continue, change direction or stop, based on the goal and the numbers you set at the start.

Common mistakes

  • Judging results after only a few days.
  • Sending clicks to a page that does not match the ad.
  • Running ads without tracking.
  • Changing many things at once, so nothing can be learned.
