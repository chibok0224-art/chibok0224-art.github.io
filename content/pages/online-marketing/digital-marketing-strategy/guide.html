What a good digital marketing strategy package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Research: market, competitors, current performance.
  • Goals and success measures.
  • Audience and positioning.
  • Channel priorities with reasons.
  • Budget split.
  • Roadmap and responsibilities.
  • Review sessions and delivery format.

Ask for a short list of priorities rather than a long list of tactics. Most businesses grow faster by doing two or three channels well than by spreading effort thin.

Request a one-page summary your whole team can follow. Detailed documents are useful, but a clear summary is what keeps everyone aligned week to week.

Make sure each recommendation has an owner and a first step. Strategy only matters when someone knows what to do on Monday.

Ask the strategist to walk you through a past plan and what happened after it was delivered. Look for evidence that clients could follow the plan and that results were measured, not only that the document looked impressive.

How to brief a digital marketing strategist

  1. Your business, offers and margins.
  2. Customers and how they buy.
  3. Current marketing and results.
  4. Analytics access.
  5. Budget and team capacity.
  6. Competitors.
  7. Goals and timeline.

Share what has not worked as well as what has. Failed campaigns contain useful lessons about audiences, messages and channels, and they prevent the strategist from repeating them.

Be realistic about team capacity. A plan that requires daily video content is useless if nobody can produce it. Good strategies match your people and skills.

Ask the strategist to explain trade-offs, such as quick results from ads versus slower but cheaper results from SEO. Understanding trade-offs helps you make informed budget decisions.

Plan regular reviews. Markets, platforms and costs change, so a strategy should be checked against results and updated rather than followed blindly.

What drives the price

  • research depth
  • number of channels and markets
  • workshops and interviews
  • detail of the roadmap
  • execution support
  • review frequency

A short strategy session with a summary costs much less than a full research-based plan with workshops, budgets and quarterly reviews.

Execution, such as running ads or creating content, is usually priced separately.

Red flags

  • Generic templates without your data.
  • Every channel recommended at once.
  • No budget or priorities.
  • No success measures.
  • Guaranteed growth promises.

Compare results with the plan each month. Small, regular adjustments keep the strategy relevant.

Keep the strategy document updated so new team members understand current priorities.

Share the strategy with everyone who will carry it out, including freelancers and agencies. When designers, writers and ad managers understand the priorities and audience, their work fits together instead of pulling in different directions.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared data, results and margins.
  • Priorities are few and clear.
  • The budget split is explained.
  • Each action has an owner.
  • Reviews are scheduled.
