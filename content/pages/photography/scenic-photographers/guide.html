What a good scenic photography package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Images: existing or new shoot.
  • License and exclusivity.
  • Resolution and file formats.
  • Editing level.
  • Location and timing, for new shoots.
  • Weather policy.
  • Delivery time.

Ask for full-resolution samples or crops before buying for print. Zooming in reveals sharpness and noise that small previews hide.

Look at the photographer's portfolio for the kind of scenes you need, such as mountains, coastlines or city lights. Different environments require different skills with light, weather and timing.

Think about seasons and timing for new shoots. Autumn colors, spring blossoms and winter snow happen only briefly, so plan well ahead and allow flexible dates.

How to brief a scenic photographer

  1. Location or type of scene.
  2. Use: print, web, tourism, book cover.
  3. Season and time of day.
  4. Orientation and space for text.
  5. Mood and style.
  6. License needs.
  7. Deadline.

Mention if you need space for text, such as a sky area for titles. Composition can be planned for it.

For tourism or marketing, keep images honest. Over-edited skies or removed buildings can disappoint visitors and harm trust.

Ask about the photographer's process for commissioned shoots, such as scouting, checking weather forecasts and returning for better light. Patience and planning are what produce the most striking landscape images, and they explain why such shoots take time.

What drives the price

  • license type
  • exclusivity
  • travel and location
  • print resolution
  • editing
  • rush delivery

Licensing an existing image for a website costs much less than an exclusive commissioned shoot at a remote location.

Ask whether the photographer can supply variations, such as different crops or times of day. Options are useful for different layouts.

Consider how the images will be displayed. Large wall prints, small website banners and printed brochures each need different crops, resolutions and sometimes different images altogether.

Red flags

  • Unclear licensing.
  • Low resolution for print.
  • Misleading heavy edits.
  • Drone shots without permits.
  • No weather plan.

Keep license documents with the image files.

For aerial views, see our drone photography guide. For editing, read our image editing guide and book cover guide.

For prints, ask about color profiles and printing partners. Color management makes prints look like the image on screen.

Consider exclusivity only when needed. Exclusive licenses cost more, and many uses work well with non-exclusive rights.

Credit the photographer where agreed. Credits are often part of the license and build good relationships.

If you represent a destination, consider showing real seasons and conditions. Honest imagery attracts visitors who know what to expect and leave positive reviews.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The license fits my use.
  • Resolution suits my print size.
  • Edits are honest.
  • Drone rules are followed.
  • Licenses are saved.
