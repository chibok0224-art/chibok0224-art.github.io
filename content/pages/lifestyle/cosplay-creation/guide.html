What a good cosplay order includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Pieces included: costume, props, wig, accessories.
  • Measurements and fittings.
  • Materials.
  • Progress photos.
  • Making time.
  • Shipping and customs.
  • Revisions and refund policy.

Ask for progress photos at key stages. Small changes are easy while pieces are being made and nearly impossible after finishing.

Look at photos of the maker's finished costumes worn by real people, ideally at conventions or photoshoots. Real wear shows fit, movement and durability much better than costumes displayed on a mannequin or in edited photos.

Discuss comfort for long days. Conventions involve walking, standing, heat and crowds. Ventilation, breathable fabrics, secure fastenings and lightweight props make the experience enjoyable instead of exhausting.

How to brief a cosplay maker

  1. Character and reference images.
  2. Measurements.
  3. Event date.
  4. Comfort needs: heat, movement, weight.
  5. Budget.
  6. Shipping address.
  7. Event prop rules.

Share several reference images from different angles. Costumes often look different in games, shows and art, so pick the version you want.

Think about transport. Large props and armor may need to come apart for travel or fit in luggage. Ask the maker to design pieces that can be packed and assembled easily.

Discuss how accurate the costume should be. Some cosplayers want screen-accurate details, while others prefer a comfortable, budget-friendly version that captures the character's spirit. Being clear about this helps the maker choose materials and techniques that match your expectations and budget.

What drives the price

  • costume complexity
  • props and armor
  • materials
  • wigs and accessories
  • shipping
  • rush making

A simple costume costs much less than full armor with lights, props and a styled wig.

Plan a test fitting before the event, wearing the costume for a few hours at home. It reveals pressure points, loose parts and anything that needs adjusting while there is still time.

Ask whether the maker can provide care instructions for each piece, such as how to clean fabric, store foam armor or protect paint. Good care keeps the costume looking great for many events.

Red flags

  • No measurement process.
  • No progress photos.
  • Unrealistic deadlines.
  • No shipping details.
  • No refund policy.

Store costumes carefully after events and keep spare parts for repairs.

For printed items, see our 3D product modeling guide. For photos, read our portrait photography guide and arts and crafts guide.

Bring a small repair kit to events: safety pins, glue, tape and spare fasteners. Quick fixes save the day when something breaks.

Keep reference images and measurements for future costumes. Repeat orders are faster and more accurate.

If you compete in costume contests, share the rules with the maker. Many contests require a portion of the costume to be made by the entrant, and clear communication avoids disqualification.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My measurements are accurate.
  • References show the version I want.
  • Event prop rules are checked.
  • I will see progress photos.
  • Delivery is before my event.
