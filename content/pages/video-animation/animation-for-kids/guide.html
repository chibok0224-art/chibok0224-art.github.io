What a good kids animation package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Video length and number of episodes.
  • Original characters and backgrounds.
  • Animation style: 2D, 3D or simple motion.
  • Voice-over, music and sound, with licenses.
  • Formats for your platforms.
  • Ownership of characters.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for character designs and a storyboard before animation. Children notice characters most, so getting faces, colors and expressions right early is worth the time.

If you plan a channel, agree on owning the characters and receiving source files. A recurring cast is a valuable asset, and you may want other animators to work with it later.

Look at complete episodes in the animator's portfolio rather than short clips. Children's content needs consistent characters, clear storytelling and steady pacing across several minutes. Full episodes show whether the animator can keep quality high for the whole video.

How to brief a kids animator

  1. Age group and learning goal, if any.
  2. Story or script.
  3. Characters and setting.
  4. Style references from original works.
  5. Songs and voices needed.
  6. Platforms and formats.
  7. Deadline and episode schedule.

Keep stories simple and positive. Young children follow one clear idea at a time, with repetition and friendly characters. Complex plots and fast cuts can confuse or overwhelm them.

Ask parents or teachers from your audience to watch a draft. Their reactions show whether the pace, language and content feel right for children of that age.

Plan for accessibility: clear speech, readable on-screen words and colors that are easy to tell apart help more children enjoy and learn from your videos.

What drives the price

  • video length
  • 2D versus 3D
  • character design work
  • original songs and voices
  • number of episodes
  • rush delivery

A short simple nursery rhyme video costs much less than a fully animated 3D episode with original songs and several characters.

Red flags

  • Characters that copy famous shows.
  • Unlicensed songs or voices.
  • Scary or unsuitable scenes for the age group.
  • No storyboard stage.
  • Unclear ownership of characters.

Follow the platform rules for children's content, including how videos are labeled. Correct labeling protects your channel and helps parents find suitable videos.

Store character sheets, backgrounds and project files in one place. They are the foundation of every future episode and make it easy to keep a consistent look.

Think about how often you will publish. Regular episodes help channels grow, so agree on a realistic schedule and a reusable production process. Templates for openings, endings and backgrounds can cut the time and cost of every new episode.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know the age group.
  • Characters are original.
  • Songs and voices are licensed.
  • I will approve characters and a storyboard first.
  • I own the characters and get source files.
