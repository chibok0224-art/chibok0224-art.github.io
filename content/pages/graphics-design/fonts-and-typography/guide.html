What a good font design package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Character set and supported languages.
  • Weights and styles: regular, bold, italic.
  • File formats: OTF, TTF, WOFF2.
  • Spacing and kerning work.
  • Testing in common apps and browsers.
  • Ownership or license terms.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a test document showing the font in real text: headlines, paragraphs, numbers and your brand name. A font that looks great in a single word can feel uneven in a full paragraph.

Spacing and kerning take more time than drawing letters. Check that pairs like AV, To and rn look even, because poor spacing is what makes amateur fonts look wrong.

Look at the designer's fonts in use, not only on specimen sheets. Fonts shown on packaging, websites or signs reveal how well they work at different sizes and with other typefaces. Real-world examples are the best proof that the letters hold up outside the design software.

How to brief a font designer

  1. Where the font will be used: logo, headlines, body text, signs.
  2. Brand personality and references.
  3. Languages and special characters.
  4. Weights and styles needed.
  5. Existing logo or lettering, if any.
  6. Technical needs: web, apps, print.
  7. Deadline.

Explain how the font will be used. A display font for headlines can be expressive, while a font for long text must stay readable at small sizes. Mixing these goals in one font often leads to compromises.

Share typefaces you like and say exactly why: the round shapes, the narrow width or the friendly feel. Specific reasons help the designer understand your taste faster than general words like modern.

If you only need a logo, consider custom lettering instead of a full font. Lettering is drawn for a single word or phrase and costs much less than a complete typeface.

What drives the price

  • number of characters and languages
  • number of weights and styles
  • display versus text font
  • kerning and hinting depth
  • ownership versus license
  • rush delivery

A single display weight for headlines costs far less than a full family with several weights, italics and many languages.

Red flags

  • Missing characters you need, such as accents or numbers.
  • Uneven spacing in sample text.
  • No clear ownership or license.
  • Fonts copied or traced from existing typefaces.
  • Files that fail to install.

Install the font on every device and app you use before final approval. Problems with missing characters or odd line spacing are much easier to fix before launch.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan how the font will be shared inside your team and with partners. Keep the files, license text and a short usage note in one shared folder. It prevents people from installing old versions or substituting similar fonts that break your brand's consistency.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I know where the font will be used.
  • The character set covers my languages.
  • I will see the font in real text.
  • Ownership or license is in writing.
  • Files install correctly on my devices.
