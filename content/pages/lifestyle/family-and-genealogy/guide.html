What a good genealogy package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Research goal.
  • Records and databases searched.
  • Citations for every fact.
  • Family tree format.
  • Report of findings and gaps.
  • Privacy for living people.
  • Hours and pricing.

Ask for a report that separates proven facts, likely connections and open questions. Honest labels prevent errors from spreading through your tree.

Request copies or links to original records where possible. Seeing the source builds confidence and helps future research.

Ask researchers about their experience with the countries, languages and record types you need. Records in different regions are organized very differently, and familiarity saves time and leads to more reliable results.

Be patient with old records. Spellings change, dates vary and documents may be damaged or lost. A careful researcher explains these challenges rather than forcing connections.

How to brief a genealogy researcher

  1. Known family members with dates and places.
  2. Documents and photos.
  3. Family stories.
  4. Research goal.
  5. DNA results, if any.
  6. Budget and hours.
  7. Deadline.

Set a focused goal, such as finding the immigration record of one great-grandparent. Focused questions produce better results within a budget.

If DNA is involved, discuss surprises in advance. DNA can reveal unexpected relationships, and a thoughtful researcher handles sensitive findings with care and privacy.

Ask how the researcher handles conflicting records, such as different birth years on two documents. Explaining which source is more reliable and why is a sign of careful, professional work that you can trust and build on later.

What drives the price

  • number of generations
  • countries and languages
  • record access
  • DNA analysis
  • written family history
  • travel to archives

A short search for one ancestor costs much less than a multi-generation tree across several countries with a written family history.

Red flags

  • Facts without sources.
  • Guaranteed results.
  • Careless sharing of living people's data.
  • Merging people with similar names without proof.
  • No report of gaps.

Keep a research log with sources and dates. It helps you or future researchers continue the work.

Tips for a smoother project

Consider how results will be shared with family. A printed tree, a digital tree or a short family history book each suit different audiences.

Agree on how often you receive updates during longer projects. Short progress notes keep you involved and let you share new memories or documents that might open fresh research paths.

Respect the privacy of living relatives and ask before publishing information about them online.

Store copies of documents and photos in more than one place. Family records are irreplaceable.

Talk to older relatives early. Their memories, names and stories often provide clues that records alone cannot.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I shared what I already know.
  • The goal is focused.
  • Every fact has a source.
  • Living people's privacy is protected.
  • Gaps are reported honestly.
