What a good menu design package includes

Menu design gigs are priced by pages, formats and concepts. Check that the package states:

  • Formats: printed, table card, board, delivery app or digital.
  • Pages or panels and size.
  • Layout: sections, item names, descriptions and prices.
  • Allergy and dietary symbols.
  • Print-ready files for your printer.
  • Editable template for price and dish changes.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for a legend of dietary symbols that is easy to read, such as vegetarian, vegan or common allergens, and consistent across all formats. Guests with allergies rely on it, and staff answer fewer questions when it is clear.

How to brief a menu designer

  1. Your full menu: sections, dish names, descriptions and prices.
  2. Allergen and dietary information for each dish.
  3. Your concept: cuisine, atmosphere and price level.
  4. Brand files and interior photos.
  5. Formats and sizes you need.
  6. Dishes to highlight, such as signatures or high-margin items.
  7. Printer specifications.

Share photos of your restaurant's interior and lighting. A menu that looks great in bright daylight may be hard to read in a dim dining room, and the design should match the atmosphere guests experience.

Check readability with real guests and staff. Ask a few people to read the menu in the restaurant's actual lighting, including older guests who may struggle with small text. Adjust font sizes before the final print run.

Plan how often the menu changes. Seasonal menus or frequent specials suit a template with a flexible section, while a stable menu can have a more fixed design.

Consider how the menu will be handled. Menus are touched, spilled on and wiped every day. Discuss the material, finish and binding with the designer and printer, so the layout suits the format and the menu still looks good after weeks of service.

What drives the price

  • number of pages and formats
  • custom illustrations or photography
  • number of concepts and revisions
  • editable templates
  • translation into other languages
  • rush delivery before opening

Durable printing, such as laminated or thick paper, costs more but lasts longer in daily use. Discuss the material with your printer before the design is finalized.

Red flags

  • Small, low-contrast text that is hard to read in dim light.
  • No editable template for price changes.
  • Missing or unclear allergy information.
  • Images with no license.
  • No questions about your formats and printer.

Double-check every price and allergen before printing. Errors on a menu can cause disputes with guests, and allergy mistakes can be dangerous.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have the final menu with prices and allergens.
  • I know every format I need.
  • I will get an editable template.
  • Text is readable in my restaurant's lighting.
  • I will check prices and allergens before printing.
