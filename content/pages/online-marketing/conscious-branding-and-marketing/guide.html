What a good conscious branding package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Values and purpose workshop.
  • Positioning.
  • Messaging guidelines.
  • Proof points for claims.
  • Content examples.
  • Claim review.
  • Timeline and deliverables.

Ask for a list of proof points behind every message: actions, data or partnerships. Messages without proof should be removed or rewritten.

Request examples of language to avoid, such as vague terms that cannot be supported.

Ask experts for examples of brands they have helped and how those brands backed up their messages. Concrete actions behind the messaging, such as published supplier lists or measured impact, show the expert values honesty over clever wording.

How to brief a conscious branding expert

  1. Your values and why they matter.
  2. Actions you already take.
  3. Customers.
  4. Competitors.
  5. Goals.
  6. Evidence available.
  7. Deadline.

Share real actions and their limits. Honest stories about progress and challenges build more trust than perfect-sounding claims.

Involve employees. People inside the business know whether values are lived or just written down.

Check local rules on environmental and social claims before publishing.

Plan how you will share progress over time, including setbacks. Regular, honest updates build lasting trust and show customers that values are part of daily decisions, not a one-time campaign.

Think about how your values show up in everyday details: packaging, customer service, hiring and supplier choices. Customers notice consistency between what a brand says and what it does, and small aligned details often build more trust than big campaigns.

What drives the price

  • workshop depth
  • messaging scope
  • content creation
  • claim reviews
  • number of channels
  • ongoing support

A messaging review costs much less than a full positioning project with workshops, guidelines and content.

Consider partnering with credible organizations. Independent partners can strengthen claims, but only if the partnership is meaningful.

Plan how you will measure trust and reputation, such as reviews mentioning your values, repeat customers or community engagement. These signals show whether your messaging resonates with the people you want to reach.

Red flags

  • Exaggerated impact claims.
  • Causes used without commitment.
  • Vague language.
  • No proof points.
  • Copying other brands' purpose statements.

Revisit messaging as your actions grow so your communication always matches reality.

Ask for guidance on responding to criticism. A calm, transparent response protects your brand if customers question your claims.

Ask the expert to review your website and packaging for claims that could be misunderstood. Words like natural, eco-friendly or ethical mean different things to different people, and replacing them with specific facts, such as recycled content percentages or named certifications, makes your message clearer and more credible.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Every claim has a proof point.
  • Values reflect real actions.
  • Vague terms are avoided.
  • Employees are involved.
  • Local claim rules are checked.
