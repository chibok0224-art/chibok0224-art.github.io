No-code chatbots

No-code chatbot builders let you design conversations visually and connect them to your website, messaging apps or help desk. Many now include AI features that answer questions from your help articles. They are quick to launch, and you or a freelancer can update them without a developer. They suit common goals such as answering frequent questions, booking appointments, collecting leads or routing customers to the right person.

The limits are monthly subscription costs, features set by the platform and less control over how data is handled. If you need something the platform does not support, you may hit a wall.

Custom AI chatbots

A custom chatbot is built by a developer using AI models and your own systems. It can connect to your database, look up orders, follow specific rules and run inside your own app. It suits businesses with complex products, strict data requirements, or chatbots that are a core part of the product rather than a support tool.

The trade-offs are higher build cost, longer timelines and ongoing maintenance. AI models and APIs change, and someone needs to keep the chatbot working, monitor its answers and manage usage costs.

How to choose

  • Answering common questions on a website: start with a no-code builder.
  • Booking, lead capture or simple routing: no-code is usually enough.
  • Looking up orders, accounts or internal data: consider a custom build or advanced integrations. See our APIs and integrations guide.
  • A chatbot that is part of your product: custom, built with a developer experienced in AI.

The content matters more than the tool

Whichever route you choose, a chatbot is only as good as the information and conversation design behind it. Gather your most common questions, write clear answers, decide when the bot should hand over to a person and plan what it should say when it does not know. A UX writer can help make the conversation clear and friendly.

Privacy and accuracy

AI chatbots can give confident but wrong answers. Limit what the chatbot is allowed to answer, show sources where possible and give customers an easy way to reach a person. Before collecting personal information, check where conversations are stored, who can see them and whether they are used to train models. Ask the freelancer to explain these settings in writing.

The middle ground: builders plus integrations

The choice is not always all or nothing. Many projects start with a no-code builder and add a few custom pieces, such as a small integration that looks up order status or sends conversations to your help desk. A developer can build these connections while the conversation design stays in the builder, where your team can still edit it. This keeps costs lower than a fully custom build and gives you room to grow.

If you later outgrow the builder, the work is not wasted. Your list of questions, answers and handover rules, and the lessons from real conversations, carry over to any new system. Export conversation logs regularly so you keep that history, and ask early whether the platform lets you export your content if you decide to move.

What to ask freelancers

  • Which tool or approach do you recommend for my goals, and why?
  • Who will own the accounts, data and code?
  • How will the chatbot hand over to a person?
  • How will we test answers before launch?
  • What will it cost each month to run, including AI usage?
