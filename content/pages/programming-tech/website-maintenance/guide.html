What a good maintenance service includes

Maintenance gigs are sold as monthly plans or one-off tasks. Check that the service states:

  • Updates: core software, themes and plugins, and how often.
  • Backups: frequency, where they are stored, and restore testing.
  • Security: scans, login protection and malware cleanup terms.
  • Monitoring: uptime alerts and what happens when the site goes down.
  • Small fixes and content changes: how many, or how much time, per month.
  • Speed and health checks: broken links, forms and page speed.
  • Reporting: a short monthly summary of what was done.
  • Response time for urgent problems.

What to prepare before you hire

  1. Your site address and what it is built with, such as WordPress, Shopify or custom code.
  2. A list of accounts: domain, hosting, site admin, email and any paid plugins.
  3. Known problems, with screenshots.
  4. What matters most: sales, bookings, forms or simply staying online.
  5. Changes you expect each month, such as new blog posts or price updates.
  6. Who to contact in an emergency, and how.

Give the expert their own user account rather than yours. Keep the domain, hosting and site admin accounts in your name, and remove the expert's access if you change providers.

Ask for a restore test in the first month: the expert restores a backup to a test copy of the site and shows you it works. A backup that has never been restored is only a hope.

What drives the price

  • platform and how complex the site is
  • number of plugins, integrations and pages
  • frequency of updates, backups and checks
  • hours of fixes or content changes included
  • online stores and sites handling payments or personal data
  • response time for emergencies

Red flags

  • No written list of what the plan covers.
  • Backups stored only on the same server as the site.
  • Updates applied to the live site with no backup first.
  • The expert asks to move your domain or hosting into their name.
  • No reports, so you cannot see what you are paying for.

Keep a simple log of every change made to the site, with the date and who made it. When something breaks, the log usually points straight to the cause, such as a plugin update the day before. Ask the expert to add to it as part of the monthly report, so the history stays with you even if you change providers later.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have a written list of what is included and what costs extra.
  • Backups are stored off the server, and a restore will be tested.
  • The expert has their own user account, not my login.
  • My domain and hosting stay in my name.
  • I know how to reach the expert in an emergency and how fast they respond.
