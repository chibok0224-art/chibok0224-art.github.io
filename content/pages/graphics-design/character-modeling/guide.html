What a good character modeling package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Purpose: game, animation, VR or printing.
  • Polygon budget or detail level.
  • Textures and materials.
  • Rigging, included or separate.
  • File formats and software.
  • Ownership.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask for turnaround renders of the model from all sides, plus a wireframe view. The wireframe shows whether the topology suits animation or games, which a pretty render can hide.

Test the model in your own engine or software before final approval. Scale, materials and orientation often need small fixes when a file moves between programs.

Look for modelers whose portfolio matches your style and platform. A sculptor known for detailed collectibles may not be the best fit for an efficient mobile game character, and the reverse is also true. Matching experience to purpose saves revisions.

How to brief a 3D character modeler

  1. Concept art or detailed references.
  2. Purpose and platform.
  3. Style: realistic, stylized, low poly.
  4. Polygon and texture limits.
  5. Software and engine you use.
  6. Rigging and animation needs.
  7. Deadline.

Provide front and side views of the character if possible. Concept art from several angles removes guesswork and leads to fewer revisions than a single illustration.

Tell the modeler the technical limits early: polygon counts for mobile games, texture sizes or a specific engine. These limits shape how the model is built from the start.

For a series of characters, ask the modeler to set up a consistent scale and naming system. It saves time when characters appear together in scenes or levels.

Agree on how feedback will be given at each stage: blockout, sculpt, retopology and texturing. Approving the rough shape before detail work starts prevents large changes late in the project, when they are slow and costly to make.

What drives the price

  • character complexity
  • realistic versus stylized
  • polygon and texture detail
  • rigging
  • 3D print preparation
  • number of characters

A simple low-poly character costs much less than a detailed realistic hero character with high-resolution textures and a full rig.

Red flags

  • No wireframe or topology views.
  • Models far over your polygon budget.
  • Missing texture maps.
  • Characters copied from existing games or films.
  • Unclear ownership.

Keep the source files as well as exports. Later changes, new outfits or different formats are much easier from the original project files.

Tips for a smoother project

Plan the full pipeline before modeling starts: who will rig, animate, light and integrate the character. Sharing the plan lets the modeler prepare the model for the next steps, such as clean joints for rigging or texture layouts that suit your engine.

Questions to ask before you order

  • Can you show wireframe and topology views of characters you have built?
  • Can you keep within my polygon budget and target engine or software?
  • Which texture maps will I receive, and at what resolution?
  • Will the model be ready for rigging and animation, or only for still renders?
  • Who owns the model and source files after delivery?

Send the artist your technical limits before any modeling starts: engine, polygon count, texture sizes and file format. Fixing these after the model is finished often means starting again.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I sent concept art from several angles.
  • The polygon and texture limits are clear.
  • I will check wireframes and test the model.
  • Formats work in my software.
  • Ownership is in writing.
