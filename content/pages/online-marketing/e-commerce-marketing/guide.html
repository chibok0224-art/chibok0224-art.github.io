What a good e-commerce marketing package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Audit of store, data and current channels.
  • Strategy with priority channels.
  • Tracking setup or checks.
  • Execution: what is done and by whom.
  • Reporting on sales, costs and returns.
  • Access and data handling.
  • Timeline and contract terms.

Ask for a clear list of priorities after the audit, with the reason for each. A short plan focused on the biggest opportunities is more useful than a long list of tactics.

Agree on which numbers define success: revenue, profit after ad costs, repeat purchases or new customers. Clear goals keep the work focused on what actually helps your business.

Ask for examples of stores the expert has helped and what changed: which channels they focused on, what they fixed first and how results were measured. Clear stories about decisions and trade-offs show real experience better than screenshots of large revenue numbers.

How to brief an e-commerce marketer

  1. Your store platform and products.
  2. Margins and average order value.
  3. Current traffic and sales sources.
  4. Customer profile.
  5. Budget for ads and tools.
  6. Past campaigns and results.
  7. Goals and timeline.

Share your margins. An expert who knows how much profit each sale makes can judge which campaigns are worth running, rather than chasing sales that cost more to win than they earn.

Start with the basics: product photos, descriptions, reviews, shipping information and a smooth checkout. Sending more visitors to a store with weak pages wastes ad money.

Use existing customers. Email follow-ups, loyalty offers and reminders to reorder often bring sales at lower cost than finding new customers.

What drives the price

  • number of channels
  • ad management versus strategy only
  • store size and catalog
  • content and creative production
  • reporting depth
  • contract length

A one-time audit and plan costs much less than ongoing management of ads, email and content across several channels.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed revenue or sales.
  • No interest in your margins or data.
  • Requests for your passwords.
  • Long contracts before any results.
  • Reports with vanity metrics only.

Keep ownership of your ad accounts, analytics and email lists. If you change experts, your data and history should stay with your business.

Review results together on a regular schedule, such as monthly. Look at sales, profit after marketing costs and repeat customers, not only traffic. Regular reviews help you stop what is not working early and move budget to what is.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Tracking of sales and sources works.
  • I shared margins and goals.
  • Access uses user roles, not passwords.
  • Success is measured by profit, not only clicks.
  • I keep ownership of accounts and data.
