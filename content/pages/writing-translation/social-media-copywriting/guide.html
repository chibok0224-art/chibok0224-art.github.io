What a good social media copy package includes

Social media copy gigs are usually priced per post or in monthly bundles. Check that the package states:

  • Number of posts and platforms.
  • Formats: captions, carousel text, hooks or short video scripts.
  • Content plan: topics and schedule.
  • Voice guidelines.
  • Hashtags and calls to action.
  • Revisions and delivery format.
  • Originality and AI policy.

Ask for a mix of post types: helpful tips, stories, behind-the-scenes, questions and offers. A feed that only sells tires people out; a feed that helps and entertains gives them reasons to follow and eventually buy.

Ask the writer to prepare a short voice guide after the first batch: words you use and avoid, how you address followers, and example phrases. It keeps posts consistent even if someone else on your team writes occasionally, and it makes briefing future writers much faster.

How to brief a social media copywriter

  1. Your business, audience and goals.
  2. Platforms and posting frequency.
  3. Voice examples: posts you like, yours or others'.
  4. Topics and upcoming dates, launches or offers.
  5. Words and claims to avoid.
  6. Visuals: whether copy should match existing images or videos.

Share your best-performing past posts. They show what your audience already responds to, which is a better guide than general trends.

Review a small batch before committing to a full month. Check tone, facts and whether the posts sound like something you would say. Once the voice is right, later batches go faster.

What drives the price

  • number of posts and platforms
  • short video scripts or carousels
  • content planning
  • research into your industry
  • voice guide creation
  • monthly volume and turnaround

Ask whether the writer can also repurpose longer content, such as blog posts or videos, into social posts. It often gives better material for less cost.

Red flags

  • Generic posts that could be for any brand.
  • Captions copied from other accounts.
  • Misleading claims or fake urgency.
  • Long, irrelevant hashtag lists.
  • No questions about your audience or goals.

Track which posts lead to comments, saves, clicks or sales, and share that with the writer each month. It helps them improve, and it helps you see whether the copy is worth the investment.

Plan who will reply to comments and messages. Good copy invites conversation, and unanswered questions under a post look worse than no post at all. If the writer does not manage replies, decide who on your team will check and answer them.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have shared my audience, platforms and goals.
  • The writer has examples of my voice.
  • I will review a small batch first.
  • Posts mix helpful, personal and promotional content.
  • I will share results each month.
