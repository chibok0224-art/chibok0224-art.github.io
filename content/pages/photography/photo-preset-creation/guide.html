What a good preset package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Number of presets and variations.
  • Apps and formats supported.
  • Testing on your photos.
  • Before-and-after examples.
  • Installation instructions.
  • Usage rights, including resale.
  • Revisions and delivery time.

Ask the creator to test presets on your own photos from different situations: indoors, outdoors, bright and dim. A preset that only works on one perfect sample image will disappoint in daily use.

Check skin tones and product colors carefully. Strong styles can make skin look orange or products look the wrong color, which can confuse customers who buy based on photos.

Look at the creator's before-and-after examples across many photo types. Strong preset creators show consistent results on portraits, products, interiors and outdoor scenes. If every sample is a perfect sunset, you cannot tell how the preset handles your everyday photos.

How to brief a preset creator

  1. Your photos in different lighting.
  2. The look you want, with examples.
  3. Apps and devices you use.
  4. Main subjects: people, products, food, travel.
  5. Colors to keep accurate.
  6. Number of variations.
  7. Deadline.

Send original or raw files if you have them. Presets work best on unedited images, and testing on originals shows how the preset behaves in your real workflow.

Describe the feeling of the look: warm and soft, clean and bright, moody and dark. Pair each word with an example image so the creator understands exactly what you mean.

If you edit on a phone, ask for mobile-friendly versions and instructions. Mobile apps sometimes handle presets differently from desktop software.

Check how the presets handle different cameras and phones if you or your team use several devices. Each sensor renders color slightly differently, and a preset tuned for one camera may need a small variation for another to keep results matching across all your images.

What drives the price

  • number of presets
  • variety of lighting tested
  • desktop and mobile versions
  • commercial or resale rights
  • extra adjustments or tutorials
  • rush delivery

A single preset for personal use costs much less than a collection with variations, mobile versions and resale rights.

Red flags

  • Only tested on the creator's own photos.
  • Unnatural skin tones.
  • Files that fail to install.
  • No usage rights for business.
  • Generic presets sold as custom.

Use presets as a starting point. Small tweaks to exposure and white balance for each photo keep results consistent and natural.

Tips for a smoother project

Keep your presets organized with clear names, such as the look and the lighting they suit. Over time you may build a small library for different seasons or campaigns, and good naming keeps your editing fast and your feed consistent.
