What a good interpretation package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Languages and direction.
  • Mode: consecutive or simultaneous.
  • Duration and breaks.
  • Platform or equipment.
  • Preparation time.
  • Confidentiality.
  • Certification, if required, and pricing.

Schedule a short technical test before online events. Audio problems are the most common cause of failed remote interpretation.

Plan breaks or a second interpreter for long sessions. Simultaneous interpretation is tiring, and quality drops without rest.

Ask interpreters about experience in your field, such as technical, business or community settings. Specialized vocabulary and context make a big difference to accuracy, and an interpreter familiar with your topic will need less preparation.

Think about the audience's needs. Some participants may need interpretation only part of the time, or in different languages. Planning channels and seating, or online language options, ensures everyone can follow comfortably.

How to brief an interpreter

  1. Event type and participants.
  2. Languages and accents.
  3. Agenda and materials.
  4. Glossary of names and terms.
  5. Platform or venue.
  6. Duration.
  7. Confidentiality needs.

Send materials early: slides, scripts, names and specialist terms. Prepared interpreters are far more accurate.

Ask speakers to talk at a steady pace and pause between ideas. It helps the interpreter and the audience.

Check certification requirements for official settings, such as courts or hospitals.

For online events, ask participants to use headphones and good microphones. Clear audio from speakers is essential for accurate interpretation and reduces strain for everyone.

Confirm the time zone and schedule for international events. Interpreters may be working early or late, and clear timing, including buffer time before the start, prevents missed sessions and rushed preparation.

What drives the price

  • languages
  • mode
  • duration
  • specialized topics
  • equipment
  • travel

A short consecutive call costs much less than a full-day conference with simultaneous interpretation, equipment and two interpreters.

Red flags

  • No preparation request.
  • No technical test.
  • One interpreter for very long simultaneous sessions.
  • No confidentiality.
  • No certification where required.

Collect feedback from participants after the event. It helps you choose and brief interpreters better next time.

Tips for a smoother project

Brief speakers about the interpreter. Speakers who know someone is interpreting tend to slow down, avoid jokes that do not translate and pause naturally, which improves the experience for all.

Record the session only if everyone agrees and the interpreter consents. Recordings can be useful for minutes, but they must respect privacy and confidentiality.

Prepare a short glossary of company names, product names and acronyms with pronunciations. It is one of the simplest ways to improve accuracy.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The mode fits my event.
  • Materials are sent early.
  • The setup is tested.
  • Breaks or a second interpreter are planned.
  • Certification is checked if needed.
