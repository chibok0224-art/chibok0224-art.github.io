What a good beat package includes

Beat gigs range from leases of existing beats to custom production. Check that the package states:

  • License type: lease or exclusive.
  • Limits: streams, sales, videos, performances.
  • Files: MP3, WAV, stems or trackouts.
  • Samples: original or cleared.
  • Credit and royalties.
  • Customization: changes to structure or length.
  • Delivery time.

Read the license before paying, not after. Check how many streams or copies are allowed, whether music videos and monetization are included, and what happens if your song grows beyond the limits.

Check the sound quality of previews on good headphones. A beat that sounds exciting on a phone speaker may have muddy low end or harsh highs that become obvious once vocals are added and the song is mixed. Good beat makers deliver clean, balanced files that leave room for your voice in the arrangement.

How to brief a beat maker

  1. Genre and mood.
  2. Tempo and key, if you know them.
  3. Reference tracks, with what you like.
  4. Song structure: intro, verses, hooks.
  5. Your vocals: range and style.
  6. How you will release it: streaming, video, live shows.

If you plan a serious release, consider exclusive rights or a custom beat. Building a song on a leased beat that others also use can limit your options later, especially if the song becomes popular.

Ask for tagged and untagged versions where relevant. Tagged previews contain the producer's audio tag; the final release version should be clean.

Keep a copy of the license and the purchase receipt. Distributors and platforms may ask for proof that you have rights to the beat.

What drives the price

  • lease versus exclusive rights
  • stems and trackouts
  • custom production
  • number of changes
  • sample clearance
  • the producer's experience and popularity

Exclusive rights and stems usually cost more than basic leases, but they give you far more freedom when releasing and mixing.

Red flags

  • Beats sold as exclusive that are also leased to others.
  • Unclear or missing license terms.
  • Uncleared samples.
  • Producers asking for royalties not mentioned before purchase.
  • Only low-quality MP3s for release.

Search for the beat on streaming platforms before releasing. If another artist has already released a song on the same beat, especially under an exclusive claim, sort it out with the producer first.

Think about your long-term catalog. Working with the same producer across several songs can give your music a consistent sound, and many producers offer better terms for regular clients. Keep all licenses organized by song, so you always know what rights you have for each release.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • I have read the license terms and limits.
  • I know whether the beat is leased or exclusive.
  • Samples are original or cleared.
  • I will get the files I need, including stems if mixing.
  • I have saved the license and receipt.
