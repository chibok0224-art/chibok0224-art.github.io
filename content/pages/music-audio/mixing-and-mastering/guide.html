What a good mixing and mastering package includes

Audio gigs are usually priced per song and by the number of tracks in the session. Check that the package states:

  • Service: mixing, mastering, or both.
  • Track limit: how many stems are included before extra charges apply.
  • Vocal work: whether tuning and timing correction are included.
  • Revisions: how many rounds of changes after the first mix.
  • Deliverables: WAV master, MP3, and versions such as instrumental or clean.
  • Delivery time per song.
  • Rights: confirmation that you keep all rights to your music.

How to prepare your song

  1. Stems: export each track as a separate WAV file from the same start point, at your session's sample rate, with clear names such as "Lead Vocal" or "Kick".
  2. Effects: send stems dry, plus a copy of any effect that is part of the sound, such as a special delay on the vocal.
  3. Your rough mix, so the engineer hears your intended balance.
  4. One or two reference tracks in the same genre, with a note on what you like about their sound.
  5. Tempo and key of the song.
  6. Where it will be released: streaming, video, radio, vinyl or live use.
  7. Notes: anything that must stand out, and anything you are unhappy with.

If your song needs a spoken intro or ad read, our voice over guide covers hiring a voice actor. For a music video, see our video editing guide.

Before sending files, play the stems together once in a new session to check that nothing is missing, silent or out of time. A missing track found halfway through the mix costs everyone a revision round.

What drives the price

  • number of tracks in the session
  • mixing and mastering together, or mastering only
  • vocal tuning, timing correction and noise cleanup
  • extra versions such as instrumental, clean or stems of the final mix
  • number of revision rounds
  • the engineer's experience and credits in your genre, and rush delivery

Red flags

  • No before-and-after samples, or samples only in a genre far from yours.
  • Very fast mastering at a very low price with no mention of listening to the song. This often means an automatic preset.
  • No clear track limit, leading to surprise charges after you send files.
  • Claims to "guarantee" chart or playlist placement.
  • Any request to share rights, royalties or credits you did not agree to.

Before you approve the final master, listen on headphones, a phone speaker and a car or home speaker. A good master sounds balanced on all of them.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • My stems are exported from the same start point and clearly named.
  • I have a rough mix and at least one reference track.
  • The package states the track limit, revisions and deliverables.
  • I keep all rights to my song.
  • I have listened to before-and-after samples in my genre.
