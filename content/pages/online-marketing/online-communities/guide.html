What a good community management package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Platforms managed.
  • Hours and coverage times.
  • Tasks: moderation, content, events, support.
  • Community rules and enforcement.
  • Reporting and insights.
  • Access levels.
  • Pricing and contract terms.

Ask for a sample monthly report: member growth, active members, top topics, issues and suggestions. Good managers turn conversations into insights your business can use.

Write community rules together before launch or renewal. Clear rules make moderation fair and predictable, and members feel safer participating.

Plan coverage for evenings and weekends, when many communities are most active. Slow responses to problems can damage trust quickly.

Ask candidates about a community they grew and a difficult situation they handled, such as a heated argument or a spam wave. Their answers show both their engagement skills and their judgment under pressure, which are the two halves of the job.

How to brief a community manager

  1. The purpose of the community.
  2. Members and their interests.
  3. Platform and current size.
  4. Tone and brand voice.
  5. Rules and sensitive topics.
  6. Goals: support, loyalty, feedback.
  7. Hours and budget.

Define what members get from the community: help, access, connection or fun. A clear purpose guides content, events and moderation decisions.

Agree on escalation paths for serious issues, such as safety concerns, legal threats or product problems. The manager should know exactly who to contact and when.

Give the manager access to people who can answer product questions. Communities thrive when members feel heard and get real answers.

Avoid constant promotion. Members leave communities that feel like advertising channels; value comes first.

What drives the price

  • number of platforms
  • community size and activity
  • hours and coverage
  • events and content creation
  • moderation complexity
  • languages

Light moderation of a small group costs much less than daily management of a large, active server with events and multiple languages.

Weekend and evening coverage often costs more, so plan it into the budget.

Red flags

  • No moderation plan or rules.
  • Promotion-heavy content.
  • Slow response to problems.
  • No reporting or insights.
  • Requests for owner-level access without need.

Review member feedback and activity monthly and adjust. Communities change as they grow, and rules and events should evolve too.

Tips for a smoother project

Thank and recognize helpful members. Active contributors are the heart of most communities.

Give the community manager a voice inside your company. Regular summaries of member feedback shared with product, support and marketing teams turn the community into a source of ideas and early warnings, not just a place to post announcements.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The community has a clear purpose.
  • Rules are written and shared.
  • Coverage times are agreed.
  • Escalation paths are clear.
  • Monthly insights are reported.
