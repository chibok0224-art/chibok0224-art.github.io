The short answer

Among the website developers we feature, starting packages were mostly between 100 and 300 dollars when we checked them in early October 2026, with one custom-website seller starting at 900 dollars. Those starting packages usually cover a small site: a few pages, a standard design and content you supply. Online stores, custom designs, copywriting and integrations add to the price, and hosting, domains and paid apps are separate costs.

What a starting package usually covers

  • A small number of pages, often three to five.
  • Setup on one platform using a theme or template.
  • Your text and images placed on the pages.
  • Basic mobile adjustments and a contact form.
  • One or two revision rounds.

What adds to the price

  • More pages and unique layouts. Ten pages with their own designs cost more than ten pages from one template.
  • A store. Products, variants, shipping rules, taxes and payment setup.
  • Booking, memberships or custom features.
  • Custom design instead of adapting a theme.
  • Copywriting and images. If the developer writes text or sources photos, expect to pay for it.
  • Speed, SEO setup and analytics. Sometimes included, often an extra.
  • Ongoing maintenance after launch.

Costs outside the gig

Hosting or the website builder's subscription, the domain name, premium themes, paid plugins or apps, and Fiverr's buyer service fee at checkout. List these before you compare quotes so you know the real total. Our article on how much a website costs covers these in more detail.

How to estimate your budget

  1. Write down the pages you need and any features (store, booking, blog, languages).
  2. Decide what you will supply yourself: text, photos, logo.
  3. Pick the platform you want to manage after launch.
  4. Send the same short description to two or three sellers and ask what their package includes for it.
  5. Add the outside costs and the checkout fee.

Questions to ask before ordering

  • How many pages and unique layouts does this package include?
  • Which theme or template will you use, and does it need a paid license?
  • Which plugins or apps will you install, and what do they cost each month?
  • Who adds the text and images, and in what format should I send them?
  • How many revision rounds are included, and how long do you support the site after launch?

Common mistakes

  • Comparing only the starting price, not what each package includes.
  • Letting the developer register the domain or hosting in their own name.
  • Starting before the text and images are ready, which stretches the timeline.
  • Forgetting who will update the site after launch.
