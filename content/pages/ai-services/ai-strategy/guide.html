What a good AI strategy package includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Discovery: interviews and process review.
  • Use cases ranked by value and effort.
  • Pilot plan with success measures.
  • Risk and policy recommendations.
  • Tool options with independent reasoning.
  • Roadmap and next steps.
  • Timeline and format of deliverables.

Ask for one or two pilots you can start within weeks. A strategy that leads to small, measurable experiments quickly is worth more than a long report with distant plans.

Ask consultants for an example of a past plan and what happened next. Strategies that led to working pilots, measured results and clear decisions show practical experience, while plans that never left the slide deck show the opposite.

How to brief an AI strategy consultant

  1. Your business and goals.
  2. Teams and processes to review.
  3. Data you have and its sensitivity.
  4. Current AI use, official or not.
  5. Budget and appetite for risk.
  6. Decision makers involved.
  7. Deadline.

Involve the people who do the work. Staff know which tasks are repetitive and where errors happen, and their input makes the use cases realistic and builds support for change.

Be honest about current AI use. Many employees already use AI tools informally, and a good strategy addresses that with clear, practical guidelines.

Ask how success will be measured for each pilot, such as hours saved or faster responses. Measurable goals make it easier to decide what to scale.

Discuss how staff concerns will be handled. People may worry about job changes or being judged by AI-assisted work. A strategy that explains goals openly, invites feedback and offers training usually gets far better adoption than one announced from the top.

What drives the price

  • number of teams involved
  • depth of discovery
  • workshops and training
  • policy and risk work
  • implementation support
  • follow-up reviews

A short workshop with a use case list costs much less than a full program with interviews, policies, pilots and implementation support.

Red flags

  • Generic slides without your processes.
  • No pilot or success measures.
  • Tool recommendations tied to commissions.
  • No privacy or risk discussion.
  • Promises of large savings without evidence.

Review the plan every few months. AI tools and costs change quickly, and a short update keeps priorities realistic.

Tips for a smoother project

Make sure the plan includes simple guidelines employees can follow today, such as which tools are approved and what data must never be entered. Clear rules reduce risk immediately, even while larger projects are still being planned and tested.

Assign an owner for AI work inside your business. Even with an excellent plan, progress stalls without someone responsible for pilots, guidelines and follow-up. The consultant can help define the role, but the owner should be on your team.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • Staff who do the work are involved.
  • Use cases are ranked by value and effort.
  • Pilots have success measures.
  • Privacy and risk rules are included.
  • Tool advice is independent.
