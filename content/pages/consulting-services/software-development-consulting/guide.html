What a good software development consultation includes

Check that the offer clearly states:

  • Review scope: code, architecture, process.
  • Access needs.
  • Findings report with severity.
  • Recommendations with priorities.
  • Effort estimates.
  • Presentation to your team.
  • Follow-up and timeline.

Ask for findings ranked by risk and effort. A short list of critical fixes is more useful than hundreds of minor notes.

Request read-only access where possible, and remove access when the review ends.

How to brief a software development consultant

  1. The product and its users.
  2. Tech stack and hosting.
  3. Team size and skills.
  4. Problems you see.
  5. Goals for the next year.
  6. Budget and constraints.
  7. Deadline.

Explain the business situation behind the request, such as slow releases, rising costs or a planned investment. Business context helps the consultant focus on risks that matter rather than on technical perfection.

Share past decisions and why they were made. Knowing the history prevents advice that ignores real constraints, such as legacy customers or compliance needs.

Involve your lead developers in the review. Their knowledge speeds up the process, and their buy-in makes recommendations far more likely to be implemented.

Ask consultants for examples of reviews they have done and what changed afterward. Practical improvements such as faster deployments or fewer incidents show real value.

Ask for a short summary for non-technical leaders explaining risks and costs in plain language.

Consider asking for a short technical roadmap alongside the review. A sequence of improvements over the next few months, with rough effort for each step, helps you plan budget and hiring and turns a one-time report into a practical path forward for your team.

What drives the price

  • session length and number
  • review or audit depth
  • written report or roadmap
  • size and complexity of your systems
  • follow-up support
  • consultant experience

A short architecture call costs much less than a full code and process audit with a written report and team workshop.

Be cautious of advice to rewrite everything. Rewrites are expensive and risky; good consultants usually suggest gradual improvements first.

Red flags

  • Generic advice without looking at your code.
  • Undisclosed vendor ties.
  • Recommendations that require a full rewrite without reasons.
  • No priorities.
  • Requests for broad admin access.

Plan a follow-up a few months later to check progress on the recommendations.

Tips for a smoother project

Agree on how questions after the report will be handled, such as a short call or written answers.

Ask the consultant to point out what is already working well. Knowing your strengths helps you protect them during changes and gives your team confidence, which matters when a review also brings difficult news.

Keep the report and decisions in your documentation so new team members understand the reasoning.

Quick pre-order checklist

  • The review scope is clear.
  • Access is limited.
  • Findings are prioritized.
  • Advice is independent.
  • Recommendations fit my team.
